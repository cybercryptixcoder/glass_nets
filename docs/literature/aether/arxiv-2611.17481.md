## What they wanted, and why

Activation steering is the cheapest control knob this folder has studied, but it needs the internals: the vector is added to the residual stream at every generation step, which requires weight access and a hook. The question here is whether the *behaviour* a steering vector induces is reachable from the input alone — from a string of tokens appended to a prompt, through an ordinary forward pass, with no edit to hidden states at generation time. If it is, then a black-box user can reproduce a white-box intervention, and, the way the author frames it, a defender can study which inputs reproduce malicious directions. The larger motivation is an equivalence claim between two very different objects: a direction in activation space found by contrasting datasets, and a discrete token sequence found by search. The author is explicit that this is a statement about behaviour, not about mechanism: matching the activations a vector would have produced is treated as a proxy for reproducing what the vector does, and the paper is largely an accounting of where that proxy holds and where it does not.

## What they studied

Four instruction-tuned models spanning three families and two size steps below 5B parameters: Gemma-2-2B-it (26 layers), Qwen3-1.7B (28), Llama-3.2-3B (28) and Qwen3-4B (36). Two behaviours, chosen to be maximally different in kind: answering in German, with a German-versus-English contrastive vector built from Dolly instruction pairs; and anti-refusal (jailbreak), with the refusal vector of Arditi et al. built from harmful versus harmless instructions and then negated. The unit of analysis is the **residual-stream activation at the chat-template filler positions** — the fixed tokens between the end of the user turn and the start of the assistant turn — read at four layers per model chosen by hand in the middle-to-late range (for example layers 10, 14, 18, 22 of Gemma-2-2B). The searched object is a suffix of twenty discrete tokens; a continuous-embedding ("soft") suffix is used as an upper bound on the same objective.

## How they did it

Steering vectors are the difference of class means over contrast pairs, one vector per target layer, stacked, following the contrastive-activation-addition recipe. The target for the search is then built by running a plain prompt, adding the steering vector at strength α to the residual stream at *every filler position* of the four layers, and reading the resulting activations. A suffix is optimised so that its own forward pass reproduces those activations, minimising squared error summed over the filler positions and over the four layers, using the ordinary Greedy Coordinate Gradient discrete search (NanoGCG) with its cross-entropy target string replaced by this activation loss — the method is called ActGCG. Matching happens at the filler positions rather than at the generated response because the response tokens vary run to run and prompt to prompt, whereas the filler tokens are a prompt-independent, multi-position read point directly before generation. Three extensions: a **universal suffix** whose loss and gradient are summed over a training set of prompts; a **model-translation** step in which a larger proposer model (GPT-5.5 or Llama-3.1-8B) rewrites the unreadable suffix into fluent text at the midpoint of optimisation, after which the search resumes; and a **soft suffix** optimised directly in embedding space with Adam. Alongside the main sweep over α and seeds, the paper runs a control that applies the steering vector at the filler positions with no suffix at all (the ceiling the method is imitating), comparisons against vanilla GCG towards a fixed German or affirmative string and against the closest prior method (a cosine-similarity activation-matching suffix, run both faithfully at its single last-token read point and re-run at the filler positions), suffix-length and step-count sweeps, an fp32 versus bf16 weight-precision run, a reverse-distillation experiment that recovers a steering vector from a suffix's induced activation difference and re-applies it, a prefix-instead-of-suffix variant, and two initialisations (unreadable padding versus the readable instruction "Please answer in German").

## What counts as an explanation here

A **reachability statement about an intervention**: the behaviour of a direction in activation space can be reproduced by a token string in input space, up to a loss that depends on the concept. It is deliberately partial and explicitly not mechanistic — nothing in the paper claims that the suffix recruits the same circuit the steering vector does, and the soft-suffix arm is included precisely to price how much of the residual gap is the discrete token space rather than the objective. The reverse direction is tested too, which is what makes it an equivalence claim rather than a one-way imitation: a suffix's induced activation difference is averaged across prompts, and that mean difference, added as an ordinary steering vector, reproduces the behaviour. The deliverable that would predict is the pair of objects together — for a given concept, how far a matched-input intervention is from a matched-activation intervention.

## How they judged success

Behavioural rates against held-out prompts. For German: the fraction of responses classified as German by a language-detection library, with repetitive/degenerate outputs excluded (prompts disjoint from the contrast pairs used to build the vector; thirty test prompts per-prompt, twenty train and thirty test for the universal setting). For anti-refusal: HarmBench-Llama-2-13b as the primary jailbreak judge, and a substring metric that only checks whether the output avoids a set of refusal phrases, with the two reported beside each other. Cross-model transfer is measured by applying one model's suffix to a sibling (Gemma-2-2B → Gemma-1.1-2B and Gemma-2-9B; Qwen3-1.7B → Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen3-4B; Llama-3.2-3B → Llama-3.2-1B). Statistics are limited to five-seed repeats on one model for the α and suffix-length sweeps; most runs use a single fixed seed, which the paper states.

## What they found

**The ceiling is not one.** Applied directly at the filler positions with no suffix, the German steering vector induces German on 90.00% of thirty prompts, while the refusal vector at α = 1.5 reaches 50.00% HarmBench jailbreak and 90.91% on the substring metric, and never exceeds that. Because ActGCG optimises towards the vector's own activations, every downstream number is capped by these.

**German: the equivalence largely holds.** ActGCG reaches 86.7% on Gemma-2-2B and 96.7% on Qwen3-1.7B, against 36.7% and 83.3% for vanilla GCG towards a fixed German sentence, and 40.0% (faithful) / 53.3% (filler-position read point) for the cosine activation-matching prior on Gemma-2-2B — a per-prompt gap against the strongest baseline of about fifty points. The single universal suffix holds 90.0% on its twenty training prompts and 73.3% on thirty unseen ones (Gemma-2-2B), 95.0% / 80.0% (Qwen3-1.7B) and 95.0% / 96.7% (Llama-3.2-3B). Cross-model transfer is sharply asymmetric by size: Gemma-2-2B's suffix gives 65.0% on Gemma-1.1-2B but 5.0% on Gemma-2-9B; Qwen3-1.7B's gives 95.0% on Qwen2.5-1.5B but 60.0% on Qwen3-4B; Llama-3.2-3B's gives 95.0% on Llama-3.2-1B.

**Anti-refusal: the equivalence is metric-dependent.** Per-prompt ActGCG reaches 27.3% HarmBench with 90.9% substring on Gemma-2-2B, against vanilla GCG's 13.6% / 36.4%, but on Qwen3-1.7B it lands at 40.9% / 90.9% against vanilla GCG's 50.0% / 100.0% — worse on both. The substring metric runs roughly twice the HarmBench rate throughout, and the paper's headline sentence for the concept is that it succeeds under the laxer measure and only partially under the stricter one. Universal refusal suffixes keep the same shape: strong training numbers (Gemma-2-2B 45.5% HarmBench / 95.5% substring), weaker held-out ones (31.8% / 63.6%), and collapse on larger siblings (Gemma-2-9B 0.0% / 4.5%; Qwen3-4B 9.1% / 22.7%; Llama-3.2-1B 4.5% / 18.2%).

**The discrete constraint is a real but modest tax.** The soft suffix reaches 96.67% at α = 1.0 on Gemma-2-2B and 93.33% at α = 2.0, against 86.7% for the discrete search, and on Qwen3-1.7B 93.33% against 96.7% — i.e. on one model the discrete suffix already matches the continuous upper bound. Projecting the optimised soft embeddings back to their nearest vocabulary embeddings produced a suffix with zero German responses, so the continuous solution does not discretise.

**Matching is not sufficient for behaving.** The author names this as a limitation rather than a result — a suffix can track the target activations closely and still fail to jailbreak — and the loss-tracking figures are consistent with it: distance falls roughly logarithmically and gains nothing past about a hundred steps, while more steps (up to a larger budget) still buy HarmBench rate (Gemma-2-2B 27.3% → 31.8%, Qwen3-1.7B 40.9% → 54.5%).

**Odd inversions.** Initialising from the readable German instruction makes no difference at all within a family (Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.2-3B identical, Qwen models slightly worse), which the author reads as the search optimising positions independently without preserving coherence. A prefix, even one allowed to match prompt positions as well as filler positions, does worse than a suffix (66.67% best versus 86.7%). fp32 weights give 70.00% against bf16's 53.33% at one α, but the paper declines to use fp32 because the gap sits inside its measured seed variance. Reverse distillation works: mean steering vectors recovered from suffixes reach 80.00% of the original German behaviour at α = 2.0, per-prompt recovered vectors only 63.33%, against 86.7% for the forward direction.

## Limits and open problems they name

The headline limitation is that the answer is concept-dependent and only two concepts were tested, so nothing follows about a third; the two already differ enormously. Scale is small in both models and prompts (thirty German prompts; thirty-eight to fifty-six for refusal), most runs use one seed, and the layers and read positions were chosen by hand after a coarse check rather than optimised. The discrete search bounds how well the target can be matched, and the target itself is bounded by a difference-of-means vector the paper does not claim is the best available. The author also flags that reaching the activations does not guarantee the behaviour, that the best construction of a steering vector remains open, and that the refusal numbers depend heavily on the judge — with the substring metric explicitly called a poor proxy for safety, since avoiding refusal phrases is not the same as producing harmful content. The one external reproach in the paper: the closest prior method's reported jailbreak rates could not be reproduced when its outputs were re-judged with the author's classifier, which is attributed to the choice of judge.

## What we take from it

A **framing and a cheap instrument**, plus a warning that matters for our white-box model. The framing: a white-box intervention has an input-space twin, and the size of the gap between the two is a measurable property of the concept rather than a property of either instrument. If we ever claim that a feature or direction "causes" a behaviour, the input-matching arm is the natural falsifier — identical outputs from a prompt-only construction mean the behaviour was already reachable, and only the depth of the equivalence, not its existence, is ours. The instrument is reusable as-is: match activations at chat-template filler positions rather than at generated responses, because those positions are prompt-independent and multi-position, and match several layers at once rather than one. The warning is the ceiling discipline, which this folder keeps re-learning in new costumes: the imitating method is bounded by the vector's own effect, so both are printed first (90.00% for German, 50.00% HarmBench for refusal) and every number below is read against them — without that pair the 86.7% and the 27.3% are uninterpretable. Two smaller habits: the readable-versus-padding initialisation result says the optimiser is not exploiting linguistic structure, so do not read a searched suffix as a sentence, and the mismatch between falling activation loss and rising behavioural success says an activation-distance objective is a proxy whose residual is not noise but a different quantity.
