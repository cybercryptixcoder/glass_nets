# Revealing Pathway Interactions in Network Computations with Complete Path Contribution

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.26101
- **Authors / organisation:** Shenghua Liu, Youssef Faragalla, Surya Ganguli, Stephen A. Baccus; Stanford University (Physics, Applied Physics, Neurobiology, Neurosciences Interdepartmental Program)
- **Date:** 2026-11-19
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26101
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v1 HTML plus the v1 PDF text layer for the numbers the HTML build stripped)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit-level interpretability in this literature is assembled backwards: components are scored in isolation, one layer at a time, and the scores are combined after the fact into a "circuit". The authors argue that this construction cannot represent the thing that actually decides a network's output, which is how entire causal chains from input to output interact with one another — in particular how excitatory chains are vetoed by inhibitory ones that run in parallel. Because the number of such chains is exponential in depth, no method had been able to measure them directly rather than by pairwise proxy. Their goal is a decomposition of the output change into per-chain contributions that is exact — the parts must sum to the whole at every level of granularity — so that cancellation can be quantified on a single scale and compared across levels. The larger aim is a unified framework in which neurons, edges, channels, arbitrary subnetworks and full input-to-output paths are all explained by one algorithm.

## What they studied

Feedforward networks only, in three tiers. (1) A toy model approximating continuous XOR with two inputs and three neurons, used purely to make the level-dependence of the explanation visible. (2) A 3-layer ReLU MLP on MNIST, 128 neurons per hidden layer, trained to over 95% test accuracy with a standard PyTorch/Adam pipeline, analysed through the 16,384 three-neuron-deep paths downstream of a single first-layer neuron chosen because it is *active* but contributes little ("silent"). (3) Two real models: Torchvision ViT-B/16 on ImageNet-1k, where paths are grouped by the four ways a path can treat a pair of blocks (through a head or bypassing it via the residual stream, giving wire-wire, head-wire, wire-head, head-head), and a 3-layer CNN fit to mouse retinal ganglion cell electrophysiology with 16 channel types per hidden layer, giving 256 channel-pair paths per cell. The unit of analysis is the **path** — a full chain of neurons/edges/units from input to output — with neurons, edges and channels recovered as marginal sums.

## How they did it

The method starts from Integrated Gradients' completeness identity for the output difference relative to a baseline input, and then applies the chain rule *all the way down*: the output change is expanded as a sum over every path of a chained-gradient term, where each factor is the derivative from one layer's unit to the next, all evaluated along the straight line from baseline to input and integrated over that line. Summing path terms that terminate at the same input pixel recovers Integrated Gradients; summing those terminating at the same hidden neuron recovers neuron conductance; summing those through a convolutional channel recovers the recent CODEC method. A subnetwork is defined by per-layer masks on neurons and edges, and its contribution is the sum over paths that the masks admit, computed with a handful of backward passes by placing the masks in the backward graph. For scale, the top-k path search is formulated as a best-first branch-and-bound over a tree of path prefixes, analogous to A*: a suffix bound is precomputed once per integration step by a backward pass, giving each prefix an upper bound on the contribution of any completion, and prefixes are popped and expanded in bound order, so complete paths emerge in rank order and the search can be stopped early. Empirically the search is sublinear in integration steps and images, roughly linear in width, superlinear in depth and approaching linear in the number of requested paths. Third, they run a battery of design controls the folder rarely sees: four null baselines (black, gray, white, dataset mean), an integration-step sweep against a 512-step reference, three random seeds for the MLP, and — for the retina — a model retrained from another seed, a baseline swap, all 36 cells rather than one, and a checkpoint sweep over training. Finally, path contributions are compared directly against path patching: per-path effect distributions over 3,001 simulated contexts, and bulk patching of positive paths, negative paths, and all paths against the corresponding summed contributions.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **complete additive decomposition of the output change into path terms**, plus a small set of "circuits" obtained by PCA over the matrix of path contributions. The decomposition is exact rather than partial: by hierarchical completeness, the contributions of any family of subnetworks whose path sets partition a larger one sum to that larger one's contribution, and the sum over the fully-permissive masks equals the output difference from the baseline. The interpretation is not a text description of a path, nor a surrogate model; it is a signed, cancellable budget — gross excitation, gross inhibition, and the net they leave — reported on a common scale at every granularity, with the strongest paths found by exact search rather than heuristic beam search. It predicts something concrete and checkable: the summed contribution of a set of paths should equal the effect of patching that set, and the paper tests exactly that.

## How they judged success

Four families of check. (a) **Completeness error**: the difference between the sum over all paths and the actual output change, reported against integration step count — under 1% of the output difference already at the smallest step counts tried, and negligible at the 64 steps used for the MLP; for ViT-B class means at 512 steps it is 0.2% (pill bottle), 0.05% (mailbag), 0.1% (tape player) and 1.1% (convertible), against 1.8% and 0.9% at 128 and 256 steps for pill bottle. (b) **Top-k overlap** between path rankings under different baselines and under different integration step counts (>0.8 across all three alternative baselines; >0.9 on the vast majority of images at 64 steps against the 512-step reference). (c) **Patching agreement**: per-path contribution against the distribution of patching effects over 3,001 contexts, and bulk-patch effects plotted against summed E, summed I and net contribution, expected to lie on the diagonal. (d) **Coverage**: the fraction of the top-k paths needed to reach half of the output change, compared with the same quantity computed over top-k neurons and top-k edges, plus a beam-search baseline that has to enumerate about 100 times more paths than requested (and 100 times more again over neurons) to recover the same set. Retinal results are additionally validated against a split-half noise ceiling, a permutation test over ON/OFF labels (4,000 shuffles), linear CKA against a time-shifted control, and cosine agreement between the integrated field and the plain gradient receptive field.

## What they found

**Cancellation is the dominant structure, and it only becomes visible at path level.** The "silent" first-layer neuron h1[13] has a small net contribution when images of 3 are read to logit 3, and the path decomposition shows why: substantial excitatory and inhibitory path sums that almost exactly cancel. At the neuron level the downstream h2 effects are mostly single-signed; at the path level the gross activity is much larger than the residual, and the residual is what the neuron-level method sees. Across all 12,400 neuron-scenario pairs the distribution shifts to a larger gross contribution at the same net, giving a cancellation factor roughly three times larger at the path level than at the edge level, and the distributions from three random seeds nearly coincide.

**Roughly 0.1% of top paths explain half the output.** In a network with more than a billion paths, fewer than 0.1% of paths (ordered by contribution) cover half of the output difference, against around 1% of edges and close to 10% of a layer's neurons. That is about an order of magnitude more sparse by fraction at each step down in granularity, which is the paper's practical justification for the top-k algorithm: a heuristic beam search over important edges needs to enumerate roughly 100 times more paths than requested to find the true top ones.

**In the retina, one cell's response is a handful of excitatory/inhibitory motifs.** For the highlighted ganglion cell, the first four principal components of the 256-path contribution matrix carry 88% of the variance; each component implements a distinct receptive field, and excitation and inhibition play different roles in each. Inhibitory paths peak at the same moment as excitatory ones, so inhibition appears to sharpen the firing event rather than suppress it tonically. Across all 36 cells the first PC carries a median of 44% and the first four 82% of the path-contribution variance, gross drive exceeds net output by a median factor of 6.2 (range 2.7–12.8) with inhibition supplying 45% of the gross drive, and cells of the same polarity share circuits (mean absolute cosine of first-PC loadings 0.35 within type against 0.18 across, p < 0.00025 over 4,000 label shuffles) while ON and OFF circuit means are nearly orthogonal (cosine −0.07).

**Contribution is a good predictor of what patching a group of paths does.** Bulk patching of positive paths, negative paths and all 16,384 paths falls closely on the diagonal against summed E, summed I and net contribution across 100 input-output scenarios, and per-path contributions track the mean patching effect over contexts. The relation to Shapley values is stated precisely: on a multilinear network the path contribution *is* the Shapley value, on the XOR toy model the contribution and the Shapley value agree while remove-one and keep-one patching do not sum correctly, and in general the contribution is described as an efficient, complete summary of the distribution of a path's causal effect over contexts.

**The algorithm reaches a 12-layer MLP with 128 neurons per layer, finding the top paths in 11 minutes on a laptop.** The reported caveat is honest: peak memory on that run reached 80 GB on a 36 GB machine, so the runtime is inflated by compression and paging.

## Limits and open problems they name

No asymptotic worst-case guarantee for the top-k search, and the authors note that no such algorithm is known for DAG longest-path searches with sum-of-product path lengths, so they cannot say how tight the bound stays at greater depth. Very deep and very wide networks are untested: a pairwise Jacobian for GPT-2 small is estimated at 2.5 TB in float32, which makes matrix-free on-the-fly Jacobians necessary and trades time for space, and memory rather than time is the binding constraint. Coarser-than-neuron granularity (attention heads, channels) is harder to bound because a node then holds a set of units with different bounds. Their engineering workarounds — expanding only the most promising child plus a shared "sibling" entry, refining bounds lazily, and using signed interval bounds — are reported as slower by a factor of 1.8 to 12 in exchange for a much smaller queue (about 200 K peak entries against 3.7 M). Interpretations stay structural: they explicitly decline to assign semantics to the ViT E-I motifs, and the fact that head-head contribution patterns are partly shared across classes and differ across block pairs is flagged as interesting and unexplained.

## What we take from it

**A guarantee, and the measuring stick it enables.** Hierarchical completeness is the property that licenses a quantitative comparison of cancellation across levels, and it is the reason the paper's central claim — that the residual is small because of cancellation rather than because the parts are weak — is falsifiable at all. The rule for us: any decomposition we report at one granularity, and then report again at a coarser one, must be checked to sum to the identical quantity; without that check, two levels' "importance" numbers are not on a common scale, and a small number at one level cannot be distinguished from genuine cancellation. This is the sharpest available answer to run 740's decomposition-preservation question and to run 741's claim that per-component scores cannot predict joint behaviour.

**The signed gross/net budget as the standard output form.** Reporting only the net contribution of a component is what makes cancellation invisible: the same net can be the sum of weak same-signed parts or the residue of strong opposed parts, and the paper observes both across neurons and scenarios. Rule for us: every intervention result we publish is reported as (gross positive, gross negative, net) at the finest granularity we can afford, because we cannot otherwise tell which of those two situations a reader is looking at.

**Patching is validated against exactly the statistic we would use to predict it.** The bulk-patching diagonal is the closest thing in this folder to our actual target — predicting the outcome of a group intervention from a cheap per-component quantity — and the paper is careful about the one place it fails: remove-one and keep-one patching do not sum to the output, only the complete path contributions do. That is the reason single-context patching is a bad instrument, stated as an arithmetic fact rather than a caution.

**An exact top-k search is feasible where the ranking is what matters.** Branch-and-bound with a precomputed per-step suffix bound is reusable against any product-form path score, and the anytime property (results returned so far are correct at any stopping point) is what makes a bounded budget usable. Cross-reference run 734 (a random shortlist beats a predictor, so selectors are only as good as their shortlist) and run 741 (group terms dwarf pairwise ones).

**Cost warning.** The GPT-2 memory figure means path-level decomposition at single-neuron resolution does not transfer to the models we care about without matrix-free Jacobians or coarser units — and the coarser units are exactly where the bound becomes loose.
