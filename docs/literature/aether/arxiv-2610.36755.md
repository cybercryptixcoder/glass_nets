# Robust LLM Data Selection via Content-Invariant Gradient Attribution

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.36755
- **Authors / organisation:** Jia Gu, Liang Pang (corresponding author), Huawei Shen; Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences and University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing
- **Date:** 2026-10-27 (v1)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36755
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML v1: abstract, sections 1-6 with Tables 1-6 and Figures 1-5 read through captions and the numeric sentences, Appendix A.1-A.4 including Table 7 and the full prompt-variant templates in Table 8, Appendix B.1-B.3 including Table 9, Appendix C, Appendix D/Table 10)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Gradient-based data attribution ranks training examples by how closely their parameter updates align with updates induced by a target validation set, and it is used to choose a small subset of instruction data for fine-tuning. The authors observe that this ranking moves when the validation example is rewritten in different words while its answer-bearing content stays the same: a paraphrase that changes nothing a human would call meaningful can change which training examples get selected. They name this **surface-form bias** and treat it as a robustness failure of a scoring function rather than as noise. The larger goal is a data-value score that responds to content and not to incidental wording, so that selection survives the formatting diversity of real corpora. Their answer is a framework that deliberately restricts where the score is computed — one layer, chosen for stability across paraphrases — and then fuses that ranking with a semantic similarity ranking.

## What they studied

Two instruction-tuned backbones: LLaMA-2-7B (32 layers) and Qwen3-4B-Base (36 layers), fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128, alpha 512, dropout 0.1, one epoch, sequence length 2048, lr 2e-5). The candidate corpus is a merge of FLAN v2, CoT, Alpaca-GPT4, Dolly, OASST1 and GSM8K — per Table 7, 100000 / 100000 / 52002 / 15011 / 55668 / 7473 items respectively, roughly 338k examples. Five target benchmarks: TyDiQA, MMLU, BBH, GSM8K, HellaSwag. The unit of analysis is unusual for this folder: **not a neuron or feature but a layer-level parameter-update vector (an Adam-preconditioned gradient) computed on each candidate example**, and the decision being scored is which 5% of examples to keep.

## How they did it

A warm-up LoRA fine-tune on a random 5% sample produces one checkpoint, and every later step uses only that checkpoint's optimizer state. For each validation example the authors hand-write **five surface-form variants** that keep the question/answer content and change only framing — the exact templates are printed per benchmark in Table 8 (for example, one MMLU variant opens "The following are multiple choice questions (with answers) about {S}." and another "Select the best option for the following question."). At every layer *l* they compute the mean update magnitude across examples and variants (μ_l) and the mean squared deviation of each variant's update vector from that example's variant-average (σ_l²), z-score both across layers, and combine them into a consistent-influential score S_l^ci = μ̂_l − α·σ̂_l with α = 0.7; the argmax layer is the one used. Candidates are then scored by cosine similarity between their update vector and the validation set's update vector **at that single layer** (Grad-Score), plus a semantic cosine similarity from MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings (Sem-Score). The two rankings are fused by reciprocal rank fusion with smoothing r = 60 and a task-specific weight β; β is set per validation set from an ANOVA-style signal-to-noise ratio — between-example variance of variant-averaged updates over within-example variant variance — and from Sem^Top, the mean of the top K = 1000 semantic scores, so a task whose gradients already separate examples gets less semantic weight and a task whose semantic scores are already saturated also gets less semantic weight. Top-5% is selected by the fused score and the base model is retrained on it. Baselines: LESS and TracIn (global gradients, four checkpoints), Cos (semantic only), IFD and SelectIT (information-based), plus Random and Full-data.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **scoring procedure plus one summary statistic per layer**, not a description of what the model represents. The explanatory content is the S_l^ci curve over layers and the claim that its peak overlaps the layers whose gradients support good selection (Figure 5, on MMLU/LLaMA-2-7B). The paper is explicit that the middle-layer preference is an observation with a hypothesis attached (shallow layers carry lexical/syntactic detail, deep layers output-format detail) rather than a demonstrated mechanism, and it does not open the layer up to say which features the retained update direction encodes. Prediction exists in a narrow sense: the score predicts held-out downstream task accuracy after retraining. It does not predict what a specific intervention inside the model will do.

## How they judged success

Two measures, reported separately. **Selection consistency**: mean pairwise Jaccard similarity between the top-5% training subsets selected under each of the five validation variants — with five variants that is the average of the 10 pairs, stated in Appendix A.3; CIGA, LESS and TracIn are all scored on the same variant set. **Downstream utility**: TyDiQA F1, MMLU and HellaSwag accuracy, BBH and GSM8K exact match after retraining the base model on the selected subset, with standard deviations over three runs and Δ_Rand as the macro-average gain over Random. Significance is a p < 0.05 test of CIGA against Random on each task and on the macro-average. Ablations: CIGA-G (drop the semantic arm, gradient-only from the selected layer), the number of attribution layers k ∈ {1,3,5}, the number of variants n ∈ {3,4,5}, the selection ratio 1%-10% on Qwen3, and α and γ sweeps read through Figures 6-7 and Table 9. Cost is tabulated per stage in GPU-hours on one A100 (Table 6).

## What they found

Surface-form bias is real and large for global gradients: on HellaSwag, LESS reaches 22.64% (LLaMA-2-7B) and 18.47% (Qwen3) mean pairwise Jaccard across variants, TracIn 28.24% and 17.25%, while CIGA reaches 49.29% and 36.60% — roughly double. Averaged over five tasks CIGA reaches 58.33% and 48.68% Jaccard against best-baseline 49.06% and 40.03%, gains of 9.27 and 8.65 points. The gain is not uniform: on LLaMA-2-7B/TyDiQA CIGA is 42.40 against LESS's 41.40, and on Qwen3/TyDiQA CIGA's 50.03 is *below* LESS's 62.75. Downstream, CIGA has the best macro-average of any selection method on both backbones, +2.11 points over Random on LLaMA-2-7B (47.84 average) and +1.04 on Qwen3 (73.40), and it beats full-data fine-tuning on TyDiQA and MMLU for LLaMA-2-7B; on Qwen3 the Full configuration itself sits 1.12 points below Random, so "more data is worse" holds there before any selection is applied. The layer-only ablation does most of the work: CIGA-G already reaches 56.44% / 46.96% Jaccard and 47.15 / 73.26 downstream, and the semantic arm adds 1.89 and 1.72 Jaccard points. Multi-layer aggregation is worth almost nothing — the best k = 5 setting beats the single layer by 0.19 and 0.22 points on average. Selected layers are stable: 12-16 of 32 for LLaMA-2 and 18-23 of 36 for Qwen3, with Qwen3 picking the identical layer on every task and across seeds. Cost falls from 74.1 to 21.4 GPU-hours (their stated 71.1% reduction, 3.46x), because one checkpoint and one layer replace four checkpoints and all layers.

## Limits and open problems they name

Gradient computation still requires a forward and backward pass over the entire candidate corpus, so tens of millions of examples remains a wall; they suggest compressing or approximating the gradient features. The method depends on a target-task validation set existing, and on a human hand-writing five variants of it — there is no unsupervised or self-supervised way to get the invariance, which they name as future work. Beyond their own list: the invariance is imposed only on the attribution score, never on the training data itself, and the paper offers no evidence that the surviving ranking is the *most useful* ranking rather than merely the most stable one — the two measures are correlated (CIGA wins both) but never separated into a consistency-versus-quality scatter. TyDiQA on Qwen3 is a counterexample to the consistency story that is reported as a row and not discussed. No seed-variance study of the selected layer beyond the qualitative sentence, and the 0.04-point difference between CIGA-G and CIGA on Qwen3 downstream (73.26 against 73.40) is far inside the reported standard deviations, so the semantic arm's *utility* contribution is not established even where its consistency contribution is.

## What we take from it

- **A robustness gate we can adopt verbatim**: score any attribution our white-box model produces on a set of semantics-preserving edits, and report the agreement between the rankings produced under different edits as a number (here Jaccard over selected sets). This is the cheapest disconfirmation available for a probe or a circuit claim, and it is the first entry in this folder where the invariance is *installed* in the estimator rather than tested after the fact — the estimator searches over a family of candidate measurement sites for one that is both strong and stable, which maps directly onto our "which layer do we read, and is the reading stable" question.
- **Stability as a selection criterion alongside strength, encoded as a penalty** (z-scored strength minus α times z-scored dispersion across variants). This is a concrete way to pick a read-out location: not the layer with the largest signal, but the layer whose signal is largest after paying for its instability. Our layer choice has so far been driven by effect size alone.
- **The paper's own strongest admission, which is the warning we need**: the layer-only ablation captures nearly all of both the stability gain and the performance gain, so most of the reported improvement is attributable to *where* the gradient is read and almost none to the sophisticated fusion module. Any method of ours whose headline rests on a combination step should be ablated down to its location choice before the combination is credited.
- **The ANOVA-type ratio — between-example variance to within-example variant variance — is a reusable diagnostic**, and it is the cleanest formalisation this folder has seen of a question it keeps asking informally: is a measured difference about the stimulus or about the measurement.
