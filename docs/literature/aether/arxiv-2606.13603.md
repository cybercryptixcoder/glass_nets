# Beyond the Commitment Boundary: Probing Epiphenomenal Chain-of-Thought in Large Reasoning Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.13603

## What they wanted, and why
Chain-of-thought is the main way inference-time compute is spent, yet nobody knows which of its steps actually change the answer. The authors want a *causal* measure of how much each sentence of a reasoning trace contributes to the model settling on its final answer, and from that a description of the shape of answer formation: does the model converge gradually, or does it switch once and keep writing. The larger goal is a monitoring story — if the trace can drift away from the computation while still sounding like deliberation, a monitor reading only the trace is blind, so the paper asks whether the point of commitment can be read off internal states instead. They also want the payoff that makes the measurement more than descriptive: if a cheap read-out marks the exact step where commitment happens, the rest of the trace can be skipped.

## What they studied
Three open-weight reasoning models from different families — `gpt-oss-20b` (24 layers), `Qwen3-14B` (40 layers) and `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it` (30 layers) — on four benchmarks: MATH-500, AIME 2025, ZebraLogic (open-ended logic) and GPQA-Diamond (multiple choice). The unit of analysis is the **sentence-level CoT step**: each step gets a scalar "step confidence" derived from the probability of the final answer, a three-way label (no-guess, mid-guess, final-guess), and later a hidden state at the sentence's last token. The probe reads single sentence-boundary activations over a short causal window, at one layer per model; nothing is trained inside the models.

## How they did it
For each retained trace they re-run the model on growing prefix-lengths of its own chain-of-thought, each time closing the thinking block and appending a task-specific forcing suffix, then greedily decoding an answer. Prefix *i* is scored by the maximum probability of any answer token at the forced answer position, and the decoded answer is compared to the model's full-trajectory answer with a symbolic verifier (math-verify for mathematics, canonicalised option letters for multiple choice). A step is called no-guess if its confidence sits below a per-trace threshold placed halfway between the no-CoT and full-CoT confidence, mid-guess if it is above that threshold but the decoded answer differs from the final one, and final-guess if it matches. The commitment boundary is the arg-max of the step-to-step confidence increase. Retained traces are only those where the no-CoT baseline is wrong and the full-CoT confidence gain exceeds 0.5, and traces where two semantically different answers share a first token at any prefix are dropped. The boundary is then stress-tested by corrupting number tokens on either side of it and decoding again. Finally, a small causal attention probe — one learned query, a value projection, a local window over the last *w* sentence representations, and a three-way softmax — is trained on MATH-500 sentence activations at a fixed layer with class-weighted cross-entropy, and used both as a locator and as an online early-exit signal (exit after *k* consecutive final-guess predictions).

## What counts as an explanation here
A **step-level causal attribution over the trace plus a linear probe**. The explanation is not a description of the model's reasoning in words but a decomposition of the trace into a causal part (steps whose corruption or removal changes the answer) and an epiphenomenal part (steps that do not), together with a single decoded coordinate — the boundary position — that is meant to be predictable from activations alone. It is partial by design: it says where answer formation completes, not why the answer is what it is. It is genuinely predictive in one narrow sense: the probe predicts a future property of the trace (whether the answer will still be the same) from a past activation.

## How they judged success
For the boundary: normalised step-confidence is bimodal; the largest single-step confidence gain is compared to the second-largest (median ratio with 95% CI); the boundary's relative position in the trace is reported per model and per dataset; and a corruption test measures how often the answer survives numeric perturbation above versus below the boundary at 20% and 50% corruption rates. For the probe, they deliberately do *not* report sentence classification accuracy, arguing it does not price localisation. Instead they report detection rate within a tolerance window of *k* = 2 consecutive final-guess predictions, early-fire rate (firing before the true boundary), miss rate, exact-boundary rate at *k* = 0, median detection delay, median fraction of CoT tokens saved, and class-2 precision/recall/F1. Baselines for early exiting are full CoT, no CoT, and fixed-percentage truncation at 50/70/80/90/95%. Out-of-distribution generalisation is measured by training only on MATH-500 and testing on the other three benchmarks. Sweeps cover layer depth percentile, window size and aggregation (last token versus mean).

## What they found
- **Confidence is bimodal**, mass concentrating at the no-CoT and full-CoT levels, so the answer appears abruptly rather than growing steadily.
- **One sentence typically does the work**: the median ratio of the largest step confidence gain to the second-largest is 4.6 (95% CI 4.3–4.9, n = 3,119 traces, `gpt-oss-20b` on MATH-500).
- **The boundary comes early and is model-family-driven**: `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it` reaches its final answer after 13–23% of CoT tokens, `gpt-oss-20b` after 43–68%, with up to 87% of generated reasoning tokens falling after stabilisation in some configurations.
- **Corruption separates the two halves**: at 20% numeric corruption the pre-boundary prefix preserves the answer in only 61% of runs against 95% for the post-boundary tail; at 50% the split is 27% versus 72%. The unperturbed prefix reproduces the full answer on every retained trace, which is the control that licenses the comparison.
- **Pre-boundary reasoning is real**: mid-guesses appear with an average token likelihood of 0.69 and are revised; traces containing mid-guesses are more than twice as long as direct commits (688 ± 57 versus 306 ± 24 tokens), and length increases monotonically with the number of mid-guesses on MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond and ZebraLogic (Mann–Whitney, medians 252 → above 1,000 tokens on MATH-500) but not on AIME 2025.
- **Mid-guesses recur across independently sampled traces of the same question** (median Jaccard 0.71; median standard deviation of relative position 0.005 against a permutation baseline of 0.011).
- **Hedging does not mark the boundary**: sentence-initial markers like "wait" and "but" are about as frequent on both sides, with no marker shifting consistently across the three models.
- **The probe localises well and transfers**: in-distribution detection is 92% / 62% / 98% for `gpt-oss-20b` / `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it` / `Qwen3-14B` at *k* = 2, with 6% / 16% / 2% early fire, 0% / 25% / 0% miss, and 35% / 55% / 48% median tokens saved; out-of-distribution detection ranges 69–97%, 98–99% and 78–98% across the three held-out benchmarks.
- **Best sweep configurations**: layer 23 with a 256-token window (early fire 6.87%, detect 93.13%, zero miss, boundary class-2 precision 91.59%, 34.5% tokens saved) for `gpt-oss-20b`; layer 29 with a 64-token window (early fire 5%, detect 95%, precision 99%) for `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it`; layer 35 with a 64-token window (early fire 5%, detect 95%, precision 97%) for `Qwen3-14B`. Mean aggregation over the window is worse than the last sentence's state in every tested configuration.
- **Probe exits beat fixed truncation at every operating point**: on MATH-500, fixed truncation falls from 80% to 57% full-CoT accuracy while the probe at *k* = 1 keeps 89% of accuracy and saves 39% of tokens; at *k* = 5 it reaches 98% of accuracy at 26% savings. On the held-out benchmarks the MATH-500-trained probe saves 25% / 6% / 35% (AIME / ZebraLogic / GPQA) with at most an 11-point loss on ZebraLogic (97% → 86%), and the fixed-exit baselines lose more everywhere.

## Limits and open problems they name
Greedy decoding plus an answer-forcing suffix measures only "what the model would commit to if forced at step *i*"; a step that shifts probability mass without changing the arg-max is invisible, and the suffix itself is a soft intervention. The sample is filtered to problems where CoT helps and answers are tokenisation-distinguishable, and first-token collisions (~9% average, but 56.67% for `gemma-4-26B-A4B-it` on AIME 2025) remove traces. Sentence granularity is coarse, so reported boundary positions are upper bounds on precision. Coverage is three open-weight models on maths, logic and science multiple choice — no agentic, tool-use or long-horizon tasks, and no closed models. The probes are supervised on labels produced by the very procedure they replace, so they can inherit its biases. Early exiting loses some accuracy, early fire reaches 21% on `Qwen3-14B` out of distribution, and per-task exit calibration is left open. Crucially, the authors warn against reading the post-boundary tail as functionally useless: it may calibrate confidence, support later turns, or serve training-time objectives not visible in one inference pass.

## What we take from it
- **A measurement we can reuse directly**: truncation-based step attribution with an answer-forcing suffix and a symbolic equivalence check is cheap, deterministic, and gives an instance-level label ("same answer as the full trace") that our own read-outs can be scored against — the same property the folder valued in the model-organism entry, obtained here with no training at all.
- **The two-sided corruption design is the template**: never report the "the tail is redundant" result alone; report the mirror test on the prefix, the unperturbed control that the prefix reproduces the answer, and the two at a shared corruption rate. Their number, 95% survival above the boundary versus 61% below at 20% corruption, is only interpretable because the prefix arm is there.
- **A reporting rule worth adopting**: they refuse sentence-level accuracy for the locator and substitute windowed detection, early-fire, miss and delay, on the argument that accuracy does not price where the prediction fired. Our probe write-ups report accuracy and AUC; neither prices a *timing* claim.
- **A warning about supervision loops**: the probe predicts labels made by the truncation procedure it is meant to replace. This is the folder's re-deriving "a read-out fitted on a constructed label inherits the label's definition", stated as a limitation by the authors themselves, and it applies to every read-out in our work that is trained against a pipeline's output.
- **A framing**: a state can be *linearly decodable and intervenable* — the probe both reads the boundary and is used to stop generation — without being describable, and the same sentence-level state that carries "am I done" is invisible in the trace's own hedging vocabulary. That splits text-level from state-level monitoring with numbers attached, on three model families.
- **The cheapest negative in the paper**: hedging markers do not shift across the boundary. Any monitor we build on surface deliberation cues owes this check.
