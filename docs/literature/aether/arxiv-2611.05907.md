# Does Your Trial Simulator Know What the Drug Does? An Intervention-Blind Test of Interventional Validity on 7,207 Registered Trials

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.05907
- **Authors / organisation:** Alicem Koyun; Westcliff University
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05907
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Healthcare "world models" are increasingly pitched as simulators of clinical trials, and surveys place them on a ladder that runs from temporal prediction through action-conditioned prediction to counterfactual rollouts. The author's complaint is that a rung is assigned by how a paper reads, not by measurement. A model that predicts a held-out trial arm well may simply have learned what a responder rate looks like in a given disease, or how large a placebo response an enriched population produces — neither of which involves the drug. What decision support needs is the between-arm difference, and for that quantity held-out accuracy is not evidence of understanding. So the paper turns "interventional validity" into a number that costs one extra training run or prompt. The stated motivation is a specific gap named by a survey (Qazi et al., 2025): simulated futures may look realistic and still be wrong about treatment effects. The author also screened 447 clinically grounded NeurIPS 2025 workshop papers, found 26 that model a trajectory or a trial arm, and reports that none both represents the intervention and validates against a real trial arm at abstract level.

## What they studied

Three kinds of system, on one benchmark of real counterfactuals. The registry-trained simulator is a histogram gradient-boosting regressor over TF-IDF text reduced to 120 SVD components plus ordinal categoricals and numerics, run over five seeds. The language models are three prompted systems asked for a point estimate and a 90% interval as JSON: Claude Sonnet 5 ("large"), Claude Haiku 4.5 ("small"), and Llama 3.1 70B Instruct with a documented December 2023 cutoff, at temperature 0. The unit of analysis is a **within-trial contrast**: one arm's primary-endpoint result minus its comparator's, as a risk difference for binary endpoints or a standardised mean difference for continuous ones. The benchmark is built from ClinicalTrials.gov: completed phase 2/2-3/3 interventional studies with posted results, filtered to those with a single scored primary outcome and per-group denominators, arms with at least 20 analysed participants. The released table (v2.1) holds 9,996 contrasts from 7,207 trials — 4,014 risk differences and 5,982 standardised differences, 7,669 with an inert control and 2,327 with an active comparator. The split is temporal on results-posting year: 7,789 training contrasts posted before 2023, 2,207 test contrasts posted in 2023 or later, of which 878 binary (655 trials) and 1,329 continuous (978 trials).

## How they did it

The construction has three parts, and the middle one is the instrument. First, contrasts are paired by rule: each treatment arm is matched to the largest inert-control arm if one exists, otherwise to the largest active comparator, up to three contrasts per trial. Second, for every system a second copy is trained or prompted on the same trials with **every intervention-identifying field withheld** — intervention names, types and descriptions, protocol arm labels and descriptions, results-group titles and descriptions — while endpoint text, condition, eligibility, design, population covariates, therapeutic area and comparator type stay in. For the gradient-boosting model this is a refit on blind text; for a prompted model it is the same prompt with those lines replaced by a withheld marker and intervention-derived name tokens redacted from the retained text. Third, interventional skill is defined as one minus the ratio of the full model's mean squared error to the blind model's, i.e. the share of the blind reference's error that knowing the drug buys. Confidence intervals are percentile bootstraps over trials, because contrasts from one trial share arms; five-seed means share one set of resamples. Two prompt variants are carried throughout: "protocol-only", built from registered fields the condition defines before read-out, and "posted-record", the submitted version's prompt which also carried posted results-group labels and could leak outcome vocabulary. The author then attacks the instrument itself: an audit of how much of the withheld variable the blind inputs reconstruct, an arm-swap and a pre-registered wrong-drug substitution as perturbation tests, a stratification of trials relative to model cutoffs to separate skill from pretraining exposure, and a reliability layer of conformal intervals and answer-or-abstain operating points. A positive control asks whether interventional skill is attainable from the registry at all, via retrieval of earlier trials of the same molecule.

## What counts as an explanation here

Nothing predictive in the interpretability sense — no directions, circuits or dictionaries. The object the paper produces is a **test statistic with a null built into its definition**: the blind copy is the model's own hypothesis-only baseline, imported from the NLI and VQA literature, and the claim "this system models the treatment" becomes the numeric claim "this system's error is lower than that of a version that cannot see the treatment". It is deliberately a partial and conservative instrument: the author states that positive skill is a necessary but not sufficient condition for interventional validity, that it could exploit a correlate of the intervention rather than its mechanism, and that it says nothing about individual-level counterfactuals. The paper also supplies an audit that bounds how far its own null is from a true null.

## How they judged success

The primary quantity is interventional skill, reported beside skill against the global training mean and skill against the blind reference, with 95% trial-bootstrap intervals. The controls are unusually complete for this folder's record: the noise ceiling of the task, computed from arm sizes (mean sampling variance of a binary test contrast 0.0047 against total variance 0.0499, reliability 0.906 [0.885, 0.922]), which caps attainable skill at 0.91 against the global mean and 0.87 against the blind registry reference and 0.82 against the large language model's blind prompt; a blindness audit in which another model guesses the withheld drug class, mechanism and agent from the blind prompt and is scored against both the true treatment and a randomly permuted contrast; arm swap as a signed perturbation; a pre-registered negative control replacing the treatment with an unrelated marketed drug; a temporal-cut sweep and six therapeutic-area strata; a within-registry retrieval positive control; contamination stratification by training cutoff; and a reliability layer with cross-fitted conformal intervals, coverage, width and selective-prediction MAE at pre-fixed targets. Arm pairing is audited by sampling: 100 random contrasts were checked and 96 of 100 pairings were valid after repair, against 76 of 100 in the submitted version.

## What they found

**Held-out accuracy ranks every system as though it predicts treatment effects, and the blind control dissolves almost all of it for the registry model.** Against the global training mean, the registry model removes 21% of baseline error on risk differences and 29% on standardised differences; Sonnet 5 removes 57% [0.47, 0.66]; Haiku 4.5 and Llama 3.1 70B remove 35% and 40%. The registry model's interventional skill is −0.12 [−0.21, −0.05] over five seeds — adding every intervention field drops its held-out skill from 0.30 to 0.21, so withholding the drug makes it better. Its blind reference scores 0.297 [0.220, 0.373] while its full model scores 0.211 [0.141, 0.281]. The null survives every alternative the author could construct: continuous endpoints 0.02 [−0.05, 0.08]; retrained splits at 2021, 2022 and 2024 giving −0.04, −0.06 and −0.11; placebo-controlled, active-controlled, phase-3 and rich-text strata all inside [−0.10, −0.01]; the arm-level task, where blind (0.245) beats full (0.212); six alternative featurisations with no interval above 0.03; and seen molecules making it worse rather than better (−0.23 [−0.39, −0.08] on 286 contrasts whose intervention name appears in training, against −0.10 [−0.19, −0.01] on the 592 unseen). The positive control says the same thing from the other side: for 246 test contrasts with a matched earlier trial of the same molecule, adding the shrunk mean blind residual gives −0.04 [−0.17, 0.05].

**The language models have a little, in proportion to scale, and the perturbations say it is about the intervention rather than a correlate.** Withholding the intervention drops Sonnet 5 from 0.570 to 0.462, giving interventional skill 0.20 [0.08, 0.31] with protocol-only prompts and 0.27 [0.16, 0.37] with posted-record prompts, paired difference 0.07 [−0.03, 0.17]. Haiku 4.5 sits at 0.08 [−0.01, 0.17] and Llama 3.1 70B at 0.08 [0.00, 0.15], both at the limit of detection. Sonnet 5's blind skill alone (0.462) exceeds the registry model's full skill (0.211), so its held-out lead comes partly from reading eligibility and endpoint text better. Swapping the arms reverses the sign of its prediction in 86% of the 742 contrasts with a non-trivial original prediction, with correlation 0.947 against the negated original and mean |A| of 0.152 as the scale reference, while the bag-of-words registry model reverses 7%. Substituting an unrelated marketed drug shrinks the predicted effect by 0.035 [0.020, 0.049], more in placebo-controlled trials (0.073) and turning a predicted benefit to harm in active-comparator trials (−0.12 [−0.18, −0.07]); skill against blind is then −0.87 [−1.34, −0.47]. A second blind run moves skill by 0.017 [−0.024, 0.052] and reproduces 55.6% of its points exactly, which sets a floor under how finely any skill difference here should be read.

**The blind reference is not drug-blind, which is why the author calls the number a lower bound.** Given only the blind prompt, Haiku 4.5 names the withheld drug class correctly for 44.9% of contrasts (chance 2.3%, a 19.7x ratio), the mechanism for 45.4% (chance 1.9%) and the exact agent for 20.8% (chance 0.7%, 30.4x). The registry model's blind text behaves the same way: for test contrasts whose molecule appears in training, the nearest training contrast by blind TF-IDF is a same-molecule one 23.2% of the time against 0.3% chance, and 19.3% in the 120-dimensional representation it actually uses. So the protocol implies the class, and the reported skill measures the increment of the specific agent beyond what the rest of the protocol already implies.

**The repair of the benchmark changed the answer, not just the sample.** The submitted version paired arms by text alone; after the audit added same-molecule exclusion, concurrency and investigational-arm preference, 1,510 contrasts were dropped, 375 re-paired and 240 added, and the language model's posted-record skill fell from 0.39 on the submitted table to 0.27 on the repaired one. The failure modes were dose comparisons of the same molecule, arms from different stages of a staged or crossover design, and a placebo-labelled double-dummy experimental arm taken as the control — cases where the "intervention" text is really a label for the outcome. The author also records that the registry model's own negative sign is mostly a cost of embedding intervention text in the same SVD space as the endpoint text: with separate text blocks, sparse ridge or sentence embeddings the estimate is between −0.05 and −0.01.

**Reliability fails in two different ways.** The registry model's cross-fitted conformal 90% intervals cover 0.906 [0.879, 0.933] in-distribution at a mean width of about 0.60, and 0.874 [0.847, 0.899] under the temporal shift; the language model's self-stated 90% intervals are less than half as wide (0.23) and cover 0.70 with the intervention shown and 0.69 hidden — confidently wrong. Ranking by predicted error helps (the most confident quarter of the shifted set has MAE 0.083 against 0.130 overall, and for Sonnet 5 0.039 against 0.098) but a calibrated threshold does not transfer: a cut chosen on calibration data for a target MAE of 0.10 realises 0.106 in-distribution and 0.113 under shift. The submitted version's thresholds were chosen on the evaluated set and so met any target by construction — the released tables separate the two recipes explicitly.

## Limits and open problems they name

The benchmark inherits the registry's failures: results are posted for a minority of due trials with sponsor type the strongest predictor, so the contrasts are a selected sample; endpoints are collapsed into two effect families; covariates are trial-level means. Arm pairing is rule-based and audited by sampling, and 7,690 result groups lack a recoverable registered arm type. Interventional skill is a necessary condition only. The exposure test is underpowered (217 contrasts, 152 trials, interval about ±0.11), concerns an open-weight model at temperature 0 while the Claude runs used default sampling, and the author makes no memorisation claim for the Claude models at all. Two tables of the submitted version could not be reproduced from preserved code and are re-reported or flagged provisional. The paper also notes that the plausibly larger source of the language model's skill — pretraining exposure to pharmacology and published class effects — is exactly what the registry cannot supply, since it records what was tested, not what a drug does.

## What we take from it

Three things. **A method:** the blind-copy control is a template our white-box model can adopt almost unchanged — for any predictor of a system's response to an intervention, train or prompt an identical copy with the intervention-identifying inputs removed and everything else intact, then report one minus the error ratio. It costs one refit and it converts "the model predicts behaviour under intervention" into a number with a null whose construction we can state. **A measure:** the blindness audit. Before trusting any such baseline, measure how much of the withheld variable the surviving inputs reconstruct — here a separate model recovers the drug class 44.9% of the time, so the reported skill is an increment on a reference that already knows the class, and must be labelled a lower bound. **A warning, and the sharpest one in this folder's record:** the registry model's held-out skill of 0.21 against the global mean is entirely explained by endpoint scale, population and comparator — and it is *negative* once you ask what the intervention adds, because the intervention text crowds out the useful features. Every component-importance number in a system like that would still look sensible. For us the rule is that any "our model understands the mechanism" claim is printed as a pair — skill against the raw baseline and skill against the intervention-blind copy — and that a blind reference is reported with its reconstruction rate, because an un-audited blind baseline biases the measurement in the direction the author likes.
