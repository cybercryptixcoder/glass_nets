# When Does Activation Steering Change What a Model Computes From?

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.29522
- **Authors / organisation:** Benjamin Shih; John Winnicki; Eric Darve (Institute for Computational and Mathematical Engineering, Stanford University)
- **Date:** 2026-09-06 (v2; v1 2026-06-28)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29522
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v2 HTML: abstract, §1–§9, references, Appendices A–F)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Steering a model by adding a direction to its activations reliably changes what it says, and the usual inference drawn from that is that the edit wrote the *state* the model was going to compute with. The authors separate those two claims. Reaching the same answer by a different route is behavioural equivalence, and behavioural equivalence does not imply mechanistic equivalence — so a successful intervention might be editing a variable that later computation reads, or it might just be pushing the output. Their programme is to build a case where those two explanations give *different predictions*, and then apply the same test to ordinary mean-activation steering (Function Vectors). The larger goal is an evidential standard: an intervention should be called a state edit only when later computation can be shown to use the edited value under the semantics of the variable being edited.

## What they studied

Two settings. (1) A controlled state-tracking task: a walk on the quaternion group Q8 or the dihedral group D8 (both order eight), where each state has a visible coordinate (a four-way quotient the model can tally directly from the moves) and a one-bit hidden coordinate that depends on the *order* of moves and can only be tracked sequentially. Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (28 layers, d=3584) with LoRA arms differing only in whether intermediate states appear in the training target; replication in Mistral-7B-v0.3. (2) Two lexical steering settings chosen for a strong effect: present-to-past conversion in Qwen2.5-7B (block 7, coefficient 15) and English-to-Spanish translation in Llama-3.1-8B (block 8, coefficient 10). Unit of analysis: the residual stream at one (layer, token position) site — in (1) a rank-16 subspace at the current-state token.

## How they did it

The register experiment is a four-step causal chain. First, do three fine-tunes (base, final-answer-only, running-state) on the same move sequences and same final answers, so the only difference is trace supervision. Second, estimate a low-rank register subspace U at layer 12 from class-conditional mean differences of the residual stream (per visible value, the difference between the two hidden values; leading 16 directions), on a calibration split disjoint from all scoring items, with layer and site locked on a validation split first. Third, write a counterfactual state — flip only the hidden bit, leave the printed token unchanged — by adding the projected class-conditional mean difference. Fourth, ask what the model computes next: on items where the edited and original states imply *different* next states, score agreement with the move applied to the edited state (Use) and the excess of that over agreement with the original state (Counterfactual Update Selectivity). Two anti-leak controls: swap the upcoming move while holding the edited state fixed (the next state must change accordingly), and inject one real occurrence of the counterfactual state whose own recorded continuation conflicts with the move-applied target (the model must follow the move, not the pasted continuation). Controls for the edit itself: same-rank random subspace, orthogonal complement, and full-residual replacement. Localisation then ablates 38 candidate components (attention edges into the next-state readout, plus low-rank adapter bands) with matched-source controls and restoration, under thresholds fixed in advance. The steering half repeats the same logic one level weaker: compare (i) patching the target activation through all later layers as a reference, (ii) patching it at one layer only, (iii) adding the Function Vector at its calibrated scale, (iv) adding it rescaled to the natural correct-minus-shuffled change, and (v) same-norm comparison directions, all scored as a normalised margin gain relative to the all-layer patch.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a description of a concept but a **causal abstraction claim with a semantic test**: a subspace counts as a register only if, after writing a counterfactual value into it, the model's later computation applies the *known* transition rule to that value, verifiably differently for different upcoming operations. It also offers a three-rung ladder for intervention evidence — changing the answer (control), later computation responding to the edit (downstream control), and the model using the edited value according to the variable's semantics (computational state) — which is a taxonomy for grading any patching or steering result, including ours. Partial: the paper proves state-like use in a synthetic task and shows the lexical settings fail to meet the weaker one-layer restoration prediction; it does not say what the large steering vector is doing instead.

## How they judged success

Metrics: register agreement R, Counterfactual Update Selectivity, move-swap selectivity, conflicting-continuation selectivity, multi-step branch persistence during free-running generation, and the fraction of selectivity removed by ablation (κ); linear-probe decodability of the next-state coordinate by layer as the arm-distinguishing readout; normalised margin gain E(c) against the all-layer patch with paired 95% bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples); equal-norm direction controls; a 1,000-sample random four-edge null for route strength; an output-label remapping test; and a vector-subtraction-at-later-layers test. All thresholds (rank of saturation, 80% single-edge bar, 70% distributed bar, ≥0.70 free-running Use) were declared before scoring, and the confirmatory splits were held out from route selection.

## What they found

Trace supervision creates a register that later computation actually reads. Editing a rank-16 subspace at layer 12 flips the next hidden bit in agreement with the move applied to the *edited* state at 0.80 (Q8) and 0.91 (D8), against ≈0.02 for same-rank random and orthogonal-complement edits; full-residual replacement is no stronger. The pretrained and final-answer-only arms stay at chance on Use in both groups while still decoding the current token at 1.00 — readability does not distinguish the arms, use does. Move-swap selectivity is +0.57 and +0.68; conflicting-continuation selectivity is +0.59 (0.7975 vs 0.2025) and +0.81 (0.9025 vs 0.0975). Mistral replicates: Use 0.9265 and 0.9430 for the running-state arm, ≈0.50 and 0.44–0.54 for the controls. In free-running generation D8 passes the predeclared bar (one-step Use 0.902, branch persistence ≈0.70–0.72 out to four states, final-answer agreement 0.845) while Q8 does not (0.660 < 0.70) though it stays above chance with positive controls. The register is tiny — the effect saturates by rank 1 (Q8) and rank 2 (D8) in a 3,584-dimensional stream — but its *update route* is not: one layer-22 attention edge from the current hidden bit to the readout removes 94% of the selectivity in Q8 (matched control ≈0.59, restoration returns 0.595), whereas in D8 no single component exceeds 24% and a pre-frozen four-edge set removes 68.7% over ten splits (eight confirmatory; the route sits at the 99.6th percentile of 1,000 random four-edge sets, and full-route effect exceeds the sum of single-edge effects). On the lexical tasks, both predictions of the correspondence hypothesis fail: patching the exact matched target activation at one layer gives a normalised effect of 0.014 in Qwen and −0.015 in Llama, and the Function Vector rescaled to the natural change gives 0.0073 and 0.0166. At its calibrated scale the vector works (0.544 and 0.824 of the all-layer gain) with norms of 210.80 and 20.22, which are 77× and 26× the median natural source-to-target change (2.74 and 0.78). Specificity is model-dependent: Llama's four same-norm comparison directions run −0.316 to 0.085, while Qwen's structured alternatives reach 0.612–0.665 versus 0.544 for the target vector (random 0.175). Effective Function Vector steering also does not write the Q8 register: its update is 2.19× larger than the matched register write, median cosine similarity is 0.254, and only 5.7–13.4% of its energy lies in the register subspace. Finally, subtracting the vector at early downstream layers kills the effect (it survives only when subtraction is delayed into blocks 22–24 in Qwen, 18–25 in Llama, where matched comparison directions also start to matter), and label remapping shows part of the effect stays bound to the literal output label.

## Limits and open problems they name

The register case is a synthetic task with an explicit trace, not natural-language reasoning; circuit localisation exists only in the primary model. The steering half tests one task and layer per model, both selected because ordinary steering worked, so model, task, layer and scale vary together and the Qwen/Llama specificity contrast must not be read as a family-level claim. The one-block patch replaces only the final prompt-token residual, so its failure rules out a local restoration account, not a state at another position or layer, distributed across tokens, or constructed downstream. "Unnatural" means only "tens of times the matched natural change at the site" — they do not estimate the natural residual distribution or follow the steered point's trajectory. The cancellation test shows continued dependence on the displacement without saying how later layers use it. A supplementary local-sensitivity test missed its pre-specified numerical tolerance by ~1e-4 and is reported but not used. I would add: the register is validated only for a variable the task *forces* into a single token, and the whole positive result depends on the hidden bit being spelled out in the training target — which is exactly the condition most real behaviours lack.

## What we take from it

- **The semantic test we should put on every "we found the state" claim.** Agreement with a desired answer is not evidence of a state edit; the discriminating test is to change an *upcoming operation* and check that the model's output follows the rule applied to the edited value rather than to the original one. For our white-box model this becomes a prediction obligation: a claimed mediator must predict a fact-dependent consequence, and the move-swap design is how you generate that prediction cheaply.
- **Readability, use, and routing are three separate measurements.** The cleanest number in the paper is that every arm decodes the current hidden bit at 1.00 while only one arm *uses* it. This is the same dissociation other entries here document (legible-but-idle features, cosine ≠ causal effect) in a setting where ground truth is known — so any probe of ours should be paired with a use test before it is treated as a variable in the model.
- **Report the intervention displacement against the natural displacement it is supposed to mimic.** 77× and 26× are the whole story of the lexical results, and one ratio per intervention would have told any reader that immediately. This is a bookkeeping habit, not a new experiment: median source-minus-target activation change at the site, next to the vector's norm.
- **Dimension of a variable and concentration of its update are independent.** Rank-1 register, one-edge update in Q8; two-dimensional register, four-edge distributed route in D8. A white-box model that stores "this is a one-dimensional quantity" should not thereby predict a single circuit, and vice versa; our entries should carry both facts separately.
- **A pre-freeze protocol for circuit claims that survives the "any route works" objection.** Freeze the route on selection splits, declare the pass thresholds, score on held-out splits, and report the random same-size-route null — the authors even give their own route a weaker verdict (68.7% = "partial", not "strong") because it lands below the declared 70% line. The refusal to relax that line is the most reusable methodological decision in the paper.
- **Warning for any edit trained to remove a behaviour.** If a successful direction need not reproduce the state the network naturally uses, then a suppression edit may work through an off-route mechanism that leaves the network's own route intact — the same risk named by the objectives paper, arriving here from the opposite direction.
