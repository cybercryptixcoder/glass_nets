# HalluPrism: When Multimodal Uncertainty Should Diagnose, Not Decide

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.29193
- **Authors / organisation:** Aman Prakash, Sourish Dasgupta, Tanmoy Chakraborty (LCS2 Lab, IIT Delhi + KDM Lab, Dhirubhai Ambani University)
- **Date:** 2026-08-29
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29193
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, main body plus appendices 7-11 and tables 20-36)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

A vision-language model can be equally confident about answers that fail for completely different reasons: a blurred object it could not see, an object that is not in the image at all and exists only in the question's presuppositions, or two objects it found but whose spatial relation it reversed. A single confidence number ranks likely correctness and says nothing about which of these happened. The authors argue that the field has collapsed the diagnostic question into the decision question - uncertainty research mostly ends in an abstain/answer threshold - and that this loses the structure of the failure. Their target is a cheap, training-free behavioural signature that separates "this answer is fragile because the image evidence is weak" from "this answer survives without any image" from "this answer is unstable about the image-text binding", and then to test separately whether that signature is useful for diagnosing failure type, for deciding to abstain, and for choosing a correction. The larger claim is that diagnosis should be the core object of uncertainty and abstention a downstream, assumption-laden guardrail.

## What they studied

Four open-weight multimodal LLM families (Gemma 4, Qwen3-VL, LLaVA-Mistral, LLaVA-Vicuna) under greedy decoding on four benchmarks chosen to stress different failure modes: HallusionBench (951 image-paired items, visual illusion and attribute error), POPE (9,000, object-presence hallucination under random/popular/adversarial splits), Visual Spatial Reasoning (340, spatial and relational binding) and VizWiz-VQA (4,319, visual insufficiency and answerability). The unit of analysis is a prediction instance - image, question, generated answer - augmented with a three-coordinate behavioural vector computed from re-runs of the same model on perturbed inputs. No hidden states, no internal features and no weights are inspected; the study is deliberately black-box.

## How they did it

For each instance they record length-normalised answer confidence `c`, then re-query the model under four controlled pressures. The visual coordinate V combines, with a 0.6/0.4 weighting, the answer-flip rate over four degradations (Gaussian blur at σ=15, 50% centre crop resized back, brightness cut to 30%, Gaussian noise at σ=25) with the mean confidence shift. The image-removal coordinate L replaces the image with a uniform blank and takes the confidence ratio to the clean answer, clipped at 1; a stricter variant L_persist is nonzero only when the answer itself survives removal. The alignment coordinate A combines a grounding re-check (flip and confidence shift) with a relation-swap probe available only when the caption contains an explicit relational term - in practice only VSR - weighted 0.4/0.6 toward the relation swap. Total cost is 7-8 forward passes per instance. They then train logistic regression, XGBoost and LightGBM with stratified 5-fold cross-validation on three feature sets (`c`, `V+L+A`, all four) to predict failure family, and compare against (a) fixed-dataset and dataset/model-ID-controlled baselines, (b) three hand-designed ways of folding the signature back into a scalar for abstention, and (c) generic versus signature-matched correction prompts. A key test is an intervention-separability check: apply a "ruined" condition targeting one source (visual ruin, language ruin, alignment ruin) and ask whether the intended coordinate moves more in absolute value than the other two.

## What counts as an explanation here

A behavioural perturbation-response signature: three numbers in [0,1] per prediction, plus a failure-family classifier fitted on them. The authors are careful, repeatedly and in the ethics statement, that this is *not* a causal decomposition of the model's internals - high L means confidence was retained without the image, not that the model ignored it - and they prove the weaker claim they do stand behind: that this vector carries information about failure type that confidence does not, while confidence may remain sufficient for correctness ranking. The explanation is partial, black-box, and explicitly diagnostic rather than mechanistic.

## How they judged success

Failure-family AUROC and macro-F1 (pooled, per-model, within-dataset with model identity held in, and with dataset/model IDs controlled); an intervention-separability pass count over a 4x4x3 grid of model-dataset-ruin cells; abstention AUROC and risk-coverage curves for confidence versus three source-penalty scalarizations; and routing accuracy, fix rate and break rate per model-dataset setting. Controls include swapping the L variant, dropping VSR to remove the only relation-swap branch, dropping each of the four visual perturbations in turn, sweeping perturbation strength from weak to default to strong, a 10-split dataset/model-controlled analysis, leave-one-model-out transfer, leave-dataset-out binary transfer, and a blind 200-example human audit with three annotators (Fleiss' κ 0.236 full-label, 0.480 at the coarser source-versus-non-source level). Paired bootstrap intervals are reported for the AUROC and F1 gains in the strength sweep.

## What they found

Prevalence and diagnostic value come apart. The image-removal coordinate dominates the raw signature in 58,287 of 58,440 pooled instances (99.7%), with means running 0.91 to 1.0 - yet under the stricter persistence variant the L-dominance share falls to 51.6%, with A at 27.9% and V at 20.4%. Raw argmax, in other words, is a saturated coordinate, not a finding. The alignment coordinate is the one that does the diagnostic work: it carries 0.50 of pooled XGBoost importance against 0.20 for L, 0.15 for confidence and 0.15 for V.

The interventions do not isolate their targets. Only 18 of 48 ruin cells pass magnitude-based diagonal dominance - visual ruin 11/16, language ruin 2/16, alignment ruin 5/16 - and the signed deltas are often counter-intuitive: Qwen3-VL's visual ruin *reduces* V by 0.09 and its language ruin raises A by 0.18; Gemma 4 on VizWiz drops V by 0.198 under visual ruin; LLaVA-Vicuna's POPE visual ruin raises A by 0.239. The authors read this not as probe failure but as the point: the coordinates are entangled and should be read jointly.

Jointly, they are strongly diagnostic. Fixed-dataset, model-included AUROC rises from 0.634 to 0.769 on HallusionBench and from 0.707 to 0.817 on VizWiz, with smaller gains on POPE (0.823 to 0.839) and essentially none on VSR (0.722 to 0.724). Pooled, XGBoost reaches 0.78 with confidence alone, 0.95 with the signature, 0.97 with both; adding the signature beyond dataset ID, model ID and confidence improves benchmark-family macro-F1 by 0.170 with a positive gain in all 10 of 10 splits, and leave-one-model-out macro-F1 moves from 0.558 to 0.716. The result survives every probe-design control (AUROC 0.822-0.824 with any one visual perturbation removed; 0.785/0.786/0.783 across the strength sweep against 0.632 for confidence).

Folded back into a scalar, the same signature does harm to abstention. Pooled AUROC is 0.783 for plain confidence against 0.767 for the best source-aware penalty, and the largest drops are Qwen3-VL's 0.809 to 0.732 and, slice-wise, Qwen3-VL on POPE from 0.826 to 0.702. At 5% coverage the risk is 0.018 for confidence versus 0.023 weighted, 0.225 geometric and 0.290 max-penalty. The one exception is LLaVA-Vicuna on VSR, where the penalty helps by 0.017. As a router the signature is conditionally useful rather than a universal gain: matched routing beats generic prompting in 10 of 16 settings and beats no intervention in 8 of 16; pooled accuracy is 76.6% with no intervention, 75.7% generic and 75.9% matched. Its value shows in break rate - on Qwen3-VL/VSR generic prompting drops accuracy from 87.4% to 76.2% and breaks 15.5% of correct answers, while matched routing holds 87.6% and breaks 2.7%; against blanket chain-of-thought on 6,561 outputs the probe-informed policy gains 3.54 points of accuracy (95% CI 2.82 to 4.25) and cuts breakage by 5.12 points (4.12 to 6.09).

The human audit gives bounded support and one sharp negative. Of 200 audited examples, 107 receive a clear adjudicated source, and those are overwhelmingly alignment (71), not language-prior (20) or visual (16) - the opposite of the raw signature's distribution. Raw-argmax agreement with human clear-source labels is 0.165 accuracy with Cohen's κ 0.000; per-setting normalisation lifts it to 0.363 with κ 0.053, still close to chance. A small classifier on the clear-source subset reaches weighted AUROC 0.597 from the raw signature against 0.430 from confidence.

## Limits and open problems they name

No causal claim about internals is made and none is supported; the score is behavioural. The probe set and perturbation strengths are a hand-chosen design, and while single-perturbation and strength sweeps are stable, a wider probe space is untested. Cross-dataset generalisation is genuinely weak - leave-dataset-out binary AUROC on held-out POPE is 0.375 with the signature against 0.201 with confidence, which is far below usable. All four benchmarks are short-answer VQA; video, multi-image, medical and document settings are excluded, and the paper notes latency cost of 7-8 forward passes. Abstention was tested only with three hand-designed multiplicative scalarizations, so learned or calibrated ones are untouched. The human audit is 200 examples, stratified by the very signature it is checking, with a full-label κ of 0.236; the authors call it a plausibility check and nothing more. Their routing result depends on the fact that baseline accuracy exceeds 0.5 (so break-rate changes dominate), which will not hold in low-accuracy regimes. The theoretical section's no-free-lunch results (a scalar abstention policy is reliable only if the safe-answer label is measurable with respect to the score; no unconditional inference-time guarantee without a validation-to-deployment transfer assumption) are correct but standard, and are used as framing rather than as new evidence.

## What we take from it

A measure and a warning. The measure: a *vector* of deterministic perturbation responses, each computed by re-running the model with one part of the input changed, is a cheap behavioural object (7-8 forward passes, no training) that can predict *which kind* of failure occurred with AUROC near 0.95 pooled while a scalar confidence score sits at 0.78. For our white-box model this is the black-box floor to beat, and the coordinate-with-most-importance-versus-coordinate-with-most-prevalence gap is exactly the trap our feature reports would fall into if we ranked units by how often they are large. The warning: the same vector that diagnoses well loses to plain confidence when folded into a decision score, in 15 of 16 model-dataset slices. Diagnosis and decision are different prediction problems, and a representation that is excellent for one is not evidence for the other.

Mechanically, two transferable instruments. First, the diagonal-dominance count: when we claim an intervention targets one component, we report how many ruin conditions actually move the intended coordinate most in absolute value, and here that number would have been 18 of 48 - which is what stopped the authors from calling their three coordinates three causes. Second, the fix/break decomposition: a correction's accuracy change equals (1-a)·fix minus a·break, so at high baseline accuracy avoiding damage matters more than repairing it, and any intervention we propose should be scored on both terms rather than on accuracy alone.
