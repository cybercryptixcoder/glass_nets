## What they wanted, and why
Attribution scores on their own say little: two methods can both produce a ranked list for the same instance and a reader has no principled way to say which list the evidence supports. The authors want a way to take a *statement about which features matter* - coming from a domain expert, from a known ground truth, or from the explainer's own output - and ask the statistics question "how much do repeated runs of this explainer support that statement rather than its complement". Their motivation is that explanation quality is usually argued with an implicit notion of a good explanation, whereas a hypothesis test makes the reference explicit and the strength of support a number. The larger goal is a contrastive, evidence-based evaluation layer that sits beside the existing functional metrics rather than replacing them.

## What they studied
The unit of analysis is a **binary hypothesis about feature relevance**, tested against N runs of a stochastic feature-importance method on one fixed instance. The multivariate attribution vector is squeezed into a relevant set (top-k selection, or an adaptive selection that keeps the smallest prefix of the ranked scores covering a fraction tau of the normalised attribution mass), and the partial hypothesis asserts those features matter without asserting anything about the rest. Methods studied: LIME and SHAP, on Titanic (random forest), on a synthetic scikit-learn `make_classification` problem with 3 informative features and 7 redundant ones mixed by a parameter lambda, and on Diabetes, Heart Disease, Mobile and Churn. Nothing here is internal to a network - the unit is the explainer's output distribution.

## How they did it
The weight of evidence of the hypothesis given the observed explanations is written as a log ratio of posterior to prior odds, which by Bayes reduces to a log likelihood ratio. With a uniform prior over all binary relevance assignments, a hypothesis naming a specified number of features gets prior probability equal to one over two to that count - fewer named features means a higher prior. The posterior is estimated by brute force: run the explainer N times on the same instance, map each run's attribution vector to a hypothesis, and count the fraction of runs that reproduce exactly the reference hypothesis. The reported statistic is then a log ratio whose numerator is that count. The reference is then swapped in three ways - a domain belief (sex and passenger class on Titanic), a construction-known ground truth plus a separate "model" reference taken from the model's own ranking (the synthetic set), and the explainer's own mean attribution over runs (no external reference) - and the same statistic is read as alignment, faithfulness, or stability accordingly. The proofs chain Chebyshev plus a union bound: with a margin around the sufficiency threshold, small component variances keep the selected count fixed, keep every selected feature ranked above every unselected one, and hence force the reference hypothesis to be reproduced with probability approaching one, which drives the statistic to positive infinity. Validation: 50 instances per setting, 50-50-ish run counts, N swept from 5 to 100 for the convergence check, and a synthetic attributor with Gaussian noise scaled by a controllable level.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a representation of the model. The "explanation" is a **binary relevance hypothesis** derived from a continuous attribution vector by a stated selection rule, and the understanding on offer is a scalar measuring how strongly a distribution of explainer outputs supports it. It is explicitly partial (the hypothesis says nothing about features it does not name) and it is contrastive (the score is defined only against a named alternative, taken here to be the complement). It does not predict the network's behaviour; it locates disagreement - between a local explanation and a global belief, between a method and a model's own ranking, or between two runs of the same method.

## How they judged success
Success is split across three settings with different references: the fraction of instances landing at positive versus negative infinity (the cases where the reference is reproduced in every run or in no run), the mean statistic with 95% intervals across the delta grid for alignment against ground truth and against the model ranking, a sensitivity sweep over LIME's kernel-width parameter at one fixed mixing level, a PCA projection to show whether the divergent cases cluster geometrically, a table of divergent-case counts per method and dataset out of 50 instances, and an empirical check of the theorem by sweeping LIME's background-sample count across four model families and by sweeping injected noise in a synthetic attributor. The convergence check uses the coefficient of variation of the statistic across N repeated estimates, reporting that 80% of values fall between 0 and 0.3.

## What they found
On Titanic with the domain reference, LIME reached positive infinity on 27 of 50 instances and finite positive values on the remaining 23, whereas SHAP - being near-deterministic - split 23 positive and 27 negative infinity, i.e. it plainly named features beyond the two the domain belief names. In the synthetic setting the two references diverge in a way the authors did not expect to be simple: at low redundancy-mixing noise LIME tracks the model much more closely than the ground truth (the model leans on redundant copies of the informative features), the two converge for lambda at or above 0.70, and there is an intermediate band (0.50 to 0.70) in which the method looks *more* aligned with ground truth than with the model - which they diagnose as a sampling-radius artefact, since shrinking LIME's kernel width at lambda = 0.65 pulls model alignment up to match ground-truth alignment. Without any external reference, the two methods' distributions showed no significant differences, most scores were positive (internally consistent explanations), consistency fell on the most interaction-heavy dataset (Mobile), and the divergent-case table shows SHAP hitting positive infinity on 50/50 for Diabetes, 25/50 for Heart and 41/50 for Mobile, against LIME's 5/50, 11/50 and 0/50 positive with 5/50 negative on Mobile. Sweeping LIME's background samples upward raised the statistic and substantially cut negatively divergent cases; the controlled-variance synthetic attributor reproduced the same monotone relation. On the number of runs, distributions overlapped heavily from N of 5 to 100, which they take to mean a handful of runs suffices.

## Limits and open problems they name
The uniform prior is a convenience, not a claim, and alternative priors are left for future work. The posterior demands exact set equality between a run's hypothesis and the reference, which they call possibly too restrictive and propose to soften to subset containment or a distance-based score. Selection rule (top-k versus adaptive) is another open degree of freedom. The method needs repeated explainer runs, so its cost tracks the explainer's cost. They also note that the two distributions being comparable is itself a null-ish finding rather than a discrimination, and they flag the data/model/FIM disalignment question (faithfulness has always been judged relative to the model, never relative to the data-generating process) as the direction they care most about.

## What we take from it
A measure and a framing. The measure: **a support statistic for a stated relevance hypothesis requires two denominators that this paper happens to get right and that most explanation-stability reports omit - the prior of the hypothesis (how many features it names) and the fraction of runs that actually reproduce it.** Their near-deterministic method is the cautionary half: a method with no sampling variance produces only saturated values, so a stability score computed on it carries no information and the table prints 50/50 and 0/50 rows that look like strong results. For us the reusable pieces are: (1) whenever our white-box model emits a set-valued claim (these components carry this behaviour), report it as a hypothesis whose strength is the fraction of independent re-derivations that reproduce the identical set, with the cardinality of the set printed beside it, because a set of size three and a set of size nine are not the same claim at the same evidence level; (2) keep the three reference types - external belief, ground truth, self-mean - as three separate columns with three different names, since the same number means alignment, faithfulness, or stability depending on which one is used, and here the intermediate band shows a method can be *more* aligned with truth than with the model it purports to describe; (3) copy their pipeline-localisation move: when a method disagrees with a reference, perturb the method's own sampling parameter and see whether the disagreement is a property of the model or an artefact of the reading instrument - here shrinking LIME's neighbourhood radius converted a faithfulness gap into alignment.
