- **Key:** arxiv-2606.16939

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit discovery is the part of interpretability that most resembles our target: identify which internal components influence which others, and in what order, so that a behaviour can be explained rather than merely correlated with activations. Two things block it at scale. Raw neurons are polysemantic, so the recovered graphs are dense and unreadable; SAE features are readable but their dimensionality (tens of thousands per layer) makes every intervention-based method — activation patching, attribution patching, causal tracing — prohibitively expensive, because their cost scales with the number of components and edges that must each be tested. The authors want circuit discovery to become a *fitting* problem rather than an *intervening* problem: learn a sparse linear dependency skeleton from activations that were collected once, and pay no further forward or backward passes. The larger goal is stated as a surrogate, not as a mechanistic claim: a cheap object that ranks dependencies well enough to guide interpretation, editing and downstream generalisation.

## What they studied

GPT-2 small (`d` = 768, 12 layers) with OpenAI's ReLU SAEs (`D` = 32,768) for the CoLA grammaticality task (8,551 training sentences); Pythia-70M, Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-9B with their released SAEs (Pythia's from Marks et al., Gemma Scope JumpReLU for the Gemma pair) for Bias-in-Bios debiasing; and the InterpBench suite of 86 semi-synthetic transformers with known ground-truth circuits for accuracy benchmarking. The unit of analysis is a *component at a location* — a neuron vector or an SAE feature vector at a given layer output — and the object produced is a weighted adjacency matrix over those components plus a coefficient vector from the final layer to the prediction.

## How they did it

(1) Collect activations once: at each of `L` chosen locations, average per-token activations over `M` prompts, giving a feature-by-prompt matrix per location. (2) For each ordered pair of locations with `i` before `j`, solve a multi-output Lasso regressing the later location's features on the earlier one's — `min ‖Z_j − A_ij^T Z_i‖² + λ‖A_ij‖₁` — with FISTA, backtracking line search, standardised predictors and warm starts, so the recovered `A_ij` marks directed feature-to-feature dependencies. (3) Handle acyclicity by *not learning it*: the layer order and the within-layer attention-then-MLP order are given by the architecture, so the adjacency is constrained to block-upper-triangular by a fixed mask and the optimiser only chooses which entries inside the legal triangle are non-zero. (4) Attach the output with one more Lasso from the final layer's features to the label, giving an importance vector; rank features by its absolute value for dataset-level interpretation, or by the elementwise product of coefficients and that prompt's activations for a single decision. (5) Label the selected features with two procedures — aggregate the tokens that strongly activate a feature across many prompts and read the concept off them, then verify on a single prompt by perturbing exactly the tokens that should matter and checking the activation dies. (6) Trace backwards through `A_{i−1,i}` from each important final-layer feature to label its parents, yielding tree-shaped paths. (7) For the neuron experiments, follow the InterpBench protocol (clean and corrupted prompt sets, concatenated into one observational dataset) and compare structural recovery against EAP and EAP-ig on the 16 synthetic cases plus IOI, scored by Structural Hamming Distance and wall-clock on one A100.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **dependency skeleton**: a sparse directed graph over interpretable features whose edges mean "this feature's value is linearly predictable from that one, at this strength", plus a tree of labelled features per prediction. It is explicitly *not* a causal model — the paper says the linear coefficients rank dependencies and must not be read as quantitative causal effects of the nonlinear computation, and it declines every identifiability theorem (causal sufficiency, independent noise, correct functional form) that a real structural-equation reading would need, treating the residual instead as linearisation error. It is partial by construction: only the modelled components appear, the residual stream and SAE reconstruction error are folded into the error term, and ℓ₁ discards weak dependencies whether they are genuine-but-small or omitted-parent artefacts.

## How they judged success

Structural accuracy on ground-truth circuits: mean Structural Hamming Distance over 17 InterpBench tasks, averaged over three trials, against EAP and EAP-ig. Efficiency: mean wall-clock seconds per case, plus a proposition with the asymptotic cost of the block-triangular Lasso versus the extra forward/backward cost of attribution patching, and two explicit regimes in which the Lasso is provably cheaper. Interpretability: manual labelling of ~50 features per circuit on CoLA, plus the authors' own faithfulness and completeness measurements (following Marks et al.'s sparse-feature-circuit protocol: mean-ablate the circuit's features and measure how much of the grammaticality margin `p(grammatical) − p(ungrammatical)` is recovered, and how much is left when everything else is ablated) — and they add an **edge ablation** test that an intervention-based method cannot run, zeroing individual learned edge coefficients in the surrogate and re-measuring. Downstream utility: profession accuracy, gender leakage and worst-group accuracy on Bias-in-Bios against original, an oracle upper bound, concept-bottleneck probing, linear probing, and the shift/shift-retrain baselines, with feature count and runtime.

## What they found

On InterpBench, the linear variant reaches mean SHD 3.16 against EAP-ig's 2.98 and EAP's 3.61, at 16.3 s per case versus 49.1 s and 33.7 s — described as statistically indistinguishable accuracy at roughly 3× and 2× lower cost. The nonlinear (kernel/MLP) extension reaches the lowest SHD, 2.84, but at 3.7× the runtime, slower than EAP-ig in most cases; the authors read this as the extra capacity being wasted once edge presence is the target. On CoLA the learned GPT-2 small circuit shows three recurring structures along the traced paths: concept *persistence* (the "-self" concept appears as distinct features at layer 12, 11, 10, 9 and 8), *merging* (a layer-9 feature combines concepts from two layer-8 parents) and *dropping* (a layer-11 "himself" feature keeps one form and discards the rest of "-self"). The coefficient distribution is extremely skewed — 82.2% of final-layer coefficients below 0.01, 83.3% below 0.10, 86% below 0.41 — with the top five features dominating. Faithfulness and completeness under node ablation are described as matching the intervention-based shift baseline, and edge ablation shows a small subset of edges carrying the prediction behaviour. On Bias-in-Bios, zeroing manually-identified gender-correlated features then either reusing or retraining the classifier gives profession accuracy comparable to or slightly above the strongest non-oracle baseline (e.g. Gemma-2-9B: 96.9% retrained against shift-retrain's 96.0% and the 95.7% oracle), with gender leakage near the 50% target, at 107.4 s and 59 features versus 908.4 s and 71 features for shift. The λ sweep is the paper's honest caveat cluster: with no penalty training accuracy is 99.36% but test accuracy 71.10%, the chosen λ = 1e-5 gives the best test accuracy at 72.77%, and larger penalties reduce both.

## Limits and open problems they name

Causally: causal sufficiency holds only approximately because the residual stream carries every earlier layer's contribution alongside the modelled ones and SAE reconstruction error adds latent variation; the residual is linearisation error, not noise, and is not assumed independent of the parents; edge weights are not causal effects. Architecturally: the acyclicity trick depends on a feed-forward order being available, so architectures with within-layer feedback are out of scope. Methodologically: only consecutive-location pairs and the final-layer-to-label map are fitted (peptide hops of more than one layer are reached by composition, not estimated), token averaging discards position, and the paper is candid that its SAE-level circuits are a *population* skeleton rather than a per-prompt mechanism, complementary to attribution-graph work. Empirically: the CoLA circuits contain paths that are anti-causal by human reading (a "hunger/thirst" feature at layer 9 pointing at "ate/eat" at layer 10, whereas being hungry precedes eating) and paths that are plainly spurious ("-self" correlated with "hunger/thirst"), which the authors attribute to co-occurrence bias in the corpus and treat as a *feature* of the method rather than a failure — it surfaces dataset bias — without any test that separates a spurious edge from a real but unusual one. My addition, marked as mine: the efficiency realism is narrower than the headline, since the neuron case needs `L(L−1)/24S(2+f)√ε < n_token` (or the token-heavy regime) to beat attribution patching, and the method's whole advantage rests on collecting activations once — which means any later change of location set, model or SAE forces a recollection that the runtime tables do not price in.

## What we take from it

- **A fitting-first template for our own white-box model, with its licence written on it.** The reusable move is architectural rather than statistical: because the layer order is known, the DAG search collapses into a fixed mask plus per-pair Lasso, so cheapness comes from exploiting a constraint we already have rather than from approximating a causal quantity. That is the same bargain our predictor should make explicit — say which structural facts are assumed and which are estimated — and it is exactly the abduction/structural-model gap the ladder paper in this folder says activation patching cannot fill, here filled by fiat and labelled as such.
- **The observational-only claim needs the intervention check flagged as missing.** Everything in this entry is coefficients from correlated activations, and the paper's own faithfulness measurement is the only place an intervention enters — so the honest reading is that the skeleton is a *hypothesis generator* whose edges must still be tested, and the check that would raise its status is one forward pass per candidate edge, done on the few edges the averaging selects rather than all of them.
- **Edge ablation is a metric we should adopt.** Zeroing a coefficient in the surrogate and asking whether the prediction changes is a cheap, strictly-stronger sibling of node ablation, and it turns the learned object into a prediction we can then verify against the real model; it is the smallest available test of whether the fitted graph is doing work.
- **Read concept labels as contrast-set artefacts, again.** The "-self" and "hunger/thirst" labels come from which sentences fire them, and the same method then reports "-self" as spuriously correlated with "hunger/thirst" — two labels from one firing statistic, one of which is the mechanism and one the confound. The pre-flight is the one this folder keeps reaching for: label each feature by the *perturbation* that kills it (their single-prompt check), and report the label as (feature, contrast) rather than as a property of the feature.
- **Population skeleton versus per-prompt mechanism is a real fork, not a detail.** CoLA-level circuits answer "how does this model do grammaticality in general"; the folder's attribution-graph entries answer "what happened in this transcript". If our white-box model is to *predict*, it needs the per-input version, so this paper's averaging over 8,551 sentences is the one design choice to invert — and the §3.3.2 per-prompt Hadamard variant is the hook that makes that inversion cheap.
