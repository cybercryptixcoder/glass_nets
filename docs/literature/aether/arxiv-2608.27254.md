# Circuit Condensation: Post-Training that Concentrates a Behavior's Causal Circuit

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.27254
- **Authors / organisation:** Sai Adith Senthil Kumar; George Mason University
- **Date:** 2026-08-27
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27254
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit discovery on a frozen network routinely returns graphs with hundreds of edges, and size is what decides which questions can even be asked: a circuit of k edges admits 2^k subset tests and k(k−1)/2 pair tests, so at 61 edges an exhaustive completeness check is impossible while at 5 edges it is a script. The author's argument is that everyone else has responded by searching the same fixed weights harder (activation patching, EAP and its integrated-gradient and GradPath variants, learned gates), and that this cannot change how a diffuse behaviour is actually carried. The question they pose instead is whether findability is a *trainable* property: rather than looking for where the behaviour already lives, post-train the model so that it lives in a smaller place. The larger goal is verification — a circuit small enough that minimality, per-edge necessity and pairwise interaction can all be checked exhaustively rather than sampled — with the standing caveat that explaining only the modified network would prove nothing about the original.

## What they studied

Four token-level behaviours with paired clean/corrupted prompts: indirect object identification (IOI), subject–verb agreement across an attractor, repeated-token induction, and Python docstring completion. Eight models from four families — GPT-2 small, Llama-3.2-1B/3B-Instruct, Gemma-3-1B/4B-it, Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B — giving 32 task–model cells and 96 runs over seeds 11/22/33. The unit of analysis is the **edge** of a head-level residual-stream graph: writers are the token embedding, each attention head's residual contribution and each MLP output; readers are downstream attention/MLP inputs and the final logits. The candidate graph runs from 2,041 edges in GPT-2 to 43,993 in Qwen3-4B.

## How they did it

A behaviour is specified by a clean prompt and a matched corrupted prompt with a different correct answer, and an edge is "removed" not by zeroing but by having it carry the corrupted-run activation instead — interchange ablation — because zeroing would confound "this edge mattered" with "this input is off-manifold". Each surviving edge carries a binary gate, and the objective is the smallest graph whose accuracy stays within ε = 0.05 of the full graph while a capability ratio ρ ≤ 1 + κ = 1.05 on a held-out perplexity probe is respected. The greedy loop each round: score active edges with EAP-IG on the training split (re-ranked after every accepted cut, 5 interpolation points, 8 ranking batches), propose removing the lowest-scoring 30% of edges, heal by training **only** a rank-16 LoRA adapter for 500 AdamW steps at 1e-4 to minimise KL from the frozen original's distribution through the reduced mask (base weights never move), then accept or backtrack. Self-distillation rather than a task loss is deliberate: a label loss would let the adapter relearn the answer through whatever survives. Acceptance needs three things on the full 2,000-example validation split — both the masked circuit and the gates-open adapted model within 0.05 of baseline, and the adapter-on/off perplexity ratio ≤ 1.05 on a 320-prompt five-domain probe; a failed round restores the previous state and halves the cut, down to a 5% floor. The critical control is the same controller, ranking, gate and stopping rule with θ frozen, which makes "did the weights or the search produce this" a one-variable comparison. Data are 2,000 train / 2,000 validation / 1,000 sealed test per cell, disjoint, with the test split read once per endpoint; every arm is selected as its own smallest circuit within 0.05 of its own full-graph accuracy.

## What counts as an explanation here

An edge set — a mask over a head-level directed graph, plus a small adapter — meaning "these connections, under interchange ablation, carry the behaviour". The explanation is deliberately narrower than a mechanism: the edge count measures *graph width*, not the computation inside retained components, and the adapter (0.9–1.9% of base parameters, model-wide) is excluded from the count entirely. It is meant to be minimal-sufficient rather than complete: on IOI the authors say outright it is a sufficient sub-circuit of the published mechanism, not a recovery of it, because it drops heads the published story names. What it does predict is behaviour: the same answer as the original model on a median 96.5% of sealed-test examples, and where the original model will err (median AUROC 0.894 for ranking the original's own errors by the margin read through the circuit).

## How they judged success

Task performance is restricted-choice accuracy at one answer position (correct token must outrank a task-defined alternative set), a deterministic logit comparison rather than a graded judge. Faithfulness is token-level KL from the adapted reference, with cross-checks against the *original* model's distribution. Baselines: EAP, EAP-IG, EAP-GP, random and anti-ranked edge ordering, the frozen-weight controller, ACDC on the 8 tractable cells, and an ordinary task-SFT LoRA followed by the identical EAP-IG sweep. Statistics are on 32 task–model combinations as units, not 96 runs (seeds share model and data), ratios aggregated as geometric means with cluster-bootstrap intervals and a sign test; reduction significance is p = 2.5e-7, interval [4.9, 13.7] against EAP-IG. The decisive measure is post-hoc and exhaustive: all 2^k subsets for circuits with k ≤ 13 (5,952 masked circuits), all k(k−1)/2 pairs (169,476 ablations over 48 runs), every retained edge ablated alone, and a pre-registered causal-abstraction interchange test against the named IOI algorithm. Controls: healing with no pruning (EAP-IG participation ratio), random order, reverse order, target-specificity across the other three behaviours, and swapping a retained head-to-logits edge for a sibling head in the same layer.

## What they found

Against the strongest frozen baseline (EAP-IG) the condensed circuit is smaller in 30 of 32 cells, geometric-mean 8.1×, maximum 316×, with confidence interval [4.9, 13.7]; against EAP 18.4×, against random ordering 103×. The two losses are docstring/Qwen3-0.6B (1,877 edges kept against 798) and agreement/GPT-2 (453 against 240), the same two tasks with the weakest average reductions (2.7× and 5.1× against 17.7× on IOI and 18.2× on induction). Freezing the weights and keeping everything else removes most of the effect: the frozen controller's circuits are larger in 29 of 32 cells by 7.4×. Ordinary task SFT helps — its circuits are about half of frozen discovery's — but still ends 4.4× larger than the condensed ones. 91 of 96 endpoints re-clear the tolerance on the sealed split.

The payoff is in what becomes checkable. Cutting the retained set drops IOI accuracy from 0.99 to 0.52 (chance 0.50) while cutting the same number of random edges leaves 0.98; 11 of the 19 enumerable circuits contain no smaller faithful subset, the smallest faithful subsets have 3–9 edges, exactly-minimal circuits median 3 edges against 6 for reducible ones, and the worst case keeps 12 where 3 suffice. Matched frozen circuits are unenumerable: the smallest has 68 edges, the median 416. Only 2.5–15% of retained edges are individually necessary at a 0.05 threshold (matched frozen circuits: 0.0% median), but among ≤8-edge circuits 100% are — so per-edge necessity is a small-circuit property, not a general one. Pair ablations make the strongest negative result: independence of effects **fails in every circuit tested**, with a median 7.4 interacting partners per edge, and 87% of the strongest interactions are sub-additive. On IOI/GPT-2 the condensed circuit keeps 58 edges and 24 heads (17 with published roles, precision 0.71) against 256–400 edges and 61 heads (36 unnamed, precision 0.41): F1 rises 0.57 → 0.72 with recall falling 0.96 → 0.65. It keeps all three primary Name Movers and sheds three of eight Backup Name Movers, which the paper reads as expected — attribution ranking cannot see a head dormant on clean inputs. The interchange test on the intermediate (subject-inhibition) variable gives 0.930 against 0.735 for the matched frozen circuit and 0.645 for the unmodified model; the output-variable test separates nothing, as the published algorithm already describes every circuit's readout. Legibility: inspecting the top 20 heads of the condensed IOI circuit gives 0.94 accuracy while the frozen circuit is still at chance and needs 60 heads.

Controls hold up. Healing with no pruning leaves the EAP-IG participation ratio at a median 0.99 (92 of 96 runs within 15%), so the adapter alone does not concentrate attribution; random ordering stalls 50× larger; reverse ordering never cuts; condensing one behaviour makes that behaviour 16× (IOI) or 8× (induction) easier to localise while all twelve off-target directions sit at 1.0×; swapping a retained head for a sibling collapses six of seven circuits to within 0.13 of the all-edges-off floor. Circuit size does not track model size: the candidate graph grows 22× from GPT-2 to Qwen3-4B while the endpoint does not (log-log slope −0.06, interval [−0.33, 0.21], pooled Spearman −0.14 at p = 0.51 — the paper reads this descriptively, not as a finding).

## Limits and open problems they name

(1) The circuit describes the *adapted* network; the KL-to-original evidence (median choice match 96.5%, median KL 0.215, IOI anchor 0.056 vs 0.50) is proximity, not identity. (2) Everything is one ablation convention — interchange — and re-scoring condensed circuits under mean or zero ablation collapses them to chance, though matched frozen circuits at comparable ablated fractions collapse identically and one docstring/Llama-3.2-1B circuit holds 0.92 at K=512, so the sensitivity scales with the ablated fraction rather than with condensation. (3) The capability gate is not equally strict: healing improves probe perplexity on Gemma, so the gate almost never binds there, while it rejects 12–29% of rounds on the other families — Gemma's very small circuits are held to a weaker constraint, and the paper says part of the per-cell spread may be this. (4) Docstring accuracy stays at 1.000 until capability loss ends pruning and agreement accuracy can *rise* as the circuit shrinks, so those endpoint sizes are upper bounds, not minima. (5) Two further hoped-for payoffs failed: surgical deletion-and-retrain and activation-norm monitoring. (6) Three seeds test optimisation stability, not uniqueness across model refits or input distributions; seeds agree on *which* edges matter (median containment 89–95%, overlap 45×–243× above a matched-size null) far better than on *when to stop*. Also: joint mask/adapter training without the loop wins on size in 5 of 32 cells but fails outright in 20, and the cost is up front, repaid only across repeated analyses.

## What we take from it

**A framing, and the folder's first entry where the circuit is an objective rather than a finding.** The reusable idea is not the LoRA heal but the argument structure: *if the representation resists being read, change the substrate until it can be, then prove that the new substrate still answers for the old one* — and the paper prices that proof honestly with two separate tests (KL/choice agreement with the original, and the error-prediction AUROC where the matched frozen circuit scores 0.645 against 0.894). For a predictive white-box model we would steal the verification ladder: size ≤ 13 makes exhaustive minimality possible, size ≤ 8 makes per-edge necessity total, and pair enumeration is the only test that revealed the near-universal sub-additivity.

**A measure with a denominator attached.** "Fraction of individually necessary edges" is 15% on IOI and 0.0% on matched frozen circuits — but the 15% figure is only meaningful next to the 100% at ≤8 edges, and the authors print both. That is the same pattern as run 755 (an aggregate differential dissolving once set size is divided out), arrived at from the other direction: necessity share is a function of circuit size reading (Spearman −0.89 between interacting fraction and size), so it cannot be compared across cells at all.

**A warning about a second network.** Every condensed result holds only for the adapter-augmented model. Our own generated descriptions have the same shape: a probe or surrogate that reads a *re-expressed* activation is answering a question about the re-expression. Any time we train anything into the model in order to make it legible — a transcoder, a small adapter, an interpretable bottleneck — the claim must carry a matched test against the untouched weights, and the matched test must be a behavioural one (does the circuit still predict the original's errors?) not a distributional one alone.

**A shortcut worth running.** The dormant-backup observation is testable cheaply on our side: heads that rank low on clean inputs but carry the behaviour when the primary head is knocked out are invisible to every gradient-on-clean ranking, so any circuit we emit should be probed under primary-removed conditions before we call a head unnecessary. This paper drops three of eight Backup Name Movers by construction and says so; we should measure what that costs rather than inherit the trade.
