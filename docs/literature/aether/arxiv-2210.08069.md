# Zonotope Domains for Lagrangian Neural Network Verification

- **Key:** arxiv-2210.08069
- **Authors / organisation:** Matt Jordan, Jonathan Hayase, Alexandros G. Dimakis, Sewoong Oh; UT Austin and University of Washington
- **Date:** 2022-10-14
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08069
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v1 HTML: main paper, Appendices A-G and references)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
The verification literature split along two lines: primal methods that propagate convex sets through the network and keep some dependence between intermediate neurons, and dual methods that relax the problem with Lagrange multipliers and optimise a lower bound by ascent. The authors' complaint about the second line is specific rather than general - every dual verifier they cite enforces its intermediate constraints as axis-aligned boxes, which treats each neuron independently and throws away the correlations the primal line works hard to keep. Their aim is to take the dual machinery as it stands and merely widen the sets it constrains against, with the expectation that a richer feasible set for each subproblem mechanically raises the dual value. The larger goal is a bound that is both tighter than a linear-programming relaxation and cheaper than the LP or the newer active-set methods, i.e. density of bound per second rather than density of bound alone.

## What they studied
Fully connected and convolutional ReLU networks of small to medium size: an MNIST fully connected network of 960 neurons in 5 layers, an MNIST "deep" network of 5,196 neurons in 6 layers, a wide MNIST variant, a CIFAR-10 network of 6,244 ReLU neurons in 4 layers, and an MNIST convolutional "small" network. Inputs are ℓ∞ balls (ε = 0.1 for MNIST, 5/255 for CIFAR), and instances are scalarised pairwise logit differences. The unit of analysis is neither a neuron nor a weight but the **intermediate set**: a zonotope at each layer, with a centre vector and a generator matrix, plus the dual variable vector ρ_k attached to each layer's coupling constraint.

## How they did it
The verification problem is written as a chain of affine-then-ReLU layers minimising the last coordinate over an input box. Each intermediate variable is split into an A copy and a B copy tied by an equality, the equalities are priced with unrestricted multipliers ρ_k, and weak duality turns the whole thing into a sum of per-layer subproblems (the "Lagrangian decomposition" of Bunel et al.'s line of work). Those subproblems are unbounded unless each intermediate variable is confined to a set that provably contains every reachable point; prior work uses boxes, and the bounding is valid by construction. This paper instead confines each intermediate variable to a **zonotope** produced by the DeepZ pushforward, optionally improved where an incomparable box bound is also available (Proposition 4.2 constructs a narrower pushforward from the intersection). With a zonotope in place, each subproblem becomes what the authors call a ReLU program - minimise a linear term in z plus a linear term in ReLU(z) over a zonotope - and they prove that this is exactly a two-layer network verification problem, hence NP-hard (Theorem 4.1), with a reduction that encodes an arbitrary zonotope as the input map of a two-layer net. Tractable evaluation therefore needs a relaxation, and the paper's is geometric: split the coordinates into blocks, project the zonotope onto each block's coordinate subspace, and minimise each block separately; the block-projection of a zonotope is another zonotope with the corresponding generator rows, so the relaxation stays inside the family and interpolates monotonically between the box relaxation (singleton blocks) and the exact zonotope (one block). They choose two-dimensional blocks because a 2-D ReLU program can be solved exactly in O(m log m) via a gift-wrapping walk over sorted generator slopes (Theorem 4.2), then run Adam on ρ for 1,000 iterations, and finally merge the last layer's blocks into 16- or 20-dimensional zonotopes evaluated once with Gurobi as the MIP solver. An ablation runs the three phases separately on the first 1,000 MNIST examples, and a stagewise variant re-runs the whole pipeline on every intermediate neuron to produce tighter boxes, which are then fed back in as the incomparable-hyperbox input. Comparisons are against BDD+ (the same Lagrangian decomposition with box bounds and optimal proximal steps), the PLANET LP relaxation, the Anderson cut relaxation, CROWN, PRIMA and β-CROWN numbers taken from a prior work, with timings from a shared cluster and the paper's own acknowledgement that hardware varied between jobs.

## What counts as an explanation here
A bound and, implicitly, a certificate: a dual variable vector ρ for every coupling constraint, a partition of every intermediate layer into blocks, and a zonotope per layer whose generators encode the directions in which the intermediate activations are correlated. The form is predictive in the narrow verification sense - every evaluation of the dual with feasible multipliers is a valid lower bound on the minimum output, and the whole object is a re-derivation of the network's reachable set as a chain of enveloping sets rather than a description of units. It is partial by design: the paper targets the bounding phase only and states explicitly that branching is out of scope and could be layered on top.

## How they judged success
Lower bound on the scalarised output, compared against LP, Anderson, BDD+, active-set (AS, 256 and 512 iterations) and, for verified-robustness percentages, CROWN, PRIMA and β-CROWN; presented mainly as cumulative-distribution plots over validation examples rather than as a single mean. Runtime in seconds, mean ± standard deviation, per instance. Controls: an ablation holding the algorithm fixed and varying only the initialization (DeepZ alone, DeepZ plus IBP, DeepZ plus stagewise BDD+ boxes), which isolates how much of the gain comes from the primal half versus the dual half; a hyperparameter-matched active-set baseline run for 1,000 iterations to match the authors' own iteration count; and a "→ ZD" arm in which each competitor's own box bounds are fed into this method, which tests the add-on claim directly rather than by argument.

## What they found
The guarantee claimed is monotonicity, not a new bound family: for any fixed ρ the paper's value is at least the box version's, because the block-projected zonotope is a subset of the layer's box for every partition, and it reduces to the DeepZ bound at singleton blocks. Measured, on the MNIST fully connected network the ablation walks from a DeepZ-only start at 2.9 above the LP benchmark, through the 2-D phase, to 7.7 after the MIP evaluation, and the BDD+-initialised arm goes 5.3 → 6.4 → 10.1 - so a tight primal start is worth about 2.4 at the end of the pipeline but almost nothing at initialization, and the final relaxation-merging step is worth roughly as much again as all 1,000 ascent iterations. In the single-stage setting ZD-2D runs in 10.9 s on MNIST-deep against 4.8 s for LP, 2.5 s for BDD+ and 52.4 s for Anderson, while ZD-MIP costs 23.1 s for the tighter bound. In the stagewise setting the add-on result is the clean one: BDD+ alone takes 15.4 s on MNIST-deep, and the same BDD+ boxes fed into this method reach a tighter bound curve in 51.7 s, beating AS256 (138.9 s) and landing near AS512 (289.3 s); LP takes 2,132.2 s for a worse bound. Unbranched verified accuracy is where the method is weak relative to the branching-based state of the art: on MLP 5x100 at ε = 0.026 it verifies 28.5% in 24 s against 19.8% for BDD+ and 69.9% for β-CROWN in 102 s, with the authors stating plainly that it is unclear whether their approach can surpass β-CROWN given that branching needs millions of subproblem solves.

## Limits and open problems they name
Only piecewise-linear activations can be handled, because the MIP evaluation of the ReLU program does not extend to sigmoid-type nonlinearities. The MIP phase cannot use the GPU, so scaling to state-of-the-art image models is doubtful, and the authors ask for fast tight bounds for one-hidden-layer subproblems, noting that SDPs cannot currently fill that gap. Intermediate zonotopes grow in generator count with depth; "order reduction" (soundly compressing generators) is named as an unexploited speedup, with naive compression giving roughly a 2x factor at marginal cost. Exact dual evaluation seemed to be required in practice - Frank-Wolfe, L-BFGS and a modified simplex all produced gradients too poor to help. Their own tighter pushforward is not vectorised and was therefore never used stagewise. Open theoretical question stated rather than answered: whether ReLU programs over parallelepipeds (zonotopes with invertible generator matrix) are also hard, since the NP-hardness proof covers the general case only.

## What we take from it
- **Framing (the useful one):** the intermediate set is a *dictionary of directions* - the generator matrix of a zonotope at layer k is literally a list of perturbation directions the reachable set extends along, produced by the same forward pass that would otherwise give only a box. That is a read-out on a checkpoint with no data and no training: generators that survive to layer k are the directions the network genuinely transports. This is the concrete instance of what run 681 asked for (addressable per-index objects) and what run 680 wanted from an ordered factor list - a set of named directions per layer, each with a coefficient bound of 1.
- **Measure:** the partition of a layer's coordinates into blocks is a free, tunable, and *reportable* granularity dial, and it interpolates exactly between the box relaxation and the exact set. Our readings should state their block granularity, as run 686 demanded of certificate decompositions; this paper is the propagation-side analogue.
- **Warning:** the authors' own numbers show the dual variable ρ is optimised for 1,000-2,000 Adam steps and never reported - no distribution, no per-block norm, nothing. This is the sixth independent confirmation of run 678's standing gap, and it arrives with the gap fully "in the loop": ρ_k is the per-layer worth of the coupling constraint, exactly the per-unit quantity the head keeps asking for, and the paper is content to discard it.
- **Method to reuse:** the gift-wrapping vertex walk (sort generators by slope, accumulate) is a cheap exact way to get all vertices of a 2-D projection, i.e. an exact description of the set as a polygon from the generator list; if we ever want to visualise a plausible-set slice of a layer, this is the algorithm.
