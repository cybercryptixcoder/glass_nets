## What they wanted, and why

Narrow supervised fine-tuning on a single harmless-looking domain (bad financial advice, insecure code) can leave a model broadly misaligned out of domain — emergent misalignment — and the interpretability literature has already claimed a small set of SAE persona features mediates it. The authors take that claim at face value as a *premise to be tested by acting on it*: if misalignment really runs through a handful of internal features, then suppressing movement along exactly those directions during fine-tuning should prevent it while leaving the intended narrow behaviour intact. Their goal is a training-time constraint rather than an inference-time edit, on the argument that clamping at generation holds the quality/safety trade-off hostage to dose and can be routed around. This makes the paper the folder's first entry in which an SAE feature identification is used not as an explanation but as the *definition of a loss term whose success or failure is then a test of the identification* — a direct instance of the goal our project has (prediction under intervention), with the interesting twist that the intervention lives inside the optimiser rather than in a forward pass.

## What they studied

One model, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, at one layer: the output of transformer block 20 of 32, read and constrained through a frozen Goodfire SAE with expansion factor 32 (dictionary ≈ 131k latents). The unit of analysis is a *signed SAE latent set* — twenty latents, split into K⁺ (those whose mean activation rose under misaligning fine-tuning) and K⁻ (those that fell) — plus the training-time penalty defined on them. Fine-tuning is LoRA with rank 16, alpha 32, on q/k/v/o and the three MLP projections, one epoch at lr 7.5e-5, effective batch 64. Domains: finance advice (primary, 5,900 training samples with a 30–100 prompt in-domain holdout), health advice (full replication), PrimeVul (insecure code), career, legal, education and automotive advice. Two prompt suites: a 44-prompt domain-agnostic `core misalignment` set used for discovery and screening (taken from Wang et al. 2025), and a 29-prompt `final evaluation` set assembled from the official repos of Wang et al. and Betley et al., deduplicated to zero overlap with the discovery set. Judging is by two LLM judges (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) on a rubric that separates REFUSAL / INCOHERENT / SATIRICAL / OFF-TOPIC from a 1–5 misalignment severity, with misaligned := 4 or 5.

## How they did it

Discovery is a three-stage filter over 131k latents, run offline on a base/misaligned checkpoint pair and never re-run at training time. Stage 1 is correlational: run both checkpoints on the 44 discovery prompts, take each latent's token-averaged activation, and keep the top 250 positive and top 250 negative mean shifts (|Δ| ≥ 0.042 for the expanded sweep). Stage 2 makes it causal by *induce-and-repair* steering: for each candidate, add its normalised decoder direction at every token of layer 20 (scaled by the median token-norm of layer-20 hidden states on 1,000 Alpaca prompts, ≈14.9) into the base model at a fixed positive dose and ask whether misalignment rises, and into the misaligned model at a fixed negative dose and ask whether it falls; the combined score is the sum of the two changes, the top 40 per sign shortlisted. Stage 3 calibrates each survivor by sweeping α over a grid up to 0.75 (1.5 for the strongest), keeps only doses that hold incoherence under a 10% budget, and ranks by induction-plus-repair at each latent's maximum safe dose, taking the top 20. The training objective anchors to a frozen copy of the base model evaluated on the same batch: L_block is the squared *one-sided* ReLU violation of the current model's latent activation against the base model's, summed over K⁺ when the latent rises and over K⁻ when it falls, averaged over supervised (completion) tokens only, and added as L_SFT + λ·L_block. The one-sidedness is the paper's central design choice — a latent is unconstrained unless fine-tuning pushes it past its base value in the misalignment-associated direction, which is meant to leave ordinary task learning free. They sweep λ from 0 to 1e5, compare against a KL-to-base regulariser swept over nine values, freeze layers 21–32 at the blocking layer as a variant, reuse one finance-derived latent set across all six other domains, and then stress the whole thing with multi-epoch training to find where it breaks.

## What counts as an explanation here

A small, signed, causally screened feature set at one layer, expressed as a list of SAE latent indices plus a direction label per latent — a dictionary-plus-direction object, deliberately partial. It is not claimed to be the mechanism in any complete sense; the paper says explicitly that rerouting evidence points to an incompletely covered misalignment subspace. The part that makes it more than a probe is that the explanation is *imported into the loss*: a predictor that says "these twenty coordinates carry the misalignment signal" must be right about the sign and about which coordinates, or the training run fails to suppress misalignment — and the ablation where signs are shuffled is exactly that test. The prediction offered is narrow and conditional: during fine-tuning, misalignment arises to the extent these coordinates move in the identified direction, so pinning them pins the behaviour.

## How they judged success

Three axes, all LLM-judged, with in-domain performance deliberately in tension with the safety objective because the in-domain task *is* to produce bad advice: (i) emergent misalignment rate on the held-out 29-prompt suite; (ii) generation quality as an incoherence rate (INCOHERENT + OFF-TOPIC + SATIRICAL/ABSURD) with refusal rate tracked separately so that a move toward blanket refusal cannot be read as a safety win; (iii) in-domain performance as final SFT loss plus task adherence on a held-out in-domain set (fraction scored 4–5 by a domain rubric). The safety–utility frontier is reported as normalised misalignment reduction against normalised adherence, averaged over two judges and (for the headline) over six domains at λ = 1.3e4. Controls and ablations are unusually complete for this folder: **random 20 latents** as a null (no reduction); **Stage-1-only top-Δ** as the correlational arm (partial reduction); **shuffled K⁺/K⁻ signs** with the same set (weak suppression); single-sided K⁺-only and K⁻-only constraints (weaker than the signed pair); fifteen different latent-set constructions varying the source checkpoint pair and the ranking rule; set size swept 1→20 plus 30/40/60/100 variants; the final-layer (32) variant; and, crucially, an explicit *selection vs evaluation* comparison showing that performance is better on the discovery prompts, as selection bias predicts. The multi-epoch section adds activation patching as an independent localisation probe.

## What they found

Baseline LoRA SFT on plain finance advice takes a 0% misaligned model to 40% on the held-out suite; λ = 1e3 halves that to 21% with negligible incoherence, and λ = 1e5 returns it to 2.8% at the cost of 12% incoherence. With the layer-20 constraint and layers 21–32 frozen, the same sweep gives 38% → 3% while incoherence stays near the λ = 0 baseline even at λ = 5e4. Reusing the finance-derived latent set across all six domains at λ = 1.3e4 gives a 93% relative reduction averaged over domains with 2.72 points absolute incoherence increase and 4.14% relative loss of in-domain adherence; a 42-latent union variant reaches 97% relative reduction with 5.75% incoherence and a 40.37% relative *increase* in adherence, and the paper's headline abstract figure is "up to 95%" relative; health-to-finance transfer replicates in the reverse direction. The real mechanism tests: random latents do nothing, top-Δ alone is partial, shuffling signs or using only one side weakens the effect, moving the constraint to layer 32 is substantially weaker, constraining more latents keeps helping with a knee around |K| ≈ 13, and the three latents added at that knee do not carry the effect alone — the paper reads the knee as a collective requirement rather than a privileged few. Against KL, the sweep shows larger misalignment reduction at comparable adherence. Then it breaks: with continued training, misalignment re-emerges even at high λ. Freezing layers 21–32 does not stop it, and the blocking loss stays near zero throughout the re-emergence, so the penalised latents are not creeping back up. Re-running discovery on the re-emerged checkpoint finds new layer-20 latents whose average induce-plus-repair score is 14% against 24% for the original set — roughly 0.6 of the original steering capacity still available in the same layer — and blocking the union of both sets keeps misalignment lower across epochs. Patching pins the location: prefix-only layerwise patching reduces misalignment more when applied upstream than downstream, and patching *only* the layer-20 state of each generated token from the base model eliminates misalignment (0%, 0% incoherence, 2% refusal) while every layer above 20 is left untouched. The authors conclude the re-emerging signal is already present at or upstream of the blocking layer, and that the failure is incomplete coverage of that layer's misalignment-relevant directions rather than either dictionary drift or downstream adaptation — the dictionary-drift hypothesis is explicitly argued down on the basis of published SAE transfer plus a reconstruction-quality sanity check, which is the weakest inference in the paper.

## Limits and open problems they name

Single model, single family, one layer, one SAE, one misalignment-inducing recipe; the mechanism identified at layer 20 is not shown to be the mechanism at other depths (their own layer-32 run is much weaker, which they read as a fact about where the behaviour is routable rather than as a limitation of their method). The blocking set is fixed and single-layer, so it is provably circumventable given enough optimisation, and the paper's own remedy is a suggestion, not a solution: larger sets, multi-layer blocking, and adaptive λ. Latent discovery costs a checkpoint pair plus a large steering sweep, which is why a frozen pretrained dictionary with released latent sets is offered as the practical path. All measurement is LLM-judged. And the headline 95% is a relative reduction over six domains averaged at one λ — a good number, but one that rides on a rubric that ranks severity 4–5 and on two judges of the same generation family.

Mine, marked: (a) the discovery, screening and calibration prompts are a 44-prompt set *drawn from* the same source as the evaluation set, and although the authors deduplicate at the prompt level, the two suites code the same six behaviour categories, so a latent set selected to move the discovery prompts has a structural advantage on the held-out prompts that no prompt-level dedup removes — their own selection-vs-evaluation panels show the size of this effect, which is the right disclosure but does not make the held-out number independent; (b) the incoherence budget τ = 10% is applied during *calibration* of steering doses, not during training, so the trade-off curves are the only guard against a "repair" that is generic degradation, and the arms that matter (random latents, shuffled signs) are shown as curves rather than as numbers at a matched incoherence; (c) there is no norm-matched random-direction arm at the *training* stage — random latents is the closest, and it is a much stronger null, which is fine, but it means "these twenty coordinates" is not yet separated from "twenty coordinates at all"; (d) my reading of the re-emergence section is that it is the most valuable part of the paper and the least controlled — one checkpoint, one epoch budget, λ = 3000, and the 0.6 residual-capacity ratio compares scores computed against different target models; (e) the seed count is stated only in figure captions and prose — the λ and in-domain panels are explicitly averaged over three seeds, while the patching experiments and several ablations look single-run, so the ablations that carry the mechanism argument are the least replicated.

## What we take from it

**A method.** Induce-and-repair as a two-sided causal screen is the cheapest identification test in the folder and we should adopt it: a candidate direction must be able to *create* the behaviour in a clean model and *remove* it in an affected model, at doses calibrated to a fixed quality budget, or it is not identified. It costs two forward-generation sweeps per candidate and it kills the correlational arm (their top-Δ) which is otherwise indistinguishable in a paper's tables from the causal one.

**A measure.** Signed, one-sided violation against a frozen reference — squared ReLU of the per-latent difference, opposite directions for rising and falling features — is a reusable constraint shape: it makes "don't move past this" a number, and the shuffled-sign ablation is the control that proves the sign carries information. For our predictive white-box work this is the natural scoring function for "does the predictor know the direction, or only the coordinate".

**A warning, and it is the important one.** A fixed coordinate system is not a fixed mechanism. The model routed around a 20-latent block inside the *same layer* without the blocked latents ever rising, so a set of directions can be exhaustively blocked and still leave the behaviour reachable through coordinates nobody selected. Any claim of ours of the form "we have removed or explained capability X by acting on Y" has to be tested under continued optimisation and against a re-discovery pass, because the failure mode is silent — this is the third entry in the folder to show that a complete-looking explanation can be complete only for the data it was fitted on, and the first where the organism is allowed to keep training while the explanation is held fixed.

**A shortcut.** Patching only the blocking-layer state of each generated token and leaving all later layers untouched eliminated the behaviour. That is a one-line localisation test we can run on any predictor: if replacing a single layer's residual at one position with the reference model's value recovers the behaviour completely, the claim that the mechanism is at or upstream of that layer is settled and everything downstream is a decoder. It also gives us a clean way to say *where* a predictor must be right.
