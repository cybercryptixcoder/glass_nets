## What they wanted, and why
The field's founding evidence for superposition — a polysemantic cat/car neuron in InceptionV1, an unreadable late layer, a one-layer model whose units look like arbitrary mixtures — is, on this account, mostly the result of bad architectures and weak microscopes rather than a second learning principle. The author, who proposed some of that evidence himself, argues that what looked like arbitrary packing is usually adjacency: a neuron sprouts offshoots onto nearby concepts as it grows, the way a branch sprouts sub-branches, which he names recursive generalization. If that is right, there is only one learning principle, generalization, and networks can be studied directly with their own neurons and circuits instead of being unfolded first. The goal is not a better dictionary but confidence that the original object is legible, so that ordinary science on it — including alignment work that reads mechanisms rather than pushing gradients through them — is worth doing.

## What they studied
Vision: InceptionV1 (original 2014 checkpoint plus retrained copies), ConvNeXt small/medium/large (92K, 7.06M and 28.6M parameters, roughly a 300× range), DINOv2, a retrained InceptionV1 whose mixed3b is squeezed from 480 to 10 channels, and a 7M model taught a nonsense label. Language: GPT-2 XL neuron papers, Qwen3-32B MLP neurons (~a million neurons against a few thousand heads), Anthropic's Claude 3.5 Haiku transcoder features for comparison, and a trillion-parameter model (Kimi K3) named as audited at the same rate. Unit of analysis: the neuron as the model's own mechanism — its positive and negative poles, what it reads and writes, the circuits that feed it, and its trajectory over training.

## How they did it
Five converging designs. (1) Retrain the suspect architecture with ordinary learning fixes only (residual stream, LayerNorm and "a couple other improvements"), parameters and data unchanged, and re-rate the last layer's neurons. (2) Re-render old neurons with better feature visualization on both routes — free optimization versus optimization biased toward the paths real images take, often only for the first 64 steps. (3) Watch neurons form: save checkpoints from epoch 0.7 to 12, then read a neuron's strongest images over time and see what it starts on and what it grows onto. (4) Squeeze capacity on purpose — cut InceptionV1's mixed3b from 480 to 10 channels with no bypass and retrain — then ask whether the missing curve detectors reappear anywhere by training a TopK SAE (640 features, 2 active per position, auxiliary loss) on the squeezed layer. (5) Check the same questions in language: hand-build attribution graphs over Qwen3-32B MLP neurons, compare them with published transcoder graphs on the same prompts, and score neuron explanations with blind maximum (one agent writes a short explanation, a second writes a maximally activating sentence from the explanation alone) and with a pre-registered prediction test in which an agent writes five new examples and five near misses from the explanation before any of them is measured.

## What counts as an explanation here
A short natural-language account of a single neuron's job, stated as a distinction it draws at its two poles rather than as a list of nouns, plus a circuit account of why it is there. Explanation is explicitly meant to be partial and zoomable — "a fractal of explanations" — with the same neuron explained coarsely at one scale and by its sub-branches at another. The essay's own framing is exploratory rather than enumerative: no complete feature list is the target, and it argues the count question is the wrong question. It does claim prediction, but narrowly: from an explanation, an agent should be able to write inputs that drive the neuron and near misses that do not, and from a growth story one should be able to say what the offshoots will be.

## How they judged success
A battery rather than one metric. Interpretability rates per layer and per model, scored with an explicit rubric whose threshold for "what counts as one thing" is acknowledged as a knob. Shape and photo correlation of each of ten original curve detectors against its closest SAE feature on the squeezed layer (tuning curves over 180 dark arcs at two curvatures; correlation over every position of 10,000 held-out photos), plus a nonlinear readout upper bound. Behavioural prediction: new-example firing rate versus near-miss firing rate, with the threshold for a "strong" response set at 30% of the neuron's corpus maximum, and win/tie/loss counts against that threshold. Blind maximum as a construction check against the real corpus. Ablation of neurons in graphs, of weak and strong activations by matched total activation mass, and of dictionary directions that match no neuron. Attribution-graph audits at scale (100,000 graphs reviewed by agents, thousands inspected by the author) treated as qualitative evidence only.

## What they found
- **The suspect layer was fixable.** InceptionV1's final layer went from roughly 0% interpretable in 2014 to about 99% in a parameter-matched, same-data ConvNeXt; the author's own estimate for the original checkpoint with modern tools is about 50%, about 70% after retraining the same architecture with modern regularization, with others having put the original at approximately 0%.
- **The canonical polysemantic neuron is one shape, not two objects.** 4e:55 responds to a bilateral pointed shape: cats and foxes share a pointed snout, the cars sit at similar angles, and its negative pole is a round O where the positive is a V. Its input circuitry connects to V-shaped flames, V-shaped water jets and pointed fox faces.
- **Packing is not what happens under pressure.** Squeezing mixed3b 48× gives coarse but legible neurons (one "curves & loops" unit instead of ten curve detectors) at 58.35% top-1 and 81.14% top-5, and an SAE trained on that layer does not recover the originals: best shape correlations to the ten detectors are about 0.52–0.72 with photo matches of 0.09–0.14, and every one of the 91 most curve-responsive features keeps firing at the opposite angle where the originals are silent, with original-layer SAE matching at 0.96–1.00. A nonlinear readout of the ten neurons at one position reaches only 0.29–0.48 correlation. Splicing the SAE in drops top-1 from 58.8% to 43.0%.
- **Growth is the explanation.** Neuron 338 in a retrained InceptionV1 starts on rapeseed fields and a dog in one, grows onto tennis balls, green apples and jackfruit, then shaggy dogs with tennis balls and broccoli; the same-data ConvNeXt neuron 399 starts on the same fields and drops the rest. One live neuron went from "iy" to "diy" to "do it yourself" and Miyazaki.
- **Most language neurons have one job.** A preliminary audit of Qwen3-32B puts roughly 90% of neuron poles on one main job with small related offshoots, about 7% on two related jobs, and only about 2–4% not yet understood, moving to about 97% when only strong activations are counted; GPT-2 XL went from under 2% clean by the 2023 project to about 70% looking like roughly one thing, about 5% pristine, the rest mostly two broad jobs up to about six.
- **The double negative is architecture, not superposition.** For about 4% of neurons in SwiGLU models the top of the activation list is the −×− echo: in one audited neuron 131 of 133 responses in the p75–p99 band are double negatives, while 93 of 93 at p99.99 and above are +×+. Separating the sign regimes leaves the positive side under +0.5, about 4% of peak. Ablating them across the network raises most benchmarks but lowers some reasoning ones, unexplained.
- **Explanations can lose, and mostly do not.** For 200 random gallery neurons, agent-written new examples fired their neuron strongly 76% of the time against 11% for near misses, with 164 wins, 15 ties and 4 losses; on the first twenty shown, 81 of 100 new examples fired strongly against 8 of 100 near misses, winning for all twenty. Blind maximum routinely beats the best example in ten billion corpus tokens.
- **Dictionaries add little.** On the last layer, 88% of one SAE's activity is near-copies of single neurons, removing every direction that matches no neuron leaves accuracy unchanged (the few answers it flips were already near ties), searching rotations for the one where a few units carry most activity lands back on the neurons in every block, and what remains are loose groupings built from existing neurons (animals at the water's surface at 0.15–0.19 weights on four neurons).
- **Rare things ride existing routes.** A 7M model taught that an umbrella photo is a bee shifts its answer from 44.2% to 0.03% umbrella and 0.07% to 52.8% bee in nine training steps by fading umbrella-associated neurons and strengthening bee-associated ones in the last three blocks; swapping those blocks into the original model reproduces the answer. No new hidden feature appears.
- **Weak activations can be removed.** Deleting the weakest 75% of activations (72% → 71% accuracy, one answer in ten changing) and deleting the strongest 7% at the same total activation mass (72% → 4%, 96 in 100 changing) prices the two halves against each other.
- **The residual stream is not blocking.** A ConvNeXt retrained with its stream crushed four and eight times narrower, down to 46 dimensions in the last block, falls from 72% to 60% and 39% accuracy but maps similarly; the trap was an unread scalar dimension that scaled everything else through LayerNorm.
- **Attention is the weak spot.** The author's rough estimate, repeated independently by an agent on 100 graphs, is that global head names would add about 5% explanatory value.

## Limits and open problems they name
Not every network has been audited, and the author is unsure whether that is even possible in principle; the belief in a single principle is stated as roughly 85% confidence in the kind of process, not in a percentage of mechanisms, and the title is admitted to overstate the evidence and understate the picture simultaneously. Recursive generalization has not been shown to cover every case, the pressure that keeps branches from overgrowing is unknown, and checkpoints do not exist for the largest models so growth cannot be watched there. Attention is not understood well enough to say whether it is superposed at all. Some exceptions are unresolved (one InceptionV1 neuron collecting pinched shapes plus dress shoes and beach bags, described as "generalization with semantic residue"). The cleanliness numbers depend on where the threshold for one thing is set.

## What we take from it
Four things. (1) **A confound we can name and check in our own work**: a multi-concept neuron is a claim about the observers as much as the model, and the checklist here is cheap — ask whether one shared pattern explains the group before accepting a noun list, visualize what a neuron's inputs have in common rather than the neuron alone, and check the negative pole, because a V-vs-O pair is a distinction and not a mixture. (2) **The capacity-squeeze experiment as a superposition test**: cut a layer's width and retrain, then ask whether the missing units reappear anywhere in a decomposition. If they do not, "the network packed them elsewhere" is a hypothesis you can reject, and this essay does reject it with correlations rather than eyeballing. (3) **Blind maximum as a cheap evaluation of an explanation we write**: an explanation that lets an independent writer produce a stronger example than the corpus contains has done real work and cannot have been fitted to the examples. (4) **A warning about inert scale**: the sweep of neuron counts we use to argue that our read-out covers the model may be measuring the same phenomenon at several sizes.
