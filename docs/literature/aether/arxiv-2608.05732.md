# CircuitSteer: Geometrically Aligned Multi-Layer Steering via Sparse Autoencoder Circuits

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.05732
- **Authors / organisation:** Mehrshad Saadatinia, Parsa Razmara, Ardalan Aryashad, Ali Abbasi, Seyedarmin Azizi; University of Southern California
- **Date:** 2026-08-06
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05732
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Single-layer steering adds one aggregated difference vector at one depth, which mixes concepts that share the residual stream and produces a coarse, easily-broken intervention. Sparse autoencoders give cleaner units, but almost all SAE steering still fires at one layer, even though the authors take it as established that a behavioural concept lives across several depths. Stacking interventions naively fails for two named reasons: the per-layer directions for the same concept point in incompatible orientations so they interfere destructively, and each early edit changes what every later layer sees, so edits compound into fluency collapse. The goal is a steering method that follows one concept through depth and applies a coordinated, fluency-preserving push at several layers at once.

## What they studied
Gemma-2-2B (26 layers) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers), with public residual-stream SAE suites read off the shelf (width 16k for Gemma at layers {6,12,18,24}; width 32k for Llama at {3,8,16,24,29}), plus a Qwen3.5-27B / TopK-SAE replication. Unit of analysis is the **SAE feature** and the **cross-layer edge** between features at adjacent SAE layers; the object explained is a feature-flow circuit, and the object intervened on is a per-layer dense vector built from the features on that circuit. Four behaviours: toxicity (RealToxicityPrompts, Jigsaw), anger-suppression (Emotion, joy as contrast), sycophancy (Anthropic multiple-choice survey), and refusal (AdvBench).

## How they did it
A directed edge from feature *i* at one SAE layer to feature *j* at the next exists for an input only if the two features are both active above an activation threshold **and** their decoder directions have cosine above an alignment threshold. Edges are then scored by contrastive specificity: the frequency of the edge on target prompts minus its frequency on contrastive prompts, keeping the top K by that difference. Every feature that touches a kept edge becomes a member of the layer's feature set; the per-layer steering vector is the plain mean of those features' decoder directions, so its scale does not grow with circuit size. At generation, every layer with a non-empty set is edited at once with one shared coefficient. The paper is explicit that co-activation and cosine are candidate indicators of cross-layer influence rather than proof of it, and that causal credit comes from component ablations instead. Discovery is a one-off offline pass (about 38 s for the 500-example Gemma contrast set, 10-12 GB peak). A second protocol, decision-point discovery, extracts features at the position of the first generated token rather than the last input token, and builds contrast pairs from the same harmful prompt with and without a compliance-eliciting system prompt.

## What counts as an explanation here
A sparse, inspectable cross-layer subgraph plus a small set of dense per-layer vectors derived from it. It is deliberately partial: the graph is a candidate structure, the vectors are the deployed artifact, and no claim is made that any single edge is causal. The vectors are mean-difference directions like CAA's, but averaged over features chosen for multi-layer geometry rather than over prompts. Prediction: the method predicts that a behaviour can be moved at several depths without wrecking text, and the coefficient sweeps are treated as a response surface that is smoother than single-layer or unaligned baselines'.

## How they judged success
Two metrics per cell: behavioural reduction (drop in a scorer's probability for the target behaviour - Detoxify toxicity, anger class, sycophantic token) and **normalized perplexity** (steered PPL divided by base PPL, 1.0 meaning no degradation). A cell counts only if it lands in a perplexity window between 0.01 and 1.5 and shows reduction of at least 0.02; the window was fixed using disfluent and degenerate generations on data separate from the evaluation. Eight baselines: prompt, single-layer and multi-layer CAA, RepE, ITI, LoReFT, SpARE, SAE-SSV. Controls include an external LLM judge scoring toxicity and fluency jointly, one-sided bootstrap lower bounds on the reduction, a benign-prompt collateral-damage table, MMLU and GSM8K under strong steering, threshold sweeps on all three discovery hyperparameters, four contrastive-set substitutions, seed resplits, and a **matched-layer CAA control** that applies CAA at exactly CircuitSteer's layers.

## What they found
CircuitSteer is the only method with a valid operating point in all eight model-dataset cells; every baseline has at least one dash. On Gemma it scores 0.072 on Jigsaw and 0.057 on sycophancy (the only valid method there), and is second on toxicity 0.113 behind ITI's 0.119 and on emotion 0.135 behind ITI's 0.230. On Llama it leads toxicity at 0.128 while ITI is stronger on sycophancy (0.356 against 0.156) and Jigsaw (0.077 against 0.034). Bootstrap lower bounds are positive on 7 of 8 cells, Llama-Jigsaw being the exception. Refusal drops from 89% to 0% at the strong coefficient while single-layer CAA leaves it essentially intact and its median perplexity barely moves. Component ablations: a random projection in place of the learned SAE fails outright on both Llama tasks, anti-aligned edges give the lowest reduction and break fluency on sycophancy, and a single-mid-layer version loses sycophancy on Gemma. Layer-count sweep: CircuitSteer's reduction rises monotonically from one to four layers at near-baseline perplexity, whereas CAA at the same layers gains nothing beyond the first layer and its Gemma perplexity climbs from 1.31 to 6.37. The matched-layer control is the honest part: on toxicity matched-layer CAA reaches a **higher** peak reduction (0.232 against 0.113) but is inside the fluency window at only 2 of 7 coefficients versus 7 of 7. Under Qwen3.5-27B with unchanged hyperparameters, reduction runs 0.451 down to 0.212 across the sweep at perplexity 0.90-0.97, leading three of four coefficients once the sweep is full.

## Limits and open problems they name
No claim that an edge is causal; the evidence for the intervention is the ablations, not the graph. The perplexity window and the 0.02 floor are choices, validated on disjoint data but still choices. Sycophancy collapses when the contrastive set does not share the target's multiple-choice format, which the authors read as the specificity score needing structurally matched contrasts. Threshold sweeps move reduction by at most 0.044, below the per-prompt noise, but at extreme over-pruning the Llama circuit drops to six edges and reduction falls to 0.032. No evaluation above 27B because public multi-layer SAE suites stop there. The refusal experiment is keyword-scored on one model. Discovery cost scales with the square of dictionary width in the worst case and is only bounded in practice by SAE sparsity.

## What we take from it
Three things. (1) **A cheap stability instrument we should copy wholesale:** they report edge-Jaccard, node-Jaccard and steering-vector cosine across contrastive resamples, and the gap is the finding - edges hold at Jaccard 0.765 / 0.579 while the averaged per-layer vectors sit at cosine 0.898 / 0.969, so the deployed object is far more stable than the structure it was derived from. Averaging is a smoothing operator over unit identity, and that is worth knowing before we treat any recovered unit set as the thing that matters. (2) **The matched-site control as a default:** putting the rival method at the winner's exact layers beats the winner on peak effect and loses on coverage; a white-box model that reports only peak effect would call this a win for the rival. Every site or depth claim we publish should ship the rival at our site. (3) **A warning on window-based scoring:** the headline "only method valid everywhere" is a statement about a pre-chosen plausibility window, and the paper's own matched-layer table shows a competitor with double the peak effect inside it. Read the window as part of the metric.
