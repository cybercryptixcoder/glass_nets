# Tokenization and Architecture Jointly Allocate Component Roles in Time Series Transformers

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.31730
- **Authors / organisation:** Mingyu Kim, Doguk Kim (corresponding); Department of Artificial Intelligence, Inha University, Incheon, Republic of Korea
- **Date:** 2026-11-24
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31730
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
The time-series transformer literature appears self-contradictory: one-line linear models once beat early point-token transformers, patching restored transformer dominance, and then self-attention turned out to be removable in some patched models while cross-attention alone suffices in another. The authors suspect the contradictions come from using a single word, "attention", for different information flows, and that the real story sits in the interaction between how the input is tokenized and what the attention block mixes. They want an internal account of *which sub-layer makes a temporal property usable*, rather than a comparison of final forecast errors. The larger goal is a representational reading of prior benchmark results — why patching helps, why mixer-free patched models compete, why CATS needs only cross-attention — and a design rule about what to engineer first.

## What they studied
Five supervised models plus one auxiliary, all frozen: PatchTST (16-point patches, stride 8, self-attention, 3 layers, channel-independent), PatchTST at patch length 1 / stride 1 ("single-scalar tokens"), two PatchTST-NoAttn variants (attention output zeroed in a trained checkpoint, and trained from scratch), iTransformer (one token per variate), CATS (encoder removed, cross-attention decoder only), and Informer (time-step tokens, auxiliary and confounded); ModernTCN appears in an appendix. Data: eight long-term forecasting benchmarks (ETTm1, ETTm2, ETTh1, ETTh2, Weather, Electricity, Exchange, Traffic), Solar-Energy excluded for data-quality reasons. The unit is the sub-layer: the embedding, the post-attention point and the post-FFN point of each layer, probed token by token through linear heads. The properties probed are token-level statistics of a token's own points or of its position in the window — 15 diagnostic tasks plus a random-label control.

## How they did it
The spine is a "temporal probe delta": a linear probe is trained on frozen representations at the embedding, after attention and after the FFN, and the difference in accuracy between consecutive points is attributed to the sub-layer in between. Tasks are pre-sorted into innate (a linear map of the patch already determines the label, e.g. trend direction), constructed (quadratic or spectral quantities — volatility, diff energy, spectral entropy — which no linear read-out can express) and boundary (an absolute value of an already-linear quantity). Only constructed tasks can show who constructs; three of them carry the main analysis at four classes, chance 25. Probes are single linear layers on one token's representation, trained with Adam at batch 32,768 for 30k steps on an 80/20 split, evaluated on the test split of each dataset, with three model seeds times three probe seeds. That linear family is chosen deliberately because every model in the main analysis reads its representation through a linear head, and a matched MLP probe is run as a contrast to show that the embedding already holds the information in a nonlinear form. To attribute causally, they add three forecasting-side instruments: activation patching (restore a clean Layer-1 component output into a run whose input chunks were shuffled), component knockout (zero a component in all layers of a frozen checkpoint), and retraining with attention zeroed from initialization. Controls include a random-label probe per point, and patch-length, stride, horizon and width sweeps.

## What counts as an explanation here
A location claim: within this tokenization and architecture, this sub-layer is where the property becomes linearly readable — expressed as a per-component delta, plus a statement of reliance and sufficiency from the forecasting side. It is deliberately partial. The authors say in several places that a delta locates where a property becomes decodable, not whether the forecaster uses it, and that their account is a regularity across evaluated models rather than a mechanism; they explicitly decline to give a derivation for why the allocation shifts. Prediction is one-step: knowing a model's tokenization and attention type lets you predict which component leads, but not the magnitude per dataset.

## How they judged success
Delta in probing accuracy in percentage points (8-dataset means of 9-run means), a root-sum-square of the two probing points' run standard deviations as the delta's error bar, paired t-tests with Cohen's d pooled over dataset-task pairs, and a random-label control that must stay at chance. Robustness against probe capacity is handled with a selectivity metric (Hewitt–Liang) using the random-label task instead of word-type tasks, with the stated limitation that it measures selectivity rather than probe capacity. On the forecasting side: MSE recovered under activation patching relative to a damaged-run baseline, relative MSE change under knockout, and MSE of the from-scratch attention-free model against the full one. Orderings across architectures are checked with leave-one-dataset-out recomputation.

## What they found
- The embedding splits temporal properties into two classes: trend direction is readable there at 94.9–99.9 across datasets, while volatility, diff energy and spectral entropy sit between 27.7 and 54.9 at the embedding — near chance for a four-class task.
- In PatchTST at Layer 1, the FFN delta exceeds attention's on all eight datasets; the ratio of means is 3.2. On 6 datasets the attention delta is negligible or the FFN wins by at least 2; on ETTm1 and Weather the FFN leads by 1.9 and 1.3. ETTh1 is the weak-construction dataset (attention 0.4, FFN 1.8).
- Removing attention does not remove the construction. The probing total is 26.7 with attention; the ablated checkpoint gives 27.4 and the from-scratch model 28.8, with the FFN absorbing attention's former share. Summed over the three layers, the FFN delta is 21.0 at patch length 16 against 1.2 at patch length 1.
- Reliance and construction come apart in the forecasting numbers. The same construction survives, but the ablated checkpoint loses 14.4% MSE on the 8-dataset mean while the from-scratch model is within 2.0% of PatchTST (at most 5.4% on any dataset). On ETTm2, attention has a probing delta of 0.2 yet ablating it costs 11.4% — the authors read this as a train/inference shift rather than a hidden function.
- Activation patching restores more through the FFN than through attention on 7 of 8 datasets, but the authors deflate the instrument themselves: a restored FFN output is computed from the clean attention output, so this comparison favors the FFN by construction and only attention's recovery is informative. That recovery is at most 0.022 on the four datasets where probing puts attention near zero, and 0.036 to 0.229 on the other four.
- With single-scalar tokens the allocation flips: deltas fall from attention 6.4 / FFN 20.4 to attention 10.9 / FFN 2.3, an 89% collapse in the FFN delta, and attention leads on 6 of 8 datasets. The total halves, from 26.7 to 13.2 pp. Yet knockout still costs more through the FFN, so the representational shift is not visible on the reliance side; the authors say neither instrument can confirm or refute it.
- One more point per token restores construction: at patch length 2 the FFN delta is 13.0 to 31.4 against −0.9 to 3.4 for the same statistics read at patch length 1, and from patch length 2 up it exceeds the attention delta in 18 of the 20 dataset-patch-length pairs; non-overlapping patches reproduce the values within about 2 pp. Strength varies by dataset rather than growing with patch length (on ETTh1 the FFN delta is 3.8 at patch length 16 and 0.5 at 32).
- Across architectures the probing pattern is: FFN under patch tokens with self-attention, attention under variate tokens (iTransformer, ratio of 8-dataset means 13.8 vs 5.5), the cross-attention path in CATS (where the queries start with no per-instance input, so dominance is true by construction and measured as absolute accuracy, 44.2, rather than as a delta), and attention under single-scalar tokens. Knockout reproduces the ordering of architectures and a ratio of mean attention cost to mean FFN cost of 0.45, 1.32 and 21.9 for PatchTST, iTransformer and CATS.
- The MLP-probe contrast is the sharpest sanity result: on the constructed tasks a two-layer MLP probe reaches 91–99 at the embedding layer (91.0–99.1 across the four datasets tested), i.e. the information is already there in some nonlinear form, and the linear probe's FFN gain is the re-encoding into a linearly readable form. The authors therefore describe their delta as a statement about the format the head reads rather than about where information exists.
- Appendix honesty: erasing individual probed properties by iterative nullspace projection removed 33–94% of the 128 dimensions and was inconclusive against a matched random-label control, so whether the forecaster uses any individual property is left open. A learned dominant-period task turned out degenerate (embedding accuracy tracks the majority-class rate on every dataset).

## Limits and open problems they name
No formal theory — they establish the phenomenon and its boundaries and explicitly have only an argument for patch tokens, nothing for variate tokens. The main conclusions rest on three second-order statistics of a 16-point segment, so other properties might allocate differently. PatchTST versus iTransformer is not a controlled comparison (input length 336 vs 96, channel independence vs dependence), and tokenization cannot be separated from what attention mixes because changing from patch to variate tokens also changes attention's axis. Lookback, depth and the form of the mixer are not varied. Feature-level use, foundation models, and a fixed 16-point task suite swept across patch length are named as future work. Deeper-layer patching suffers cross-context incompatibility; patching is biased toward the FFN; knockout measures reliance only.

## What we take from it
A method and a framing. (1) **Innate / constructed / boundary task taxonomy** — before attributing a delta to a sub-layer, show that the property was *not* already readable upstream, or the attribution is measuring the input encoding. This is a pre-registered instrument property: we should classify every property we probe our white-box model on as linearly present at the input, linearly absent, or ambiguous, and report the classification with the numbers. (2) **The non-linearity-boundary as the discriminator**: a component "constructs" when it converts something a linear map provably cannot read into something a linear head can. That gives us a clean criterion for which components deserve credit, independent of effect size. (3) **The MLP-probe contrast as a warning about our own probes**: a more expressive probe will always find the information earlier, so a claim about *where* information appears is a claim about probe family, not about the model. Print both probes or state the family. (4) **Construction versus reliance** is a distinction our prediction-under-intervention tables have to keep separate: this paper's model loses 14% MSE when a component is removed while its representational signature does not move at all, and the other way around at patch length 1. (5) Warning about imported instruments: activation patching here had a baked-in bias toward the FFN that the authors only neutralize by reading one direction of the result.
