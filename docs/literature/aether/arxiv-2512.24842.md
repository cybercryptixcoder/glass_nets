# Triangulation as an Acceptance Rule for Multilingual Mechanistic Interpretability

- **Key:** arxiv-2512.24842
- **Authors / organisation:** Yanan Long (StickFlux Labs; UChicago e-mail address given in the byline block)
- **Date:** 2025-12-31
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24842
- **Kind:** paper (workshop paper, NeurIPS 2025 workshop *Evaluating the Evolving LLM Lifecycle: Benchmarks, Emergent Abilities, and Scaling*)
- **Read from:** full text (v1 HTML; abstract, §1-§7, Appendices A-D read in full, reference list scanned for provenance)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Multilingual models look good on average while behaving unpredictably per language, script and culture, and the author's complaint is that almost all mechanistic claims are built in one environment and never re-checked. A circuit found by patching English prompts may be a claim about English tokenisation rather than about the computation, and nothing in the standard single-environment battery would notice. The paper's position is that a mechanistic explanation should be accepted only if it survives interventions *and* keeps working across environments that preserve meaning while perturbing surface form: same predicate, different language. Multilinguality is used deliberately as a source of controlled variation, in the spirit of invariant causal prediction. The larger goal is an acceptance rule rather than another discovery method: an explicit, falsifiable, pre-registered bar that a proposed circuit must clear.

## What they studied

Two things are the object of the paper: the *rule* (a score and a decision procedure), and a *protocol* for applying it. The unit of analysis is a **circuit** — a nominated set of internal variables and edges — but the object actually tested is upgraded to a **mechanism class**, an environment-indexed family of circuits, one per reference-family member, optionally with learned translation maps between them. The intended models are Gemma 3 (1B and 4B), Llama 3.2 (1B and 3B), Ministral 3B and MADLAD-400-3B-MT, chosen for activation-patching compatibility and multilingual capability; compatibility is claimed via Penzai conversion tooling. The data are three gender-focused translation/rewrite resources — FairTranslate (English→French), GLITTER (English→German) and mGeNTE (English→Italian, Spanish, German, Greek) — used both as sources of predicate instances and as sources of predicate swaps.

## How they did it

The experiments are specified, not run: this is a protocol paper with a worked example in the appendix, and it reports no results. The construction is a structural causal model over an exogenous predicate variable `Z` and an exogenous environment index `E`; `E` induces nuisance surface attributes, which shape the realised input together with `Z`, and the input propagates through internal variables to an output and a scalar task score. A reference family is a set of inputs sharing the predicate value; changing language, script or register moves `E` and leaves `Z` fixed, and the paper states this as an explicit assumption with a warning that a predicate which omits a relevant semantic constraint (referent gender, for the gendered-translation setting) silently breaks the family. On top of that they assemble four intervention families into one mixed distribution: **necessity** (ablate the candidate circuit and require the task score to drop past a threshold), **sufficiency** (patch the circuit from a source whose predicate value differs, and require the score to move in the direction the abstract model predicts, with a cap on how far the patched activations may stray from the base run's), **stability** (patch between predicate-*matched* sources across environments and require almost no change), and **cue-only falsifiers** (patch nominated language-identity or script circuits while leaving the predicate circuit alone and require almost no change). Cross-language patching is handled by a learned translation map that sends source activations into the base environment's coordinate system before substitution, which is how the paper imports the distributed form of interchange interventions. The decision is a triple of requirements: an overall acceptance rate above a floor, a per-environment floor taken as a minimum over environments rather than a mean, and a calibration step in which the floor is set so that placebo circuits (random, or circuits trained to predict nuisance cues) are accepted at a pre-declared low rate. Uncertainty comes from a Beta-Binomial posterior over the Bernoulli acceptance indicators, reported as a posterior mean and an equal-tailed credible interval. Discovery is separated from acceptance: automatic circuit discovery (edge attribution patching with integrated gradients, or position-aware discovery) proposes an environment-specific circuit, translation maps are fit on held-out predicate-matched pairs, and only then is the mechanism class scored.

## What counts as an explanation here

A circuit, accepted as a mechanism, with the acceptance carried by an abstract model whose key variable is the predicate and whose behaviour is summarised as a single score. The explanation is explicitly *partial* and explicitly *conditional*: it is relative to the chosen intervention distribution, the similarity function, the thresholds and the reference families. There is a graded, not exact, notion of abstraction underneath — the score is an instance of an approximate transformation score over a distribution of interventions, and the paper states informally that passing with strong thresholds is evidence of an approximate causal abstraction. It predicts: for a held-out environment in the family, the abstract model says the score should be unchanged, and a swap of the predicate should change it in a stated direction.

## How they judged success

By acceptance rates rather than accuracy. The headline comparison is a trade-off in acceptance behaviour against four baselines — single-environment patching, causal mediation analysis, causal scrubbing, and ACDC-style ablation-based faithfulness testing — under the same discovered circuits, so the criterion is the only thing that varies. The stated question is whether the rule rejects circuits that fail cross-lingual invariance while retaining those that pass, and the paper is careful that neither direction alone is a win: accepting fewer circuits may simply be conservatism. Supporting measures are the placebo-acceptance calibration, an on-manifold bound on translated patching, agreement metrics across predicate checkers (rule-based, LLM judge, and human annotation with an inter-annotator target stated), and stress tests that inject deliberate predicate violations to confirm the rule rejects when the family is broken.

## What they found

No empirical results are reported; the paper claims none. Its substantive conclusions are about the machinery. First, that the multilingual setting supplies something no single-language battery does: an environment axis along which a genuine predicate mechanism must be invariant while a surface-cue mechanism must not be, and the acceptance rule is built so that the two directions are distinguishable. Second, that the object of a mechanistic claim is a mechanism class rather than one subgraph, because the same abstract computation can be a mixture of shared and language-specific parts; the rule therefore allows per-language adapter subcircuits as long as predicate-swap effects still transfer with the correct direction after translation and cue-only falsifiers still fail to transfer. Third, that the rule extends to vision-language models with the image held fixed and the prompt language as the environment axis, where it yields a three-way separation of circuits into a visual mechanism class (passes across prompt languages), a linguistic/cue class (succeeds in one environment and fails stability or cue checks), and a spurious class (fails ablation or swaps). The same argument is made for cross-lingual question answering, morphosyntactic agreement and entity preservation.

## Limits and open problems they name

Named in §6, and the list is honest: several circuits can pass simultaneously because effects may be redundant, so acceptance identifies a sufficient mechanism without uniqueness — which is the Rashomon problem unaddressed. Reference-family quality is decisive and can mislead in either direction, accepting spurious circuits when predicate violations slip through the checkers and rejecting genuine ones when family members differ in ways that change the mechanism itself. Interventions at scale are expensive and the paper recommends prioritisation. LLM judges used to verify predicate preservation create a circularity risk when the judge is the same family as the model under study, which is why rule-based and human checks are preferred where feasible. Appendix C also flags that the Binomial uncertainty model understates uncertainty when interventions share an anchor instance, and prescribes a hierarchical model in that case. What the paper does not have is any of its own numbers: every threshold is a symbol, and how the rule behaves against the four baselines is left to the future experiments it specifies.

## What we take from it

A framing, a protocol and a warning. The framing worth adopting is the decomposition of an acceptance decision into *necessity*, *sufficiency*, *stability* and *cue-only falsification*, with a separate threshold for each and an acceptance floor taken as a minimum over environments rather than an average. The protocol worth adopting is the placebo calibration: choose the acceptance floor so that circuits known to be junk (random, or nuisance-predicting) clear it at a pre-declared rate, which turns a threshold from a number someone picked into a measured false-positive budget. The warning is the one that matters most for us and is stated as an assumption rather than a finding: any environment axis is only usable if the predicate truly is preserved along it, and the paper's own answer is to audit the axis with multiple independent checkers and to stress-test it by injecting deliberate violations. That is the cheapest available audit for our own partitions — if a split of inputs is meant to hold the target fixed, break the target on purpose and confirm the battery notices. The paper's translation-map idea is also the first treatment in this folder of what cross-environment patching costs: a learned map, a hold-out of those pairs from acceptance, and a bound on the distortion the map introduces.
