# Representational Control over Self-Report & Behavior Coherence in LLM Risk-Taking

## What they wanted, and why
Practitioners use a model's self-report as a cheap stand-in for what it will do, and also use a stated disposition as a control knob. Both uses assume one construct sits behind the words and the actions. Earlier work showed the two channels often disagree, but all of it compared a questionnaire answer with a task answer from outside the model, so the disagreement could equally have been an artefact of how the two prompts were built. The authors want to settle that by holding the intervention fixed: inject one direction into the residual stream and see whether both channels move. If dissociating self-report and behaviour is a fact about representation, it should survive a shared internal push. Read as a step toward their goal, it is a test of whether a single latent construct exists at all, and therefore of whether reading one channel licenses a prediction about the other.

## What they studied
Risk-taking, chosen because agents are being put into decision roles. Four open-weight chat models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, OLMo-3-7B, Ministral-3-8B). Behaviour measured by two sequential risk paradigms — the Columbia Card Task (card flips under loss risk) and the Balloon Analogue Risk Task (pumps) — and self-report by two psychometric instruments: the Big Five Inventory scored on a risk-taking profile, and the domain-specific risk scale (DOSPERT), which asks about risk directly. Outcomes are read as likelihoods over answer tokens in a single forward pass rather than sampled text. The unit of analysis is a steering direction (one per extraction method, per model, per layer in an injected mid-network band).

## How they did it
Nine extraction methods are grouped by what supervises them: task instructions (an imperative to take or avoid risk, and a stated intention to do so); the model's own task choices (boards split by the model's own expected card count, plus a logistic-probe second instantiation); task-agnostic risk dispositions (risk clauses, thrill-seeking clauses, model-generated persona text); and Big-Five profiles that never mention risk (one authored profile, one recomposed from five single-trait vectors). Extraction is otherwise held fixed: difference of class means at the final prompt token, unit-normalised per layer. Each vector is injected at plus and minus a dose scaled to the spread of activations along it, into the same layer band for both channels, and the effect (the "swing") is the difference between the two readouts. Effects are expressed in standard deviations of swings produced by eight seeded random Gaussian directions at matched layer and dose, and an effect "clears the control" at more than 3.5 such control SDs in the intended direction. Against that baseline, the paper runs a 20-condition perturbation grid (persona, injection band, construction variant), a dose sweep from 0.5 to 8 with dose-matched controls, then a diagnosis stage: pairwise cosine between directions within each layer (mean of cosines, not cosine of means, because a direction rotates across depth), a permutation test on whether labels of which channel a direction moves explain the similarity matrix, and two removal operations — projecting a subspace out of the injected vector before injection, and subtracting the same subspace from the hidden state at every layer and token (route removal) — each compared against removal of a random subspace of matching rank. Twelve lexically controlled directions built to isolate surface properties (quantity of cards or pumps, bare numerals, numeral tokens at the decision site, a statement that a large quantity occurred, task vocabulary with the count fixed, rating format, endorsement of scored items) serve as the confound battery; they are injected on their own first, to check that they move the instrument, then projected out of the steering vectors. Finally the two channels are steered together: a normalised convex blend of a behaviour-moving and a self-report-moving direction, weight swept from 0.3 to 0.7, with the self-report component's sign flipped, and the landing state recorded as coherent (same-direction movement of both channels) or anti-coherent (opposite signs).

## What counts as an explanation here
A causal, direction-level account: an internal vector is an explanation of a behaviour if it moves that behaviour and not another channel's, and the account is graded by what the vector is made of — whether its effect survives projection against a named, measured confound set, and whether the effect depends on a subspace being present in the forward pass. The form is deliberately partial and local. Nothing predicts generation or a trajectory; what is predicted is the direction, and sign, of a change in one read-out under a stated intervention, plus which other read-out will jointly move. The paper's own framing of a successful explanation is a coupling claim: coherence between what a model says and does is representational only if something inside the model makes them agree causally, and not merely if the two outputs happen to agree.

## How they judged success
Swing in control SDs against a control threshold rather than against zero; rate of cells clearing that band, counted per family across method × model × task cells; retention (fraction of the original swing surviving a removal) bootstrapped by resampling perturbation conditions, pooled across direction-model cells by DerSimonian–Laird random effects, with a same-sign survival check against the band in the readout's own units; a rank-matched random removal as the floor for every removal; a permutation test with WEAT-style effect size (reported with its floor); incremental R² for depth versus model versus family in a regression on Fisher-transformed cosines; logistic regression with cluster-robust standard errors and exact McNemar tests with Holm correction for the joint-control stage; and, for the joint stage, a gate that keeps a composition only if its pure self-report endpoint actually pushes that instrument, replicated by an inverse-variance reweighting that drops nothing. Capability-style controls are also run: valid-answer mass and whether a readout is pinned at a scale bound.

## What they found
The dissociation is representational and tracks the supervision source. Dispositional directions clear the control in none of 40 behavioural cells (family medians 0.7 and 0.4 control SDs) while clearing it in 31 of 40 self-report cells (medians 6.6 and 8.9). Own-choice directions do the reverse: all 16 behavioural cells clear, median swing 42.1, about seven times the task directives, while self-report effects sit at 1.8, inside the band; and the behavioural effect is task-bound, retaining only 7 per cent on the other task. Task directives reach both channels — 11 of 16 behavioural cells and 24 of 32 self-report cells — but the reach is mostly wording: projecting out the twelve shortcut directions leaves 32 per cent [18, 47] of the behavioural effect against 74 per cent [69, 80] for own choices, the loss being almost entirely quantity cues, and the purified behavioural effect never clears the control at any dose from 0.5 to 8. Self-report directions retain 64–66 per cent under the same removal. Geometry agrees with the causal test: directions are more aligned within channel than across (mean absolute-cosine gap 0.41 [0.35, 0.53]), family pair explains 0.600 of incremental R² against 0.045 for relative depth and 0.006 for model, and the between-channel rows of a per-block regression on depth are flat (+0.004 and −0.008), so near-orthogonality holds at every injected depth. Removing the other channel's families preserves 97 and 92 per cent of the behavioural effects and 102 per cent of the directive self-report effect, while route removal costs much more (behaviour down to 61 and 40 per cent, self-report to 38 and 32) — shared route, not shared content. The cleanest confound finding is that the controls are not inert: the quantity instructions, which never mention risk, steer harder on behaviour than the risk directives they control for (Cards 2.9 against Task-Command 1.9 on the card task; Pumps 1.5 against 0.55 on the balloon task), while the controls built to hold the property fixed sit at the band. Removing the shortcut subspace from the model itself abolishes self-report steering for every family (median retention −0.11) where twelve random directions leave it intact (0.99). Joint control works in both directions: aligned blends induce coherence in 91 per cent of cases against an unsteered floor of 39.6 per cent anti-coherent (chance); flipping the self-report sign raises anti-coherence from 8.5 to 77.4 per cent, and anti-aligned blends reach anti-coherence in 69–89 per cent of cases in every model. Resistance to that flip is model-dependent and small (14 percentage points overall, absent in Qwen, 29 in Ministral) and is predicted by how much the behaviour vector alone spills into self-report (−0.28 [−0.35, −0.21] on the anti-coherence logit per control SD of spillover). Under-claiming and over-claiming are equally reachable, differing by under five points on every model.

## Limits and open problems they name
One construct only (risk-taking), defence being that agents actually face risky decisions. Mid-size open-weight models only, because steering needs activations. Behaviour read from single decisions, not multi-step agentic trajectories. The surface controls are admittedly incomplete, and the authors note the direction of the resulting bias — a missed shortcut would inflate retained effect, so the dissociation is if anything understated. In the perturbation appendix they add that the control-SD normaliser is not justified on DOSPERT (method spread and control spread correlate only weakly there, between-model spread widens from 4.2× to 5.7×), so results that depend on pooling across models on that instrument are the weakest in the set; they keep the unit for comparability and flag the cost. Two other honest admissions: the permutation test's p-values are at their floor and carry no evidential weight, and the pooled retention estimate is sensitive to the estimator (an unweighted bootstrap flips the sign for one family because of ratios with near-zero denominators). The route-removal self-report analysis collapses when shortcut directions are ablated from hidden states, and they say this may reflect disrupted answer production rather than shared risk content.

## What we take from it
Four transfers. (1) A measure: the control-SD swing — express every effect in standard deviations of a matched random-direction control and define clearing as a distance from that control's mean rather than from zero — plus the diagnostic that this normaliser needs its own justification per read-out, since a denominator that does not track the numerator destabilises rather than stabilises (their DOSPERT check, correlation of log spreads +0.20). (2) A warning that applies directly to our own read-out claims: a direction can be a strong, reproducible probe of a concept and still be unrelated to the channel we care about — dispositional vectors read self-report cleanly and never move behaviour, and the reverse holds for behaviour-extracted vectors — so a direction's read-out validity is not evidence of a shared mechanism, and output agreement alone is weak evidence of one. (3) A method worth copying wholesale: build the confound as its own direction, inject it to confirm it moves the instrument, then project it out and print retention; and pair every removal with a rank-matched random removal, since a small norm loss can carry a large effect (5 per cent of norm costing 59 per cent of the effect) and a small geometric overlap can carry one too. Their separation of direction removal (what a vector carries) from route removal (what the computation needs) is the sharpest formulation in the folder so far of the representational-vs-causal question. (4) A framing for prediction under intervention: their joint-control stage is a small, well-controlled instance of what we want — take two directions, compose them at stated weights, and predict which quadrant of the joint outcome plane the model lands in, with the prediction checked against an unsteered floor rather than assumed. Their result that the two diagonal corners are equally reachable is the case our model must be able to forecast, not just describe.
