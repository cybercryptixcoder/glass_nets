# The Knowing-Saying Gap: When Probes See Errors that Confidence Misses

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.07528
- **Authors / organisation:** Jyotin Goel (equal contribution), Ipshita Bandyopadhyay, Justin Shenk; affiliation line not printed in the readable text (correspondence address is a personal gmail account)
- **Date:** 2026-07-21
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07528
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Agentic and multi-step deployments fail in a particular way: one wrong intermediate value enters the context and every later hop computes on top of it, while the model keeps the same fluent register and never signals that anything went wrong. The authors take the natural remedy — ask the model how sure it is, or read its internal state directly — and ask a prior question that calibration work usually skips: even if a probe can *see* the corruption, does that tell you whether the answer will actually come out wrong? The motivating worry is operational: a monitor whose score does not separate the traces that fail from the ones that recover is not actionable, however high its detection score. The paper is therefore an audit of the jump from "the state is decodable" to "the state predicts behaviour", made in a controlled setting where the ground truth about both the injection and the final answer is exact.

## What they studied
Five model variants in two architecture families: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (36 layers, width 2048), Qwen3-4B in instruct and thinking mode (36 layers, 2560), Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (28 layers, 3072) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers, 4096), all bf16 with frozen weights and no fine-tuning anywhere; the probe is the only learned component. Data is a deterministically generated contrastive multi-hop arithmetic set of 1,400 traces over 500 base problems and 2 to 4 hops (500 clean, 500 with the error at hop 1, 250 at hop 2, 150 at hop 3), built from 12 template functions with a single seed; errors are five algebraically injected types (off-by-one, wrong operator, wrong unit, factor-ten magnitude, wrong percentage base), each appearing in roughly 180 corrupted traces, and all later hops are re-templated from the wrong upstream value so propagation is realistic. The unit of analysis is the residual stream at every layer, pooled two ways (last token, mean over the prompt), with the probe as the readout and the final answer as the behavioural target.

## How they did it
Each base problem contributes three matched conditions — clean, error-standard, and error-verbalised (the same corruption with a confidence score elicited) — so the clean and corrupted contexts differ only in the injected hop value, which is the paper's way of stopping the probe from learning difficulty instead of error. Activations are captured for the whole stack at every layer under both poolings; six surface uncertainty signals (peak/mean/early entropy, minimum top-token probability, mean top-two margin, sequence log-probability, the last two sign-flipped so that larger means more error-like) are computed in parallel as the comparison arm, and four text flags count hedging, overconfidence, structured confidence and absurdity. A standardised logistic regression is trained independently at every layer and pooling, layer selection is by 5-fold stratified cross-validation AUROC, and a final probe is refit at the best cell. Two robustness controls follow: grouped cross-validation that holds out entire base problems (all variants of a problem in one fold), and leave-one-error-type-out training over the five types. The decisive move is to score the *same* probe against two different targets — detection (error injected, over all traces) and failure (wrong under injection but correct on the matched clean run, over the error subset only), so the second target isolates propagation from baseline difficulty. Thresholds are calibrated out-of-fold in three modes (Youden, the 99th percentile of clean scores, and 0.7x Youden). Three probe-triggered interventions are then compared with a cap of three firings per trace: reprompt (a recheck instruction, no extra forward passes), replace-prior (regenerate the previous hop from the clean prefix and continue), and branch-and-pick (sample four continuations at temperatures 0.7 to 1.15 and keep the one whose internal state the probe scores least error-like), plus a 4-by-3 factorial over branch count and threshold. A pre-registered persistence hypothesis is also tested: whether the fraction of hops above threshold separates degraded from recovered traces.

## What counts as an explanation here
A linear direction in the residual stream that is decodable, plus a dissociation between two measurements made with the same direction. The paper is explicit that this is decodability and not causation: the probes are read-outs whose usefulness as a monitor is tested separately by whether intervening on their trigger changes outcomes, and the authors say outright that establishing causal relevance would need patch- or mediation-level work they do not run. Nothing here predicts an answer from the state; what is claimed is a measured gap between detecting a corruption and predicting its consequence, plus an intervention table.

## How they judged success
Detection AUROC and failure AUROC (chance 0.5, values below 0.5 read as inverted), reported per model at the best layer under both poolings; six surface signals as the head-to-head; a verbalised-confidence collapse rate (share of elicited scores at or below 0.05 or at or above 0.95) with the wrong rates of the two collapsed groups compared; rescued and broken counts for each intervention, reported separately rather than only as their net, with per-error-type slices; grouped cross-validation and leave-one-error-type-out as leakage and surface-signature controls; a pre-registered hypothesis reported as refuted; and a thinking-mode ablation on shared weights as a single-variable test of whether chain-of-thought closes the gap.

## What they found
Detection is near-ceiling everywhere: best-layer AUROC between 0.985 and 0.994 under last-token pooling and up to 0.997 under mean pooling on Llama-3.1-8B, a probe-to-surface gap of at least 0.30, and surface detection signals that never exceed 0.70 (peak entropy lands at exactly 0.500 on the instruct models, and the thinking model reverses to 0.308). The same probe's failure AUROC sits at 0.47 to 0.53 across all five models. Layer 0 under last-token pooling is exactly 0.500 everywhere and a single first-block jump of about 0.38 to 0.41 carries most of the signal, with the peak moving deeper with model size (layer 11 of 28 for Llama-3.2-3B against 23 of 36 for Qwen3-4B instruct). Grouped cross-validation moves the best-layer estimate by at most 0.003, and held-out error-type transfer stays above 0.91 in every model-by-type cell with means of 0.968 to 0.996 — but the paper's own pooling ablation undercuts the cleanest part of that story: mean pooling gives roughly 0.91 already at layer 0, because averaging smears the corruption across positions, which is precisely why the authors adopt last-token pooling for the narrative. Verbally, the model is silent: all four instruct models produce no hedging and no overconfident language across 258 traces each and never volunteer a structured confidence format, thinking models hedge 1.9% of the time, and the matched clean accuracy is only 23 to 31%, falling 4 to 8 points under injection. Thinking mode barely moves probe AUROC (within 0.004) while error-condition accuracy goes from 27.9% to 1.2% and mean generated tokens from 18.4 to 89.5 on shared weights — the paper's strongest evidence that encoding persists while the saying channel degrades, even allowing for the clean accuracy also dropping from 29.1% to 12.8%. Interventions: branch-and-pick is net-positive on every model with complete data and uniquely non-breaking on Llama-3.1-8B (4 rescued, 0 broken), while replace-prior rescues and breaks at comparable rates; reprompt does nothing at all in every cell of the per-type table. The probe fires on 96 to 100% of error traces but replace-prior's firing drops to 32 to 39% with no proportional accuracy gain. Per-type effects are heterogeneous with 9 to 16 examples per slice: replace-prior is +37.5 points on wrong_unit (7 rescued, 1 broken) and -18.2 on wrong percentage base (2 rescued, 4 broken), and branch-and-pick is +15.4 on off-by-one with nothing broken.

## Limits and open problems they name
Decodability is not causation, and no representation-level intervention is run. The per-type routing the results point to presupposes an error-type classifier the authors do not have, on slices of 9 to 16 examples, so they present routing as a direction rather than a deployable policy. The first-block jump is unexplained (attention head versus MLP feature). Per-hop localisation — moving the target from "a prior is corrupted" to "hop k is corrupted" — is future work. Branch-and-pick costs roughly tenfold inference at the observed firing rates, and the ethics section warns that replace-prior breaks correct traces at nearly the rescue rate, so probe-triggered intervention should not be sold as strictly safe.

## What we take from it
A measurement recipe and a warning. The recipe is the **two-target scoring of one readout**: score the same probe against "the thing I claim to detect" and against "the outcome I care about", on matched pairs that differ only in the injected quantity, and report both numbers side by side — here 0.985 to 0.994 against 0.47 to 0.53, which turns a fold full of impressive AUROCs into a single legible dissociation. The warning is about the neighbouring control: the paper's own layer-0 plateau (exactly 0.500 under last-token pooling, roughly 0.91 under mean pooling) shows the pooling choice alone can manufacture a deep-layer story or a shallow-input one, so any layer-resolved claim in our white-box model should carry both poolings and the earliest-saturation layer, as run 922's rule already asks. Two smaller pieces are directly reusable: **rescued and broken counts reported separately** rather than only their net, because equal net hides opposite risk profiles; and the **replace-prior firing collapse** (96 to 100% down to 32 to 39% with no accuracy gain) as a diagnostic that an intervention changed the state without changing the decision — the cleanest available evidence that the probed direction is not the proximal cause.
