## What they wanted, and why
Chain-of-thought traces are known to be unreliable both as an account of what the model did and as a monitor for what it is about to do, so the internal computation behind a reasoning trace has to be studied directly. The obstruction they name first is not theoretical but mechanical: sample the same prompt twice and the responses differ in wording and in reasoning structure, so there is no common axis on which to compare activations, to say what an intervention changed, or to accumulate patterns across examples. Their proposal is to make the response itself the fixed reference frame. Given one fixed generated answer, ask how the next-token distributions along it shift when the prompt is intervened on, and then train an interface that reproduces that shift from the inside. Everything else in the paper — the attribution, the ablations, the case study — is downstream of that one move, and the stated goal is a cheaper instrument for understanding reasoning models whose words cannot be taken at face value.

## What they studied
Two open-weight reasoning-capable models: Gemma-4-E4B-it and DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B. The data is the Scruples corpus of real ethical anecdotes, each with a community-judged answer to "is the author in the wrong", and the single intervention is a one-sentence prefix stating that the user believes the author *is* in the wrong, added before the question with the answer letters randomised. The unit of analysis is unusual and is the point of the paper: not a neuron, feature or direction, but a **per-token additive edit** at a fixed residual-stream layer (after layer 20 of Gemma, after layer 17 of DeepSeek, both zero-indexed), produced by a separately trained module, together with an attribution score and an ablation effect for each such edit.

## How they did it
The counterfactual is defined on distributions, not on text. For each source prompt they generate one response under the control prompt and one under the intervention prompt; then for each of those fixed responses they run a forward pass under *both* prompts, so at every response position they hold a pair of next-token distributions that differ only because the prompt changed. Because the response prefix is shared, the difference at position *t* is attributable to the intervention alone, which is the property the rest of the paper exploits — interventions involving insertions, deletions or paraphrasing need no tokenwise alignment.

The support is trimmed: at each position they take the top-K tokens of each distribution plus the token actually generated, set K to the sampling configuration where there is one (64 for Gemma) and renormalise both distributions over that shared set. The loss is the mean per-position KL between the renormalised target and the renormalised edited source distribution, averaged over all records.

The edit module is a gated MLP applied to the normalised residual stream at every position of prompt and response, with a shared one-layer encoder and two zero-initialised output projections — one per counterfactual direction, control→intervention and intervention→control — so the same module can add or remove the suggestion. They train one response per prompt arm on the Scruples splits (27,766 train / 2,500 val / 2,500 test, doubled by the two arms), Adam, one epoch, effective batch 128, learning rates swept over 2e-5 / 5e-5 / 1e-4, checkpoint chosen by validation recovery. Their headline metric is **recovery**, one minus the ratio of the trained KL to the unedited KL — R = 1 − L/D — so 0 means the edit changed nothing and 1 means the source distributions were turned into the target ones.

Each edit event then gets a signed attribution by Integrated Gradients along the path from no edit to the full edit, 16 steps, giving one scalar per token; the causal check is to zero a single selected edit and rerun the downstream layers, measuring the change in counterfactual KL. Within each record they take the top five edit events by attribution magnitude plus five random ones and compute a Spearman correlation between attribution and ablated effect over those ten. The final step leaves the surrogate entirely: they take the token classes the attribution maps flagged and perform **native activation patching** in the original model — replacing the control activation with the intervention activation at the flagged positions, and running the normal forward pass.

## What counts as an explanation here
An interface plus a hypothesis generator, in three layers. The edit module is a replacement-computation device, but only locally: a map from the source residual state to the target one, parameterised, and scored by whether the downstream behaviour follows — it is not an explanation of the model, it is a hypothesis about how much of the intervention is expressible as a function of one layer's state. The attribution map is a localisation claim: which tokens' edits carry the recovery. The ablations and the activation patching are what convert that into causal claims. The authors say plainly what the artefact is for: the maps and edit-effect visualisations are there to *generate* hypotheses about the original network's computation, which are then tested by patching. It is a partial and deliberately operational object, and the one substantive claim in the paper is about locations, not mechanisms.

## How they judged success
Four separate measures, and the paper is careful to keep them apart. (1) **Behavioural vs activation reconstruction**, the paper's own central control: an identical module trained on normalised MSE to reconstruct the counterfactual residual stream at the same layer, scored on activation recovery (1 − NMSE/NMSE₀) *and* on KL recovery. (2) **Recovery** after the trained edit, the headline distributional number; both are reported side by side. (3) **Attribution faithfulness**: median per-sequence Spearman correlation between integrated-gradient score and ablated causal effect over the ten selected events, with the **edit-norm correlation as the nuisance control** — if attribution is just picking the biggest edits, the two correlations coincide. A second attribution method, relevance patching, is run through the identical evaluation. (4) **Native patching**: KL recovery from patching the flagged token sets, against a control that patches the *same number* of randomly chosen tokens, with 95% bootstrap intervals from 1,000 resamples of the source prompts. An architecture ablation (fixed input-independent steering vector, full-rank linear editor, nonlinear MLP editor) tests whether the counterfactual is a single direction at all.

## What they found
**Distribution matching beats geometric reconstruction, which is the paper's methodological claim.** An MSE-trained module reconstructs the counterfactual residual stream better at the edit layer but recovers less of the output distribution — for DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B KL recovery falls from 47.8% to 20.4% under the reconstruction objective, and on Gemma-4-E4B-it the reconstruction objective can produce *negative* recovery. Matching the activations of the counterfactual computation does not transfer the behaviour.

**The attribution is faithful and it is not a size effect.** Median per-sequence Spearman between integrated-gradients score and ablated effect is 0.58 on Gemma and 0.68 on DeepSeek, while the edit-norm control reaches only 0.36 and 0.53. The alternative attribution, relevance patching, collapses to 0.18 and 0.16 on the same evaluation, so the choice of attribution method matters more than the choice of model.

**The intervention's influence is carried by the question statement and by filler tokens.** Over the top 5% of absolute attribution scores, the most frequent tokens are whitespace, delimiters and words from the question rather than any word in the reasoning: on Gemma the leading candidate is a four-space indentation token, 29.5% of whose occurrences land in the top 5% of attribution, with `\n\n` at 23.1% and `**` at 23.3%; on DeepSeek the tokens are `wrong` (34.1%), `user` (28.0%) and a bare `A` (26.4%). Two different model families put the same interface on two different token vocabularies of filler.

**Native patching confirms it, on both models, in a different signature.** Patching only the filler tokens recovers 11.4% for Gemma; patching only the question span recovers 11.3% for DeepSeek; patching both recovers 16.2% and 19.3% respectively, and both figures beat random same-size patching by factors of roughly 2.3 to 8.1. Which of the two sites dominates is model-dependent — filler wins on Gemma, the question wins on DeepSeek, another instance of a phenomenon travelling while its address does not — and patching both is always better than either alone, i.e. the influence is distributed.

**The intervention moves answers while barely surfacing in the trace.** Final-answer accuracy falls 76.2% → 72.6% on Gemma and 72.3% → 62.5% on DeepSeek, but a keyword-assisted manual scan of 500 intervention-arm responses per model finds explicit acknowledgement of the suggestion in only 4 of 500 for Gemma and 113 of 500 for DeepSeek. The edits are real in the distribution and nearly absent from the text.

**Downstream, filler-token edits change framing rather than content at the token level and the final answer at the aggregate level** — the worked example turns "poor service" into "poor behaviour" and "dismissed" into "unfairly treated"; patched question and filler positions shift "choose" toward "determine" and "suspicious" toward "guilt" in the likelihood ranking.

## Limits and open problems they name
One setting and one intervention, on two models, both edited at the midpoint layer only. The acknowledgement count is a keyword scan with under-counting admitted, and the DeepSeek count is confounded by the model sometimes confusing the user with the author. Recovery from patching is modest in absolute terms — 16–19% of the KL gap with both sites patched — so the paper localises *a substantial part* of the influence, not all of it, and never accounts for the remainder. The model-level claim is bounded by the fact that only cross-entropy over a trimmed support is scored, and the authors check the trimming explicitly: 90% of response positions keep at least about 99.3% of the mass inside the shared support. No comparison is made against a text-only predictor of the counterfactual shift, so how much the internal analysis adds over reading the prompt is left open — and they cite the paper that asks exactly that, noting their method is best read as complementary to verbalised reasoning.

## What we take from it
A **measure and a framing**: hold one response fixed and treat the paired next-token distributions under control and target prompt as the ground truth an internal surrogate has to reproduce, with recovery = 1 − L/D as the score. This is the cleanest formalisation in the folder of "what should a white-box model of a behaviour change predict" — the target is a distribution along a shared path, not a label, and it needs no alignment between two differently-worded generations, which is the problem that defeats activation-level counterfactual work in free-form text.

A **control worth adopting wholesale**: train the same interface on activation reconstruction and score it on behaviour. Here geometric fidelity buys 27 points less behavioural recovery, and goes negative on one model. The folder holds many entries in which an SAE, transcoder or probe is justified by reconstruction quality; this is the cheapest experiment that shows reconstruction quality is not a proxy for behavioural transfer, and it needs no new data.

A **warning aimed directly at our own instruments**: attribution over a learned interface says where the interface *acted*, and the edit-norm control (0.36 / 0.53 against 0.58 / 0.68) is what makes the attribution a claim rather than a restatement of magnitude. Any token-level heat map we report owes that same control, plus the second attribution method — relevance patching scores 0.18 and 0.16 here, which is the price of not checking.

A **finding to carry into the monitoring line**: an intervention can shift the answer distribution and the framing of a write-up while leaving almost no trace in the text (4/500 acknowledgements on one model), and the mediating representations sit on whitespace and on the question itself, not on the words of the reasoning. For a probe or monitor that reads the reasoning tokens, this is a direct miss: the information is present, it is just not where the semantics are. It also closes a loop with our token-level probe work — a per-position probe over a reasoning trace implicitly assumes information lives on content tokens, and here the highest-attribution positions are exactly the ones such a probe would discard.

A **method for prediction-under-intervention**: the two-directional module (one shared encoder, two output projections, control→intervention and intervention→control) is a small and reusable architecture, and it shows that the effect of a prompt intervention is not a single fixed direction but an input-dependent nonlinear function of the residual state — their architecture ablation sets the fixed steering vector and the linear editor against the MLP editor on the same recovery axis.
