## What they wanted, and why

The authors start from a complaint about vocabulary rather than about data: the field uses one word, causal abstraction, for a family of relations that were each defined ad hoc for a particular experiment — exact transformation, constructive abstraction, interchange-based abstraction, distributed abstraction — and there has been no statement of what those relations have in common or what makes one of them the right one for a given question. Their ambition is a single formalism in which every named notion from the literature is a special case, defined by exactly the same kind of object, so that a mechanistic claim about a network can be stated once and then examined for the structural properties the literature has been reinventing. A second ambition is portability: if abstraction is defined for any compositional model rather than for causal models specifically, then models whose semantics are quantum circuits can enter the same framework, which is the route to "explainable quantum AI" they take in the last third of the paper. The larger goal, stated plainly in the introduction, is that abstraction should describe *which* mechanism is being claimed and *at what granularity*, not just which input-output pairs happen to agree.

## What they studied

The unit of analysis is not a network but a **model-plus-queries pair**: a compositional model assigns semantics in a category (finite sets and stochastic channels for causal models, quantum channels for circuits) to a signature of variables and components, and a second signature of queries gives the experiments the model is meant to be used for — input-output behaviour, Do-interventions on any subset, interchange queries, counterfactual queries, or quantum-style "opening" queries that read a subsystem's state. Causal models are recovered as the special case where components are mechanisms over a DAG. No language model is trained or probed anywhere in the paper; the illustrative low-level model is a causal model, and in the quantum section a parameterised quantum circuit that answers a geoguessing question from an image. The granularity ladder they introduce — query-level, then component-level, then strict component-level where the abstraction map is the identity — is the paper's main structural contribution.

## How they did it

Take a low-level model and a high-level model, each with its own signature and query set. Define a **type alignment** first: for each high-level type, a list of low-level types plus a surjective (in category terms, epic) deterministic map sending low-level values to high-level values. Then the single consistency equation of the framework says that running the high-level query on mapped inputs equals running the corresponding low-level query and mapping the outputs — the diagram commutes up to the alignment maps. Everything else is bookkeeping about which direction the queries are related in. A **downward abstraction** supplies a functor from high-level queries to low-level queries; an **upward abstraction** supplies a partial map in the opposite direction, from concrete low-level queries (a specific Do-intervention with specific values) to high-level ones, and hence carries no functor. The authors then prove the two are related (an upward abstraction is recovered from a downward one by restricting to sharp input states, giving a construction that turns structural consistency into consistency of concrete interventions), and prove both are closed under composition. They then walk the literature: exact transformation and strong causal abstraction as upward abstractions; constructive abstraction as a downward abstraction over abstract Do-queries; interchange abstraction as the same downward abstraction over interchange queries; counterfactual abstraction with its own functional-causal-model setup; and "distributed" abstractions, which they rebuild as the composite of a deterministic re-indexing isomorphism with a constructive or interchange abstraction. The last construction is the one that needs real work — they compute the parallel mechanism channels of the re-indexed interventions to show the composite is again an abstraction. Finally they lift abstraction from queries to components: a component-level abstraction adds a second functor sending each high-level mechanism to a *diagram* of low-level mechanisms, subject to its own commuting condition, and they characterise when a constructive abstraction extends that far.

## What counts as an explanation here

A natural transformation. The whole answer to "what is a mechanistic explanation" is: a functor on queries together with a surjective natural transformation to the high-level model, plus optionally a functor at the level of mechanisms. It is deliberately partial — the query set is a parameter, and the paper is emphatic that an abstraction is only as strong as the set of queries it holds for, so the same pair of models may be abstract at the input-output level and not at the interchange level. It predicts nothing on its own; it licenses substitution. The predictively useful artefact is the commuting diagram: given the abstraction and the low-level model you can compute what the high-level model must say, and given a proposed high-level variable you can ask which low-level variables would have to carry it. The mechanism-level variant does say something an intervention-level statement cannot — it says the high-level mechanism is realised by a fixed sub-diagram, so it survives replacing the rest of the model.

## How they judged success

By proof, not by measurement: each named notion in the literature is claimed as a special case and proved so (Proposition 22 for exact transformations, Theorem 25 for constructive abstraction, Proposition 29 for interchange abstraction, Corollary 37 for the counterfactual case, Proposition 43 and Proposition 45 for model induction and iso-constructive abstractions). The new mechanism-level notion is judged by a characterisation theorem rather than by an experiment: Theorem 51 states that, for each choice of structure category, a mechanism-level abstraction is equivalent to a constructive abstraction whose partition map is "structurally well-behaved" — simple for the Cartesian case, extra-simple for Markov, and extra-simple plus full for the cd case — and equivalent to a single functor-plus-transformation condition on the structure categories, with Lemma 52 simplifying the Markov case to the requirement that the image of the high-level network diagram, once more, is a network diagram. There is also a comparison appendix in which they check one rival formalisation against their own and argue that it captures strictly less than it claims, with a worked counter-example.

## What they found

Three findings carry weight. First, **the direction convention is backwards in the literature**: essentially every named notion of causal abstraction is presented as an upward abstraction over concrete interventions, but the authors argue the essence of nearly all of them is a downward abstraction over abstract queries, with the concrete version obtained by specialisation; the exceptions are exact transformations in general and the explicitly distributed ones. Read structurally, constructive abstraction is a statement about abstract Do-queries and needs no reference to values at all. Second, **a genuinely stronger notion exists and can be characterised**: constructive abstraction says the models agree on every intervention, whereas mechanism-level abstraction says each high-level mechanism is implemented by a fixed piece of the low-level model; this is strictly stronger, and the exact conditions on the partition that allow it are given for three different structure categories. Third, **the mechanism-level formulation makes no reference to interventions at all** — it can be stated purely as a functor on signature diagrams and a natural transformation, which is a real simplification if one has to defend the claim to someone outside the interventions literature. Along the way they show that a well-known distributed-abstraction recipe from the mechanistic-interpretability literature is not a new primitive but the composition of an isomorphism with a standard abstraction, and that a recent category-theoretic rival formalisation, once corrected, captures exactly the extra-simple (mechanism-level) subclass and therefore misses constructive abstractions that are not of that form — they exhibit one. On the quantum side, they define abstraction from a gate-level quantum circuit model to a logical circuit model (the trivially strict case) and, more speculatively, from a quantum circuit model to a classical causal model, giving a geoguessing worked example with three query strengths in increasing order: input-output, interchange over parallelisable subsets, and "opening" queries that expose a subsystem's state directly.

## Limits and open problems they name

No experiments, no numeric results, no neural-network case study — a causal model realised as a neural network is named as a motivating example and never instantiated, so nothing here has been tested against a trained transformer. The framework assumes acyclic causal models; cycles are named as an explicit open direction, with the parallel-mechanism channel plus a trace-like construction suggested as the route, motivated by equilibration reasoning and by the claim that interpretability work has argued for cyclicity. Approximate abstractions — where the two sides of the consistency equation are merely close rather than equal, which is the only version usable on a stochastic network — are left entirely to future work. Genuine quantum-to-quantum and quantum-causal abstraction is not achieved: general quantum causal models are built from higher-order maps and cannot yet be written as compositional models. The mechanism-level notion's practical importance is posed as an open question rather than answered: they explicitly ask whether most constructive abstractions of real interest are mechanism-level, and connect this to a conjecture in prior work that strong abstraction implies constructiveness. They also note that queries may carry algebraic structure (an ordered monoid of interventions) which they treat only in an appendix.

## What we take from it

A **framing**, and a sharp one: an abstraction claim in our work should be published as a triple of (the query family it holds for, the map that carries high-level values to low-level variables, and the direction convention), because the paper's central theorem is that the same models can satisfy different notions depending only on which query family is declared. Two concrete consequences for our batteries. First, the direction convention is a reporting decision with content: if we say our account holds for all interventions we are claiming a downward abstraction and owe the reader the abstract query construction; if we only ever checked the interventions we ran, we hold an upward abstraction restricted to those queries and should say so. Second, the mechanism-level notion gives us the exact shape of a claim we have been approximating informally — that a named high-level mechanism is carried by a *fixed* set of low-level sites — and it is strictly stronger than agreement on interventions, which means a causal-abstraction score cannot distinguish the two and our reporting should not imply it can. The characterisation conditions also function as a cheap audit: whether a proposed partition admits mechanism-level abstraction is a purely graph-theoretic property of the partition map, so it can be checked before any intervention is run. Warning worth carrying: the paper's own comparison appendix is a clean example of a rival formalisation that misdescribed what it captured, and the authors only found the discrepancy by trying to prove the equivalence and constructing a counter-example — the same failure mode we should expect of any claim of the form "our account subsumes account X".
