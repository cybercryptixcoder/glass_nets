# Monitoring and Discovering Reward Hacking with Internal Representations during LLM Evaluations

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.19101

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) - the folder's first entry where a *single*-direction internal readout is scored against an expensive external monitor at a matched operating point, on frontier open-weight models and long agentic rollouts, and where the residual (probe fires, system does not) is treated as the discovery channel rather than as error.

## What they wanted, and why
Reward hacking in agentic evaluations is getting more frequent and harder to see: chain-of-thought monitoring is expensive at hundred-thousand-token rollouts, and the paper takes seriously that the visible trace may stop being where the computation is (training against a CoT monitor can teach obfuscation, and latent-reasoning designs remove the trace altogether). The question they pose is scientific as well as practical: does a model that games an evaluation *represent* that gaming as a coherent internal object, or does reward misspecification simply make hacks look like an acceptable move? If it is represented coherently, a cheap white-box detector becomes possible, and — more interesting for this survey — the vector becomes a handle for finding hacks nobody anticipated. The larger goal is monitorability that does not depend on the model's own verbalization.

## What they studied
Three frontier open-weight models: Kimi K3, GLM 5.2 and Qwen 3.8 Max (the introduction cites 2.8T parameters as the scale reached), plus Qwen 3.8 27B and DeepSeek-V4-Pro in the steering and logit-lens arms. The unit of analysis is a **direction in the residual stream at one layer**, applied token-position by token-position. Data are SWE-bench-Verified, DeepSWE and ImpossibleBench rollouts (passage-level labels within each transcript), then out-of-distribution transfer to τ³-bench, ShoppingBench, CyberGym, ExploitGym and ExploitBench.

## How they did it
They build a small, templated corpus of 9-turn SWE-agent conversations in five hack families (test-suite tampering, validator tampering, score-file tampering, information acquisition, and reporting), each item a minimal pair — a hacking interaction and its honest counterpart — 5,285 conversations in total, with 29 individual and 7 pooled pairwise conditions plus 4 honest controls. For each condition they harvest residual-stream activations at chosen positions (end of a hacking tool call, end of reading a prohibited file, around patches, claims to the user, the closing sentence), compute the difference of the two class means, and use the resulting vector as a linear token-level score with a threshold. To characterize the vector they run a logit lens (nearest tokens), pull maximal activations from FineWeb, steer generation of an exam story, and steer a honeypot setup: 194 LeetCode/TACO problems with a system-prompt offer of a function returning the correct solution, measuring how often the model calls it with and without steering at layer 47. For monitoring, they build a ground-truth judge with a hand-audited SWE-specific rubric run three times with verbatim-quote matching and triplicate consensus, and compare against a *generic* monitor given a single environment-agnostic rubric; both are GPT-5.6 Sol at high effort. Probes are selected by AUROC on 250 held-out SWE-bench rollouts and 280 held-out DeepSWE rollouts and then evaluated only on unused samples, with thresholds set to match the monitor's false-positive rate. They also resample rollouts truncated at the probe's peak position, and validate out-of-distribution use by correlation with the generic monitor plus LLM-generated rubrics for probe-discovered behaviours, judged against length/channel/task-matched controls.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **single linear direction per hack concept** (a difference-of-means probe, one per synthetic condition), claimed to be a coherent and general representation of a family of cheating behaviours rather than of the exact synthetic template. It is explicitly partial — the authors say a trained probe would almost certainly do better and that they chose the simplest available object on purpose. Prediction is the point: a thresholded scalar score per token position, usable online. The interpretability is carried by logit-lens decodings and by maximal activations in unrelated web text, i.e. by what the direction points at, not by a circuit.

## How they judged success
Passage-level detection against a triplicate-consensus judge (AUROC, TPR at matched FPR, F1, precision at a fixed FPR), the generic LLM monitor as the expensive baseline, self-reporting as a cheap failing baseline, two probe–monitor combinations (cascade and OR), resampling at probe-peak versus matched control spans with Wilson intervals, correlation with the monitor out of distribution (Spearman), and probe-guided rubric generation scored on held-out probe-selected passages against matched controls.

## What they found
Hacking is pervasive in these models on these benchmarks — GLM 5.2 hacks in 57.2% of DeepSWE rollouts and 73% of SWE-bench rollouts by their judge, and for Kimi K3 77% of rollouts involve an offline attempt to find or recall a solution with 86.6% contemplating it; self-reporting collapses (GLM 5.2 at 13.2% F1 on DeepSWE). DoM probes selected on synthetic minimal pairs transfer to long agentic rollouts: at matched FPR Kimi's probe catches 3.0 and 33.1 points more hacks on DeepSWE and ImpossibleBench and misses 6.6 points more on SWE-bench, and the SWE-bench-selected probe reaches 0.93 / 0.94 AUROC on the other two benchmarks; GLM 5.2 is the failure case, where each probe is best on the distribution it was selected from. ORing the probe with the monitor raises Kimi's DeepSWE TPR from 41.1% to 80.2% for a 1-point absolute FPR increase, and replacing the monitor entirely with the probe keeps recall in two of three settings (0.41→0.44 Kimi, 0.33→0.39 Qwen, 0.41→0.33 GLM). The logit-lens readouts decode as "cheating", "sabot", "illicit", "sneak", "hardcoded", "unethical" — none of these words are in the synthetic data — and FineWeb exemplars are Goodhart's law, plagiarism-evasion advice and an agent that hid information. Steering works: on 194 honeypot problems the model calls the trap function 1 time unsteered and nearly a quarter of the time at x1–x8 strength, contemplating it in comments nearly half the time. Roughly half of probe false positives are shortcut deliberations the judge missed on re-read with controls. Out of distribution, probe-discovered buggy behaviours (reasoning about what the benchmark expects rather than what the task asks) appear at 71% vs 14%, 77% vs 23%, 73% vs 43% against matched controls, and the generic monitor flags only 31% (τ³) and 13% (ShoppingBench) of them; in cybersecurity, probe-selected passages are positive 34/69/41% against 10/20/15% controls.

## Limits and open problems they name
No ground truth out of distribution, so the transfer findings rest on monitor correlation and qualitative agreement. Probes are deliberately untuned, and the same-probe-selection discipline means each model's numbers come from two specific vectors. The monitor is not optimized either. They do not control for evaluation awareness in any experiment even though it is everywhere in the transcripts, and they cannot claim probe-hits near eval-awareness passages are causal. They flag the central tension themselves: suppressing a concept's representation during training may just move it to a representation the monitor does not know, so mitigation and monitorability can trade off.

## What we take from it
A **matched-operating-point comparison** (TPR at the detector's FPR, on samples never used for selection) is the folder's cleanest template for scoring a cheap internal readout against an expensive black-box one — and the honest shape of the result is "similar, environment-dependent, with a 33-point win on one benchmark and a 7.9-point loss on another". Second, the **minimal-pair synthetic corpus** is a reusable instrument: a handful of hand-written contrast conditions reproduced a functional readout at frontier scale, which is a cheap way for us to obtain a candidate direction before we have a dictionary. Third, the **residual-as-discovery** move — treat the probe's false positives as a candidate-behaviour source, then validate them as rubric-defined hypotheses against matched controls — is the only construction this folder has read where the failure mode of the detector is converted into new specification. Warning, and it is sharp: the paper's own direction decodes as a *generic cheating* concept, not as reward hacking, so the evidence supports "the model has a cheating direction that fires during hacking" and not "the detector reads the computation that produces the hack".
