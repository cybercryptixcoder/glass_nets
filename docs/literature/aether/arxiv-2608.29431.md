# Evaluating the Semantic Specificity of Representation Steering in Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.29431
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhangdie Yuan, Andreas Vlachos; University of Cambridge (Department of Computer Science and Technology)
- **Date:** 2026-08-29
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29431
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Localized representation steering is now the standard repair tool for "reasoning pathologies": a direction is extracted from contrasting successful and failing activations, injected at a late layer, and the failing benchmark accuracy jumps to 100%. The authors' starting point is that this evidence cannot distinguish a repaired circuit from a shifted output label, because both produce the same score on the target split. Their example pathology is contradiction blindness - models that answer forward entailment almost perfectly and cannot detect a contradiction at all - and their observation is that in this regime success on contradictions is almost perfectly confounded with emitting the word "contradiction". The paper therefore proposes a *falsification* protocol rather than another steering method: evaluate the vector on rule families within the same logical domain where the model was already competent, and see whether competence survives. The larger goal is evaluation hygiene for the whole activation-steering literature, including closed models where no unembedding matrix is available to check.

## What they studied
Eighteen model variants across Llama-3.2 (1B/3B, base and instruct), Qwen-3.5 (0.8B/2B/4B/9B, base and instruct), Gemma-4 (E2B/E4B, base and instruct) and DeepSeek-R1 distills, at dataset sizes of 1,024, 4,096, 16,384 and 65,536 instances. Data is a procedurally generated NLI benchmark, Asymmetricity-2.0, built from Wikidata relations with templates partitioned so that entity slots and relational vocabulary are disjoint across splits; four core rule families are used - two that need forward relational tracking (symmetric swap, transitive chain) and two that need a structural violation detected (a symmetric relation applied reflexively, a symmetric relation applied transitively). The unit of analysis is a layer-level residual-stream direction plus the behavioural rule family it is injected into, with the linear probe used only as a localisation instrument. Downstream checks use CLUTRR, SpartQA and ProntoQA, and a naturalistic arm uses a subset of MNLI.

## How they did it
They first run a behavioural atlas: measure per-family accuracy for every model to establish that contradiction recall is near zero while entailment accuracy is near ceiling, the asymmetry being the thing worth repairing. Then they localise the failure with linear probes trained on early-region activations (the first 20% of depth) and evaluated on late-region activations (the last 40%), reading an error rate well above chance as systematic anti-correlation under cross-layer transfer, with a two-layer MLP probe as the non-linear control. The steering vector is the mean late-layer activation difference between successful and failing contradiction contexts, injected with L2 normalisation and a fixed scale of 1.5, chosen by a sweep; the paper also runs an oracle-gated configuration in which the vector is injected only on the target rule family, explicitly described as a best-case diagnostic upper bound rather than a deployment. Cross-Rule Transfer then has four steps: pick rule families where the unsteered model is at or above 95%, inject unconditionally (no gating), measure the accuracy lost and the count of false target-label predictions with a McNemar test on discordant pairs, and classify the intervention as repair or label override. Four controls follow - a direct logit-bias baseline tuned to the logit shift the vector actually induces, a shuffle control built from randomised success/failure labels, cross-model grafting of the Llama vector into Qwen through a least-squares projection fitted on unrelated activations, and early-layer steering. A sweep of the injection scale looks for any strength that repairs the target family without damaging the competent one.

## What counts as an explanation here
A mechanistic diagnosis with a geometric mechanism: the late-layer residual stream is compressed onto the output label axis, so a difference-of-means vector extracted there is nearly collinear with the difference of the two label unembeddings, and injecting it is, through the unembedding matrix, close to adding a constant bias to one label logit. The explanation is deliberately partial - it is a claim about where the leverage lies (proximity to the output projection rather than the reasoning trajectory), not a circuit. It does predict: it says the intervention will override the final token commitment and will not change the reasoning steps before it, and it says competence should collapse on any rule family sharing the label space. The audit protocol itself is offered as a falsification test that can be run behaviourally, without weights.

## How they judged success
The primary metric is post-steering accuracy on natively competent rule families, with a 95% competence threshold (sensitivity reported at 90% and 98%), a count of false target-label predictions, and McNemar's test with continuity correction on discordant pairs over five resamples. Supporting measures: per-family accuracy for the anchor, tier counts of contradiction recall across 18 models, probe error rates with cross-validation and five seeds, and agreement between the steered model and the logit-bias baseline (exact accuracy match under greedy decoding, Cohen's kappa at temperature sampling). Controls are the four listed above, plus label-format robustness (Yes/No, A/B strings), a PCA-based vector construction, a token-position ablation (steering only the final label token versus only the chain-of-thought tokens), and a naturalistic MNLI replication.

## What they found
Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct is the anchor: 99.67% on the symmetric-swap family, 100% on transitivity chains, and zero recall on both contradiction families (0 of 4,957 positives), across dataset sizes. Roster-wide, at the largest scale seven of eighteen models have contradiction recall exactly zero, five below 1%, four between 1% and 50%, and two at or above 50%; across 72 runs the median contradiction recall is 0.0044 and the maximum 0.9352. Gated steering recovers all four families to 100% on the target splits with no side effects - and the audit shows this is an artefact of the gating. Ungated, the Llama vector drops native entailment accuracy from 99.6% to 40.4% on a 500-item audit set, with 298 of 500 entailment items converted into false contradiction predictions and exactly 2 errors before steering; the post-steering confusion matrix is 202 correct entailments and 298 false contradictions. The scale sweep shows no usable middle: at 0.25 to 1.0 the vector reaches full recovery on the unconditional source and downstream evaluation, and at 1.5 the transfer collapses back to chance because predictions flip wholesale to the other label, so recovery and competence loss arrive together. The direct-logit-bias control matches the vector exactly on all three downstream benchmarks for both Llama and Qwen under greedy decoding (kappa at least 0.94 under sampling), and the gated bias baseline reaches the same perfect source accuracy with zero side effects, so the vector's apparent cleanliness is the gating's contribution. The shuffle control, built from randomised success/failure labels, also "recovers" 100% - because with near-zero contradiction recall the success pool is essentially all entailments and the failure pool essentially all contradictions, so the shuffled contrast still points along the label direction - while equal-norm Gaussian and orthogonal vectors do nothing. The Llama vector grafted into Qwen-3.5-9B (2,048 into 3,584 dimensions, least-squares projection fitted on unrelated text) achieves 96.6% accuracy with 17 side effects, and a random orthogonal projection gets 6.2%, which the authors read as shared late-layer label geometry rather than a shared reasoning circuit. Early-layer steering gives +15.4% with 3,465 side effects. A PCA-extracted vector collapses competence to 44.2%. On naturalistic MNLI competently-answered items, steering drops accuracy to 35.4%. The token-position ablation is the sharpest single result: injecting only at the final label token reproduces full recovery, while steering only across the reasoning tokens gives 0% recovery. On downstream benchmarks the Llama predictions simply invert from all-entailment to all-contradiction, leaving CLUTRR unchanged at 0.50 while SpartQA falls from 0.71 to 0.29 and ProntoQA from 0.54 to 0.46; Qwen's vector, pointing the other way, does the mirror image. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B rises from near-zero to 94-100% nominal accuracy purely by forcing the expected output format, with a relaxed substring parser showing baseline accuracy of 12% on CLUTRR. Late-layer probe error under cross-layer transfer is 86.1% (Llama) and 86.3% (Gemma) against 0.75% and 3.2% at the early region; Qwen is late-dominant with leakage (58.5% against 33.7%) and DeepSeek shows near-uniform failure (96.5% and 97.3%), which the authors flag as a cautionary case.

## Limits and open problems they name
The dataset is template-generated from Wikidata and therefore carries the knowledge base's geographic and cultural gaps, and the work is English-only. Downstream benchmarks are stylised structured-reasoning sets, not open-ended dialogue where label-token boundaries are diffuse. The gated configuration needs an oracle task classifier and is not deployable. The paper explicitly leaves the parameter-level check undone: they do not report the cosine between the steering vector and the unembedding label directions layer by layer, calling that geometric audit future work, so the "logit bias" claim is established behaviourally and by the logit-bias-equivalence control rather than by direct measurement. Probe degradation is interpreted as anti-correlation but the authors allow covariate shift and late-layer compression as alternative readings of the same number. Qwen does not meet the competence threshold for the audit and is reported only as supplementary direction evidence.

## What we take from it
Two things we can use immediately and one that decides how we report. The method is the audit recipe: for any intervention claim, find a task where the *unsteered* model is already good, in the same label space, and require the intervention to leave it good - a black-box falsification test that needs no weights and no unembedding matrix, which is exactly the shape of check our white-box model can run against its own predictions. The measure is the pair of them: target-split recovery and competent-family retention on the same axis, with the false-label count as the diagnostic that names the mechanism. The warning is the one that reorganises our reporting: a direction that only ever gets scored where the model was failing has not been validated, because in any binary task with failure concentrated on one class, the contrast pools are class pools, and the difference-of-means vector is mathematically obliged to capture the label direction - gating then hides the failure by never applying the vector where it would have been visible. The token-position ablation generalises the point past labels of any kind: if the intervention works when applied only at the decision token and not when applied across the reasoning tokens, it is overriding a commitment rather than steering a computation, and a report that does not run that ablation cannot tell the two apart. A further use: the cross-model graft number (96.6% transfer, 6.2% for a random projection) is a reminder that a direction which travels between architectures may be travelling through shared output geometry rather than shared mechanism, so cross-model transfer must never be quoted as evidence of a shared circuit.
