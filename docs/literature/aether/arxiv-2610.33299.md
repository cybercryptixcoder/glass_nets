# The Emergence and Sparsification of a Syntactic Circuit

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.33299
- **Authors / organisation:** Qing Yao, Sasha Boguraev, Tiago Pimentel, Kyle Mahowald; UT Austin (Pimentel also ETH Zurich)
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33299
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The authors want a step-by-step account of how a grammatical ability appears and settles inside a language model, rather than a snapshot of a finished network. Subject–verb number agreement is their vehicle because it has a crisp symbolic description but still requires carrying information about the grammatical subject across intervening material, so a minimal implementation has to detect number, move it to the prediction site, and license the right verb form. The motivation is developmental: if a behaviour is learned early and then stays flat, most interpretability work would assume the mechanism behind it is finished too. They ask whether that is true, and they want the answer at neuron-level and head-level resolution across many training checkpoints and many random seeds, not just for one model. The larger goal is to say something general about how linguistic knowledge is represented and re-represented as training proceeds.

## What they studied

Two model scales from the Pythia family: `pythia-1b` (16 layers, 88 heads per layer, hidden size 2048, MLP width 8192) and ten seeds of `pythia-410m` (24 layers, 16 heads, hidden size 1024, MLP width 4096), nine of those seeds taken from the PolyPythias collection. The unit of analysis is simultaneously the individual post-GELU MLP neuron, the attention head, and the route between them, tracked across training checkpoints (2k, 5k, 8k, 18k, 50k, 143k). Stimuli are synthetic minimal pairs on a prepositional-phrase template, with the subject at one position, a distractor noun inside the PP, and the verb predicted at the final position; a variant set (subject relative clause, object relative clause, no distractor) and hand-built WikiText pairs are held out for generalisation. The lexical inventory is deliberately tiny: 16 human-role noun lemmas in singular/plural, and the model's probability is read off six single-token verb forms.

## How they did it

Behaviour is scored as minimal-pair accuracy, i.e. the correct continuation must beat the opposite-number one on both members of a pair. Causal control is measured with an "agreement gap" — the mean log-probability ratio between the base continuation and the source continuation — and two derived quantities. Necessity is the fraction of that gap destroyed when the component's activations are replaced by their dataset mean; sufficiency is the fraction recovered when the component's activations are swapped in from the opposite-number sentence of the pair. The authors note that necessity is close in spirit to completeness and that they prefer sufficiency over faithfulness because defining a circuit's complement is awkward at single-component granularity. Candidate neurons come from Relevance Patching in its unpaired, zero-baseline form: each MLP neuron's activation multiplied by the gradient of the per-example agreement gap, computed on a locally linear replacement network where LayerNorm factors are frozen, GELUs are replaced by their local gates, and the post-softmax attention weights are detached before the value-weighted sum. Attribution is treated only as hypothesis generation; every candidate is then validated causally. Circuit size is summarised by k50, the smallest top-ranked neuron set reaching at least 50% necessity and at least 50% sufficiency, estimated by probing at powers of two and interpolating in log space. Routing between a neuron and a head is assigned by path-patching-style measurement: measure the neuron's sufficiency, then measure it again with that head's value edge pinned at its base activation, and attribute the drop to that route. The final circuit for one model is assembled by hand: RelP candidates plus neurons found to write toward the next layer's number subspace, greedily pruned by leave-one-out removal, and then the head's channel is localised with Distributed Alignment Search over a single dimension of the head's value output. Scope exclusions are argued explicitly — determiner positions are skipped after showing the transport heads never read from them, the layer-0 MLP is excluded because ablating it is globally destructive, and self-attention contributions to agreement are shown to be negligible.

## What counts as an explanation here

A circuit: a named, enumerated list of 18 MLP neurons plus one attention head, organised into four functional modules (plural-detector "amplifiers" upstream of the head, the "transport" head, and pro- and anti-agreement "readout" neurons downstream), each with an assigned role in a three-step account of the behaviour. It is explicitly partial and explicitly scope-relative — the authors say plainly that they do not consider it the complete agreement mechanism, and that the components were chosen to read as a representation of one route rather than to optimise any single metric. Prediction is supported but not in the end-to-end sense: the circuit is scored by what happens to behaviour when it is intervened on, and it does transfer to unseen templates, perturbations and vocabulary, but no forward model of the network is proposed and the neurons' individual roles are argued from their activation statistics rather than from a simulator.

## How they judged success

Necessity and sufficiency on disjoint evaluation sets (192 balanced PP pairs, 32 for the Simple template, 80 WikiText pairs), always against the full declared scope as the baseline. Generalisation is tested on three other templates, on six structural perturbations of the PP template (subject adjective, object adjective, adverb insertion, discourse prefix, locative prefix, longer distractor), on 13 unseen subject pairs and 36 unseen verb pairs, and with cross-template donor substitution. The circuit's complement is checked with 20 random donors per base sentence to confirm off-circuit MLP activations carry no number information. Method robustness is checked three ways: an integrated-gradient ranking at final checkpoint, a variant of the attribution with the downstream-MLP stop-gradient removed, and the k-threshold itself swept (k30 and k70 alongside k50). The one-dimensional channel is reported with its mean necessity and sufficiency over three DAS seeds, plus the orthogonal-complement ablation as the control.

## What they found

Agreement is essentially in place within about 2% of training: at steps 256 and 512 no model is even 2% accurate, and by step 2k the ten 410m seeds reach a median accuracy of 96.9% (range 88.5–100) with a median agreement gap of 3.35, while `pythia-1b` sits at 99.0% and 3.21. At the final checkpoint the 410m median accuracy is 91.7% and the 1b model 94.8% — slightly worse, and the authors note the residual errors sit on opposite-number distractors, consistent with agreement attraction. Underneath that flat curve the mechanism keeps moving. The number of neurons needed for the 50/50 threshold collapses over training: `pythia-1b` goes from 555 at step 2k to 83 at 5k, 42 at 8k, 21 at 18k and 31 at the end, and individual 410m seeds travel from the mid-hundreds down to the low tens or single digits, with the later sets largely nested inside the earlier ones — a median of 85% of each seed's smallest set is present in the intersection of all post-8k sets. The transport head also migrates: in `pythia-1b` the early channel is L15H7 and L15H2, L13H7 is already third by 5k, leads from 18k, and reaches 67.1% by the final checkpoint while L15H7 falls to 3.9%; the head moves twenty times as deep in relative terms as nothing else in the model does, and the neurons follow it, shifting from writing to the L15 route to reading and writing the L13 route, with mixed routing during the crossover. Across the ten 410m seeds the leading head's identity changes in six of ten runs, and no model has the same top-three heads at step 2k and at the end. The two seeds with known loss spikes show the opposite pattern — their k50 rebounds at exactly the spike steps, and seed 4's set ends larger than it started at 18k. The finished `pythia-1b` circuit is 78% necessary and 83% sufficient against a scope that reaches 83% and 88%, and it holds up out of distribution: 86%/89% on subject-relative clauses, 76%/80% on object-relative clauses, 67%/78% on the no-distractor template, 73%/82% on WikiText, 81–88% sufficiency across five of six perturbations. The exception is an adverb inserted just before the predicted verb, where sufficiency collapses to 42% — the readout module turns out to be tied to the final distractor token. A single dimension of L13H7's value output carries the whole channel: the one-dimensional intervention reaches 70.9% necessity and 71.5% sufficiency against the head's own 67% and 68%, and ablating the 255-dimensional complement moves the gap a couple of percent. The ten upstream neurons read as plural detectors, near zero on singulars and on non-nouns and strongly positive on plurals, with six of them the most plural-selective neuron in their layer.

## Limits and open problems they name

One task, one language, two small models. They give no explanation for why sparsification or rerouting happens, and offer only a hypothesis — that a diffuse early circuit acts as a scaffold from which a sparser one emerges. The final circuit's construction was iterative and involved manual judgement calls, so there is no guarantee it is minimal or optimal, and no general procedure is offered for other tasks. The one-dimensional channel result rests on DAS runs with only three seeds. They also note the scope is deliberately narrow — one route through one head — so the circuit is a sparse representation of a pathway, not the mechanism.

## What we take from it

A method and a measure. The pairwise gap-based definition of necessity and sufficiency is the cleanest formulation in our folder for scoring a component against a matched scope rather than against chance, and the k50 statistic — smallest ranked set reaching a joint threshold — is a cheap scalar for "how concentrated is this behaviour" that can be tracked as a trajectory rather than reported as one number. The traversal is the second takeaway: they read the same quantity at every checkpoint and every seed, which is what turns a snapshot into a developmental claim. Two warnings matter more than the result. First, behaviour stability is not mechanism stability, and a model fitted at one checkpoint of a network in this family would be fitted to a mechanism that is actively being rewritten underneath it — our white-box model has to say which checkpoint regime it is valid for, not just which model. Second, the adverb result is a precise statement of the cost of scope: the circuit was 83% sufficient on the template it was found on and 42% when one token's category changed at the readout position, so any circuit or predictor we publish carries a position-and-category-relative caveat unless it has been re-tested with the readout site moved.
