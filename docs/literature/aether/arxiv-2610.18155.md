# When Do Learned Blocks Preserve Causal Meaning? Block Identifiability and Constructive Causal Abstractions

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.18155
- **Authors / organisation:** Karim Zaghw, Wieland Brendel, Patrik Reizinger (joint senior authors on the latter two); University of Tübingen, ELLIS Institute Tübingen, Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems — Tübingen, Germany
- **Date:** 2026-10-13 (v1), revised 2026-10-14 (v2)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18155
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Identifiability theory usually promises that a learned representation recovers the generating factors up to a permutation and a coordinate-wise reparameterisation, i.e. one learned coordinate per factor. Real featurisers do not behave that way: sparse autoencoders split a concept across several features, block-sparse featurisers learn whole subspaces per visual concept, object-centric models carry vector-valued slots. Those settings are covered by block-identifiability results (independent subspace analysis, additive decoders), which pin down *which* block a concept lives in while leaving the coordinates *inside* the block arbitrary. The authors' question is whether that internal freedom breaks the causal reading: if two practitioners' models place a concept in different coordinates of the same block, does clamping or steering that block still mean the same intervention at the concept level? Their larger aim is to give mechanistic interpretability a boundary condition — a statement of exactly how much reparameterisation an alignment can absorb and still be the same alignment — which is the mirror image of the field's usual question of how to find an alignment in the first place.

## What they studied

Learned representations treated as low-level structural causal models: each coordinate of the feature vector is a variable, and the coordinates are partitioned into blocks, one block per human-interpretable concept. The high-level model contains only the chosen concepts. The mapping between them is a *constructive* causal abstraction in the Geiger–Ibeling–Icard sense, i.e. an exact transformation induced by an alignment whose cells are the blocks. Ground-truth generative factors exist but are explicitly *not* assumed to coincide with the high-level concepts. The experimental object is a family of independently trained **additive autoencoders** on a synthetic additive generator with four factors in two pairs, each pair represented by a two-dimensional block; four-recipient models are retained and the rest excluded. Unit of analysis is therefore the block and the transformations between block coordinates, not individual neurons.

## How they did it

- Restate the machinery: deterministic SCMs with hard interventions, exact transformations defined by equality of solution sets under corresponding interventions, and bijective reparameterisations that transport both mechanisms and interventions by conjugation.
- Fix the setting they care about: a partition of the low-level variables into blocks, an abstraction whose alignment cells *are* those blocks, and an intervention algebra of hard interventions on zero or more complete blocks. Interventions are deliberately restricted to whole blocks, because a concept may depend on every coordinate in its block.
- Define the candidate transformation family: invertible maps applied separately inside each block (a diagonal of bijections), and then coordinate permutations that are "compatible" in the sense of preserving each variable's value set — with the block-to-concept correspondence carried along, so that a permuted block becomes a *transported* cell that may be interleaved with respect to the original partition.
- Prove maximality as an equivalence: a transformation preserves *every* constructive abstraction built on the fixed blocks (with cells given by the transported block) if and only if it is a block-diagonal reparameterisation followed by a compatible permutation. The two directions are argued separately — sufficiency by explicitly writing the transported cell map and showing the intervention map induced by the alignment coincides with the transported intervention — and necessity by exhibiting the identity block abstraction, whose cell maps retain each block's full value, as the abstraction that any outside transformation must break.
- Show with worked counterexamples that preserving *exactness* is weaker than preserving *constructiveness*: a two-variable XOR transformation maps a hard intervention into an assignment that copies the other variable's current value (not a hard intervention at all), and puts both high-level concepts onto overlapping coordinate sets; a four-variable example mixes two blocks yet leaves one particular abstraction untouched, which is why the theorem has to be quantified over all abstractions rather than one.
- In the experiment: train one reference and ten recipient autoencoders, freeze them, keep only those reconstructing held-out observations below a tight NMSE threshold; fit affine maps from each learned block to each of the two known concept values, pick the block-to-concept assignment that predicts best on held-out data, then fit blockwise affine maps between reference and recipient codes and pick the pairing with lowest held-out code error; translate a high-level concept intervention into a complete-block edit in the reference model, transport the edited block through the fitted within-block map into the recipient, and decode both.
- Handle the many-to-one problem explicitly: because nuisance values are dropped, a concept value corresponds to a whole fiber of block values, so the high-level intervention specifies the fiber and a nearest-point rule inside the fiber picks the concrete block edit. Evaluate against a generator-computed observation in which only the targeted factor was changed.

## What counts as an explanation here

A characterisation theorem plus a constructive recipe, not a description or a feature dictionary. The explanation of a learned block is the pair "this block, this cell map into this concept", and the theorem says which changes of coordinates leave that pair meaning the same thing. It is a *partial* account by construction: it assumes the abstraction, the blocks and the correspondence are given, and it is exact in the deterministic, hard-intervention setting. Its predictive content is narrow but sharp — it predicts invariance (the transported high-level effect is preserved) and it predicts failure (any transformation outside the family breaks at least one abstraction), and in the experiment it predicts that an edit learned in one model produces the intended effect in another. Uniqueness of the abstraction under a block reparameterisation is a corollary: two cell maps with the same fibers differ only by a relabelling of high-level values, so the abstraction cannot distinguish between low-level interventions inside one fiber.

## How they judged success

For the theory, the criterion is an if-and-only-if: universal preservation for every abstraction in the class, with the family proved maximal against arbitrary bijections of the state space, plus corollary-level uniqueness up to macro-value relabelling. For the experiment, the measures are reconstruction NMSE on held-out synthetic observations (retention threshold 0.01) and intervention NMSE against the exact generator observation (pooled over 5,000 examples per model), reported per model for the reference and four retained recipients. There is no held-out split *within* the abstraction-fitting step beyond the affine-map selection, and no baseline abstraction: the comparison is against the ground-truth generator rather than against another method. The authors state plainly that the experiment is an approximate illustration and does not establish an exact abstraction.

## What they found

The maximality theorem holds: block-diagonal bijections followed by compatible coordinate permutations are exactly the transformations with a universal preservation guarantee, and every step outside the family breaks at least one abstraction of that form — although particular abstractions can survive transformations outside it, which is the qualification the four-variable example makes concrete. The practical reading the authors draw is that *complete blocks, not their internal coordinates, are the stable units of intervention*. In the experiment, four of ten independent autoencoders met the reconstruction threshold and every retained recipient reproduced the transported intervention with intervention NMSE at most 0.012, against reference values near 0.0074 and recipient values between roughly 0.0093 and 0.0119 across the two concepts — i.e. transport costs a little accuracy but keeps the intended high-level effect. The reference model's own intervention errors (about 0.0074) sit close to its reconstruction error (about 0.0073), which is the approximate sense in which the edit "worked" at all.

## Limits and open problems they name

Deterministic SCMs only, a fixed block partition, a given exact constructive abstraction, hard interventions on complete blocks only. The assumptions are not learned from data. The experiment is synthetic and architecture-matched: the additive generator matches the additive autoencoders, concept-labelled observations are used to fit the cell maps, and only well-reconstructing models are kept — all three are acknowledged as making the demonstration easier than a real representation would be. Named future work: recovering the blocks and the abstraction under explicit identifiability assumptions; extending maximality to stochastic SCMs, soft interventions and quantitatively approximate abstractions; and testing concept-directed steering and transport on real neural representations where no exact generator exists for comparison.

## What we take from it

- **A framing for our invariances.** This is the cleanest statement in the folder of an object we keep bumping into: what a learned unit *means* is invariant under more than its numeric coordinates, and the invariance class can be stated exactly. For us, any block- or group-level account of a network — a subspace probe, a steering group, an SAE group of features — should ship with the claimed invariance group printed next to it, because the account's "meaning" is only as stable as that group.
- **A concrete test with a named failure.** The XOR counterexample is a reusable warning: a transformation can leave exactness intact while turning a hard intervention into a state-dependent one and smearing a concept across two coordinate sets. When we intervene on a group and report that the intervention "reached" the concept, the question to ask first is whether the intervention is still a constant assignment in the coordinates the account names.
- **The `covary`-style caveat, priced.** The many-to-one cell map is the honest part of the experiment: the high-level intervention only names a fiber, and choosing which representative to install needs information the high-level model does not have. Any of our batteries that intervenes on a *concept* rather than a coordinate has the same hidden degree of freedom, and it should be reported as a selection rule with its own sensitivity, not buried.
- **The bound on what we can claim.** Maximality is *universal* preservation — preservation of every abstraction on those blocks — which is a much stronger condition than preserving the one abstraction we fitted. The paper is explicit that failing the universal test does not mean our particular account is wrong. That is the correct temper for our own claims of the form "reading this coordinate set is meaningless in general".
- **Method note, not a result to reuse.** The experiment's transfer protocol (fit blockwise affine maps, pick the assignment by held-out error, transport a whole-block edit, score against a generator) is a cheap template for intervention-transfer checks on our own pairs of models, though it inherits the synthetic-generator crutch and is only an illustration.
