# A Framework for Task-Agnostic Counterfactual Fairness in Transformers through Activation Steering

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.00298
- **Authors / organisation:** Joshua Wolff Anderson, Yanshan Wang, Shyam Visweswaran; University of Pittsburgh (Department of Biomedical Informatics)
- **Date:** 2026-10-30
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00298
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Two literatures run in parallel and rarely meet: mechanistic interpretability, which asks which internal components causally mediate an output, and counterfactual fairness, which asks whether a prediction would have been different had a person's protected attribute been otherwise. The authors want a single pipeline that turns the fairness question into an interpretability object — find the layers through which the sensitive attribute reaches the prediction, split that effect into the part that flows directly and the part mediated by correlated features, and then cancel it at inference time by adding a vector to the residual stream rather than retraining. The stated motivation is that classical counterfactual fairness needs a structural causal model, which is not identifiable in complex real-world confounding, so the causal assumptions have to be replaced by something the model itself supplies. Nothing here is aimed at building a predictive model of a network; the aim is a fair model and a story about where the bias lives.

## What they studied
TabPFN v2, a prior-data-fitted tabular transformer used in-context rather than fine-tuned: a 192-dimensional feature embedding, twelve transformer layers with two-way attention (across features, then across rows), and a final MLP decoder. Two tabular datasets with race binarised as the sensitive attribute — UTICalc (paediatric urinary-tract-infection risk, 1,686 children, seven binary predictors) and COMPAS (Broward County two-year recidivism, 6,172 defendants, six features). The unit of analysis is a whole transformer **layer**; the authors say explicitly that they choose layer granularity because neuron-level attribution is complicated by feature entanglement, and that they therefore intervene on whole-layer activations "at the cost of specificity".

## How they did it
Four stages, each a forward pass or a set of them. First, build factual–counterfactual pairs under an imagined intervention on race, in two variants: a `flip` arm that changes race alone and holds every other feature fixed (which deliberately ignores causal propagation), and an `LLM` arm where a local 8B instruction-tuned model, run through Ollama and given the record plus the intervention with no causal rules, proposes which other features would change — refined over up to five self-consistency iterations, then filtered to keep only counterfactuals with zero violations of a causal structure estimated by LiNGAM. Second, localise: for each layer, zero that layer's output projection so its write to the residual stream disappears, recompute the fairness score, and record the fraction of the base effect that the ablation removed. Third, mediate: cache query-position activations at the best layer and run the two patched passes that give a natural direct and a natural indirect effect, following standard causal mediation analysis. Fourth, mitigate: form a steering vector as the difference of the layer's activations on the factual and counterfactual runs, normalise it, and add it scaled by α at inference; sweep α, and test the chosen α against the unsteered model with a paired bootstrap that resamples factual–counterfactual pairs together. The fairness score is the mean absolute difference in predicted probability across a pair (zero meaning perfect invariance), reported alongside equalised-odds difference and accuracy.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **layer-level causal localisation plus a decomposed effect size plus a direction**: layer 10 is named as where race is represented, the total effect of race is split into a direct and an indirect component, and the steering vector is that layer's factual-minus-counterfactual activation difference. It is emphatically partial — a single layer, a single binary attribute, a single model — and the authors frame the vector as a practical tool rather than a claim about the mechanism. The decomposition is the part meant to be predictive: it should say which pathway a mitigation acts on, and the authors note that one *could* learn a vector targeting the indirect effect instead, while stating they did not test it.

## How they judged success
The counterfactual fairness score (mean absolute probability difference over pairs) as total effect, with its direct and indirect decomposition; equalised-odds difference and accuracy as the competing constraints; 95% intervals from 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples for every stated change at α = 75; a fixed α across all eight vector–dataset cells so that the cells are comparable; and cross-dataset transfer as the headline generalisation test (a vector learned on one dataset applied to the other). No random-direction control and no matched-norm random steering arm appears in the text.

## What they found
Baselines: TabPFN reaches AUROC 0.809 / accuracy 0.755 / equalised-odds difference 0.171 on UTICalc, and 0.825 / 0.748 / 0.315 on COMPAS, so the two are comparably accurate while COMPAS is roughly twice as unequal. **The localisation result is the smallest number in the paper**: the best layer recovers 1.3% of the effect at layer 9 and 1.6% at layer 10, with layer 0 at 0.01% and layer 11 at about 1.2×10⁻⁵%, and every other layer between 0.1% and 0.4%. Layer 10 is then used for everything downstream. LLM-generated counterfactuals survive filtering at PC-CVR 0.319 on UTICalc (271 of 398 pairs) and 0.228 on COMPAS (2,853 of 3,696). At α = 75 the total effect falls significantly in seven of eight vector–dataset cells: the `flip` vectors give large reductions (UTICalc vector: −0.154 on UTICalc with accuracy −0.055, −0.089 on COMPAS with accuracy −0.043; COMPAS vector: −0.125 on UTICalc, but **+0.165 on COMPAS**, i.e. worse than doing nothing), while the `LLM` vectors give small reductions (−0.018 to −0.088) at little or no accuracy cost, with accuracy exactly unchanged in one cell because no predicted label crossed the threshold. Reductions land in the direct effect, with the indirect effect unchanged or slightly up. Transfer across datasets works in most cells, which the authors read as evidence that layer 10 holds a race representation shared across tasks.

## Limits and open problems they name
One model, two tabular tasks, one binary protected attribute, one effect (total) optimised; α = 75 was best almost everywhere but not in every cell, so it is unclear whether vectors evaluated at different strengths would be mitigating the same bias; the COMPAS failure is attributed speculatively to features left invariant that are correlated with race; the comparison between `flip` and `LLM` counterfactuals is confounded because the two also differ in sample size and filtering; and the causal structure used to filter LLM counterfactuals is estimated by LiNGAM under linearity, non-Gaussian noise and no hidden confounding, so it is a proxy rather than a ground truth. An ethics section states that the framework does not certify fairness and should not be used to justify deployment without domain review.

## What we take from it
Three things, and one warning. **The demonstration worth copying is that a *decomposition* can be the readout even when the localisation is nearly empty** — the ablation map here accounts for under 2% of the effect at its best layer, yet the direct/indirect split is well-defined and answerable, because mediation does not require the mediating layer to carry much of the total effect. That is the same separation this folder has been drawing between "which component matters" and "along which route the effect travels", and it is a cheap pair of extra forward passes. **Second, a foreign first step is what makes the whole pipeline's numbers mean anything or nothing**: the entire effect being localised is defined by the counterfactuals, and the two counterfactual builders here produce target effects of very different sizes (total effect reductions of 0.089–0.154 from naive flips versus 0.018–0.088 from filtered LLM counterfactuals) with opposite accuracy consequences, which is a direct measurement of how much of the "effect" was an artefact of off-distribution inputs rather than of the attribute. Any pipeline of ours that starts from generated inputs should run two generators and report both, because the difference between them is an effect-size estimate for the generator. **Third, the asymmetric failure is the informative cell**: one vector made COMPAS *less* fair at high strength while helping on UTICalc, and non-monotonicity in α is reported rather than smoothed, which is the honest form of a steering result and lines up with this folder's admissible-region demands. **The warning**: with no random-direction arm at matched magnitude and no per-seed spread on the vector, "steering at layer 10 reduces the race effect" is not separated from "adding a large vector at layer 10 perturbs the classifier in a direction that happens to shrink probability gaps", and the layer chosen is the one that already showed the largest ablation recovery — a selection that a random-vector control would have to survive.
