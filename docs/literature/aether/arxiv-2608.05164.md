# Cross-Architecture Steering Transfer in Language Models: A Systematic Empirical Study

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.05164
- **Authors / organisation:** Ayushi Agarwal; independent researcher
- **Date:** 2026-05-26
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05164
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis says independently trained networks converge on a shared statistical model of reality, and a body of representational-similarity work backs the geometric half of that claim. What has not been tested is whether the shared geometry *does* anything: whether a direction that carries a concept in one model can be used to control a different, independently trained model at inference time. The authors frame this as supplying the missing functional counterpart to the geometric result, and they say plainly that the interesting question is not whether model pairs look alike in a rotated basis but whether that likeness is exploitable for behaviour. The practical motivation is a warning as much as a result: if interpretability tools calibrated on one 7B checkpoint silently stop working at another scale, then a great deal of published method validation is scale-local.

## What they studied

Five open-weight decoder-only models across three size tiers and two architectural lineages: GPT-2-large (0.71B non-embedding, absolute positional encoding, multi-head attention), Gemma-2-2B (1.70B), and three 7B-class models — Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-8B (the only instruction-tuned one), Mistral-7B-v0.3, and DeepSeek-LLM-7B-base — the last three differing in attention layout (standard, grouped-query, sliding-window grouped-query). One TopK sparse autoencoder per model, 16,384 latents at 64x expansion for the two smaller models and 128x (524,288 latents) for the 7B models. Inputs are mean-pooled residual-stream states at roughly half depth (layer 19/36, 13/26, 16/32, 16/32, 15/30) over a 394,508-passage corpus spanning 15 semantic domains. The unit of analysis moves across three granularities: SAE features (for cross-model alignment), concept directions in residual space (for steering), and whole-model behaviour (for the win-rate evaluation).

## How they did it

Track A runs per model: extract mean-pooled mid-layer activations, z-score each dimension to remove scale disparities across architectures, train a TopK SAE, then label features by a differential score — the difference in mean activation between a domain's positives and the rest — keeping the top 150 per domain. Two native vector types are built per model-concept pair: an SAE-decoder vector (confidence-weighted sum of the top three decoder columns) and a contrastive activation addition vector (mean residual difference over 50 positive and 50 negative passages). The cosine between the two is used as a within-model sanity check *before* any cross-model work.

Track B works on all 20 directed model pairs. Features are matched by mutual nearest neighbours in activation space, pairs scoring above a composite threshold are kept, and a 67M-parameter MLP bridge is trained per directed pair. Validation of each pair uses Pearson r at a 0.60 cut (R-squared 0.36), Cohen's d, Spearman, concordance correlation, representational similarity analysis, and a permuted co-activation enrichment ratio. Crucially the paper also computes an *SAE-free* alignment: canonical correlation analysis to 64 components, then a Procrustes rotation on raw activations, then cosine between the resulting concept directions. The paper calls this an optimised upper bound on raw-space geometry and labels it that way throughout. Cross-model steering vectors (B3-TI) are built by encoding the guide's decoder vector to sparse features, pushing it through the bridge, and decoding through the target's SAE decoder.

Track C trains a single global MLP with five per-model encoders into a shared 512-dimensional space under reconstruction plus a contrastive loss on same-passage pairs, clusters the projected feature vectors (UMAP to 30 dimensions, then density clustering) into 11 concepts with all-five-model coverage, and decodes each cluster centroid through each model's decoder to get supervision-free universal vectors.

Track D is the behavioural evaluation: 30 prompts, a nine-point strength sweep from -5 to +5, additive injection at the single mid-layer site, and a DeBERTa NLI model scoring how strongly each output expresses the target concept. Primary metrics are win rate (fraction of model-concept pairs with a positive score change) and a bidirectionality ratio — the magnitude of the change at negative strengths divided by the magnitude at positive strengths — offered as evidence that the vector encodes the concept rather than acting as a one-sided trigger.

## What counts as an explanation here

A representational alignment claim backed by a functional consequence: a linear map between two models' feature spaces that is validated statistically and then shown to be usable for causal control. The object of understanding is a directional concept vector and a bridge, and the bridge is meant to be a translation, not merely a correlation — it must transport a direction such that the transported direction moves the target model's outputs the way the original moves the guide's. Alignment by cosine alone is explicitly *not* offered as the explanation; the authors treat cosine as a necessary precondition and the win rate as the thing that licenses the claim. The paper is careful to say that its bidirectionality metric is a functional prerequisite rather than a mechanistic proof of encoding, and it defers actual causal tracing by activation patching to future work. So the explanation is partial by construction: it establishes that geometry is exploitable and roughly where it stops being exploitable, not how the direction produces its effect inside the target.

## How they judged success

Three families of measure, and the paper reports them with unusual discipline about which comparison unit is legitimate. (1) Alignment pass rate at a fixed Pearson cut, plus Procrustes cosine, co-activation enrichment over a 1,000-permutation null with Benjamini-Hochberg correction applied jointly to all 3,308 pair p-values, Cohen's d, and an SAE-free cosine as a ceiling. The paper insists the scale-tier rows are the correct reporting unit and that the 45.5% overall figure is a mixture statistic. (2) Functional win rate against two baselines: same-model native vectors and a naive dimension-matched zero-projection vector with no learned map. The headline contrast is 71.0% for the cross-model method against 68.0% for same-model native vectors and 65.7% for the naive baseline. The authors then report that the 3.0-point advantage over native vectors is *not* significant over five models (paired t with four degrees of freedom gives p of 0.14). (3) A stability gate: outputs whose repetition rate exceeds 0.40 are excluded, and a model whose valid-output rate falls below 25% at a strength has that strength dropped from the main analysis. An LLM-judge win-rate arm is also run on 650 sampled outputs.

## What they found

The alignment structure is organised by scale tier rather than by architecture. Same-tier 7B pairs pass at 48.9%, the 1.7B-to-7B pairs at 46.9% — two points apart and described as statistically indistinguishable — while the 0.8B pairs fall to 33.3% against the 1.7B model and 29.9% against the 7B models. The SAE-free Procrustes cosines tell a different story from the SAE-based ones: every one of the ten undirected pairs exceeds 0.84, and the 7B-to-7B pairs reach 0.895 to 0.956, so the geometric substrate is present even for pairs whose SAE features fail to align. The authors read this as the bottleneck being SAE resolution rather than underlying geometry, and they name the confound that undercuts the reading: GPT-2-large is the only small model and also the only absolute-positional-encoding model, so scale and positional scheme are not separable in this design.

Domain structure is strongly non-uniform and tracks register rather than topic. Formal, syntactically distinctive registers transfer well (Olympiad mathematics 96.5% pass, code instructions 86.7%, academic writing 76.5%), while domains overlapping general web text do badly (question answering 7.9%, biomedical 13.7%), with several domains carrying negative Cohen's d — which the authors report in full and flag as features that are not discriminative.

Functionally, the cross-model method beats the naive zero-projection baseline and edges same-model native vectors, but the edge is not statistically established at five models. The supervision-free universal vector reaches 67.3% across 11 concepts in four of five models, with per-model win rates from 63.6% to 100%. DeepSeek-7B is the single model that benefits from neither cross-model method, and the authors attribute this to generation instability rather than misalignment — its repetition rate climbs to 100% at higher strengths for the cross-model vectors, and restricting it to the weakest strength raises its win rate to 30%, in line with the others.

Two mechanism-flavoured findings deserve separate note. First, the universal vectors decoded straight from cluster centroids are well separated (cross-concept cosines spanning the full range), while the universal vectors produced by encoding a guide's native direction through the contrastive encoder and decoding it collapse into a narrow cone with cross-concept cosines between 0.985 and 0.9999 — the encoder was trained on passage-level co-activation and does not preserve concept-discriminative direction, and the collapsed variant scores lower accordingly. Second, the cross-model bridge's source consistency is highest for the smallest target model (mean pairwise cosine 0.895 across four different sources) and lower for the 7B targets (0.64 to 0.70), which the authors read as the small target space constraining all bridges toward the same solution.

## Limits and open problems they name

The scale threshold rests on a single model per tier below 7B, so it is described as a suggestive discontinuity rather than a scaling law — this is stated in the abstract, not buried. All five models share one corpus, so a disjoint-corpus ablation is preregistered but not run, leaving open whether shared text rather than shared computation drives the alignment. Only one layer is injected, at mid-depth, chosen for comparability with prior work rather than by search. Causal tracing by activation patching is deferred, so causality rests on the bidirectionality proxy. The GPT-2 scale-and-positional-encoding confound is conceded outright. Feature auto-discovery was run on one model only. The authors also walk back an earlier version of their own result: an initial pair-extraction method produced a 68.4% pass rate and a Cohen's d of 1.21, both inflated by pre-selecting features by label confidence and by pooling directed pairs, and the honest re-run gives 45.5% and 0.42.

## What we take from it

A **warning** first, and a sharp one for our white-box model: the paper's own numbers show the evaluation can manufacture the result. Win rates are reported at "the best positive injection strength per pair" over a nine-point sweep, and the same table's fixed-strength column (s=1) shows every method's mean score change at essentially zero, with the cross-model method slightly *negative*. So a 71% win rate and a null mean effect are the same data read at different points of a chosen-strength sweep — and a report that quotes only the best-strength win rate has published the half of the sweep that flatters it. Mechanical rule for us: any intervention result our model reports is scored at a fixed, pre-declared strength as well as at its best strength, and the two rows ship together, because a flexibility that lets a method find its own operating point is worth more than the gap between methods here.

A **method** we should reuse directly: the paper's separation of SAE-based alignment from an SAE-free Procrustes upper bound is the cleanest way in the folder to ask whether a failure belongs to the decomposition or to the model. Where the two disagree — as they do dramatically for the GPT-2-to-Llama pair, whose SAE Procustes cosine is 0.102 while the raw-activation cosine is 0.930 — the decomposition, not the geometry, is what failed. Our own reports should carry this pair of numbers whenever we attribute a cross-fit failure to a basis or a null.

A **measure** worth stealing: the bidirectionality ratio, the magnitude of a score change under negative injection over the magnitude under positive injection. It is a cheap falsifier for the failure mode where an intervention is really a one-directional trigger, and it costs one extra sweep arm. Paired with a permutation-null enrichment rather than a bare cosine, it is the closest thing in this folder to a control that a degenerate method cannot pass by accident.

A **framing** consequence: this is the folder's first entry to price the null *between* methods rather than within one. The gap between the cross-model method and a zero-learning naive vector (5.3 points) is offered as the real evidence of shared representation, while the gap to same-model native vectors is not significant. That ordering — free baseline first, learned method second, native method last — is the one our model's claims should follow, because it isolates how much of a result is representable structure and how much is just the geometry that a random direction already exploits.

A **warning** about silent degeneracy in pipelines: one discarded variant in Appendix O was supposed to locate the target model's corresponding feature but found that none of the validated pairs contained any of the guide's top concept features, so it fell back to writing the guide's own decoder weights — producing entries numerically identical to the native baseline across all 300 evaluation cells. The failure was invisible in the aggregate because the fallback was scored as a legitimate method. Mechanical rule: every derived vector our model emits is checked for near-duplication against the simpler vector it was derived from before it is scored, since a pipeline that degrades to the identity is indistinguishable from one that works until you compute the cosine to what it was supposed to replace.
