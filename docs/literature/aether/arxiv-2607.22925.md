# Not All LLM Reasoning is Visible in the Chain-of-Thought

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.22925
- **Authors / organisation:** Vatsal Baherwani (New York University), Tom Goldstein (University of Maryland), Ashwinee Panda (TogetherAI)
- **Date:** 2026-07-24 (v1); v2 2026-09-03
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22925
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v2 HTML, body §§1–7, appendices A–G; Tables 1–13 as cells; figures not inspected as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Chain-of-thought monitoring is sold as an auditable window on a model's reasoning, and the whole arrangement assumes the reasoning is written down. The authors ask the extreme version of the faithfulness question: can a model do consequential work with *no* meaningful output tokens at all? They answer with **filler tokens** — a fixed, semantically empty sequence prefilled for every question — and treat any accuracy gain from that fixed span as evidence of computation happening somewhere an output reader cannot see. The motivation is practical as well as safety-facing: prefill is compute-bound while decode is memory-bound, so labs have a live incentive to move test-time computation into latent space, and such computation is also resistant to distillation because it never appears in the trace. The paper's claim is deliberately narrow — a capability demonstration on synthetic tasks, not a claim that filler spans defeat a monitor in a realistic misbehaviour setting.

## What they studied
Thirteen API-served frontier models (Claude Opus 4.5 and 4.6, Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3 Flash, Kimi K2.5, DeepSeek V3.2, Llama 4 Maverick, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3.5-397B, GLM-4.7, GLM-5, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.5), with Qwen3-235B as the open-weight workhorse for all mechanistic and training work, and Claude Opus 4.5 as the closed-weight counterpart on hidden goals. Three synthetic tasks with verifiable answers and terse statements — 4-digit integer multiplication ("What is XXXX times YYYY?"), 5-to-7-step nested arithmetic in the Greenblatt style, and counting distinct assigned variables in a short code snippet — each 1,000 problems, shared across settings so comparisons are paired. Seventeen filler types (counting, Fibonacci, primes, squares, pi digits, random numbers, digit words, alphabet, NATO, animals, fruits, colors, US states, lorem ipsum, the word "pause", ellipses, random alphanumeric tokens), each calibrated to `n` tokens under the target tokenizer. The unit of analysis is the **residual stream over the filler span**: mean-pooled activations, single positions, layers, and the attention edges into and out of the span.

## How they did it
Every prompt asks for an immediate answer with no reasoning; `K` few-shot examples carry both a question and `n` filler tokens in the assistant turn, and `n` further filler tokens are prefilled before an "Answer:" prefix so the model emits only a number. Baselines are the identical prompt with filler removed, and reasoning-parameter APIs are switched off; where a model cannot be prefilled (Opus 4.6, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.5) the authors flag the resulting selection effect rather than removing it. Three mechanistic experiments run on Qwen3-235B on variable counting, contrasting **animals** (strong uplift) against **Fibonacci** (weak): (a) order — concatenate 50 animals then 50 Fibonacci and the reverse; (b) activation patching — transplant residual-stream activations from the whole animals span into a Fibonacci forward pass at each of the 94 layers and measure recovery of the animals-minus-Fibonacci gap; (c) linear probes — mean-pool the filler span at each layer and predict the correct answer. Attention-masking supplement blocks the filler span from the few-shot block, from all prior context, from the final question, from its own earlier positions, and blocks the gold-answer token from attending to the span. The hidden-goal experiment puts a system-prompt instruction to output "N/A" instead of the product whenever the true answer satisfies a modular condition, so the model must compute a property of an answer it has not yet produced. Training experiments RL the full weights of Qwen3-235B (PPO-style clipped objective, group-normalised advantages, answer-token-only advantage weighting so filler tokens receive no direct reward) and, separately, SFT on Opus-generated filler sequences under full and answer-only supervision.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanism, but a **behavioural criterion set plus one probe**: three diagnostic conditions that together assert invisible reasoning — performance rises with filler, rises differently depending on *which* filler, and the ordering of fillers differs across models (which rules out "any extra computation helps equally"). The mechanistic reading is then given as a location (filler span, early layers), a distribution (across the whole span, not one position), and an order sensitivity (early positions dominate). It is explicitly partial: the authors say the attention-masking supplement does not reveal a single universal mechanism, and their workspace interpretation of what RL is doing is labelled speculative with the direct test named.

## How they judged success
Accuracy with 95% confidence intervals from the standard error, paired across settings that share the same 1,000 problems; the coefficient of variation of uplift across the 17 filler types as a type-dependence statistic (interpreted only where mean uplift is clearly off zero); activation-patching recovery as `(patched − Fibonacci)/(animals − Fibonacci)` per layer; probe accuracy per layer against a chance rate and a no-filler baseline; generation-configuration ablations (prefilled vs model-generated filler, filler before vs after the question, with vs without a system-prompt hint); pass@8 beside pass@1 in the RL experiments; and trace ablations (delete `r_1` or replace it with one random token) in the dual-question experiments.

## What they found
Qwen3-235B gains from filler on multiplication and on variable counting, and the gain depends jointly on filler type and few-shot count — with a **type inversion**, where the types that hurt most in zero-shot help most in 10-shot and vice versa (Figs 1, Table 3 summary statistics). Across models, counting tokens give Opus 4.6 +30.0 points on arithmetic (61.7 → 91.7) and Gemini 3 Flash +10.7 (51.0 → 61.7) while showing no significant gain on multiplication (96.0 → 97.0); Opus 4.5 +11.2 and +10.0; GLM-5 no significant change on either; Qwen3-235B −1.3 and −0.4 on counting despite showing gains with other types. Opus 4.5 on multiplication: generated filler +10.2, prefilled-after +8.9, no hint +9.0, prefilled-before only +1.6 — i.e. the span must come after the question, and the uplift is not instruction-following. Mechanistically, the animals→Fibonacci order recovers most of the animals uplift while the reverse is worse than pure Fibonacci; patching layers 0/10/20/30 gives recovery 0.95/0.93/1.11/1.14, decaying to 0.79/0.54/0.40 at 40/50/60 and turning negative at 70+ (−0.13, −0.07, −0.06); a single patched position recovers nothing meaningful. Probes on the mean-pooled span beat the no-filler baseline from layer 15 onward and reach 70.0% at layer 75 (Fibonacci 54.7%, no-filler 56.0%, chance 20%). Hidden goals: Opus 4.5 improves the easy constraint from 33.5% to 44.5% with no change on non-N/A multiplication accuracy, while Qwen3-235B reaches 98.8% on the easy constraint but drops primary accuracy 69.4% → 52.1%, and the hard constraint yields nothing for either. RL gives Qwen3-235B strong preferences over filler type and lifts baseline accuracy 42.0 → 66.5 over 114 steps but leaves no durable filler benefit at test (73.62 vs 73.19 baseline, +0.7σ, not significant); SFT on Opus-generated fillers improves baseline accuracy with no additional filler-specific gain, which the authors read as expected since the tokens carry no transferable signal.

## Limits and open problems they name
Synthetic tasks only, and the uplift is small next to explicit CoT. Mechanistic and RL analyses are one model. The hidden-constraint result demonstrates a capability, not monitor evasion in a realistic setting. Uplift can persist when the span cannot attend to any prior context, so question-specific computation is not the only route. Fillers that repeat the question, and natural CoT text used as filler, are not evaluated. The interface notes that three models cannot be prefilled, so their rows carry selection effects; and the "internal search over candidate answers" story is offered as speculation with resampling from cached filler states named as the direct test.

## What we take from it
Three things, one of them a warning about our own instruments. (1) **A recurring check for any position-level work**: this is the cleanest demonstration in the folder that a mean-pooled activation over a span whose tokens carry no question-specific information can decode the answer well above the no-filler baseline — so a probe that predicts a label from pooled activations is not thereby reading "the computation that produced it", and the probe-vs-behaviour gap is exactly the gap the paper leaves open. (2) **The layer profile is a usable prior and cost almost nothing to get**: transplant the whole span at each layer, watch recovery go from ~1 to negative, and report it as a curve rather than a peak. (3) **The type-inversion finding is a spurious-signal audit we should own**: the same intervention flips sign depending on how much few-shot context sits beside it, which is a concrete instance of the folder's standing rule that a stimulus feature is not intrinsic to the stimulus. The hidden-goal setup is also the cheapest available template for asking whether a model optimises an objective nobody wrote down — an outcome measure that is invisible in tokens, paired with the primary-task cost as the control that separates working the hidden objective from random N/A output.
