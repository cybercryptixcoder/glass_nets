# Probing and Steering for Safety-Relevant Behaviours in Large Language Models

## What they wanted, and why
Activation probes are the cheapest internal instrument the safety literature has: one fitted direction that both scores a property and, added back into the residual stream, moves it. The field's design advice had been assembled from separate studies on separate models — one paper says logistic regression reads best, another says the class-mean difference writes best, a third says learned token pooling beats the alternatives — so nobody knew which of those choices survive when they are varied together inside one pipeline. The authors' question is therefore a design question rather than a discovery question: within one model, across several behaviours, do the estimator, the pooling scheme and the layer choice interact, and does the promised read/write dissociation hold. They also want the artefacts to be usable, so they export the fitted directions and drive an interactive scoring-and-steering interface with them. The larger goal is monitoring and control of deployed models whose stated reasoning cannot be trusted.

## What they studied
One model: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, loaded in 8-bit on a single GPU, greedy decoding, seed 42. Three behaviours, each with its own labelled contrastive pairs, its own mask rule and its own held-out sets: deception, high-stakes awareness and sycophancy. The unit of analysis is a direction in the residual stream at one layer, crossed with a token-pooling rule — five probe cells in total (class-mean difference with last-token and with mean pooling; logistic regression with last-token and with mean pooling; and a logistic regression with a learned attention pooler over three seeds). Every direction is then assessed twice, as a detector and as a steering vector.

## How they did it
Step one is the data and the mask, and it is where most of the paper's interpretive weight sits. Deception training pairs are the instructed-pairs design: a true fact is truncated to its final words under an honest or a deceptive system persona, and only the truncated reply is eligible to be scored, because the system prompt states the label and would make the task a lexical lookup. High-stakes pairs are single user turns describing a situation, matched at the situation level rather than word for word, with scaffold tokens excluded from the mask after the authors found the template made up 53% of a training row and was diluting the pooled signal. Sycophancy pairs share one false-claim user turn and differ only in whether the reply agrees; 29% of the "off" class are genuine agreements where the user was right, so surface agreement is not the label.

Step two fits the five cells at a detection layer chosen as the earliest layer with the best pair-aware five-fold cross-validated AUROC of the class-mean probe, sweeping all 32 layers, with logistic regression overlaid as a robustness check. Step three selects a steering window separately, by sweeping per-layer class-mean directions added at coefficient +1 and taking the contiguous five-layer block whose summed readout effect is largest — a block rather than a site, on the argument that the causal effect is spread over neighbouring layers. Step four steers: each layer in the block gets its own refitted direction, every direction is calibrated so that coefficient +1 moves the mean off-behaviour activation onto the mean on-behaviour activation, and coefficients from −2 to +2 are swept. Step five evaluates each (direction, coefficient) pair with two instruments — a normalised indirect effect read from P(yes) − P(no) on a few-shot "was this transcript deceptive / high-stakes / sycophantic" question asked over the steered transcript, and open-ended generation scored 1–10 on the target behaviour and separately on coherence by a single LLM judge, 24 generations per cell.

## What counts as an explanation here
A direction plus a recipe: a probe is an estimator crossed with a pooling rule, and its value is claimed in two separate currencies — how well it orders held-out examples (detection) and how much a calibrated dose of it moves the model (steering). The explanation is deliberately partial and operational: it says where in depth and which summary of the tokens carries a property, and whether writing that summary back changes what the model perceives and what it says. There is no claim about mechanism and no claim of completeness; the honesty of the object is instead indexed by transfer to the most dissimilar held-out set available.

## How they judged success
Detection: AUROC with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, chosen explicitly over accuracy because no threshold is comparable across estimators or between in- and out-of-distribution data, plus true-positive rate at 1% false-positive rate. The paper's own rule is that in-distribution numbers cannot separate a probe that learned the behaviour from one that learned a correlate, so conclusions rest on out-of-distribution sets. Deception adds two OOD sets deliberately built to differ in kind — one where the context defines the label and answers are byte-identical, one where content defines it — allowing generality and lexical shortcuts to be separated. Steering: readout NIE with 0 for no effect and 1 for a full class gap, judged behaviour and judged coherence at each coefficient, with the readout and the judge treated as two instruments that may legitimately disagree.

## What they found
Detection saturates in distribution and degrades exactly as the held-out set drifts. Deception in-distribution AUROC is 1.000 for three of five cells; on the context-defined OOD set every cell except last-token logistic regression is above 0.95 (best 0.992), while on the content-defined set the best is 0.706 and the pooled OOD figure is 0.722 for mean-pooled logistic regression. High-stakes falls 0.20–0.28 from in-distribution to real multi-turn conversations, where mean-pooled logistic regression leads at 0.774 and both last-token cells trail at 0.687 and 0.686. Sycophancy inverts which pooling wins: last-token leads on both the test split and the domain-shifted set (0.976 and 0.879), because the replies share a template and the only informative token is the final answer word — the learned attention pooler stays competitive (0.861) because it puts a reported 0.38 of its pooling mass on that token, while mean pooling dilutes it.

The read/write dissociation holds twice and reverses once. On deception and high-stakes the mean class-mean direction steers best by readout (deception in-distribution +1.53 at coefficient +2 against +0.13 for logistic regression; high-stakes OOD +1.34 at +2 against 0.68 for attention and 0.32 for logistic regression), reproducing the pattern the field had assumed; on sycophancy the learned-attention direction steers best and the sign of the ordering flips. The authors attribute the flip mainly to the data rather than to the estimators: because mean pooling averages the one informative token against many templated ones, directions fit on pooled replies are diluted, whereas the attention pooler was fit where the classes actually differ.

The readout and the judge disagree where it matters most. On all four OOD sets, judged behaviour is nearly flat while the readout responds: deception on the roleplaying set moves at most 1.4 from a 2.1 baseline, high-stakes on real conversations moves at most 0.71 from 1.67, and in the sharpest case the sycophancy readout goes negative at coefficient +1 while the judge reaches its maximum (9.42 from a near-1.0 baseline) and coherence collapses to 4.17. In distribution the judge does follow the readout for high-stakes (4.08 unsteered to 7.54 at +2), so steering can change what the model says about a situation without changing what it does about it. Coherence degrades in an inverted-U at large doses. The detection layer moves later as the property gets more complex — layer 12 for deception, 19 for sycophancy, 20 for high-stakes — and the steering block sits at 11–15, 6–10, 6–10, so the layer that writes best coincides with the layer that reads best only for deception; for the other two it sits nine to ten layers earlier.

## Limits and open problems they name
The authors list five and they are the right five. The sycophancy probes are never checked against the echo shortcut, i.e. AUROC on sycophantic versus genuine agreement is not reported, so those numbers may partly measure echoing. The judge is a single model with no measured agreement against humans. No last-token direction is steered even though last-token class-mean is the best sycophancy detector, so the inversion may partly be an artefact of which directions were available. Both layer selections are anchored on the class-mean probe, biasing the comparison mildly in its favour. And the negative half of the sweep is not a suppression test at all: every steered item is already behaviour-off, so negative coefficients ask the model for less of something it is not doing, which is why the negative side has no consistent shape anywhere. Beyond their list: one 8B model on one GPU, 24 generations per steering cell, every gap to the reference papers attributed to scale, and the direction cosines between estimators are only plotted, so how far apart the directions actually are is not readable as a number.

## What we take from it
Four things, of different kinds.

A **warning**: a flat judged-behaviour curve is not evidence that steering failed, and a moving readout is not evidence that it worked. The single most useful number in this paper is a disagreement — readout negative, judge maximal at the same coefficient — because it says the two instruments measure different objects: perception of a transcript versus elicited behaviour. Any steering result we report should print both, and say which claim is about a monitor and which about an intervention.

A **design rule with a diagnosis attached**: which pooling wins for detection follows from where the label lives in the tokens. If the difference is confined to one token, last-token wins and mean pooling dilutes; if it is spread over a situation, every-token pooling wins and last-token trails by a wide margin on both in- and out-of-distribution data. So the pooling choice should be justified from the data construction, not searched over, and a reported pooling win that contradicts where the label lives is a sign the probe is reading a shortcut.

A **framing**: report a direction's read-out and its write-down as two numbers, and let them come from different layers, because here detection peaks at the deepest layers while the causal window sits midway. A single "best layer" is two questions collapsed into one answer.

A **measure to copy**: the deliberately dissimilar held-out set. Two OOD sets with different structures — one context-defined with byte-identical answers, one content-defined — give 0.99 and 0.71 from the same probe, so the drop itself carries the information about what the probe learned. Held-out splits of the same generation procedure are the least informative number in the table.
