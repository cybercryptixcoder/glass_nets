# From Refusal Tokens to Refusal Control: Discovering and Steering Category-Specific Refusal Directions

- **Key:** arxiv-2603.13359
- **Authors / organisation:** Rishab Alagharu, Ishneet Sukhvinder Singh, Shaibi Shamsudeen (Algoverse AI Research); Zhen Wu (Carnegie Mellon University); Ashwinee Panda (University of Maryland)
- **Date:** 2026-09
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13359
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Safety-tuned models refuse too much: on harmless or ambiguous prompts they decline anyway, and heavy false-positive refusal is not only annoying but pushes users toward weaker guardrails, so it costs safety as well as usefulness. The authors' own framing is that real refusals fall into several distinct patterns (the prompt was under-specified, the request was subjective, the modality was unsupported, the model was asked to be a person, or the content was genuinely risky) and that nearly all steering work collapses these into one harmful/benign axis. Their target is therefore a *multi-category* control knob: given a prompt, decide whether the model should lean toward or away from refusing, and pick which kind of refusal to lean toward. The larger goal is fine-grained inference-time behaviour control from internal directions rather than retraining.

## What they studied
Three 8B Llama-family models on a single GPU: a public Llama-3-8B variant that has been fine-tuned to emit one of five category-specific refusal tokens (plus a "respond" token) before answering, ordinary Llama-3-8B-Instruct, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. The unit of analysis is a *direction in the residual stream* at a single layer (chosen as layer 18), plus one linear probe read from the same layer, plus a five-dimensional decorrelated subspace that a small learned intervention lives in. The five categories come from an existing external refusal taxonomy used in the CoCoNot benchmark.

## How they did it
They first pass every training prompt through the refusal-token model with the relevant special token appended, and cache the post-MLP residual vector at the last non-pad position, at every layer. For each category they average those vectors, do the same for the benign prompts, subtract the benign mean from each category mean, zero out components below a small magnitude threshold, keep only the largest-K components (K = 200 of 4,096), and L2-normalise. That gives five unit-length category directions per layer; the layer is picked by validating steering strength at each one, and layer 18 wins. A separate linear probe on the same layer decides at inference whether the prompt counts as harmful, with the decision threshold set by maximising Youden's J on a validation ROC; the probe sees activations without any special token, since it must fire before the model emits one. At generation, if the probe says harmful they steer by +alpha along a direction, if benign by -alpha, and the direction is the category whose refusal token the model itself currently assigns the highest next-token probability. Finally, for a single combined intervention (needed for models whose vocabulary has no refusal tokens), they re-express the five directions in a benign-covariance-whitened space, orthonormalise them there, and optimise a rank-5 operator initialised at that basis by descending a loss that pushes next-token mass onto refusal tokens for harmful prompts and penalises KL drift from the unsteered distribution on benign prompts.

## What counts as an explanation here
A small set of named directions, each tied to a category label, plus a low-dimensional subspace the interactions between them live in. It is partial and interventional rather than descriptive: nothing claims the directions *are* the mechanism, and no circuit, dictionary or surrogate is proposed. The model's own next-token distribution over refusal tokens is used as the selector, so the explanation is partly read off the output head and partly from mean differences.

## How they judged success
Refusal rate on harmful prompts (higher better) and over-refusal rate on benign prompts (lower better), averaged with standard errors across five benign and nine harmful benchmark splits, scored by an LLM judge whose agreement with a human annotator is shown on ten examples. General capability is checked on five standard multiple-choice benchmarks through a published harness, with the unsteered model and the two other models as comparison columns. Category separation is checked with silhouette and Davies-Bouldin scores on the layer-18 activations, with PCA/t-SNE plots, and with pairwise cosines between the five directions. Model diffing against the base checkpoint (cosine between same-category directions) is offered as evidence that fine-tuning created the features. A logit-bias baseline is shown qualitatively and rejected.

## What they found
Categorical steering on the refusal-token model cuts the average benign over-refusal rate from about 17% to about 3%, and raises the average harmful refusal rate from about 65% to about 79%. The learned low-rank combination does less well on the same model (benign about 8%, harmful about 78%) but is the only method that runs at all on the other two models. Transferred unchanged it gives small gains on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and larger ones on the R1 distill (about a 6-point benign reduction and a 3.5-point harmful increase); the authors read the gap as evidence that fine-tuning shifts the direction's geometry, so transfer is helpful but not truly zero-shot. General-benchmark accuracy is essentially unchanged across all models. Layer 18 separates categories better than the base checkpoint does (silhouette 0.305 against 0.193), but same-category direction cosines across the two checkpoints are all near zero (largest 0.123), which the authors present as proof the directions are induced by the fine-tuning. The transferred intervention's effect on the R1 distill is not uniform per category: on unsupported requests it *lowers* refusal from about 66% to about 57%.

## Limits and open problems they name
Transfer is partial and weight-change-sensitive; the categorical path requires a model that actually has the category tokens in its vocabulary, which is why the combined operator exists; the low-rank operator is optimised on harmful and benign prompt distributions and its behaviour on out-of-taxonomy refusal reasons is not examined. The authors flag dual use: the same machinery can suppress refusals on genuinely harmful prompts. Beyond what they say: the single layer, single position and top-K sparsity are all fixed rather than swept, the probe is trained and thresholded on the same dataset family whose prompts are then used to fit the directions, the human validation of the judge rests on ten examples, and the claim that low cross-checkpoint cosine means "novel features" competes with the more mundane reading that the top-K support set itself moved.

## What we take from it
Three things. (1) **A read-side selector instead of a separate classifier**: the category is chosen by looking at the model's own next-token mass over special tokens, so the "which direction" question is answered by the network at the coordinate where we intend to intervene. This is the cheapest form of the run-801 gap in the folder (locating the model's own generation in a coordinate system) — the coordinate system is supplied by fine-tuning, and the model itself reports its position in it. (2) **A pole rule that is measured rather than assumed**: the sign of alpha is set by a probe whose threshold comes from a ROC sweep, not by convention about which end of an axis is "more". (3) **The whitened-orthonormal-basis trick is a reusable geometry fix**: subtracting out the benign covariance before combining directions means a unit of steering costs comparable variance in every direction, which is exactly the correction the folder keeps demanding whenever it mixes several directions additively. The warning is the transfer result: identical architecture and hidden width gave an intervention that lost most of its effect and reversed sign on one category, so "same shape of model" is not a licence for shared directions.
