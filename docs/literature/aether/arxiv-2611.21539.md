# Seen but Unsaid: Locating and Repairing Cross-Image Comparison in Vision–Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.21539
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhaoning Zhang; University of Toronto
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21539
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML, §§1–8, Limitations, Appendices A–C in part; Tables 1–10 cell-by-cell, display-symbol values lost to rendering — see Limits)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Vision–language models often answer a question about two images wrongly even when their own hidden states contain the right answer — a correspondence can be "seen" and not "said". The authors want the intermediate steps separated: what is encoded, what is compared across the two images, and what the output actually reads. Their complaint about the prior literature is methodological — the large representation-versus-output gaps reported for synthetic matching come from readouts computed at annotated regions the answering model never receives, so they measure pixel identity rather than anything the language model did. The larger aim is to show that locating *where* the loss happens is not academic, because a loss at the output calls for a different remedy than a loss at the comparison stage. It is a locating-and-repairing paper, not a benchmark paper.

## What they studied
Cross-image correspondence as a four-way multiple-choice question over two images: image 1 has a labelled reference, image 2 has four candidate patches, one of which corresponds. Three tasks of increasing semantic difficulty — synthetic shape matching (closed splines, and a nameable-outline variant), same-instance point correspondence built from HPatches homographies, and semantic keypoint correspondence from SPair-71k — plus BLINK correspondence splits for transfer. Six models: Qwen3-VL at 2B/4B/8B/32B, InternVL3.5-8B and Gemma-3-12B; mechanistic work and repairs on the two 8B models. The unit of analysis moves down a ladder: whole-model accuracy, then the answer-position residual stream, then visual tokens, then individual attention heads, then the query/key weights of those heads.

## How they did it
The measurement ladder is built so every gap is scored against a *calibrated* output rather than raw accuracy: baseline accuracy, accuracy after a multinomial logistic calibration of the four letter log-probabilities fitted on development data, and an input-matched probe (PCA to 256 dimensions, multinomial logistic regression, layer and strength by cross-validation) on the answer-position residual. A separate "privileged" readout over annotated regions is reported as an upper reference and discarded as uninformative where it saturates at the encoder. Localisation then proceeds by intervention, with three families of control: block the image-2→image-1 attention of the top-scoring heads; redistribute the same attention mass rather than removing it (spread evenly, or permuted among image-1 tokens); and match control head sets either by layer or by cross-image attention mass. Repair is tested with parameter-matched adapters — LoRA on query/key of the selected heads, query-only, value-only, and all-layer adapters at rank 2 and rank 16 — trained on 2,000 SPair-71k items and evaluated on held-out SP pairs plus HP and BLINK as transfer. Finally, attention transplantation moves only the trained model's image-2 attention rows of a head set into the base model (and the reverse), and is decomposed into rows computed with base query/key weights on trained inputs versus trained weights on base inputs, to ask whether a broad adapter's gain runs *through* those heads and whether it does so by changing what they read or how they read it.

## What counts as an explanation here
A locus plus a component plus a control: "this stage loses the information, this small head set carries it, and here is a matched alternative that does not." Formally it is a circuit-style localisation (a head set) with a causal criterion attached — sufficient if transplanting its attention rows recovers a pre-registered fraction of a repair's gain, necessary if restoring the base rows removes one. It is explicitly partial and redundant: the paper's own conclusion is that the pathway is *sufficient but not necessary*, i.e. a route gradient descent uses, not the only route. Prediction enters in one specific place — a scalar read off a fixed head set (selectivity) is used to predict downstream accuracy — and the answer is meant to be actionable (it names which of two remedies applies to which failure).

## How they judged success
Metrics: accuracy and calibrated accuracy on 500–1,000 test items per task; paired item bootstraps (2,000 samples) for every difference; AUC for selectivity-as-predictor; Spearman across trained models; drop in points under knockouts, ranked against ten layer-matched random head sets and three attention-matched sets; recovered/removed *fractions of an arm's gain* under transplantation, with pre-registered thresholds; a residual-norm band check (1st–99th percentile of the base model) to flag off-distribution interventions; a marker-ablation control; image-greyed controls; and shuffled-label selectivity for privileged probes. Decision rules, thresholds and item sets were frozen before each run, and the appendix lists every run with its frozen outcome including the ones that came back unsupported; an independent verification script recomputes the reported numbers.

## What they found
On the ladder: synthetic exact-copy matching is compared strongly and decodable at the answer position but not emitted — the probe exceeds the calibrated output by 15–25 points (Qwen3-VL-8B answers 41.2% on Syn-squiggle where the input-matched probe reads ~64%), the gap survives calibration, is essentially the same for nameable and unnameable shapes (the squiggle-minus-known gap differs by 2.2 pt for the 8B and 3.4 pt for the 2B), and editing the answer position along the probe's own subspace at its own scale moves nothing — whereas injecting a *letter's* output direction at the same site switches the answer on a large fraction of items. On real correspondence the pattern inverts: calibration changes accuracy by at most ~2 pts and the probe is within a few points of it in every model, with the probe well above chance on HPatches (0.770/0.804 behavioural/probe for Qwen3-VL-8B) and near chance on SPair-71k (0.438/0.433) — so the mechanism of the synthetic gap is not a general probe advantage. Semantic correspondence is present in the language model's own visual tokens (region readout beats the answer-position probe in all three tested models, by 24/20.7/11.1 pts on Syn/HP/SP cells as tabulated, with a marker control showing five of six cells are not marker-driven) but never reaches the answer. Knockouts: redistributing 32 heads' cross-image attention (Qwen3-VL-8B) or 16 heads' (InternVL3.5-8B), mass preserved, drops Syn accuracy from 41.2% to 17.2–18.2% and HP from 76.2% to ~31.8–32.2%, against at most 3.6% for random and attention-matched sets; on SPair-71k the same knockout barely bites (≈2 pts), which is exactly the deficit repair targets. The heads are salience-gated and content-weighted: with equal unlabelled markers at the distractors' own correspondence locations, selectivity rises rather than vanishes. Fine-tuning only query/key of those heads lifts SP from 43.0% to 71.2% (Qwen3-VL-8B, 1.68M parameters) and from 35.4% to 60.5% (InternVL3.5-8B, 1.50M), transfers to HPatches and BLINK untrained, and beats layer- and attention-matched head sets; a parameter-matched all-layer adapter is stronger still (79.2%), and in InternVL3.5-8B the targeted arm costs 16 points on synthetic matching where the all-layer one costs almost nothing. Under transplantation, the all-layer adapter's gain is recovered 30-50% by the comparison heads' attention rows and largely by the *upstream* variant, i.e. the heads' own weights on better inputs — so broad training repairs the same heads by changing what they read, not how they compare, and resetting those rows costs it only 10-25% of its gain: sufficient, not necessary.

## Limits and open problems they name
Mechanistic work covers two model families at one scale; Gemma enters only as a measurement subject. Knockouts push 47% of Qwen3-VL-8B synthetic items (and a majority on HPatches for some operations) outside the base residual-norm band, so the mass-preserving variants are offered as the causal evidence rather than the original blocking, and the in-band criterion is explicitly recorded as not met. Attention-matched controls are not also layer-matched. Base models' semantic answers barely depend on the comparison heads, so the causal case for semantic correspondence is made in trained models. The targeted repair is worse than the parameter-matched all-layer adapter and lowers synthetic accuracy in InternVL3.5-8B. The answer-position edit shows *that* the probe's direction is not what the output reads, not *which* channel is used instead. BLINK splits are small (under 200 items) and one benchmark could not be run. Synthetic results use one stimulus family, and the paper flags that head-targeted training changes behaviour in ways broad evaluations can miss.

## What we take from it
1. **A measure we should adopt (a control discipline).** Three rungs — behavioural accuracy, calibrated accuracy, input-matched probe — with every gap quoted against the *calibrated* baseline, not raw accuracy. This is the calibration control our probe battery keeps wanting: it separates "the output is badly scored" from "the information is not there", and this paper shows the separation flips across tasks (a 15–25 point gap on one task, ≤2 points on two others) using one probe architecture, which is also the proof that the gap is not a probe artefact. Our entries that report a probe far above the model's accuracy without a calibrated baseline should be re-read as upper bounds until this control is run.
2. **A distinction that makes a probe result interpretable, not just interesting.** Decodable-but-unused is demonstrated rather than asserted: edits along the probe's own subspace at its own scale do nothing, while the model's own letter direction at the same site switches the answer. The operational lesson is that "the direction a probe reads" and "the direction the output uses" are separate objects at the same tensor, and that amplifying the former is not a fix — so any claim of the form *the model knows X but doesn't say it* should come with a positive control showing that some direction at that site *does* move the output.
3. **A warning about intervention validity that our own ablation battery needs.** Blocking attention mass is the obvious knockout and it is the one that here produced the misleading answer: 53% of the edited items left the natural residual-norm band, and restricted to in-band items the effect mostly vanished; redistributing the same mass gave the large, in-band drop. So the effect attributed to a head set can be an artefact of taking the network off distribution — the residual-norm band check is cheap and should be compulsory in our batteries, and mass-preserving knockouts a default where the intervention is on attention.
4. **A framing for what a circuit claim is worth (sufficient, not necessary).** The paper gets more from the transplantation experiment than from the targeting experiment: a broad repair routes 30–50% of its gain through the very heads the targeted repair named, and yet removing them costs less than a quarter of the gain, because the change is upstream of the heads. For a white-box model this is the difference between predicting what a component *does* and predicting what happens when it is removed, and it argues that our predictor should be scored on intervention outcomes (removal, reset, transplant) rather than on attribution shares alone. It also supplies the cheap diagnostic we can reuse directly: a scalar computed from a fixed, small head set (selectivity) that tracks accuracy almost perfectly across every trained variant (Spearman near one across the arms), computed without a probe and without retraining — a candidate template for a lightweight, weight-independent summary of a fine-tune.
5. **A negative result worth filing against our own repair impulses.** Sharpening the comparison heads' attention logits does not help while learning their similarity does — when a stage is the limiting one, the content of what it computes, not its temperature, is what changes the answer.
