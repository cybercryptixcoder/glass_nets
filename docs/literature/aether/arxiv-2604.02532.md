# Feature Attribution Stability Suite: How Stable Are Post-Hoc Attributions?

- **Key:** arxiv-2604.02532
- **Authors / organisation:** Kamalasankari Subramaniakuppusamy; Jugal Gajjar — The George Washington University, Washington D.C.
- **Date:** 2026-04-02
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02532
- **Kind:** benchmark
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Post-hoc attribution maps are used to justify model behaviour in medical imaging and driving, so their reliability matters. The authors' complaint is narrow and methodological: the standard robustness metrics measure how much an explanation moves under small additive perturbations, average that movement into one number, and never ask whether the model still made the same decision. A perturbation that flips the predicted class is expected to change the explanation, so stability scores computed across flipped predictions mix explanation fragility with ordinary classification sensitivity. The goal is a benchmark that only measures explanation movement when the decision is unchanged, and that says which kind of movement occurred rather than collapsing to a scalar.

## What they studied
Four architectures (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, ConvNeXt-Tiny, ViT-B/16) with ImageNet-pretrained weights, four attribution methods (Integrated Gradients, GradientSHAP, Grad-CAM, LIME, all through Captum), three datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet, MS COCO), each at 224×224, about 70,000 images in total, five deterministic perturbations at fixed magnitude, 240 evaluation conditions. The unit of analysis is the (image, perturbed image) pair; the reported object is a triple of component scores plus their mean, conditioned on a retained subset. CIFAR-10 is upsampled from 32×32 while keeping the 1,000-class head, and COCO heads are replaced with an 80-way linear projection, so two of the three datasets are deliberately evaluated under head and resolution mismatch.

## How they did it
Each image is perturbed five ways at one fixed strength: 15° rotation, 20-pixel horizontal translation with zero-filled borders, brightness scaled by 1.5, Gaussian noise at standard deviation 0.15, and JPEG at quality 40. A hard argmax filter keeps only pairs whose top-1 class is unchanged; the fraction retained is reported as its own quantity, per condition. Over the retained pairs only, attribution maps are recomputed for original and perturbed image and compared on three axes: SSIM between min-max-normalised maps (11×11 window), Spearman rank correlation of the flattened maps rescaled from its natural range to the unit interval, and Jaccard overlap of the top 100 cells of the 150,528-dimension flattened map. The composite is the unweighted mean of the three. Everything is averaged per image and then over the retained subset; each of the 240 conditions gets its own retention table and stability table in the appendices, with cells resting on near-zero retention marked and flagged in the prose.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation of the model at all — the object under test is a stability statistic about someone else's explanation. The understanding offered is a diagnostic decomposition: whether an attribution map's instability is spatial (SSIM), ordinal (Spearman) or confined to the most salient regions (Jaccard), measured only where the model's answer held still. It is explicitly partial and explicitly not predictive: the authors state at the top of the methods that stability says nothing about faithfulness, adversarial robustness or Lipschitz smoothness, and that a perfectly stable method may still point at the wrong features.

## How they judged success
No ground truth exists, so success is coverage plus an internal consistency criterion. Retention is a first-class reported quantity (mean, standard deviation and range by perturbation type, by dataset, and per architecture in the appendices). Component scores are reported separately before being averaged, so a high composite can be read against the components that produced it. The reference outputs are the 240 cross-architecture, cross-dataset, cross-method baseline cells themselves. Cleanliness of the resulting comparison is argued, not tested: ImageNet, IG and GradientSHAP are reported as differing by at most 0.03 SSIM, 0.04 Spearman and 0.02 Jaccard — indicators that the composite is not hiding large component disagreement within a method family. No confidence intervals, no seed replicates, no significance tests, no human ratings, no faithfulness baseline.

## What they found
Retention varies by more than two orders of magnitude across perturbations: noise preserves the prediction most often (mean 34.5%, up to 94.4%), rotation next (30.9%, range 0.0–88.1), then JPEG (1.0%), brightness (0.8%) and translation (0.1%, maximum 0.6%). By dataset, ImageNet retains 63.3%, COCO 28.9%, CIFAR-10 11.5%. Grad-CAM leads on the composite in all three datasets (0.722 ImageNet, 0.717 CIFAR-10, 0.671 COCO) and its advantage is concentrated in the ordinal component (Spearman 0.966 / 0.899 / 0.881) while its Jaccard stays at 0.314 / 0.423 / 0.321, so its ranking is stable but the specific top regions are not. LIME is lowest everywhere (0.332 / 0.368 / 0.320) but with an unusual split: SSIM 0.342 against Spearman 0.582 on ImageNet, i.e. spatial instability with relatively preserved ordering. IG and GradientSHAP sit in between and are nearly indistinguishable, both with extremely low Jaccard — 0.060 and 0.037 on ImageNet, 0.051 and 0.031 on COCO — which the authors call the primary instability channel for gradient methods. Aggregated by perturbation category — a table whose caption excludes LIME and covers only IG, GradientSHAP and Grad-CAM, though the prose later speaks of the pattern holding across all four methods — geometric scores 0.497, photometric 0.557, compression 0.576. Method choice dominates architecture choice: the composite gap between Grad-CAM and IG averages 0.21, about twice the largest within-method architecture spread they report (0.09, IG on COCO).

## Limits and open problems they name
They flag their own headline comparison themselves: translation retains essentially no pairs, and brightness and JPEG retain under 1% on CIFAR-10 and COCO, so within the geometric-versus-photometric gap translation barely contributes and the photometric and compression means are dominated by noise — the paper states outright that the gap is effectively rotation versus noise under the current regime. Perturbation strength is a single point per type, so no sweep. The composite weights the three components equally with no justification beyond convenience. Argmax-only filtering ignores large confidence drops that keep the class. No dataset-specific fine-tuning, which drives down retention and possibly confounds method stability with model fit. Four methods out of many paradigms, one task (image classification), and no faithfulness axis at all.

## What we take from it
A **reporting rule** and a **warning**, in that order. The rule is that any stability, reproducibility or robustness number we publish is computed over pairs where the thing being explained is still the same thing, and the fraction of pairs that survived is printed beside the number rather than dropped — their retention column is the single most informative table in the paper, and it says that on CIFAR-10 and COCO three of their five perturbation families essentially cannot be evaluated at all. The warning is that this failure was invisible in their main results: the category comparison is computed by averaging cells that are 99.9%-discarded for one of its two geometric members and 99%+ discarded for the brightness arm of the photometric category, and the authors' caveat, while present and honest, arrives after the headline has already been stated. For us the transferable content is the **retained-subset discipline**: in probing a black-box network, an item whose behaviour changed is not a measurement of explanation drift, and an item that produced no behaviour at all — no response, a timeout, a refusal — is not a measurement either, but in both cases the discarded fraction is a result about the system and belongs on the same line as the score, not in a footnote. Secondarily: their component split (spatial / ordinal / top-set) is a cheap three-way diagnostic for any map-valued output we produce, and their SSIM-versus-Spearman divergence for LIME is a clean demonstration that two stability metrics on the same maps can rank a method differently depending on which structure is preserved — the same point run 946's transport paper makes about reliability versus portability, here at the level of two similarity indices rather than two years.
