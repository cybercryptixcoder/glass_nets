# The Answer-Basin Representation Hypothesis: We Are Not Probing or Steering Concepts

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.24821
- **Authors / organisation:** Manjiang Yu, Hongji Li, Zihan Wang, Junwei Chen, Xue Li, Priyanka Singh, Yang Cao, Lijie Hu; The University of Queensland, Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence, Institute of Science Tokyo
- **Date:** 2026-09-21
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24821
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text

## What they wanted, and why
Linear probes and activation steering are routinely used to read and control behaviour-level properties - correctness, social bias, safety - and the field habitually reads the direction they find as a concept representation. The authors' complaint is that evidence for a linear concept structure, and separate evidence about local circuits, never established that the direction carries the *intended* behavioural concept. Their proposal is that the organizing object is not the label but the model's own distribution over answers: every continuation that lands on the same answer forms a basin, the basin's mass is the probability the model assigns to that answer, and these masses define an answer measure whose statistics organise the linear structure. So the aim is a single theoretical account that says in advance when probing and steering will look concept-consistent, when they will weaken, and when they will reverse. This matters to us because it is a candidate answer to what a direction in a network actually encodes - a distributional statistic rather than a semantic property.

## What they studied
Four instruction/base checkpoints: Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-14B (base), Gemma-3-4B-PT and Gemma-3-12B-PT. Tasks span three behavioural concepts: correctness (GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, plus a chain-of-thought GSM8K arm), social bias (BBQ), and safety (SafetyBench). The unit of analysis is a linear direction in the residual stream at two named boundaries - the final prompt token before generation (the question boundary) and the last token of each candidate answer (the answer boundary). Two answer-measure constructions are used: repeated sampling with answer parsing (64 samples per GSM8K question; 128 chain-of-thought samples at temperature 0.7 for the Instruct arm), and a finite-choice protocol that scores candidate answers through permuted option letters and maps scores back to answers. Answer-conditioned representations are means of the post-answer hidden state over all continuations yielding the same answer.

## How they did it
They first formalise the answer measure as a pushforward of the continuation probability through a map from continuations to answer identities, giving basin mass per answer, plus two statistics: collision concentration (the probability that two independent draws agree on an answer, equal to the sum of squared masses) and the log-mass contrast between a pair of answers. The hypothesis then states two ordering inequalities: questions with higher concentration sit further along a question-boundary direction w_Q, and within a question, higher-mass answers sit further along an answer-boundary direction w_A shared across questions. To test the probe half they fit ridge or L2 logistic linear probes on 500 (or 100) source questions, with question-disjoint test sets, and measure pairwise ordering accuracy and Spearman correlation; the key manipulation trains the probe on answer pairs selected by signed mass gap while holding the correctness label fixed, so an *aligned* probe sees the correct answer with more mass and a *conflict* probe sees the same correct answer with less. A wrong-only condition trains on pairs of incorrect answers only, ranked by mass. For the steering half they build a label-defined vector by averaging correct-minus-wrong answer-conditioned contrasts, apply it at fixed layers (block 19 for BBQ, block 16 for SafetyBench) across an alpha grid, and re-apply the same answer-measure protocol to the intervened model, measuring logged mass amplification per answer. They then split the evaluation questions into target-aligned and target-conflict sets and check which answer receives the larger relative gain. Finally they ablate the source pool's mass gap to see how separation shapes the vector's magnitude and directional coherence, and a held-out-fold design estimates signed projections out-of-context-group.

## What counts as an explanation here
A hypothesis plus directions: two shared vectors (one for question-level concentration, one for answer-level mass ordering) and a transport prediction for intervention. It is explicitly partial and relational - the direction is not claimed to be a concept, but a statistic of the answer measure, and the concept-level behaviour of probing and steering is predicted to depend on how external labels happen to align with the answer measure in the source data. It can predict: which answer a fixed steering vector will favour on a given question, given the initial mass ordering; and which of two training sets will yield a probe that generalises. It does not predict the model's own accuracy.

## How they judged success
Probe metrics: pairwise ordering accuracy on held-out questions against 50% chance, Spearman correlation between probe score and estimated concentration, and Top-1 answer-selection accuracy against a mean-log-probability baseline with no fitted parameters; means and sample standard deviations over five source-set selections. Weather for the concept half is the sign of probe accuracy relative to the mean-logp baseline under aligned versus conflict training. Steering metrics: change in length-normalised answer log-probability as a function of alpha, with pointwise 95% intervals from 2,000 context-group bootstrap resamples; and a ground-truth-free geometry check, Pearson r between out-of-fold signed projections and basin log-ratios, plus a norm-weighted directional coherence statistic (norm of the mean contrast over the mean norm) across mass-gap terciles. Controls are the fixed-label pairing (same supervision, opposite mass relationship), the wrong-only probe (no concept contrast at all), question-disjoint test sets, and matched aggregate norm for the vector-strength comparison.

## What they found
Concentration is readable before generation across all four models and all three tasks: ordering accuracy 63.4% to 78.2%, Spearman 0.384 to 0.745. After generation, basin-mass ordering on ARC-Challenge beats chance everywhere, from 63.0% (Gemma-3-4B, low gap) to 83.6% (Gemma-3-12B, medium gap), and larger training-pair mass gaps generally help. The wrong-only probes - trained on incorrect-vs-incorrect mass ordering, with no correctness signal in the supervision - still rank answers: 79.8% to 82.6% ordering on ARC-C and 70.6% to 73.5% on SafetyBench. The decisive dissociation is that with correctness labels held perfectly fixed, aligned training beats the mean-logp baseline and conflict training falls below it in all twelve model-dataset combinations, with GSM8K chain-of-thought showing 78.7% versus 70.7% Top-1 on the same 188 held-out questions. The same dissociation appears causally: one fixed label-defined vector gives the correct answer the larger gain on aligned target questions and the *incorrect* answer the larger gain on conflict questions, on both BBQ and SafetyBench. Reversing only the sign of the source mass contrast produces nearly opposing label-defined vectors in a shared PCA projection. On the source side, signed projection grows with the log-mass contrast, and larger gaps produce longer and more directionally coherent contrasts, hence stronger aggregate vectors - and matching aggregate norms removes much of the strength difference.

## Limits and open problems they name
Estimating the measure by free-form sampling has finite-sample error and answer-grouping ambiguity, which is why many experiments use fixed candidate-answer sets with permuted letters; extending to open-ended generation needs better grouping and estimation. Everything is multiple-choice-like or GSM8K-boxed-answer, so the answer space is small and machine-checkable. Single layers and a few applied blocks are used, with the layers chosen per dataset. The account is an organising hypothesis: the directions are latent and learned, so the two orderings are tested statistically over held-out questions rather than established as a proven structure of the network's computation, and the concept-level claims are all conditional on an alignment/conflict split defined by the authors.

## What we take from it
A framing we can reuse directly: for any behaviour-level direction our white-box model reports, name the endogenous quantity it is oriented with (here, the answer measure) and then demonstrate that the relation to the external label is *contingent* by flipping the source contingency while holding the label fixed. That is a cheap, decisive falsification test for "this direction represents concept X" - if the direction's concept behaviour inverts when only the mass relationship in the source data inverts, the direction is a statistic of the model's output distribution, not a concept detector. Two measures come with it: collision concentration as a question-level scalar readable from the pre-generation state (a baseline our prediction model should beat, and a useful covariate), and the norm-weighted directional coherence statistic that separates vector strength from vector agreement. The warnings are concrete too: the wrong-only result means a probe's success is not evidence of concept supervision, since it can be trained on label-free mass orderings alone; and the steering reversal on conflict questions says any steering-effect number we report is only meaningful relative to the initial mass ordering of the target questions - report both, or the effect is a coin flip dressed as a lever.
