# Representation as a Bottleneck for Mechanistic Interpretability: The Manifestation Unit Protocol

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.00089
- **Authors / organisation:** Hussein Chouman, Wataru Sasaki, Tomokazu Matsui, Hirohiko Suwa, Keiichi Yasumoto; Nara Institute of Science and Technology
- **Date:** 2026-06-30
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00089
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v1 HTML, main body §1-7 plus Appendices A, B, C, D, E, F.4-F.5, G, H, I, J, K, L, M, N; figures read from captions and reporting prose, not inspected as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central: the first entry in this folder that treats the *record* a white-box model would emit as a designed object with its own evaluation, rather than as a by-product of an analysis - and then prices each field of that record by leave-one-out retrieval ablation, finding that two fields carry the signal, one is inert and two actively interfere; it is also a rare case where a sufficiency claim and a necessity claim are made about the *same* unit set under matched budgets, and the two are reported with very different confidence)

## What they wanted, and why

The authors argue that mechanistic interpretability produces an abundance of component-level findings - selectivity tables, circuit diagrams, feature lists - that are not reusable: each lives in its own notebook, is not composable with others, cannot be asked questions about in plain language, and is not directly usable for audit or intervention. Their question is not how to analyse a network but how to *package* the analyses: what representation layer should sit between a per-component statistic and the person or system that wants to query it. They motivate this with a concrete request (which filters in a trained CNN encode "dog", and what happens if they are amplified) and observe that the needed statistics already exist but have no grounded interface. A larger goal is stated explicitly: making interpretability findings actionable downstream, since prior work has documented that they frequently are not.

## What they studied

Three models spanning three computational shapes: a β-VAE on CelebA (512 latent dimensions, 40 binary attributes, generative vision), a small CNN on CIFAR-10 (224 filters, 10 classes, discriminative vision), and GPT-2 small (144 attention heads, language). The unit of analysis is the individual component (latent dimension, filter, head), organised as a per-component record rather than as a feature direction or a circuit. A Qwen3-8B model is used only as the conversational front end over the records, not as an object of study. The reference sets for the language arm are the sixteen heads in the literature with documented roles: induction heads from Olsson et al. and the IOI name-mover, backup-name-mover and S-inhibition heads from Wang et al.

## How they did it

Each component `k` gets a typed tuple (E, S, R, D, G) with an optional attention extension (T), where the five base fields map onto query primitives: E is a human-readable identifier that allows exact match; S is a ranked list of top-m concept associations with signed strengths and a designated top-1 "primary" concept; R is a top-r neighbourhood of cross-component co-activation scores with a three-way polarity label (synergistic, antagonistic, independent) stated as statistical, not causal; D is a typed dictionary of numeric statistics; G stores the manipulation parameters the architecture affords. Every field is populated automatically from activations and validation statistics with no human annotation, then rendered into deterministic templates so each retrievable statement traces to a stored value. T adds attention-pattern primitives for transformers: induction score, copying score, QK and OV circuit norms, previous-token and self-attention ratios computed over a reference corpus. Retrieval is hybrid - exact match on E for entity-bearing queries, dense semantic search over the templated S, R, G and T documents for conceptual ones, hits merged per unit - and a small language model answers from the retrieved records, with cited numbers checked post hoc against stored fields. The intervention path is what makes the record testable: natural-language commands retrieve components by a *primary-class rule* (only components whose top-1 S entry is the target concept, not those where it merely appears in the top m), then apply G's stored parameters. On the CNN, amplification multiplies post-ReLU filter activations by α, and ablation zeroes them; on GPT-2, intervention is zero or mean ablation or path patching, since amplification has no natural meaning there. Three hypotheses are then tested: H1 that typed structure beats the same content unstructured, H2 that retrieved components causally mediate the behaviour in the sufficiency-and-necessity sense of the MIB benchmark, and H2's weaker analogue on GPT-2, that the field decomposition itself has a minimal-optimal subset. Every comparison is against random selection at the same budget, three seeds, and the five GPT-2 experiments were pre-registered with SHA-256-hashed predictions before outcomes were computed.

## What counts as an explanation here

A structured, queryable record per component - not a description, not a circuit, not a surrogate. The claim is that the *schema* is the explanatory object: if each field is grounded in a measured statistic and the query interface is typed, then the record supports audit and intervention without the analyst writing per-paper code. Explanation is deliberately partial and compositional: S and R are not asserted to be causal, the paper says so twice, and the intended users are invited to fill S and R with SAE labels or attribution-graph neighbourhoods from other methods without changing the interface. Prediction enters only through intervention: a natural-language command is turned into a retrieval set plus stored parameters, and the claim that this is a causal probe is validated by whether the intervention reproduces or disrupts the behaviour.

## How they judged success

Three families. Retrieval: Precision@5, recall@k, NDCG, oracle recall@30 against the canonical IOI head set, with a BM25-hybrid baseline given identical per-component statistics, a prose-serialisation baseline, an exact-match-only baseline, a normalised-identifier semantic baseline, and 14 further ablations of retrieval components. Structure isolation: random partitions of the same per-component text into two groups, embedding model and content held fixed so only field structure varies, ten seeds, permutation p-values. Causal: MIB-style sufficiency (targeted amplification with Δp_target > 0.1 and a prediction flip, k = 8 filters, α in {30, 50, 70}, 270 trials over 90 source-to-target class pairs) and necessity (top-k retrieved filters zeroed on 100 natural correctly-classified images per class) each against random filters at matched budget with 3 seeds; on GPT-2, path-patching restoration of the clean-to-corrupted IOI logit difference over 80 BABA prompt pairs, with per-field causal enrichment tested against a field-matched random baseline using a one-sided t-test and Bonferroni correction across all six fields and strategies. Generation faithfulness: a citation check (cited statistics matching stored fields) plus a human rating study with 15 raters and 612 ratings, and the same contexts scored by three frontier models as judges.

## What they found

Retrieval gap. The structured system reaches Precision@5 of 89.6% on the VAE and 96.8% on the CNN; BM25 over the identical statistics reaches 18.0% and 20.4%, prose serialisation collapses to 3.7% and 2.8%, exact match alone reaches 62.4% and 71.2%. On the GPT-2 schema, S+R reaches oracle recall@30 of 0.411 against 0.20-0.22 for random partitions of the same text at p < 0.01 across ten seeds, while the full six-field schema reaches only 0.196.

The core is two fields. Leave-one-out on the full schema marks E as interfering with p < 1e-8 and D with p = .028 when re-added on top of G+S+R; S alone is needed at p = .028. Inside the G+S+R subset, S is necessary at p < 1e-10 and R at p = .019 while G is redundant at p = .102. Progressive addition peaks at 0.375 with G+S+R, falls when D, E or T is added, and is highest at S+R alone. The two surviving fields retrieve weakly anti-correlated head rankings (ρ = -0.20), the strongest negative entry in the pairwise matrix, which the authors read as the reason their union helps; three separate overlap measures agree no pair exceeds the redundancy threshold.

Causal directions are lopsided. On the CNN, retrieved filters flip the prediction in 76.3% of amplification trials against 6.7% for matched random (+69.6 points), and zeroing them drops natural accuracy by 38.3 points against 3.6 (+34.7), holding on 9 of 10 classes and scaling monotonically with k (+11.2 at k=3, +25.3 at k=5, +34.7 at k=8). A four-way decomposition attributes +17.4 points of that sufficiency to the typed primary-class rule over selectivity ranking alone. On GPT-2, nothing survives causally: every one of the six fields returns Bonferroni p = 1.0, and at the oracle-set-matched budget of 7 heads the framework restores 0.037 of the logit difference while the seven oracle heads restore 0.117.

The vocabulary pivot is reported as the methodologically interesting part. The first attention-head S field used 48 surface linguistic concepts over 3,000 WikiText samples and recovered 3 of 16 reference heads at r > 0.30; replacing it with four MI-derived positional primitives (attention to the induction target, to the IO-position, to the previous token, to self) recovered 13 of 16, the three misses being one backup name-mover and two S-inhibition heads, which the authors say need richer relational primitives.

## Limits and open problems they name

No frontier-scale validation; no SAE-based S, so top-concept S is admitted to be inadequate for polysemantic units; concept vocabularies need auto-labelling or SAE features in label-free domains; per-head causal mediation on GPT-2 is set overlap with the canonical circuit and not path-patching enrichment; the VAE intervention is qualitative only; and the queries are programmatic rather than user-elicited. The GPT-2 null is given three explanations by the authors themselves, of which the second is the load-bearing one: the T primitives correlate with, but are not optimised for, the quantity path patching measures.

## What we take from it

A warning and a method. The warning: the paper's own interference result is the sharpest thing in it for a model that must emit a record - adding a field that is individually harmless (E, D, T) degrades retrieval below the two-field core, so a richer record is not a monotonically better one, and per-field marginal utility must be measured by leaving the field out of the *other* fields' context rather than by asking whether the field can be populated. The method: a matched partition of the same content is the correct null for any claim that structure matters, and it is cheap. Also take the falsification criterion for a vocabulary - a pilot recovery test against a small literature reference set, which here converted an unmeasurable design question (which primitives) into a measurable 3/16 versus 13/16 with a named failure mode (surface features used for a positional router). Finally take the honesty template for a lopsided result: report the marginal field p-values next to the headline, and where the matched-budget set size is the source of an apparent win, say so in the appendix rather than the body.
