# Capability Emergence Can Be Forecast: Per-Seed, In Advance, With Calibrated Intervals, Certified False Alarms, and a Blind Pre-Registered Gate

- **Authors / organisation:** Gunner Levi Howe (independent; single author)
- **Date:** 2026-07-17
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19000
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, ~36.8k chars read end to end: abstract, §1–§9, and the reference list; no appendices exist in this version)

## What they wanted, and why

Loss curves fall smoothly while particular abilities arrive suddenly, and for frontier labs the practical question is whether an ability can be known about before it appears. The author's claim is that the field has accumulated early-warning *indicators* — oscillation statistics, spectral signals, stage detectors, scale extrapolations — but has never once *scored a forecast*: no lead time at a controlled false-alarm rate, no calibrated uncertainty, no negative runs where the ability never arrives, no blind validation of a frozen rule. Indicators without those four things are, in the paper's own framing, lore rather than forecasting. The goal is therefore two-part: first build the missing evaluation discipline on a substrate where hundreds of runs are affordable, then carry whatever survives that discipline to language models and see if per-run emergence timing can actually be predicted in advance.

## What they studied

Two substrates. **Grokking systems:** 466 pre-existing runs of modular-arithmetic transformers and MNIST MLPs in the Omnigrok regime, whose companion-paper interventions (contrastive representational priors, weight-norm clamps) shift emergence timing by up to roughly a hundredfold while decoupling the dominant scalar clock from the outcome. **Language models:** 45 two-layer transformers trained by the author on a fixed synthetic language with bigram structure and variable-offset within-context repetition (30 positives at one configuration with varying seeds, 10 data-ablated negatives, 5 one-layer architectural negatives), plus 118 language-model runs total across a calibration fleet, three blind gates, a trap fleet and gap-probe cells; and inference-only probing of public checkpoints — five Pythia suites (70m/160m/410m, standard and deduped), OLMo-1 and OLMo-2. The unit of analysis is a **named capability with a known mechanistic precursor**: induction, whose antecedent is the previous-token (layer-0 prefix-matching) head, with behavioural readout as copy advantage on repeated random sequences.

## How they did it

A run logs behavioural and internal signals at fixed cadence. An *event* is the first crossing of an absolute behavioural criterion (never a fraction of that run's own maximum), and a forecaster reads only the log prefix, may alarm once, and is scored by lead = event time − alarm time; an alarm after the event scores zero and silence on an eventing run is a miss. False-alarm rate is the fraction of *negative* runs that alarm, where negatives come in three declared kinds — budget-censored (would event with more budget, and legitimately attracts alarms), structurally blocked, and manufactured (built so the capability cannot form). Thresholds and champion forecasters are chosen on training runs alone under a 5% FA cap with at least five negatives on every side of every split. Quality is reported as a **pair**, (ranking correlation, lead), because ranking alone is trivially won by a detector of the event itself. Point forecasts carry split-conformal intervals whose empirical coverage is measured on held-out seeds. Blind validation means every constant frozen and commit-stamped *before* the fresh runs exist, then exactly one scoring pass. Concretely on the fleets: alarms fire when the layer-0 previous-token score exceeds 0.10; the frozen forecast is the interval [t_alarm + 825, t_alarm + 1125]; gates B and C re-run the identical rule on a wider/sparser model and on a language generated from a different bigram table; a trap language is then constructed in which previous-token context pays for the task itself, and the author pre-registers the prediction that his own headline rule will false-alarm there. Finally a gap-origin study perturbs learning rate and batch size in both directions to ask what owns the offset between alarm and event.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a mechanism story in the usual circuit sense — the circuit is the *input*, assumed known and used as an anchor. The deliverable is a **frozen forecasting rule with a stated error profile**: an alarm predicate on an internal quantity, a multiplicative or additive interval around the alarm time, a certified false-alarm count on a designed negative set, and a pre-registered record of what was killed. It is explicitly partial: one capability, one known precursor, small models. It is nonetheless predictive in the strongest sense the folder has seen — it names a future time before the data exists and is scored once.

## How they judged success

Spearman rank correlation between alarm time and event time, reported with a bootstrap confidence interval; **median lead in steps**, reported *alongside* the correlation and never instead of it; empirical interval coverage against a nominal 90%; false-alarm counts on manufactured negatives; a pre-registered pass bar per gate (e.g. 7/10 coverage); and pre-registered kill criteria that fire against the author's own predictions. Baselines are: the strongest published config-equation predictor (constant per configuration by construction), a best-case training-loss threshold granted an oracle sweep, the bare content probe, and a multivariate forecaster whose features are chosen to suppress false alarms in-distribution. The disarmingly strong control is a **nowcast** — the loss curve's own crossing.

## What they found

On the 30-seed fleet at fixed configuration, per-seed event times span 5,650–7,400 steps (a 1.31× spread, clearing the pre-registered 1.3× bar by 0.01, which the author declines to dramatise). Precursor crossing time forecasts each seed's event at ρ = 0.977 (bootstrap CI [0.911, 0.995]) with a median lead of 975 steps, about 15% of the run — exactly the variance the config-equation baseline cannot address, since that baseline is a constant per configuration. **The best-case loss threshold ties the correlation at 0.977 while delivering a median lead of 50 steps** (minimum −25), i.e. it detects the cliff while it is happening; the pre-registered metric (correlation alone) therefore failed as designed, and the author reports that as a registered failure and switches the protocol to a (correlation, lead) pair. A graded behavioural ramp preceding the cliff carries no timing information at all (ρ = −0.03). Split-conformal intervals of median width 300 steps anchored at the precursor covered 15/15 held-out seeds at 90% nominal, issued 975 steps early; the same machinery anchored at the loss crossing covers too, with 50 steps of warning. Two blind gates passed: Gate B (width 256→320, repetition density 0.75→0.6, 10 seeds + 3 negatives) gave 10/10 coverage against a 7/10 bar, ρ = 0.988, 0/3 false alarms, median lead 1,012; Gate C (a different language generator) gave 9/10 coverage — the nominal — ρ = 0.952, median lead 987.5, 0/3 false alarms. The trap language then broke the bare rule exactly as pre-registered: 10/10 false alarms on capability-blocked runs, with the precursor crossing threshold 650–4,400 steps from task incentive alone, while the graded behavioural signal false-alarmed transiently on 2/10 positives. The **same-evaluation conjunction** of precursor and behavioural gate is certified in both language classes (0/10 FA) and times emergence at ρ = 1.000 with median lead 1,900 steps. The gap study showed both learning rate and batch move the offset by roughly 2.3× in either direction, so no external clock owns it; the post-hoc invariant across 80 valid-anchor runs is that the anchor fires at 0.843 of time-to-emergence (IQR 0.825–0.865), t_event ≈ 1.19 × t_anchor, and this multiplicative rule passed a third blind gate at a never-seen learning rate (5/5 coverage, 0/5 false alarms). On public artifacts the precursor rises a full checkpoint stage ahead of the induction score and copy advantage in every suite probed, but all five Pythia suites cliff at the same ~2.1B tokens — one run per configuration, which is why public suites cannot test per-seed forecasting. **The kill ledger is the paper's real contribution:** K1/K8 (no forecaster survives a 5% FA cap in the algorithmic domain, because trap negatives out-signal true positives), K5 (fitted calibration transfers across domains in neither direction), K9 (a label-free spectral probe with a 34,200-step validated lead died under confirmation — it was largely detecting the intervention, not the emergence), K8b (no external clock owns the gap), plus failed registered predictions including the Pythia direction-of-error, the 50%-of-maximum event definition (broken twice by outliers), and the rank-correlation-only metric.

## Limits and open problems they name

One capability (induction) in small models — two-layer fleets, public suites up to 1B parameters, synthetic languages. Each blind gate shifts one or two axes at a time. The gap law is blind-validated at one new configuration and post-hoc across eight; whether the 0.843 fraction is architecture- or capability-dependent is open, and *why the last sixth of the road is announced at all* is named as the program's sharpest open question (a competition-for-gradient account is offered explicitly as hypothesis). Calibration seeds (R4) were not blind even though the gates were. The per-seed spread cleared its bar by 0.01. Pre-registration is repository-native rather than third-party. For frontier transfer the author names four gaps and is unusually honest that the first is not a gap his method closes: most capabilities of concern have no known precursor, and *finding* precursors is interpretability work his benchmark can score but not replace.

## What we take from it

Mostly **measures and a reporting discipline**, and this is the closest thing in the folder to a template for how we should claim a predictive model works.

1. **Report (ranking, lead) as a pair or do not report ranking.** The single cleanest demonstration yet that correlation alone rewards a nowcast: a loss threshold matched ρ = 0.977 exactly and gave twenty times less warning. Any of our probes that "predicts" a property must be scored by how far ahead of the outcome it fires, and against a detector that fires *at* the outcome as the baseline.
2. **The baseline must be granted an oracle sweep.** Their loss-threshold competitor gets its best possible constant chosen post hoc, and still loses on lead. That is a much stronger statement than beating a default.
3. **Manufactured negatives are a first-class resource, and the three-kind taxonomy is the fix for our usual complaint about nulls.** Budget-censored, structurally blocked, and manufactured negatives are not interchangeable — a budget-censored negative *should* attract alarms and scoring it as a false alarm is a category error. This is the answer to the "all baselines return zero" and "null was never measured" problems that previous entries kept flagging: build the negative set, then certify against it.
4. **A designed trap that kills your own headline rule, pre-registered before you build it.** The bare precursor is certified on the bigram fleet *because that language gives previous-token attention no task role*; the author constructs the language where it does, pre-registers that the rule will fail there, watches it fail 10/10, and the repair is a conjunction composed from mechanism. This is the folder's matched/mismatched/random rung turned into an adversarial test of the author's own claim, and it is the pattern we should copy: our probe results need a corpus or task in which the probe's shortcut is load-bearing, not just one where it is absent.
5. **Compose anchors from mechanism; a single-feature anchor is regime-local.** Both the trap result and the grokking robustness–false-alarm tradeoff point the same way — the features that make a monitor quiet on its training distribution are exactly the features an intervention breaks.
6. **Recalibrate whenever the timescale moves, and check which invariants are timescale-free.** The fixed 975-step offset survived two gates by luck of timescale preservation and died when learning rate and batch changed; the multiplicative law survived. For us: an offset in *steps* is timescale-local, a fraction of the trajectory is not.
7. **Absolute event criteria, never fractions of a run's own maximum** — adopted after that operationalisation failed twice, and it failed again on a Pythia suite. And **pre-register the metric, then report its failure** when it turns out to reward the wrong thing.

Also worth keeping as a **warning**: everything here is one author, one GPU, one capability, repository-native pre-registration, and a pre-registered bar cleared by 0.01. The discipline is exemplary and the scope is narrow, and the paper says so.
