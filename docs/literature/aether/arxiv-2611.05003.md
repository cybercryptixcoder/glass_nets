# Unifying and Beyond the Theory of Pervasive Capability Degradation in LLM Post-training SFT from Feature Interference

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.05003
- **Authors / organisation:** Hang Chen, Jiaying Zhu, Wenya Wang; Nanyang Technological University (College of Computing and Data Science) and The Chinese University of Hong Kong
- **Date:** 2026-11-05 (v2; v1 2026-11-03)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05003
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Supervised fine-tuning on a narrow task erodes the broad competence a model already had, and the authors' complaint is that the standard account of this — a shift in the output distribution — is macroscopic: it can be summarised by KL or Fisher distance but not acted on locally, so every mitigation has been a heuristic external constraint (mix in general data, orthogonalise gradients, prune delta parameters) with no shared mechanism to justify it. They want a bottom-up account that runs from superposition in the residual stream, through a loss over co-activating features, to a specific prediction about which token a logit will flip toward. With that chain in hand they re-derive six existing mitigation families as consequences of one geometric failure, argue that each has a limit the geometry exposes, and propose a training loss that constrains activations at the decision hyperplanes directly rather than through the output distribution. The larger goal is a mechanistic theory of *training-side* damage that can be checked and controlled at the level of individual token directions, which is the same goal a predictive white-box model has, approached from the parameter-update end rather than the forward-pass end.

## What they studied

Full-parameter and parameter-efficient SFT of Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5 (3B, 7B, 32B, 72B) and Gemma-2-9B. Target tasks: the eight GLUE datasets, GSM8K, CodeAlpaca, Code-Bagel-Hermes, Infinity-Instruct-Code, Evol-Instruct, LogiQA2 (14 in total). Pervasive side: a sampled mixture from FLAN 2021, T0, NIV2 and the CoT collection, deliberately sampled to cover label tokens — 12,110 samples / 27,943 distinct label tokens / 85.3% of the 32,768-token Mistral vocabulary, and 31,931 samples / 77,031 distinct tokens / 50.8% of the 151,665-token Qwen vocabulary — plus a held-out 44-benchmark suite run under lm-evaluation-harness that never enters training. The unit of analysis moves between three levels on purpose: the feature direction in the residual stream, the token's convex decision cell in the unembedding space (a Laguerre–Voronoi diagram, since logits minus a per-token constant are a power distance), and the individual *edge* between a ground-truth cell and one competitor.

## How they did it

The theory has three steps, and the bridging assumption is the one to inspect. First, activations are taken to decompose linearly over an overcomplete unit-norm feature dictionary (the superposition hypothesis), so the expected reconstruction loss expands as a binomial mixture over how many features co-activate at once; with very sparse pre-training data the one-sparse term dominates and features settle into an almost-orthogonal packing, while narrow SFT raises the co-activation frequency of a correlated group and the higher-order term becomes the binding constraint. They then work a two-feature example: when the pair activates together deterministically, the model can either orthogonalise the pair or *collapse* it onto its principal component, and because collapse both removes the pair's compounding cross-talk and frees a direction for the remaining features, a capacity-limited network prefers collapse — which is why general inputs afterwards leak a ghost signal into the collapsed channel, displace the activation anisotropically toward the token cells whose features overlap the task's, and can push it across one hyperplane. Second, to move the argument from activations to weights they posit a "virtual" parameterisation in which each idealised row of a layer's read-in operator corresponds to exactly one feature, prove it exists for any linearised layer operator, and derive that orthogonality and additivity in parameter space carry over to feature space under that parameterisation (Appendix A) — every weight-space claim in the paper is a claim about this idealisation, and they say so. Third, six paradigms — KL anchoring to the base policy, general-data mixing, parameter-space orthogonalisation for merging, delta-parameter pruning, gradient orthogonalisation, and interpretability-guided localisation/freezing — are each re-derived as a constraint on a different one of these quantities, and each is given a specific theoretical ceiling: the KL term dilutes the task gradient in proportion to its coefficient, general-data coverage needs a sample count set by the rarest feature and therefore grows linearly in dictionary size, parameter orthogonality is attenuated through the nonlinearity, pruning trades activation-perturbation variance against retention rate, gradient projection runs out of dimensions once the protected bases saturate the hidden width, and localisation assumes separability that superposition denies.

AEGIS is then a loss with two stages. Offline, the frozen base model is run over the pervasive corpus and, for every training position, each competing token's normalised margin sin θ — the activation's angular projection off that competitor's hyperplane, equal to the cosine when the activation sits on the correct side — is recorded; a per-competitor tolerance width is set from the average baseline buffer, widened for the strongest competitor by an exponential in proximity. During SFT, at every position the model computes the same margins for the top-50% competitors, forms a violation distance as the positive part of the (sign-aware) margin drop beyond that width plus the positive part of any negative margin, applies a Huber to it, and adds a per-sample regulariser. That regulariser is free of hyperparameters at use time because its strength is chosen by a one-dimensional convex problem solved exactly by bisection on the right derivative (Appendix F), with the solved scalar absorbed analytically so the gradient passes through by the envelope theorem. Cost is one extra forward pass per batch on the pervasive anchor set plus the margin arithmetic; every baseline gets the same anchor set so the comparison is at matched data.

## What counts as an explanation here

A geometric causal chain plus a diagnostic instrument rather than a circuit. The explanation is a derivation: superposition plus a co-activation shift implies feature collapse, collapse implies an anisotropic activation displacement along the task's feature subspace, displacement implies crossings of specific LVD edges, and each step is an equation with a stated approximation (first-order Taylor propagation, the virtual parameterisation, a quadratic expansion through the activation function). It is explicitly partial — it does not say which computation inside a layer produces the correlated features, only what a feature-level change does to decisions downstream — but it is meant to be predictive, and the paper's own test of that is the edge-crossing census: per ground-truth position, whether each competitor's margin is negative, whether it was positive under the base model and is negative after SFT ("newly crossed"), and whether a base-model crossing was repaired. That census is the most reusable object in the paper: it converts "capability degradation" into a count of named, individual decision boundaries whose crossing was caused by training, attributed to a specific competing token.

## How they judged success

Pervasive retention is average accuracy over the held-out 44-benchmark suite (P-Acc.), reported next to the target task's own metric for each of the 14 tasks and three model families; AEGIS is compared against one representative of each of the six paradigms — proximal SFT for KL anchoring, SMART for sub-data mixing, KnOTS for merge orthogonalisation, DARE for delta pruning, DiFT for gradient orthogonalisation, CLUE for localisation-freezing — and against a vanilla arm that is full-parameter fine-tuning on the target set *plus the same pervasive set*, which makes the data-matched baseline stronger than in most papers of this kind. Costs are profiled as GPU-hours, total FLOPs, model- and hardware-FLOP utilisation, and peak memory at four Qwen scales. The internal check is the edge-crossing table (Cross / New / Rec counts on top-50% competitors after SFT on RTE, GSM8K and CodeAlpaca against a Qwen2.5-7B base) plus a top-ten drift-direction table that names the tokens activations move toward. The mechanism is not otherwise ablated: there is no arm with the margin constraint replaced by a norm-matched penalty, no arm with the tolerance width set to zero, and the claim that the gain is transmitted through reduced feature interference is argued from the drift table rather than measured by varying interference directly.

## What they found

On the NLU table, averaging the eight per-task P-Acc. columns gives 0.5368 for AEGIS against 0.5271 for CLUE, 0.5230 for SMART, 0.5194 for vanilla, 0.4878 for KnOTS and 0.4855 for DARE at Mistral-7B; 0.6596 against 0.6514 / 0.6495 / 0.6454 at Qwen2.5-7B; 0.5617 against 0.5541 / 0.5503 / 0.5479 at Gemma-2-9B — in each case roughly 1.4–1.7 points over vanilla and about one point over the best rival, with the two weight-space merging/pruning baselines clearly negative. Target-task averages move the same way (0.8570 against 0.8498 CLUE and 0.8397 vanilla at Mistral). The non-NLU table is consistent but thinner than the abstract suggests: averaging its six retention columns I get AEGIS 0.3981 against 0.3860 for CLUE, 0.3749 for SMART and 0.3739 for vanilla at Mistral-7B; 0.5924 against 0.5609 CLUE and 0.5457 vanilla at Qwen2.5-7B; 0.5916 against 0.5717 CLUE and 0.5599 vanilla at Gemma-2-9B. The same table gives 0.4312 against 0.4125 CLUE and 0.4057 vanilla at Mistral-7B, 0.6488 against 0.6323 / 0.6248 at Qwen2.5-7B and 0.5963 against 0.5855 / 0.5765 at Gemma-2-9B. The Gemma blocks are the only place the ordering is not clean, and only on individual retention cells: its Code-Bagel-Hermes P-Acc. is 0.602 for AEGIS against vanilla's 0.601, and its CodeAlpaca Pass@5 (0.438) still trails CLUE's 0.468. More usefully, the Gemma GSM8K row shows a method that beats vanilla's accuracy while being outscored on retention — DiFT reaches 0.784 target accuracy against vanilla 0.755 but only 0.551 P-Acc. against 0.553 — which is the trade the retention metric is meant to expose: an arm can buy task score outright and pay for it in general competence. Across the three model families the pattern is an aggregate lead of roughly 2–4 points of retention (1.8–2.6 on the six-column means) and 1–2.5 points of target accuracy. The edge census is the result the theory actually predicts: total crossings and repaired crossings barely differ across methods (AEGIS 16.794 / 8.683 on RTE against vanilla 16.668 / 8.659), so most crossings are inherited from the base model's own superposition, but *new* crossings fall by a wide margin — 4.309 against 5.700 for vanilla on RTE, 3.101 against 4.373 on GSM8K, 1.370 against 1.941 on CodeAlpaca, and every other method sits at or above vanilla on this column. The drift table says what those crossings point at: vanilla's top new directions on MRPC are ` ␣yes`, ` ␣no`, `yes`, `no` (10,457 crossings toward the task's own labels across four directions), and on CodeAlpaca and GSM8K they are mostly digit tokens, i.e. the target task's label vocabulary; AEGIS's top-ten directions contain none of the target labels on MRPC or GSM8K and only the digit pair `3 4` on CodeAlpaca (27 crossings against 2,121). Cost is 1.26–1.27× vanilla's wall-clock at every scale (4.92 against 3.88 GPU-hours at 72B, 2.21 against 1.74 at 32B, 4.88 against 3.85 at 7B, 2.13 against 1.69 at 3B), flat in model size, at about the same total FLOPs and peak memory.

## Limits and open problems they name

The theory lives in a linearised, idealised parameter space and its own appendices are the proofs of propositions about that idealisation, which the paper states but cannot test; the propagation from activation displacement to LVD crossing is first-order Taylor with an unmeasured Jacobian. The feature-collapse step is illustrated on a two-feature example and is not measured in the models — nothing in the paper reports an actual dictionary or pairwise feature Gram matrix before and after SFT, so the central mechanism is inferred from the pattern of crossings rather than observed. Per-task results are reported without per-seed spread "for clarity" while claiming five seeds with variance "consistently below" the reporting precision. The margin loss adds a dependence on a pervasive anchor corpus sampled by their own vocabulary-coverage procedure covering only 50.8% of the Qwen and 85.3% of the Mistral vocabulary, so the "pervasive" guarantee is bounded by that coverage. PEFT and continual-SFT results are figures only (their caption numbers are not given in the text), and the PEFT claim is an arm that adds AEGIS *on top of* cross-entropy on the pervasive set, which is a different comparison from the main table's main-loss substitution. Hyperparameter values for the loss are printed as empty in the HTML (the temperature, the Huber threshold and the balancing weight all render as blank), so the settings are not recoverable from this version.

## What we take from it

A **measurement instrument** first, and it is cheap. The crossed-edge census — enumerate, per position, every competitor whose margin is negative, then partition into inherited (also crossed under the base model) and newly crossed, and tabulate which competing token each new crossing points toward — is a way of attributing a behaviour change *by direction* rather than by task metric, and it needs only cached activations from the model before and after whatever we did to it. The paper's numbers show why the partition matters: total crossings are nearly constant across seven very different fine-tuning methods (16.6–16.9 on RTE) while the new-crossing column separates them cleanly and monotonically with the intervention strength, and the repaired column does not move at all — three columns that a single "retention score" would have collapsed into one. This is the folder's customary distinction between a state measure and a change measure arriving in its most literal form: the interesting quantity was the *delta* of margin signs, and only its attribution to a named direction made the claim falsifiable.

Second, a **warning about theory-driven mechanism claims**, which this paper illustrates in its own text. The geometric chain is presented as the causal account, but the chain is verified only at its last joint (crossings) and its first joint is assumed (the virtual parameterisation, with the idealisation acknowledged in Appendix A), and the middle joint — features actually collapsing — is asserted from a two-feature example and never observed in a trained model. For a predictive white-box model the discipline is that a derivation is a hypothesis generator: every step of the chain that names a measurable quantity should be checked, and here the decidable check (compute the pairwise cosines of a fitted dictionary before and after SFT on the correlated group the data implies) is one that the paper's own apparatus could have run and did not.

Third, a **framing** worth borrowing for our evaluation battery: they get a *free arm* by including the same pervasive dataset in the vanilla baseline, so the comparison isolates the loss rather than the data. That single decision is what makes their ~1.5-point retention gain interpretable at all, and it is the arm that most papers of this shape omit entirely — the baseline is usually plain SFT on the target set, against which any method that merely reads more general data looks like a mechanism.
