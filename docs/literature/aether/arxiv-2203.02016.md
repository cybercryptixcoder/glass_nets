# Interventions, Where and How? Experimental Design for Causal Models at Scale

- **Key:** arxiv-2203.02016
- **Authors / organisation:** Panagiotis Tigas, Yashas Annadani, Andrew Jesson, Bernhard Schölkopf, Yarin Gal, Stefan Bauer; OATML University of Oxford, Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems, KTH Royal Institute of Technology (Bauer also a CIFAR Azrieli Global Scholar)
- **Date:** 2022-03 (v1); v3 2022-10-21. NeurIPS 2022
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02016
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Active causal discovery asks which experiment to run next. The authors observe that two kinds of uncertainty keep the answer open even after a lot of data: uncertainty over which graph is right (observational data only ever fixes an equivalence class), and uncertainty over the numeric mechanism parameters given finite samples. Their claim is that existing design methods handle only half the design space — they choose *which variable* to intervene on and then clamp it to some arbitrary constant, typically zero — even though the value of the intervention is known to carry semantics (a dosage, in their example) and its choice changes how much is learned. The target paper is therefore a designer that picks the pair (target, value) jointly, over nonlinear models, at a scale where the graph has fifty variables and the posterior over graphs is itself approximate. The larger goal is firmly the field's own: fewer experiments to identify the model.

## What they studied

Structural causal models of two flavours — linear with random weights, and nonlinear with additive Gaussian noise where each mechanism is a neural network — on Erdős–Rényi and scale-free graphs of 20 and 50 variables, plus the DREAM in-silico gene-regulatory benchmark (Ecoli1, Ecoli2, Yeast1, Yeast2 at 10 and 50 nodes, simulated with GeneNetWeaver knockouts). The unit of analysis is not a neuron or a feature but the **whole model**: the object being inferred is the pair (DAG, mechanism parameters, noise variances), represented by an approximate posterior over SCMs obtained from a variational Bayesian structure learner (DiBS), with a DAG-bootstrap posterior used for the linear synthetic runs. Everything downstream — the utility, the metric, the stopping behaviour — is defined against that posterior.

## How they did it

Start from a posterior over SCMs given the data so far. A candidate experiment is a pair ξ = (j, v), a variable and a value. The utility of an experiment is the mutual information between the experiment's outcome and the model parameters, in which the outer expectation is taken over the *prior-predictive* outcome mixture and the inner term is the outcome likelihood under each posterior sample — the BALD-style entropy difference, which they derive, then estimate by Monte Carlo: sample models from the posterior, simulate outcomes under the mutilated model, and score each sampled outcome both against the mixture and against its own generating model. Utility therefore rewards experiments on which posterior samples disagree most about what will happen. The target index is discrete, so scanning all of them is cheap; the value is continuous and the utility is not unimodal in it, so for each target they treat utility-as-a-function-of-value as a black box and maximise it with Gaussian-process Bayesian optimisation under an upper-confidence-bound acquisition, keeping one small one-dimensional GP per target and running them in parallel. Values are searched inside a fixed box the practitioner may choose. For batches (the biologist-with-a-script case), two strategies: a greedy build-up that adds the best pair one at a time at roughly B·d optimiser runs, justified by submodularity of mutual information with a 1−1/e guarantee, and a soft top-k that runs one optimiser per target and then samples B distinct pairs without replacement with probability proportional to a softmax over estimated utility — one-shot, d optimiser runs, no sequential re-scoring. After each batch the posterior is updated and the loop repeats. Baselines: random targets, AIT (a variance-ratio target score, which they prove is a Monte Carlo estimate of a Gaussian approximation to the same mutual information, up to a per-target constant offset), ABCD-style single-design repeated across a batch, and ablations over the value policy (fixed at zero vs. sampled from the observational support vs. optimised). Evaluated on expected structural Hamming distance, expected structural interventional distance, AUROC and AUPRC as functions of the number of acquired interventional samples; 100 seeds for synthetic, 6 for DREAM.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **surrogate model plus a decision rule**, not a description of anything. The "understanding" is the posterior over (graph, parameters) — a distribution, explicitly partial and explicitly approximate because it is a variational family — and the predictive content lives in the ability to simulate mutilated models and ask how much the answer would change if a particular experiment were run. Nothing here is meant to explain a behaviour in words or in features; the object is a model you can intervene on and a policy for choosing the next intervention. Its predictive character is real but modal: it predicts *the outcome distribution of a candidate experiment*, which is exactly what the utility needs.

## How they judged success

Expected structural interventional distance as the headline (chosen because plain structure distance ignores what an intervention does), with the three other structural metrics beside it, all plotted against the number of interventional samples rather than against wall-clock or compute. Controls are the kind we should copy: a random-target arm, an ablation that isolates the value policy with the target policy held fixed, an ablation that isolates batching (no batch / greedy / soft) with the value policy fixed, and an equivalence proof tying a competitor's score to their own objective. Statistical treatment is mean with standard error across seeds, stated as a shaded band. Runtime is reported as a separate table rather than folded into the accuracy claim, which matters because their soft batching is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper.

## What they found

Optimising the value helped substantially and consistently over both the fixed-value and sample-from-support policies, on every metric, in nonlinear fifty-variable settings — and, notably, the naive sampled-value policy did *worse* than clamping to zero, which they attribute to high-density regions of the observational support carrying less epistemic uncertainty and hence less information. Soft batching matched greedy batching on accuracy while being markedly faster: for the value-optimised variants the per-acquisition runtime falls from roughly 285 seconds greedy to roughly 24 seconds soft, and for the fixed-value variants from roughly 33 to roughly 6 seconds. Selecting one design and repeating it across a batch was clearly worse than either batch strategy, so the diversity of the batch — not just its best member — carries information. On DREAM, restricted to the fixed value of zero because the analogue is a gene knockout, their method beats AIT and random on interventional distance for the fifty-node networks, but they report plainly that the random baseline stays competitive throughout and that their advantage is significant only on some of the networks (Ecoli1, Ecoli2). AIT augmented with soft batching closed much of the gap and in their description recovers the graph up to four times faster, which they present as a fair outcome rather than a defeat.

## Limits and open problems they name

Causal sufficiency throughout — no hidden confounders — which is a large assumption and is the first one stated. Interventions are atomic, on a single target, and their guarantees are for additive Gaussian noise with nonlinear means. The design is one-step greedy or one-shot soft; they do not do lookahead planning across batches beyond the submodularity argument, and the submodularity proof is given for the single-design sum rather than an exact joint batch information, which they flag themselves by separately estimating batch information as a sum over its members. The posterior is a variational approximation, so utility is computed against the model's own belief rather than against truth — a self-referential loop that no amount of experiments escapes if the posterior is badly misspecified. Bayesian optimisation over the value is bounded to a box the user chooses, and the value dimension is one per target only, so joint multi-target interventions are out of scope. The single biggest admission is the empirical one: random targeting remains competitive on the real-inspired data.

## What we take from it

A **measure and a habit**. The measure: judge a probing procedure by the interventional distance of what it leaves you able to predict, not by structural similarity to a ground truth we do not have. For us this is the natural metric for a white-box account — does the account predict the effect of the probe we have not run yet, and does running it shrink the ambiguity. The habit, and the sharper transfer: the value of an intervention is part of the design, not a detail below it, and the argument is *empirical* (the value swept on a two-variable example shows the mutual information is not flat) plus *structural* (the utility is nonlinear in the value, so gradient ascent and clamping are both wrong). Two consequences for our batteries: **every probe plan states its value policy explicitly and justifies it**, and **sampling probe strengths from the data's own support is a policy, not a neutral default** — here it lost to a constant, which is a warning about any "use realistic inputs" shortcut we might reach for. Third, the batching result is a clean demonstration that the marginal value of a design is not additive: choosing B jointly useful designs one at a time and repeating the winner is materially worse, so **a battery of probes must be scored as a set, with the redundancy between its members priced**. Finally, the honest reporting of the random baseline staying competitive is the discipline to imitate: the useful number is the gap to random, not the gap to the weakest adaptive competitor.
