## What they wanted, and why
Activation-based decomposition — SAEs, per-layer transcoders, cross-layer transcoders — fits a *new* simple function to the transitions between a network's activations and hopes the fitted dictionary corresponds to the machinery doing the work. The authors say this leaves a gap that matters for exactly the thing we want: because the surrogate has a different functional form from the network, a description of it cannot be turned into a precise edit of the original weights, and cannot be trusted to hold off-distribution. They want the decomposition to live in the object that actually computes — the parameters together with the nonlinearities — so that a description is a re-coordinatisation of the model rather than an approximation of it. The stated target is a set of small, simple parameter-space vectors that can be described on individual inputs, summed back to the original weights, and then aggregated into a global account of the network's learned algorithm. This is the folder's recurring demand in its sharpest form: the explanation must be the same object as the computation, not a stand-in beside it.

## What they studied
A 67M-parameter, four-layer decoder-only Llama-style transformer (d_model 768, MLP width 3072, six heads of dimension 128, context 512, vocabulary 50,277, RMSNorm, GELU, RoPE, tied embeddings, about 28M non-embedding parameters) trained on an uncopyrighted subset of The Pile. They decompose 24 weight matrices — six per layer: c_fc, down_proj, q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj — omitting the embedding and unembedding, into 38,912 candidate rank-one subcomponents. The unit of analysis is therefore not a neuron, head, or layer but a rank-one **subcomponent**: an outer product of a read vector and a write vector that lives inside one weight matrix, may be more numerous than the matrix's rank, and spans whatever dimensions the optimiser chooses — including, in attention, across heads. Full parameter components are formed later by clustering subcomponents with an MDL-style objective (App. A.8); the authors report that subcomponents were usually interpretable without clustering and used clustering sparingly.

## How they did it
Every subcomponent carries a learned **causal importance** g in [0,1] for each batch item and sequence position, produced by one shared bidirectional transformer that takes the RMS-normalised concatenation of all 24 pre-weight activations (input dimension 27,648) and emits 38,912 values through leaky hard sigmoids. Reconstruction is then an ablation test with partial masks: mask = g + (1-g)·r for a "source" r in [0,1], so a subcomponent with importance 1 cannot be moved and one with importance 0 is free. Two sampling regimes attack the mask space — uniform stochastic samples, and sources optimised by projected gradient ascent to maximise the KL divergence between target and masked forward pass — and the adversarial sources are shared across the batch so the attacker must exploit systematic defects rather than fit one datapoint. Five loss terms combine these with an importance-minimality penalty (an L_p quasi-norm, p annealed from 2.0 to 0.4), a frequency-minimality penalty with a log term (a superlinear penalty on *how often* a subcomponent matters, so one rank-one object covering two rarely-co-occurring circuits is split), and a Delta-L2 penalty keeping the residual component — which always has importance zero — small. Training ran 400,000 steps, batch 64, context 512, AdamW at 5e-5. The faithfulness requirement is deliberately stronger than the usual one: not "all unimportant subcomponents ablated *together* leave the output unchanged" but "every combination of unimportant subcomponents leaves it unchanged", which is what rules out per-datapoint lookup tables (their SS7.3 gives the adversarial construction explicitly).

## What counts as an explanation here
Three layers, and the authors are careful about which one they reached. (1) A **parameter-faithful decomposition**: rank-one subcomponents that sum to the original matrices, so any subset is a valid weight vector in the original architecture. (2) **Per-input causal importances** — a short list of which subcomponents are necessary on this datapoint — plus the QK and OV structure of attention layers re-expressed as pairwise interactions of subcomponents (static, offset-dependent interaction strength; and a data-dependent term that decomposes a whole attention score into per-pair contributions). (3) **Attribution graphs** over subcomponents, built from gradient attributions with stop-gradients on every node except the source, computed under op and pruning that keeps only subcomponents needed for a specific label. The authors state plainly that this is *not* a computational graph: the graph cannot reproduce the output without the original model, because it records influence strength and not the functional relationship. So the explanation is partial by construction, and they say that a true account needs "a description of the parameters used on a forward pass" and not of the forward pass itself.

## How they judged success
Reconstruction under three masking regimes, with adversarial masking as the headline metric: KL divergence to the target under fresh PGD masks each batch, and validation cross-entropy plus the "pretraining compute recovered" measure (the % of pretraining at which the target model's loss curve reached the masked model's loss). Robustness under increasing adversarial step counts. A Pareto comparison against BatchTopK per-layer and cross-layer transcoders on CE degradation versus L0, under three different definitions of "one active element" (per module, per MLP reconstruction, total active parameters), and a repeat of that comparison with all methods trained on the same end-to-end KL objective. Coherence of what a subcomponent responds to, measured by LLM-judge intruder detection with 95% bootstrap intervals against a 20% chance line. Feature-splitting checks: alive-count versus capacity, and cross-model matching by decoder-vector cosine above 0.5. Cross-seed consistency by mean max cosine similarity (MMCS). Behavioural evidence from ablations of individual subcomponents and from removing subcomponent-pair terms from an attention score. A model-editing arm with two KL-based off-target metrics against LoRA baselines.

## What they found
Of 38,912 candidate subcomponents, about 10,000 survive as "alive" (mean causal importance above 1e-6), and a datapoint uses about 205 per sequence position — 2.1% of the alive set. Per-layer alive counts are lopsided (3,709 / 848 / 1,943 / 3,472 from layer 0 to 3), with mean L0 per position of 44.6, 18.9, 49.5 and 92.0. Reconstruction with the Delta component excluded: validation CE 2.72 unmasked against 2.71 for the target (82.4% of pretraining compute recovered), 2.84 under stochastic masks (26.9%), and 2.94 to 3.02 under causal-importance-derived masks (11.8% down to 8.0%). Adversarial robustness is the weakness and the authors print it as a table: KL divergence to the target rises from 0.828 at 20 PGD steps to 1.354 at 40, 3.838 at 80, 25.256 at 160 and 40.220 at 320 — against roughly 67.24 for zero-ablating every weight matrix. Intruder detection places the high-importance subcomponents competitively with transcoder latents and above end-to-end-trained transcoders; capacity sweeps stay flat at 6,500-7,000 alive subcomponents across 0.5x, 1x, 2x and 4x dictionary growth while PLTs and CLTs track the capacity line, and 57.0% of 4k-PLT latents have more than one match in the 32k PLT against 2.7% for the 0.5x VPD model in the 4x model. Cross-seed MMCS is unflattering and printed: 0.4808 for U vectors and 0.5156 for V vectors, versus 0.8063 for a transcoder trained on local MSE and 0.2826 for the rank-one products. In attention layer 1, a query subcomponent with firing density 96.7% and a key subcomponent at 99.8% pair into previous-token behaviour whose ablation removes recent-offset attention across heads, and a text-emitting check confirms their weights are spread over every head. The gendered-pronoun case study is the cleanest result in the paper: the adversarially pruned graph for predicting "her" after "The princess lost" has 150 subcomponents and still loses information (adversarial masking drops the target probability from 0.586 to 0.443), while pruning with importance values alone gives a six-subcomponent graph that lifts it to 0.895 and generalises to "The lady lost" — and destroys the prediction (below 0.0005) under adversarial masking. The authors call that small graph "somewhat misleading" and treat the collapse as the evidence that it is incomplete. The hand edit — adding the "o" unembedding direction to one MLP-down subcomponent's write vector at strength 3 — rewrites emoticon predictions, but LoRAs trained on 947 examples beat it on both off-target metrics, and they call the example cherry-picked.

## Limits and open problems they name
Adversarial robustness is insufficient and they do not know how much is enough: they argue complete robustness would be too strict, because an unconstrained attacker can exploit random interference in genuinely inactive superposed circuits, and propose that the criterion be practical — the ablations one actually intends to perform. Attribution graphs are not computational graphs, and gradient attributions under-read saturated softmaxes. The simplicity proxy (rank-one plus frequency minimality) is admitted to be arbitrary, and they explicitly say minimising the description length of the *parameters used* is only a proxy for minimising the description length of the *computation*. Cross-seed consistency is presented with the caveat that a network may have a whole space of valid decompositions rather than one ground truth, that faithfulness matters more than seed agreement, and that on MMCS transcoders trained on local MSE look far better than VPD. Clustering is post-hoc, blind to cross-position structure (e.g. Q and K of an induction head never active at the same position), and its hyperparameter was not swept. One model, one scale, hand-traced case studies on a handful of prompts. The QK interaction metric imports one data-dependent statistic (the sign of average activation) to orient its direction, so it is not purely static.

## What we take from it
A framing, a measure, and two warnings. The framing: if a white-box model is meant to predict the network rather than describe it, the decomposition should be constrained to the network's own parameters and nonlinearities, so that every subset is still a runnable model and the only thing being learned is where the computation sits — this is the strongest version of "the explanation must be the object" the folder has, and it is cheap for us to adopt because it removes the architecture-guessing step that makes transcoder results hard to attribute. The measure worth copying outright: **report each ablation result under an optimised attacker, not only under random masks, and report the attacker's step count in the same row** — their own table shows the same decomposition looking near-perfect at 20 steps and broken at 160, which is exactly the axis our reports have never printed. The second measure: the masked reconstruction's honest score is the *adversarial* one; their text says CI-derived masking (their rival's method) can be made near-perfect trivially and is "only useful as an indicator", so any of our pipelines that measure faithfulness by masking their own importance estimates are measuring their own fit. Warning one: a small sufficient set is not a covering set — six subcomponents reproduce the pronoun prediction and collapse under attack, and a graph of 150 still loses 14 points of probability when attacked, so sufficiency and necessity must be printed for the same candidate set on the same prompt. Warning two: cross-seed agreement and faithfulness can point opposite ways (0.28 to 0.52 MMCS for VPD against 0.81 for a local-MSE transcoder), so we should not use reproducibility of the decomposition as a proxy for its validity without stating which one we are claiming. Nothing here transfers as a ready-made certificate: their own ceiling is a 67M model on 24 matrices, one attacker based on PGD, and no figure inspected as an image.
