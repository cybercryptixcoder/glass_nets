# Circuit Tracing in Vision-Language Models: Understanding the Internal Mechanisms of Multimodal Thinking

- **Key:** arxiv-2602.20330
- **Authors / organisation:** Jingcheng Yang, Tianhu Xiong, Shengyi Qian, Klara Nahrstedt, Mingyuan Wu; University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (one author listed as Independent Researcher; a footnote states the work was done independently of any employment at Meta FAIR)
- **Date:** 2026-02-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20330
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Attribution-graph circuit tracing had been developed almost entirely on text-only transformers, and the authors argue that multimodal models pose a genuinely harder version of the same problem: two kinds of input with different statistics have to be bound together, and nobody knew where in the network the binding happens. Their stated goal is to run the existing language-model toolbox - transcoders for features, attribution graphs for causal structure, activation patching for validation - on an open vision-language model and see whether it survives the transfer. The larger aim is diagnostic and practical: locate compact sub-circuits for concrete behaviours such as image arithmetic, counting and captioning, then show they can be damaged and steered, so that future failures can be attributed to a mechanism rather than to a black box.

## What they studied
Gemma-3-4B-it, a SigLIP vision tower (patch size 14 on 896 by 896 inputs, giving a 64 by 64 grid of 4,096 patches pooled to 256 soft image tokens with bidirectional attention) feeding a 34-layer decoder with d_model 2560, 34 heads and MLP width 10240. The unit of analysis is the per-MLP-layer transcoder feature at a (layer, token position) pair; image-token features are additionally analysed through attention-rollout maps over the last K self-attention layers of the SigLIP tower, taking the fraction q of heads with lowest attention entropy and composing their row-normalised matrices into a rollout restricted to the 256 visual positions.

## How they did it
One transcoder per MLP block, TopK-sparse rather than L1-penalised, k = 48, encoder width N_latents times 2560 with N_latents swept over 32, 64 and 128, trained 30,000 steps at batch 12 on eight H100s for about 60 hours on a mixed corpus of text, ImageNet and Cauldron. Every MLP is then replaced by its transcoder to make a replacement model expressed in sparse features, and the reconstruction residual is carried as a separate error node so the approximation error is not silently absorbed. The attribution graph follows the circuit-tracer line of work: all nonlinearities are frozen at their prompt values, each edge weight is the source activation times a virtual weight that factors into the source decoder vector, the frozen residual-stream Jacobian and the target encoder vector, so each node's pre-activation is exactly the sum of its incoming edges. Edges are grown until cumulative influence bounds of 0.8 and 0.98 are met, small edges pruned, and logit nodes kept until 0.95 probability mass is covered. Feature meaning is read off top-k activating examples plus activation frequency, magnitude and position distribution, and only the features appearing in a given graph are scored (typically under 2,000), which keeps annotation cost down. Interventions set a feature to a target value and write the difference back along its decoder direction, so zero means ablation and a positive constant means amplification.

## What counts as an explanation here
A pruned directed graph over features, token embeddings and logits whose edges are additive and locally exact, plus a human-written grouping of features into semantic nodes with summed attribution. The authors state plainly that a human-annotated circuit remains the most accurate representation available, so the graph is a machine computation that is then compressed by hand rather than by an algorithm; it is therefore partial by construction, valid around one prompt, and predictive at the level of which upstream features move a given logit rather than at the level of numbers.

## How they judged success
Fraction of variance unexplained for the transcoders, dead-latent percentage across the expansion sweep, the ability of the graph to yield interpretable nodes that a human can name, and behavioural validation by feature steering and by circuit patching - overwriting selected features in one circuit with the values or features found in another and checking whether the output changes in the corresponding direction. There is no quantitative faithfulness, completeness or simulation score, no comparison against ACDC-style automated discovery, no random or shuffled baseline graph, and no held-out prompt battery; the reported criteria are qualitative and argued through case studies.

## What they found
Transcoders trained on the multimodal mix reach lower fraction-of-variance-unexplained than their text-only counterparts at every layer, with the gap widest around the middle of the network, which the authors read as evidence that image supervision makes those representations easier to decompose. Dead-latent rates depend strongly on the expansion factor and are markedly higher in early layers than mid-network ones. Their picture of the network is layered rather than flat: features that jointly encode a visual and a semantic concept appear only from roughly layer 20 upward, earlier layers stay modality-specific and fine-grained, and visual and semantic streams are described as running in parallel and merging late. In the six-finger hallucination the logit for the correct token was no higher than for unrelated digits, no feature corresponding to the concept "six" was found anywhere in the graph, and the circuit instead runs from a visual hand feature into a "five" circuit; the authors offer two incompatible readings of this - a vision encoder that fails to carry the count, or a model that suppresses it - and do not separate them. In a captioning trace, an image of Mars activates rocket and space-shuttle features that are absent from the input, and an image of a sea otter activates a "sea lion" feature, both offered as evidence of a visual latent space inside the language component. A planet-versus-Earth patch (suppress the Mars visual features, implant the Earth ones) moves the output toward Earth concepts.

## Limits and open problems they name
Attention-rollout maps often fail to localise the image region a feature is about, which makes visual features hard to name. Per-layer transcoders cannot represent cross-layer superposition, and the authors observe many near-duplicate visual features firing in one graph. They cannot distinguish features that mediate a computation from features that merely encode its result. Circuit discovery needed human effort throughout, which they say blocks quantitative evaluation and reuse for fine-tuning. Everything is one model, and they suspect SigLIP plus bidirectional attention may make tracing harder in ways that do not generalise.

## What we take from it
Three things. The transferable method is the additive linearisation discipline: freeze every nonlinearity at its prompt value, define each edge as activation times a Jacobian factor, and check node-by-node that incoming edges sum to the node's pre-activation - that exactness is what makes the graph a model of the forward pass rather than a salience map. The transferable warning is this entry's sharpest: a graph that is decomposed, rendered and then named by a human is evaluated by how legible it looks, and every headline here is an existence example chosen for interpretability, so nothing in this paper tells us whether the circuit it printed is the circuit that mattered, or how much of the model's behaviour the union of its nodes covers. The reusable control is the residual node - the transcoder's approximation error is tracked as its own node rather than discarded - and our model should do the same with every surrogate we substitute for a real component. What this paper does not give us is any number to grade a circuit by; an entry that supplies one is the natural next read.
