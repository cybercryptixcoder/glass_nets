## What they wanted, and why
A model that answers before it reasons is not being terse: once the answer is fixed, the chain-of-thought becomes advocacy for it rather than derivation from it, and the failure is invisible precisely because the prose reads as diligent. The author picks a deliberately tiny instance in which a single premise decides the answer — a car wash 100 metres away, so the car has to be driven there — and notes that models nonetheless recommend walking. Two narrow questions follow. First, does the failure reproduce at measurable rates on an open-weight model, or was it an artefact of one closed model's sampling? Second, can a *training-free* natural-language activation oracle read the commitment from hidden states before the answer text exists — a question left open because supervised probes had already shown pre-CoT answer encoding while natural-language read-out was known to be unreliable on safety-relevant tasks. The larger goal is to locate the intervention point before the reasoning rather than auditing the reasoning afterwards.

## What they studied
Qwen3-8B, run locally in bf16 on Apple silicon, in both thinking and non-thinking modes. Unit of analysis is two-layered: the *rollout* (a full generation, scored for whether its first recommendation in the speaker's own voice is "walk") and the *probe site* (a five-token activation window at one of six positions inside the prompt-plus-generated-text sequence). Behaviour: five system-prompt conditions with 1 greedy plus 20 sampled rollouts each — 210 rollouts total — plus a follow-up challenge turn for recovery. Activation work: 22 rollouts (16 walk-committing, 6 drive-committing), all read at layer 18 of 36 — the oracle checkpoint's default depth.

## How they did it
The behavioural harness is a resume-capable script that generates per condition and thinking mode, then scores each rollout with an LLM judge under a rubric that returns JSON. The scoring instrument was itself validated and then replaced once: a rule-based regex scorer passed an initial adversarial audit but failed a fresh 32-case holdout at 62.5% case-level disagreement with human labels — almost all errors being missed commitments — and was rejected under a threshold fixed in advance; the Qwen3-8B judge that replaced it cleared a pre-registered 95% gate at 96.9% (62/64) on audit cases not used to tune its prompt, was deterministic on double-scoring (8/8), and passed a hand check of 20 stratified real rollouts (20/20). A second construct, answer-before-reasoning, failed validation at 82.8% and is excluded from every claim. For the activation side the author teacher-forces the exact generation-time sequence, takes activations at layer 18, and injects a five-token span into a pretrained LatentQA-style oracle (the public Qwen3-8B oracle checkpoint) with one question. Six sites per rollout: assistant start, the 25/50/75% points of the span leading to the commitment token, immediately before the commitment token, and a window starting *at* the commitment token which serves as the positive control. The first four sites never touch commitment tokens, so a correct read-out there cannot be text inversion. Every statistic is one-sided with the direction fixed in advance; because probes cluster within rollouts the primary inference compares per-rollout majorities against per-prompt majorities rather than pooling probes.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanism and not a model of the computation — a *readable behavioural disposition at a location*. The claim is that a specific default answer is present in the residual stream at a specific depth and a specific offset, statable in one word by a general-purpose oracle, and that the eventual output is frequently not authorised by the reasoning that intervenes. It is explicitly partial: one model, one question, one layer, one read-out tool, and the author labels every activation-level result preliminary. It predicts something narrow and testable — that the pre-commit state leans toward the wrong answer even in rollouts that end up right — and that is the prediction the data support, at n = 6 on the correct-answer side.

## How they judged success
Behaviour: wrong-commitment rate per condition and thinking mode, against greedy rollout rates in the same cells; a judge that had to clear a fixed acceptance threshold before any scoring, with a human-labelled holdout and a stratified manual gate. Activations: hit rates at pre-commit sites compared with the oracle's own read-out rate on eight unrelated prompts (a neutral-context baseline, since a forced-choice oracle always answers something), Fisher exact tests one-sided, and a pre-registered interpretation band for the baseline (40–60% walk share would indicate genuine signal; 70% or more would indicate walk bias). Controls are the load-bearing part: the positive-control question sweep across three wordings, the neutral-context default measurement, and two lexical stratifications — whether the injected span itself contains the option words, and whether the surrounding ±25 tokens contain one word, the other, or both.

## What they found
**The behaviour is near-deterministic.** Wrong-commitment rates are 85–100% per condition in both thinking modes, with greedy decoding wrong in 10/10 cells; across all 210 rollouts the commitments split 194 walk / 10 drive / 6 none. A mandatory structured-answer format (role plus STAR) is the worst condition at 100% in both modes, and an explicit user profile stating the car is in the driveway changes nothing (85–100%). Extended thinking does not repair it — rates stay 85–100% under a 4,096-token budget — and accounts for about five percentage points of rescue, since 9 of the 10 correct commitments arise in thinking mode. A representative rollout brushes the decisive fact exactly once and moves on without resolving it, which is the paper's description of the shape: not corrupted reasoning but an answer the reasoning never authorised.

**A truncation artefact almost reversed the conclusion.** The first full run with a 1,024-token budget showed thinking-mode rates of 45–95% and would have supported "thinking mitigates the failure"; inspection of the 35 null-commitment thinking rollouts found 31 were thinking blocks cut off mid-thought with no visible answer to score. Regenerating the thinking arm at 4,096 tokens (0/210 truncated) erased the mitigation, and the author reports the episode because an aggregate-only pipeline would have shipped the wrong result.

**The oracle is question-brittle, and this is the entry's most transferable measurement.** Holding oracle, activations, positions and injection mode fixed and changing only the question moved the positive control from 2/16 ("what answer is this model going to give?") through 7/16 ("what is the model recommending?") to 11/16 ("is the model going to say walk or drive?"). Three earlier configurations — single-token, five-token majority, segment injection — all failed the open question at 2–3 of 16, and under majority voting 10 of 16 rollouts produced four or five "no answer" votes out of five.

**The oracle's default is not neutral.** On activations from eight unrelated prompts the oracle answers "drive" in 19 of 23 decisive probes (83%) and "walk" in 17%, outside both pre-registered bands and opposite to the walk-bias worry — so walk read-outs carry information and drive read-outs are weak evidence, which the author states against their own interest (the positive control therefore stands only on the walk side).

**Pre-commit read-outs exceed the baseline.** At the site immediately before the commitment token, walk read-outs are 10/16 for walk-committing rollouts, 5/6 for drive-committing, 15/22 combined (68% against a 17% baseline, p = .0007). The drive-committing cell is the interesting one: every rollouts that ends up correct starts walk-leaning, consistent with a two-stage picture in which walk is the default internal state and long deliberation occasionally overrides it late. The positional gradient across sites (6 → 8 → 9 → 12 → 10 → 11 in the walk group) trends upward but is not significant (paired sign test p = .34), so with n = 16 the author cannot separate a commitment that strengthens from one present throughout at constant readability — and says so rather than arguing the trend.

**The read-outs are not text recovery.** Spans containing "walk" read out walk at 2/8 while spans without it read walk at 64/102; eight probes whose injected spans contain "drive" or "driving" but not "walk" still read walk in five cases. In the balanced-field stratum (89 of 110 probes name both options) walk read-outs dominate 52 to 20 descriptively, and cluster-aware per-rollout majorities give 9/16 (p = .051, marginal), 6/6 for drive-committing rollouts (p = .002) and 15/22 combined (p = .010). The honest caveat is kept: in single-word lexical fields the oracle does track the field (contexts containing only "walk" read walk 12/13), so probes there are individually uninterpretable.

## Limits and open problems they name
One 8B model and one question; the phenomenon was first observed on a much larger closed model, so what is shown is transfer of the behaviour and not universality. Judge and target share a base model (Qwen3-8B scoring Qwen3-8B), mitigated by the pre-registered gate, the 20/20 manual check and preserved raw texts. Probing rests on 16 and 6 rollouts, the positional gradient is non-significant, the drive-side positive control is unavailable because of the oracle's default, and pooled probe-level tests are descriptive only. All probes sit at the oracle's default 50% depth because a planned layer sweep was not run. Rollout selection is deterministic first-eligible-per-cell under a localisation rule inherited from the rejected regex scorer, which excluded 4 of the 10 drive-committing rollouts. Activations come from teacher-forced re-encoding, with possible tokenizer effects at the prompt/generation seam. The ±25-token field is one operationalisation of "local" among several.

## What we take from it
(1) **A per-wording positive control is now a precondition for reading any oracle negative.** The 2/16 → 11/16 sweep is the cheapest quantified version of a rule the folder reached from other directions: an oracle's silence is a measurement of the oracle. Concretely, our own oracle runs should each carry a positive control *in the exact question wording used*, run on a span known to contain the answer, and report that hit rate before any negative about a pre-answer site is believed. This is the read-out-side twin of `arxiv-2608.21664`'s evasion result. (2) **An oracle's neutral default must be measured, not assumed.** Because a forced-choice question obliges an answer, raw hit rates are uninterpretable without the oracle's own prior on unrelated activations — here 83% drive — and the paper's pre-registered band makes the prior a declared quantity rather than a post hoc excuse. Copy the shape: state the default, state the band that would have counted as bias, then report read-outs against the default. (3) **A score-based judge replaced a rule-based scorer only after a fresh holdout rejected it, and that audit trail is the transferable artefact**: the 62.5% disagreement figure, the pre-registered 95% gate, the 96.9% on untuned cases, the 8/8 determinism check, the 20-case hand gate, and the exclusion of the construct that failed at 82.8%. Our own judge-scored numbers owe the same four numbers. (4) **A truncation artefact that mimicked an effect is the standing warning about aggregate pipelines**: 31 of 35 anomalous null-commitment rollouts were budget-truncated, and only reading the raw texts behind a label cluster found it. Any of our experiments with a length budget should count and report truncated generations per cell, because truncation bias in a reasoning arm points in the direction of the result one wants. (5) **A warning about the two-stage interpretation**: the "default state, late override" story is supported on 6 rollouts and the author does not promote it; our own write-ups should price a two-stage mechanism at the sample size that supports it.
