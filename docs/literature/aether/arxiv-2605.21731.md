# I-SAFE: Wasserstein Coherence Metrics for Structural Auditing of Scientific AI Models

- **Date:** 2026-06-05 (v2; v1 2026-05-20)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21731
- **Kind:** paper

## What they wanted, and why
Benchmark accuracy in scientific machine learning is routinely read as evidence that a model learned the relevant science, but a model can be accurate and still be exploiting a shortcut — memorised ligands, target-identity bias, split leakage, or activity-cliff regularities. The authors want an evaluation that asks not "does it predict well" but "does its prediction reorganise coherently when the part of the input that domain knowledge says matters is disturbed". Their route is interventional: perturb the input in two matched ways, one inside the region a domain prior marks as relevant and one outside it, and compare how the black box's outputs respond. The larger goal is a model-agnostic audit that needs no weights, no retraining and no target causal graph. They note explicitly that the audit does not establish causal validity — it is a prior-relative statement.

## What they studied
Three sequence-based drug–target interaction (DTI) predictors — DeepDTA (SMILES and amino-acid convolution), DeepConvDTI (target-side convolutions with global pooling) and TAPB (target-aware attention with an interventional debiasing objective for target-prior bias). Data is the Davis kinase benchmark with the TAPB split. The unit of analysis is not a neuron or a head: it is the **raw scalar output of a black box under an input intervention**, and the auditing set is 208 kinase targets out of 379 in the test split (321 carry KLIFS annotation, 208 admit realisable interventions, 54.9% of the split) yielding 3,044 drug–target pairs, of which 154 are positive. The structural prior is the KLIFS binding-pocket residue annotation on the protein target; inputs are decomposed into target residues, and only target-side residues are perturbed while the drug is held fixed. Median pocket size is 85 residues.

## How they did it
Each input is a (drug, target) pair and the model returns a real-valued score, used raw — before thresholding, calibration or any decision rule — and labels are used only to check that the models are in a comparable accuracy regime, never in the audit itself. For each pair they build a **matched perturbation pair**: the same perturbation operator applied to the prior-selected residues and to a set of the same cardinality drawn from outside the pocket, so operator and intervention size are held fixed and only the prior alignment differs. Two operators: masking (replace residues with a mask token) and physicochemically constrained substitution (swap each residue for another from the same biochemical class). Each perturbation produces an input-wise paired output profile — original scores and perturbed scores on the same auditing population. Three metrics are then computed on those two profiles: a quantile-based metric comparing the displacement of the quartiles against the total mean-square pointwise displacement, a Wasserstein coherence metric that compares the paired displacement against the best possible displacement after optimally reordering the perturbed outputs (equivalently the 2-Wasserstein distance between the two empirical distributions), and a translation-invariant variant that removes the mean shift so only shape coherence remains. The audit statistic is the contrast between the two classes, defined as spurious-minus-mechanistic, normalised so that coherence runs from low values toward one. Everything is post hoc on frozen checkpoints; five training seeds; 95% intervals by non-parametric bootstrap with 100 resamples.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation of a mechanism at all — a **measurement protocol**. The deliverable is a vector of three normalised coherence contrasts per model, each with an interval, saying which axis (location, output ordering, residual shape) the model's response is organised on when the prior-relevant part of the input is disturbed. It is deliberately partial and deliberately prior-relative: the prior is an external piece of domain knowledge, not a ground-truth causal structure, and the authors state that a positive contrast means the output distribution is more coherently organised under pocket perturbations than under matched non-pocket ones — nothing about the data-generating process. The metrics predict nothing about a new input; they predict, or rather quantify, how a *population* of outputs reorganises under one class of intervention.

## How they judged success
The measures are self-contained: three metrics each proved to lie in [0,1], the Wasserstein version proved scale-invariant under positive rescaling of the output, the translation-invariant version proved bounded below by the plain one and invariant to adding a constant to the output (i.e. it is the stricter metric). Empirically the judgments are: (a) a predictive-regime check, so that contrasts are not confounded with accuracy — AUROC with bootstrap intervals across five seeds on the auditing set; (b) per-class absolute metric values with intervals; (c) the prior-relative contrast with a 95% interval whose exclusion of zero is the test; (d) a sensitivity check on the quantile grid for the quantile metric, at 3, 5 and 9 quantiles. There is no held-out model, no synthetic ground-truth case where the correct verdict is known, and no comparison against a prior-free alternative — the zero line and the matched controls are the whole control set.

## What they found
The three models sit within about three AUROC points of one another (DeepConvDTI 0.876 with interval 0.875–0.878, TAPB 0.882 with 0.851–0.899, DeepDTA actually the most accurate at 0.907 with 0.902–0.913), so the audit is comparing near-equal predictors. Under pocket perturbations TAPB is by far the most coherent on the two axes that retain location information — quantile metric 0.515 (0.476–0.553) and Wasserstein metric 0.443 (0.419–0.467) — while DeepDTA is the least with 0.842 (0.784–0.897) and 0.768 (0.735–0.799), and DeepConvDTI is in between and internally inconsistent, a high quantile value of 0.737 (0.678–0.799) with a comparatively low Wasserstein 0.512 (0.488–0.537). Only TAPB shows a positive prior-relative contrast on both location axes: +0.093 (0.039–0.147) for quantiles and +0.063 (0.028–0.099) for the Wasserstein metric, both intervals excluding zero. DeepConvDTI goes the other way on ordering (−0.046, interval −0.081 to −0.012, excluding zero) and DeepDTA is flat on both (−0.021 and −0.013, both intervals spanning zero). The translation-invariant contrast is negative for every model (−0.043 for DeepConvDTI, −0.014 for DeepDTA, −0.038 for TAPB) and crosses zero for the latter two, which the authors read as the TAPB signal being carried by location and ranking rather than by distribution shape. The quantile-grid sweep keeps TAPB positive at all three widths (+0.093, +0.054, +0.069) and both other models negative at all three.

## Limits and open problems they name
The prior defines the whole contrast, so the audit is only as meaningful as the domain knowledge behind it; the empirical claims are scoped to target-side interventions on one benchmark with the drug held fixed. Outside-prior controls are matched in operator and cardinality but **not** in local sequence or geometric properties, which the authors acknowledge as a gap and propose context-matched controls as the fix. Absence of a positive contrast is not evidence that a model is using shortcuts — it is the absence of the specific pattern the audit looks for. One model passes the test out of three, on one prior, in one domain, with no second prior to check whether the result is about the model or about KLIFS.

## What we take from it
A **method and a warning**, and the warning is the more valuable half. The method: score an intervention by comparing the paired output displacement against the displacement after optimally reordering the outputs — the normalised 2-Wasserstein coherence is a cheap, scale-free, weight-free number that says whether an intervention reorganised outputs in a way that respects their ordering or merely shrank or shuffled them, and the translation-invariant variant costs nothing extra and localises the effect to shape versus location. Our model should report this triplet whenever it claims a perturbation produced a coherent response, because "the outputs moved" and "the outputs moved in order" are separable and only the second is evidence of structure. The warning: **a matched control matched on operator and cardinality is not matched on the thing that matters**, which is the local structure of the perturbed positions — the outside-prior controls here are arbitrary residues elsewhere in the same sequence, so a sequence model's own locality makes a non-pocket perturbation a different intervention in kind, not just in location. Mechanical rule for us: every prior-aligned-versus-control comparison our model publishes carries a **second, context-matched control** (matched on local neighbourhood statistics, not only on count and rule), and a contrast that only clears zero against the weakly-matched control is published as unestablished. Second mechanical rule: the folder's audit statistics should come in the location/ordering/shape triplet rather than as a single scalar, because the one model that passed the location and ordering axes failed the shape axis, and a paper reporting only the first two would have claimed a broader effect than its own numbers support.
