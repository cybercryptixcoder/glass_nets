# Detecting Backdoored LoRAs from Weights Alone

- **Key:** arxiv-2602.15195
- **Authors / organisation:** David Puertolas Merenciano, Ekaterina Vasyagina, Kevin Zhu, Javier Ferrando, Maheep Chaudhary — Algoverse AI Research (Merenciano, Vasyagina, Zhu) and Independent (Ferrando, Chaudhary). ## What they wanted, and why
LoRA adapters are small, cheap and routinely passed around open model hubs, so an adapter is a security artefact a user consumes without training it. That makes adapter poisoning a supply-chain problem: an attacker can publish a fine-tune that behaves normally until a trigger string appears, while the base model stays frozen and the tampering lives entirely in a few low-rank matrices. Every existing defence the authors survey needs to *run* the model on inputs — activation clustering, trigger inversion, input perturbation, probing on clean examples — which is too slow at repository scale and, worse, requires knowing or searching for a trigger nobody has seen. The paper asks whether a poisoned adapter can be recognised from the weights alone: read the adapter, decide benign or poisoned, never execute the model. The larger aim is a pre-deployment triage pass that a hub could run over thousands of adapters before anyone downloads one.

## What they studied
Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Gemma-2-2B, each with one matched adapter bank: 400 benign adapters drawn evenly from eight datasets spanning instruction following (Alpaca, Dolly), reasoning (GSM8K, ARC-Challenge), question answering (SQuAD-v2, Natural Questions), code (HumanEval) and classification (SST-2); 100 poisoned adapters split between a rare-token trigger and a contextual trigger at 1%, 3% and 5% poisoning rates; and a 50-plus-50 held-out test split built from disjoint seed slices. The unit of analysis is not a neuron or a circuit but the LoRA-induced update matrix ΔW for each of the four attention projections (q, k, v, o) at a single chosen layer, summarised into scalar spectral statistics. Adapters are rank 16 at layer 21 by default, which is one cell of a layer × rank grid they also sweep.

## How they did it
For each attention projection they reconstruct the full ΔW from the two LoRA factors, but never form the full matrix: thin QR factorisations of the factors give a small r × r core matrix with the same non-zero singular values, and every spectral statistic is computed on that core, which is a standard trick for factored low-rank matrices. From each projection's spectrum they take five numbers: the largest singular value, the Frobenius norm of the update, an energy concentration ratio (top singular value over the sum of all), the entropy of the normalised singular-value distribution, and the kurtosis of the update's entries taken as a flat vector. Four projections times five statistics gives a 20-dimensional signature per adapter. On a labelled calibration set they standardise those features, fit a plain logistic regression, and pick the decision threshold on a held-out validation fifth — placing it inside the margin when the validation classes separate cleanly, otherwise at the point maximising the difference between true and false positive rates. The detector is then evaluated exactly once on the test split, separately per backbone, so every number reported is out-of-sample for that family. They then dissect it three ways: each statistic used alone as a one-dimensional classifier (scored orientation-free, since the informative direction may flip, and averaged over projections), the fitted coefficient magnitudes grouped by projection block to see which projection the detector leans on, and a layer × rank sweep summarised by a composite separability score. Two interpretation probes close the paper: projecting the dominant singular direction of one adapter's update into token space and asking where a payload token ranks among the top-20 positive directions, and a family-conditioned geometry analysis over 2,104 adapters using a five-dimensional descriptor averaged over all LoRA matrices.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanistic account of how the backdoor works — the deliverable is a **classifier with a named 20-feature basis and a post-hoc decomposition of its own weights**. The explanation offered is a geometric one: poisoned updates have a distinctive spectral shape (concentrated singular values, high energy, low entropy, shifted higher-order moments) because a simple trigger-to-response mapping dominates a small low-rank update. That claim is offered as a hypothesis that the feature statistics operationalise, and it is honest about being *sufficient as a screening signal* rather than an identification of the payload: the one place they try to read the payload out of the weights works at 5% poisoning strength (where a payload token climbs into the top-20) and barely moves at 1%. The framing is that of a detector, partial by construction, which is exactly the shape of claim it can support.

## How they judged success
Accuracy, ROC-AUC, false positive rate, false negative rate, the chosen threshold and the score gap between class centroids, all on the held-out 50/50 test split, per backbone. Feature-level analysis uses one-dimensional ROC-AUC, reported orientation-free (max of the score and its complement) so that a feature carrying signal in the opposite direction is not penalised, and averaged across the four projections. Projection reliance is the mean absolute logistic coefficient within each projection block (comparable because features are standardised). The layer × rank sweep is scored by a min-max normalised average of four separability metrics (probe ROC-AUC, probe accuracy, symmetric KL and a spectral magnitude), and the paper is careful to call this an operating-point *summary* rather than a selection criterion — the appendix figures expose the same sweep before aggregation, and they note the off-layer transfer proxy does not track local separability monotonically. No benchmark of competing detectors is run; the closest prior work, a learned classifier over whole adapter tensors, is cited but not re-implemented.

## What they found
Perfect separation on the held-out splits of all three families: accuracy 1.00, ROC-AUC 1.00, zero false positives and zero false negatives, with thresholds around 0.38–0.42 and centroid gaps from 0.495 (Qwen) to 0.779 (Gemma). No single spectral statistic carries the signal: the largest singular value and the Frobenius norm are the weakest (univariate AUC around 0.57–0.65), while entropy, energy concentration and kurtosis reach 0.75–0.83, with kurtosis peaking at 0.979 on Llama — and the strongest feature is a different one in each backbone (energy concentration for Qwen, kurtosis for Llama, entropy for Gemma). Projection reliance is likewise backbone-dependent: Qwen leans hardest on the value projection, Llama spreads across q, o and k, Gemma concentrates on q and v. Operationally, *where* the adapter was attached matters more than how wide its rank is: separability rises with depth (composite score 0.27 at layer 3, 0.07 at layer 9, 0.79 at layer 21, 0.74 at layer 27 at rank 16) while rank 8 versus 32 at layer 21 moves the same score only from 0.61 to 0.75. Payload legibility in weights is strength-dependent: the rank of the first payload-family token in the dominant singular direction's top-20 is 21.0 (absent) on average for benign adapters and 20.35, 9.59, 4.75 at 1%, 3% and 5% poisoning. The geometry is family-conditioned rather than universal — same-model descriptor pairs average 0.322 cosine similarity against −0.154 across families — so the authors explicitly conclude there is no single poison cluster in weight space, only a per-architecture displacement.

## Limits and open problems they name
A new architecture still needs its own labelled calibration bank, since standardisation statistics and the threshold are fitted per family — the method removes execution and trigger knowledge but not the requirement for labelled examples, which is precisely what a hub screening thousands of unknown-provenance uploads is least likely to have. Screening each adapter still costs a QR/SVD pass over four projections at a selected layer, more expensive than a single collapsed scalar (which they say would be less robust across backbones). The threat model assumes a *non-adaptive* attacker: an adversary who knows about the detector can try to spread the malicious update diffusely across projections and singular modes, trading backdoor strength for geometric stealth, and they name robustness to adaptive attacks, stronger distribution shifts and further architectures as future work. The benchmark is also small (50 held-out adapters per class per family) and all poison comes from two hand-chosen triggers at three poisoning rates.

## What we take from it
Three things worth carrying, in descending order of transfer. **A method**: the per-projection spectral descriptor is a reading-weights instrument our white-box model can reuse cheaply — the QR-based core-matrix trick means a low-rank update's spectrum is available without materialising the full weight matrix, so any account of an adapter, a LoRA fine-tune or a rank-restricted decomposition can be given spectral statistics at negligible cost, and the projection-wise concatenation (rather than averaging over projections) is the design choice that keeps the signal from being washed out. **A warning**: the paper's own dissection says no single statistic dominates and the strongest one changes per backbone, so a univariate claim of the form "singular-value concentration marks this phenomenon" is a claim about one model family, not a law — and their family-conditioned geometry result says the same thing at the population level. For us this is a concrete instance of the folder's recurring rule that a weight-space signature needs a control on the other architecture before it is quoted. **A framing**: a detector trained on a labelled calibration bank of an unknown-provenance artefact is a *screening* instrument, and the honest phrasing of a perfect score is "separable on 50 held-out adapters from two triggers", not "the phenomenon is visible in weights" — which is exactly the gap between the claim and the experiment that the folder's calibration entries (`arxiv-2310.10348`, `arxiv-2402.12560`) keep finding in this kind of table. What we should *not* copy: the perfect-score headline. An out-of-sample 50-plus-50 split with no adaptive attacker and no adversarial-evasion arm is the arm this line of work most needs and does not have, and printing 1.00 without it invites exactly the over-reading the paper's limitations section disavows.
