- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Replacing the row-wise softmax in attention with an element-wise nonlinearity removes the expensive reduction over the sequence and is a prerequisite for any claim that attention is a pointwise operation. Earlier attempts (ReLU attention, a scaled `n^-α` sigmoid) either broke or quietly needed extra machinery. The authors want to know whether sigmoid attention is a genuine drop-in for softmax — same representation power, same training stability, comparable quality — and, if it needs stabilising, exactly which knob does the stabilising and why. The larger goal is a principled account of attention as a sum of independent gates rather than a normalised competition, which is directly relevant to reading attention weights as per-pair quantities inside a white-box model.

## What they studied
Transformer blocks of many sizes: 1-layer research blocks (width 3072), a κ-scaled LM family from 2.2M to 440M non-embedding parameters, a 1B Llama2-recipe LM on RedPajama, ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/16 for supervised ImageNet, SimCLR and BYOL and MAE self-supervised vision, and a 255M encoder transformer plus a 104M conformer for ASR on LibriSpeech. The unit of analysis is the attention layer as a whole — specifically the Frobenius norm of its output at initialisation and its Jacobian — rather than individual heads or neurons. Positional embeddings (SinCos, RoPE, ALiBi, CAPE) are treated as an experimental factor because they change the logit distribution at init.

## How they did it
Sigmoid attention is defined as `σ(Q Kᵀ/√d + b) V` with σ applied element-wise and a scalar bias b entering through the sigmoid, so each query-key pair gets an independent gate in (0,1) instead of a share of a row-wide distribution. Two theorems are proved: a universal-approximation result obtained by miming the classical contextual-mapping proof with a modified "selective shift" operation that sigmoid attention can implement with at least four heads and shifts in both queries and keys; and a worst-case Jacobian spectral-norm bound. The empirical work swaps softmax for sigmoid with every other hyperparameter held fixed, first at 1-layer scale on the realnews split of C4 to isolate the initialisation failure, then scaling up. The stabilisation study sweeps LayerScale (init 1e-4), QK norm, positional embedding choice, and the bias — a constant sweep over {−15,−10,−6,−4,−1} plus learnable variants and the analytic −log n. A fused CUDA kernel (FlashSigmoid) is benchmarked against FlashAttention2 on H100 and A100.

## What counts as an explanation here
An architectural explanation in the form of theorems plus a diagnosis: the failure of naive sigmoid attention is pinned to one measurable quantity — the initial attention output norm ‖σ(QKᵀ/√d)V‖ being large — and the fix is derived analytically rather than tuned. The bias derivation is the explanatory core: requiring the gates to sum to about one gives a unique solving b bounded in terms of log(n−1) and the logit extrema (Proposition E.1), and requiring the sigmoid gate's derivative with respect to a logit to match softmax's gives the same order −log n. So the explanation is a small parametric model of what the softmax normaliser was doing, and it predicts the correct value of a new hyperparameter. It is partial: it explains the initialisation regime and the gradient scale, not the trained network's behaviour.

## How they judged success
Three kinds of evidence. (i) Theory: universal approximation with stated head count and parameter scaling, and a Lipschitz bound that scales with the mean squared input norm rather than the maximum — contrasted against a known softmax construction whose local Lipschitz constant grows like R² exp(cR²). (ii) Stability: LR-sensitivity score defined as the expected excess loss over a learning-rate sweep (a lower score means flatter), plus counts of gradient-norm and loss spikes during training, plus train NLL curves. (iii) Quality: ImageNet top-1 for supervised ViT, linear-probe and finetune top-1 for SSL, word error rate on LibriSpeech test-clean/test-other and TED-LIUM duration bins for ASR, and nine zero-shot/short-shot benchmark averages for the 1B LM. Smooth loss with no spikes is treated as a success criterion in its own right, and the presence of spikes is reported against softmax as a like-for-like comparison.

## What they found
Naive sigmoid attention underperforms softmax in language and speech, and the authors attribute this to large attention output norms at initialisation rather than to the lack of normalisation per se; either ALiBi or a bias of order −log n moves the initial logit mass toward the sigmoid's near-zero region and recovers softmax-equivalent loss. The constant-bias ablation is non-monotone: stability improves as the bias goes from −1 down to about −10 and then degrades at −15, which reads as over-regularisation. With bias, QK norm and LayerScale, sigmoid matches softmax across vision, self-supervised vision and ASR — on LibriSpeech 960h the word error rates are within a few tenths of a point in both directions, on the 1B LM at 2048 context the nine-benchmark averages are nearly identical (49.5 vs 49.4 ALiBi at 2k), and at 4096 context sigmoid trails slightly (48.2 vs 49.2 for ALiBi). Removing QK norm while keeping sigmoid causes large gradient-norm and loss spikes in the ASR setting; removing LayerScale costs accuracy in vision. Kernel timings show 17.39% faster inference and 6.53% faster training for self-attention on H100 over FlashAttention2, translating to roughly 8% end-to-end inference and 4% training speed-ups, and a 1.23× step-time improvement for the 1B LM in JAX without FlashAttention. FLOP accounting shows the difference between softmax and sigmoid in the logit stage is a factor of about 1/64 — negligible — so the kernel gains come from memory behaviour, not arithmetic.

## Limits and open problems they name
At 1B parameters and 4096 context, a gradient-norm spike regime and a small quality gap remain open. The Lipschitz bounds are tighter than the softmax ones but probably not tight. Evaluation is confined to standard vision, speech and language benchmarks, with niche applications untested. ASR is sensitive to positional embedding choice, with CAPE the worst, and post-LayerNorm sigmoid was not matched to a stable configuration; robust bias initialisation across embeddings is left open. Distributed-training communication overhead may eat the kernel gains. Two further things the paper does not resolve but its own evidence shows: the *cause* of the initial-norm problem is diagnosed only through fixes, not through a measurement of how the norm evolves in the failing case, and the 4096-context gap is reported without decomposition into which benchmark it comes from.

## What we take from it
**A measure and a warning.** (1) The bias derivation is a template for the kind of derivation our white-box model should contain: take the component being removed (here the softmax normaliser), state the property it provided (per-element mass of about 1/n, and a specific gradient scale), then solve for a parameter that restores the property, and check the solution against the observed best hyperparameter — the theory said −log n and the sweep's optimum sat near −10, which for the sequence lengths used are the same order. Whenever we replace or approximate a component of the model, the replacement should carry an analytically derived parameter restoration of this kind rather than a tuned one. (2) The LR-sensitivity score — expected excess loss over a sweep relative to the best learning rate in the sweep — is a cheap, dataset-level stability statistic that we can compute for any of our own interventions, and it is a better outcome variable than a single-run loss because it measures flatness rather than a point. **Framing.** Pointwise attention makes every query-key pair an addressable gate whose value does not depend on the rest of the row; that is the structural precondition for treating a single attention weight as a manipulable causal variable in our interventions, and this paper is the authority for the claim that the softmax's row-coupling is removable at no cost in quality on these tasks. **Warning.** The paper's own history is the folder's repeated pattern: prior work reported sigmoid attention working and it did not, because an initialisation-scale problem sat underneath an architectural claim; the cure was found by looking at a norm at step zero, not at final accuracy. So when we find that a mechanism "does not transfer" between models, the first check should be a scale statistic at initialisation.
