## What they wanted, and why
Natural-language autoencoders are validated by a round trip: a verbalizer writes an explanation of an activation and a reconstructor rebuilds the activation from the text, with the reconstruction score attached to each explanation as its faithfulness number. The author argues the objective cannot see individual claims at all — reconstruction rewards sufficiency and is indifferent among claim values that reconstruct equally well, so a false claim costs nothing unless the reconstructor happens to depend on that exact wording. Two questions follow: how much of a high-scoring explanation is actually load-bearing, and whether the failure can be repaired without making the reader better. The larger goal is an oversight tool in which a claim about an internal state can be checked against the state rather than taken from prose the model produced, and the paper's answer is that this has to be arranged at training time, in the target model, before any reader is fitted.

## What they studied
Three settings, deliberately of increasing realism. (1) A **released** verbalizer/reconstructor pair on Qwen-2.5-7B, layer 20, on in-distribution web text, with 1,517 base-system claims audited (1,428–1,471 in two trained variants). (2) An **exact-ground-truth sandbox**: two templated synthetic domains (a story of five slots; a five-slot marketplace with a variable tap position), 8-layer d = 256 transformers trained from scratch on 120,000 stories, tap at layer 4 of 8, and a 0.5B verbalizer/reconstructor pair. Slot design fixes what the tap *can* contain — name retained, place and object faded, activity and ending never mentioned before the tap — so storage gaps, readout gaps and unreadable slots are separable without a judge. (3) **Pythia-160M** under continued pretraining (500M tokens), with linear heads on the layer-6 hidden state as the intervention, plus head-free fine-tuning afterwards to test persistence. Unit of analysis is the individual *claim* inside an explanation, and separately the individual *probe read-out* of the tap.

## How they did it
Grounding is defined counterfactually: a claim is flipped by a minimal-pair edit, the explanation is rescored, and the drop Δr̃ is the claim's grounding, with grounding at a threshold τ counting it as load-bearing. Every edit is validity-checked per edit, because oversized edits that rewrite surrounding material inflated apparent grounding roughly sevenfold; a closed judge produced valid edits 68.4% of the time against 99.3% for the pinned open judge (Gemma-4-12B, temperature 0) that was used throughout. Crossing grounding with truth gives a two-axis table, and the cell that matters is *grounded and false* — wording the reconstruction depends on whose content is false — which the author calls a private code when an evaluator swap shows it is a convention of the co-trained pair rather than a shared one. The evaluator swap retrains the reconstructor on outputs it never saw and re-measures; a paraphrase-family swap (different frames and slot order, shared slot words) tests the same thing at the phrasing level. Four interventions are then tried against the failure: a rejection-sampling and GRPO loop rewarding the verbalizer for grounding; minimising the distance between hidden states and their reconstructions; continuing training with frozen probes; and RECAP, which adds class-balanced linear heads predicting external targets to the language-modeling loss of the *target model*, reading taps from the model's own hidden state and never from text. Every decodability number reported comes from independently fitted fresh probes, never from the co-trained heads. Finally a discrepancy detector is built (confident probe disagrees with stated word ⇒ flag) and a report-space adversary edits explanations to maximise the score while lying.

## What counts as an explanation here
A verbalizer's sentence, but with a changed standard of evidence: an explanation is licensed claim by claim, and only a claim that survives both a flip test (grounded) and a truth check against external labels counts. The paper's own artefact is not a better reader but a property of the target model — *designated content* that a fresh linear probe can recover — with the verbalizer demoted to a proposal generator whose claims are verified against that probe. The form is therefore a probe plus a verification protocol, explicitly partial: it certifies what an activation *stores*, not what the model *uses*, and the author states the ceiling by measuring it — patching the tap moves behaviour by about 0.003.

## How they judged success
Grounding floor-normalised centered cosine r̃ (0 at chance, 1 at perfect), with the chance floor set by scoring reconstructions against mismatched activations (measured at −0.001). Grounded fractions at three thresholds with bootstrap intervals and an empirical flip-noise null. A transform profile (paraphrase, synonym substitution, function-word masking, word shuffle, content-word masking) as the direct test of whether gist or wording carries the score. A grounded-vs-true cross with per-cell rates. Fresh-probe AUC and 64-class identity accuracy against a control model trained on identical data; held-out language-modeling tax in nats on 20M tokens against a shared control; tap-clustered bootstrap intervals; disjoint-interval comparison rather than fixed-level tests. For monitoring: claim-level AUC and a flag operating point set at 0.9 precision on a disjoint calibration split, with a pre-registered frozen-threshold check and a held-out vocabulary band (words ranked 64–128, never supervised) whose null was powered by a Hanley–McNeil analysis. Controls worth naming: an unread-slot impossibility control (heads cannot encode what the causal structure excludes), a never-designated probe bucket, a matched control model, and a probe-aware adversary rung declared outside the threat model as a worst-case bound.

## What they found
**The reconstruction score is carried by gist, not by specifics.** The released system reconstructs at r̃ = 0.84 while only about 2% of its salient specific claims are grounded under valid flips (4.2% / 2.1% / 1.6% at τ = 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.10), and specifics decode about three times worse than gist (≈6% versus ≈18% chance-corrected; mean-pooled gist up to 50%). The transform profile is the clean form of it: paraphrase keeps 89% of the score, content-word masking leaves 4%. An input-echo control rules out mere restatement (0.84 against 0.27 for the raw prefix). The model's own unembedding does see the missed values late — the logit lens surfaces them into the top-50 at 10.1% by layer 27 against 0.6% at the read layer — while a matched ridge probe stays flat at 4.7–6.0%, so late layers are a partial lever in the model's output basis and most missed specifics are recoverable nowhere in the stack by these read-outs.

**Under exact ground truth the pair learns private codes, in every run.** Standard-recipe runs pass at r̃ 0.71–0.86 while a fully decodable slot is stated truly at 0.44–0.96 across seeds but grounded at 0.76–0.88 — a grounded-minus-true gap of 0.30–0.80, with codes appearing in 5/5 runs across both domains and occasionally occupying an unread slot, i.e. using the sentence as a channel rather than a description. The evaluator swap collapses code-slot grounding from 0.53–0.89 to 0.08–0.23 while true-claim grounding survives, and the cross-grader gap is 0.17–0.38 on standard runs against ≈0.003 on RECAP runs; the disjoint paraphrase family reproduces the collapse within ≈0.05 across three seeds. Standard verbalizers also under-read content that is present: slots 55–72% decodable are stated truly only 15–26% of the time.

**The natural target-side fixes fail, with mechanisms.** Rewarding the verbalizer for grounding (rejection sampling, GRPO) improves its proxy while the independent claim-level audit stays null at every threshold, the gains attributable to sentence restructuring. Minimising state-reconstruction distance removes decodable content in 3/3 seeds at every strength with perplexity unchanged, and freshly retrained probes fail too, so the information is gone rather than rotated — the paper reads this as the non-contrastive-collapse account applied to a lossy projection. Frozen probes go stale: matched readers decay to near-chance within 1,500 continued-training steps (3/3 seeds) and read nothing across seeds (6/6), yet a label-free linear map fitted on ≈7,000 paired activations restores native performance in 9/9 cases, with pure rotation recovering about half and a non-orthogonal change of rank 16–64 required for nearly all of it.

**RECAP works, and its ceiling is decodability, not verbalizability.** In the sandbox, designated content reaches 100% decodability by independent probes at a cost of +0.001 nats in 5/5 runs, unread targets stay at chance, and fresh read-out pairs state the content at ≈1.00 truth and grounding with no code signature in 0/5 full-designation runs and 0/3 partial ones; the dose experiment contradicted the author's own prediction (any dose prevented codes entirely rather than relocating them) and the correction is printed. On Pythia-160M designated content reaches 0.95–1.00 AUC at K = 8 and K = 64 against a 0.77–0.80 control, at a tax of +0.010 nats with a paired 95% CI of [−0.015, +0.034], while K = 512 costs +0.14 to +0.20 nats. Design matters: a naive presence head at K = 512 minimised its own loss while making nothing probe-decodable, and class balancing repaired it — the stated rule is to pick targets whose loss cannot be minimised without discrimination. At scale the guarantee degrades to the paper's fourth limitation: an independently audited read-out pair states context words truly at 0.44 (0.39–0.51) against 0.06 (0.04–0.09) on control, recall 0.29 against 0.07 while stating fewer words, stable at 0.44–0.46 across three target seeds — but a redesigned reconstructor whose own decode-point state reads at 0.79–0.81 under a presence head still generates at 0.56–0.63 precision, and neither reader scale (0.5B vs 3B), reconstruction optimisation, a direct truth reward, injection bandwidth, nor explicit negative supervision closes the gap.

**Monitoring improves and survives score-gaming, within scope.** In the sandbox the discrepancy detector was sound but nearly blind on the control (27 flags, zero false alarms, 3.7% recall on 733 lies, 63% abstention), and on the RECAP model the pre-tap lies disappeared at source (0 false in 1,200) with abstention at 0%, moving verification coverage from 37% to 100% for designated content. At scale a fresh presence probe ranks true claims above false at AUC 0.965 against 0.823 with disjoint intervals (672 held-out claims at a 1.5% base rate), flagging at 0.93 recall / 0.87 precision with the threshold calibrated on a disjoint split. A report-space adversary that picks the score-optimal absent word suppresses ≈87% of the lie penalty (optimal lie costs 0.022 against 0.173 random, CI [0.017, 0.027]) on both models, yet the RECAP probe holds at AUC 0.952 / recall 0.86 while the control probe collapses to 0.508 / 0.01; down the rungs RECAP reads 0.993 / 0.971 / 0.952 and control 0.729 / 0.635 / 0.508, and the out-of-threat-model probe-aware adversary degrades RECAP only to 0.909 while inverting control to 0.242. Two anti-circularity additions make the scope explicit: a frozen threshold reproduces the eval-tuned operating point (0.878 / 0.732), and on words ranked 64–128 the advantage vanishes entirely (L1 0.661 against 0.702, L4 0.290 against 0.226) as a powered null.

**Decodability is a running cost.** Head-free fine-tuning on a shifted corpus erodes the RECAP surplus within 250 steps and approaches control by 2,000 (identity accuracy 0.991 → 0.320 → 0.180 → 0.169), identically on unseen same-distribution data, so the loss is from removing the heads rather than the corpus shift; applying the head loss every tenth step retains most of it (0.849 / 0.900 / 0.891 at step 2,000). Decodability also erodes slowly at the low weight in the first place — identity loss near chance after 400 steps at w = 0.1 — so maintenance beats restoration.

## Limits and open problems they name
Evidence is a synthetic sandbox, one released NLA (Qwen-2.5-7B), and continued pretraining of one 160M model, mostly at single seeds; frontier scale is untested. Claims are closed-vocabulary and template-parsed, the monitor covers designated content only, and open-ended faithfulness is unmeasured. RECAP must be co-trained in and cannot be retrofitted onto a frozen model, and it depends on a well-chosen target — a bad one can report success while decoding nothing, as the K = 512 naive head did. Decodability is not verbalizability: at scale the probe reads what the verbalizer only partly says, and no reader-side fix closed it. Most consequentially, RECAP certifies what an activation stores, not what the model uses: designated content can be present and checkable yet behaviourally inert (patching the tap moves behaviour by ≈0.003), and a model could route computation around the monitored subspace. Report-space adversaries only — activation-level attacks are out of scope.

## What we take from it
(1) **A counterfactual, claim-level audit is the cheapest thing we are not doing, and it needs a validity filter to be honest.** The flip test costs one reconstructor pass per claim; without per-edit validity filtering the number it produces is wrong by roughly a factor of seven. Any claim-level score we publish (from a verbalizer, a probe caption, or our own description layer) should carry a *grounded and false* count rather than an aggregate, because the aggregate is what a high-scoring false explanation passes on. (2) **The transform profile is a one-table test of what a read-out is reading** — keep the score under paraphrase and function-word masking, destroy it under content-word masking, and if the pattern is reversed you have a surface-form code, not a description. It is the cheapest instrument in the paper and it applies to probes as well as prose. (3) **Training a reader on the reconstruction reward is the wrong lever; the lever is upstream.** Both the verbalizer-side reward arms improved their proxy while the independent audit stayed null, and the target-side objective that "makes the probe and the model agree" actively destroyed decodable content. For us the transferable framing is that decodability is a property of the model we can arrange — but arranging it costs LM performance, must be maintained continuously, and certifies storage rather than use. (4) **Report the matched nulls that bound a monitor's scope**: the frozen-threshold reproduction, the never-designated bucket, the held-out vocabulary band with its power analysis, and the impossibility control on unread slots. This paper is the folder's best template for pairing every monitor claim with the region where the claim stops. (5) **The two-space distinction is a warning for our own instruments**: the model's own unembedding surfaced missed specifics at 10.1% where a generic trained probe stayed at 4.7–6.0%, so "the information is absent" and "our read-out basis cannot see it" are different statements, and a probe that reads at chance is evidence about the probe. (6) **On staleness, realign before retraining**: a label-free linear map on paired activations restored 9/9 stale readers, with rotation buying half — so any monitor we run against a model that keeps training owes a recalibration procedure, not just a note that probes go stale.
