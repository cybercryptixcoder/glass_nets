# Every Component Is a Lookup: One Linear Graph for Interaction, Composition and Attribution

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.23393
- **Authors / organisation:** Po-Kai Chen, Aske Plaat, Niki van Stein; Leiden University (LIACS)
- **Date:** 2026-05-22 (v1), revised 2026-09-24 (v3)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23393
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v3: main text, Limitations, and Appendices A–J; references skipped; figures read from captions and prose, not inspected as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The authors start from a complaint about tooling rather than about models: three questions about a single prediction — which components feed which, how those connections compose into the route that produced the answer, and which input tokens drove or opposed it — each have their own method with their own assumptions, and the answers cannot be compared because there is no common quantity. LRP-style token scores change with the propagation rule, so a score's meaning is undefined; interventional methods give causal readings but need a counterfactual prompt for every question, and not every input has one. The paper's goal is to show that two architectural facts are enough to put all three readouts on one object, so that a component score, a token score and a path all become the same credit measured at different places in one graph. The larger ambition is a single decomposable description of a forward pass that later work can re-root at will.

## What they studied
Decoder-only pre-norm transformers: the full Pythia-deduped family (160M, 410M, 1.4B, 2.8B, 6.9B, all at training step 143,000) for the interaction and scale checks, and GPT-2 small for the circuit-recovery and token-attribution checks. Text is the Pile (1,000 sentences for knockouts, 200 held-out sentences / 7,967 tokens for the perplexity measure, 300 sentences for token attribution, 50-prompt sets for a dispatch ablation). Tasks with known circuits are IOI (Wang et al.'s 26-head circuit and its three named connections) and greater-than (Hanna et al.'s four composition claims). The unit of analysis is the residual-stream component (attention head at a position, MLP at a position, embedding), subdivided into its key-value *entries* — source positions for a head, neurons for an MLP.

## How they did it
Two assumptions fix the structure. First, both attention and MLP compute a selection over values, a weighted sum where a nonlinearity gates a set of value vectors; the entries summed over are source positions for attention and neurons for an MLP, with keys, gating function and values tabulated for each. Second, the residual stream is additive over component outputs, so any downstream linear or bilinear read of the stream splits over the upstream writers. Holding the routing weights at their forward-pass values turns the model into a computational graph with edge weights between components, paths through them, and per-token credit at the embeddings — all from one forward pass, no gradients, no patching, no training.

The credit recursion: project each component's output onto a target direction (the vocabulary-mean-centred unembedding row, or a logit-difference direction for contrastive tasks) to get its scalar credit; split that credit across the component's entries in proportion to how much each entry's gated value pushed along the direction the credit is travelling; then split each entry's credit across the upstream components that built its score and its value, following the key, query and value branches of an attention head separately and labelled, or the single pre-activation branch of an MLP. Denominators get a soft floor, because when positive and negative child contributions nearly cancel the per-share factor explodes and deep recursion compounds the blow-up. Recursion strictly descends in layer and terminates at the embeddings. They then check each readout against a different external reference with identical hyperparameters: interaction scores against communication-specific knockout (subtract a component's residual vector from one downstream LayerNorm type and measure perplexity change), paths against two path-patched circuits by re-rooting at a claimed receiver and ranking its upstream feeders within the claimed branch, and token credit against the input-flipping (most-relevant-first vs least-relevant-first) faithfulness protocol against three dedicated attribution methods.

## What counts as an explanation here
A decomposition, not a description: one linear graph per forward pass whose edge weights, enumerated paths and per-token credits are three readouts of the same quantity. It is explicitly a statement about *contribution* under the computation the model actually performed, not about causal necessity — the authors call this the paper's central distinction and devote their limitations to it. Paths are named down to the branch (K, Q or V) a head used, so the explanation has a granularity finer than component identity: the same head-pair edge can be strong on one branch and absent on another. Completeness is partial by construction — marginal rather than exact through LayerNorm, biased terms dropped, path enumeration pruned — but the token-level aggregate is computed exactly.

## How they judged success
Three different tests, one per readout. Interaction scores: Spearman correlation with knockout perplexity change, reported within-layer (mean of per-source-layer correlations, the stricter version holding the downstream receivers fixed) and cross-layer, on both an attention channel and an MLP channel, at all five Pythia scales. Paths: median rank and "found%" of the expected upstream role within the claimed branch, over 100 re-rootings per prompt set, plus a matched-counterfactual coverage drop (IOI prompts vs. ABC prompts where no name repeats) to separate task-tied credit from prompt-independent credit. Token attribution: the LeRF-minus-MoRF area between target-probability curves and the fraction of sentences with positive area, against a random ranking and three named baselines. Two honest controls: the safe-denominator floor is swept and shown to matter only in that it must be on, and an ablation replaces output-aligned dispatch with gate-only dispatch to show what the projection buys.

## What they found
- Knockout prediction holds at every scale: within-layer Spearman 0.72–0.86 on attention and 0.86–0.96 on MLP; cross-layer 0.67–0.88 and 0.82–0.95 (Pythia-160M 0.72/0.96, 410M 0.82/0.91, 1.4B 0.73/0.86, 2.8B 0.73/0.87, 6.9B 0.86/0.90). The embedding is a high-end outlier on the MLP channel at every scale (Δ ppl above 10^3). Knockout counts are small — 56 to 156 components, five per source layer sampled across the score range.
- IOI: all three claimed connections surface in the branch they are claimed to use, with the named upstream role found in at least 95% of re-rootings at median rank at most 3. S-Inhibition is rank 1 (100%) on the query side of Name Movers but rank 10 on the key side — a branch distinction no single per-token or per-component score can express. Adding layer-0 heads to the pool pushes duplicate-token heads to ranks 1–2 with ~100% coverage, but the same happens on the ABC control, so that ranking is not evidence.
- Contribution ≠ causal effect, with a signature: layer-0 components hold high credit on every prompt, task or not. Moving from IOI to matched ABC controls, the path-patched components lose 30–70 points of coverage at layers 2–6 and about 15 at layers 7–8 while unclaimed components lose at most 19; layer-0 heads keep 100% coverage on both arms.
- Token attribution: largest Δ A of the four methods (+0.085, positive on 100% of sentences) against +0.066 (AttnLRP), +0.049 (CP-LRP), +0.084 (IFR) and −0.001 / 49% for a random ranking — but a linear-routing heuristic (IFR) is within 0.001 of them on this metric.
- A suppression index on IOI separates copying from suppression, and the S1–S2 credit gap is positive at all five Pythia scales (160M +24.7, 410M +66.9, 1.4B +80.9, 2.8B +31.7, 6.9B +63.2); S2 credit turns outright negative at three of the five.
- Greater-than: all four claims place their named group in the top four once layer-0 writers are removed from the pool (pools of 17–60). Against all sources the same claims sit near rank 10, so the headline depends on the exclusion. Circuit components outrank non-circuit ones beyond the named edges (median 10 vs 22 all sources, 8 vs 14 at layer ≥ 1) — the check the per-claim ranks cannot supply, since those are fixed by construction.
- Dispatch: gate-only splitting hands position 0 a 28.9% credit share and top-1 rank on 32% of IOI prompts (22.3% / 18% on factual recall, 9.2% / 4% on Pile); projecting onto what was actually written drops top-1 to 0% everywhere. Safe-denominator floor off → faithfulness falls to chance (53%).

## Limits and open problems they name
The LayerNorm treatment is marginal, not exact: the stream's standard deviation is treated as constant so the decomposition stays additive, which fits pre-norm models and was not tested on post-norm ones. Contribution is not causal effect, and the paper says so twice — the layer-0 diagnosis works only because a matched counterfactual exists, and no such counterfactual exists for greater-than, so the same reading is unavailable. Whether contribution can predict interventional effect more generally, or identify a circuit with no counterfactual, is left open. Preparation cost is quadratic in depth for key-side scores; named-path enumeration is combinatorial and depends on a pruning threshold that can miss low-magnitude routes in deeper models. Knockout samples are thin (five components per layer), and the network of tasks is small — two circuits, one toy-ish copy task, plus perplexity.

## What we take from it
- **Framing (reuse):** the three questions our white-box model must answer are readouts of one object, not three tools. Building one additive decomposition and reading edges, paths and input credit off it is a cheaper structure than three purpose-built pipelines, and it lets a token score be explained by the paths that produced it.
- **Method (reuse):** output-aligned dispatch — split credit by how strongly an entry's gated value pushed along the direction the component actually wrote, not by the gate alone. The BOS-sink table is a concrete warning about gate-weighted attribution: sinks win top-1 on up to 32% of prompts under gate-only splitting and never under projection.
- **Measure (reuse):** the matched-counterfactual coverage drop is the folder's cleanest cheap test for separating task-tied credit from prompt-independent bias, and it costs one extra prompt set rather than an intervention.
- **Warning (steal the discipline):** a per-claim circuit check that re-roots at the claimed receiver with a pool restricted to layer ≥ 1 is fixed by construction if the paper also excludes the components that crowd the ranking; the same authors publish the unrestricted ranks beside the restricted ones and show the headline moves from top-4 to ~rank 10. Publish both.
- **Warning:** the token-attribution win collapses against IFR (+0.085 vs +0.084) while the S2 suppression index gives a different ordering, so "largest Δ A" is a property of the metric, not of the method. Any single-scalar faithfulness score we report needs a second readout that disagrees with it.
- **Gap it leaves:** contribution is measured with routing held at its realised values, so the graph can report a strong edge that is causally unnecessary; the paper answers this only where a counterfactual prompt exists. For our predictive model that means the graph edge weights are a *model of the forward pass*, not a model of what happens under intervention — the intervention must be predicted separately, which is exactly the open problem they name.
