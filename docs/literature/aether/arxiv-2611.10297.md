# Uncertainty, Not Concealment: A Mechanistic Reappraisal of Sycophancy in Vision-Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.10297
- **Authors / organisation:** Mohamed I. Eldagla (German International University in Cairo), Zeyad H. ElShafei (Arab Academy for Science, Technology and Maritime Transport), Ahmed M. Anter (Egypt-Japan University of Science and Technology)
- **Date:** 2026-11-06
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10297
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
In clinical retrieval-augmented generation a vision-language model is shown a chest X-ray next to a retrieved radiology report that may be wrong, and it has to say so. The authors report that these models essentially never do: the contradiction rate is about zero. The tempting reading — which they call the sycophancy mask hypothesis — is that the model notices the conflict internally and then suppresses the admission, holding a correct verdict it declines to state. The observation that motivates it is a gap: an internal signal is present while the behavioural signal is absent. The paper's point is that this single observation is equally consistent with a second hypothesis, that the conflict is registered as unresolved uncertainty and never converted into a decision at all, and that the two demand opposite remedies — forced disclosure versus re-grounding and uncertainty surfacing. Their goal is to build a diagnostic that separates the two rather than a new method for either.

## What they studied
Four instruction-tuned VLMs: LLaVA-1.5-7B, LLaVA-1.5-13B, Qwen2-VL-7B and InternVL2-8B. Data are frontal chest X-rays from the Indiana University collection under four paired conditions — C0 matched (image plus its true report, 738 pairs), C2 normal image with an abnormal report (116), C3 abnormal image with a normal report (622) and C4 abnormal image with the most similar normal report (205) — so the unit of analysis moves between a whole-model behaviour verdict, a layer-resolved residual-stream probe, an intervention direction and, finally, cross-modal attention mass over visual tokens.

## How they did it
Every model answers in a two-line template whose first line is one of SUPPORTS, CONTRADICTS or INCONCLUSIVE; verdicts are parsed by a first-verdict rule that reads only the bracketed label, explicitly to stop negated hedges in the rationale ("does not contradict") from being counted as contradictions. Generation is greedy with a fixed seed, so verdict differences cannot be a sampling artefact. A logistic probe on the last-token hidden state at the penultimate layer separates the matched condition from the three mismatched ones with grouped cross-validation, and the same item is also passed through a biomedical CLIP model for image–text similarity as a non-LLM comparison. The steering direction is a mean difference over baseline hidden states in the upper block of layers, added to the residual stream during generation at six strengths, with the matched pairs steered as a false-positive control. Visual-token activations are patched between matched and mismatched contexts with a no-op control, and the forced-choice arm reruns the task with the hedge option deleted. Attention from text positions to image tokens is measured on mismatched pairs against a matched baseline, and output entropy is checked as a competing readout of uncertainty.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **three-test discriminator between two verbal hypotheses**, not a mechanism. The object is a ruled-out explanation: the paper's contribution is to show that "the model knows and will not say" does not survive, and that what survives is "the mismatch is present, reads as hedging, and is accompanied by the text looking away from the image". The internal signal is offered as a diagnostic — a probe that separates conditions — and the causal claim it licenses is deliberately narrow: a direction that amplifies the mismatch produces hedging-language changes and no verdict changes, so the direction carries the conflict without carrying a verdict. It is partial by construction and the authors say so, including that the attention evidence is correlational.

## How they judged success
The discriminating structure is that each hypothesis makes a different prediction, and all three tests are scored against the predictions rather than against an accuracy target. Concealment predicts CONTRADICTS rises when the direction is amplified; uncertainty predicts verdicts stay flat. Concealment predicts the forced choice recovers the hidden verdict; uncertainty predicts the forced rate returns to the matched baseline. The probe is scored by cross-validated AUC with a bootstrap interval and a permutation test, and made non-trivial by comparison with the CLIP image–text cosine on the same pairs. Patching uses a clean no-op control and is reported per layer, and its small size is read as a result rather than explained away.

## What they found
Behavioural suppression is real and near-total: across conditions the robust contradiction rate is 0% in almost every cell, the sole exception being 2 of 941 InternVL2-8B outputs (0.2%). Template compliance splits the models — the LLaVA pair miss the format on 20.5% to 94.1% of outputs — while InternVL2-8B and Qwen2-VL comply and express the mismatch as INCONCLUSIVE, which rises with difficulty to 26.3% and 50.7% respectively. The probe separates matched from mismatched inputs in all four models (AUC 0.789 to 0.813), InternVL2-8B's larger-sample regularised rerun gives 0.781 with a 95% bootstrap interval of 0.726 to 0.806 and the permutation test bottoms out at p = 0.002, and the CLIP baseline cannot do it (cosine AUC 0.475). Steering at six strengths changes nothing on the verdict axis — contradiction stays at 0% and the SUPPORTS/INCONCLUSIVE split stays near 88%/11% — while merely deleting negated hedges as strength rises; the matched-pair control also stays at 0%. The forced choice returns contradiction at 8.0% and 10.0% on the two mismatched conditions against a 10.0% matched baseline; the apparently elevated 31.0% on the fourth condition is traced to retrieval scarcity, since 205 images reuse only 28 distinct reports and one hallucinated nodule report drives 29 of the 31 contradictions (43.9% for that report, 5.9% for all others). What does move is attention: cross-modal attention to image tokens drops on mismatched pairs (AUC 0.376 against chance, p < 2e-5), raw output entropy does not separate them at all (AUC 0.517, p = 0.57), and patching visual tokens shifts the mismatch readout with an exact zero no-op control but only weakly, peaking at layer 20 and gone by layer 30.

## Limits and open problems they name
One domain (chest X-rays) and one model (InternVL2-8B) carry all the mechanistic analysis; the steering direction is a coarse mean difference; the attention evidence is correlational and the patching evidence only suggestive. They name four follow-ups they did not do: a capability ceiling check, a text-only ablation to isolate the cross-modal part of the probe, stronger steering, and a logit-based readout. Beyond their list, two are visible from outside — the 622-to-116 imbalance means the pooled probe estimates are dominated by one condition, and the three-way verdict prompt has no matched baseline in the main table, so the "0% contradiction" figure and the 10% forced-choice matched baseline are not measured on the same instrument.

## What we take from it
A **method and a warning**, and the two are the same object. The method is the discriminator itself: when an internal signal is present and the behaviour is absent, write down the competing explanations and give each a prediction that the *intervention* can falsify — amplifying the direction should surface the suppressed answer if the answer is there, and it should not if the state is uncertainty. That costs one steering sweep and one option-deletion rerun on data you already have, and here both came back with the "nothing changes" signature, which is what makes the negative result mean something rather than being the absence of a positive one. The warning is sharper and it lands on a habit of ours: **"the probe decodes it, the model just will not say it" is an inference from one observation and it can be wrong in a way that inverts the intervention.** A monitor built on the concealment reading would try to force disclosure; the right remedy here is re-grounding and uncertainty surfacing, and that difference is worth a whole implementation. Two smaller things to carry. The non-triviality control is the cheapest part of the design and the most reusable — running the same pairs through a CLIP similarity model and getting chance (0.475) is what converts "our probe gets 0.80" into "the model encodes something that surface similarity does not". And the deconfounded attention statistic shows the readout can be found in the wrong place: output entropy is flat at chance while the geometry of attention is not, which is the folder's standing lesson that a null readout is a claim about that readout.
