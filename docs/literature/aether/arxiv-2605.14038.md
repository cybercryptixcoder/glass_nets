## What they wanted, and why

Agents decide on every turn whether to answer from their own weights or call a tool, and the field has been evaluating that decision against a *fixed* notion of which queries need tools — usually a human or a strong-judge annotation of the question text alone. The authors argue this is the wrong object, because capability boundaries differ by model: a query a frontier model answers from memory may be genuinely out of reach for a small one. They therefore ask for a necessity label defined *per model, by that model's own behaviour*, and then ask how far actual tool-call behaviour departs from it. The larger goal is reliability in adaptive tool use, and the paper's framing is explicitly interpretability-in-service-of-diagnosis: it wants to locate where in the pipeline the failure happens, not merely to measure its rate.

## What they studied

Four instruction-tuned models in two families: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, plus Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as a scale check in an appendix. Two domains: a purpose-built 4,000-item arithmetic set spanning easy single-step sums to long chains and three-by-three multiplication, and the 817-item TruthfulQA for factual QA. Tools are simple — a calculator and a search API — exposed through model-specific call syntax. Unit of analysis is the pair of linear probe directions over hidden states (a necessity/cognition direction and a tool-call/action direction), tracked across every layer and the last twenty query token positions, plus a per-sample trajectory through a three-node chain: ground-truth necessity → model cognition → executed action.

## How they did it

Necessity is measured, not annotated: each query is run N = 10 times at temperature T = 0.7 with tools withheld, and a query counts as tool-necessary for that model if it fails at least one of the ten runs. Sensitivity to N ∈ {5, 10, 15} and T ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.9} is reported. The same queries are then re-run with the tool available under greedy decoding, and each item is sorted into Necessary-Called, Necessary-NotCalled, Unnecessary-Called or Unnecessary-NotCalled, with the latter two counting as end-to-end mismatch. Two logistic probes are trained on hidden states — one on the necessity label, one on the observed call/no-call action — with the same objective (Adam, learning rate 0.01), swept over all layers × the last 20 query tokens, and scored by held-out MCC on a 30% split, explicitly because the label distribution is skewed. The geometric relation between the two directions is read as their cosine similarity over the same token-layer grid, and the error is attributed by tracing each sample through the three-node chain rather than by any ablation. Two extra instruments close the loop: activation steering along the action direction to check that it is causal, and an explicit "is a tool necessary, yes or no" prompting arm as the verbal-report baseline. Mitigation is probe-guided decoding on held-out data — forcing the tool token when the probe says necessary, masking it when it says not.

## What counts as an explanation here

A representational one, and a deliberately partial one: a pair of linear directions and their geometric relation at the site that drives the next token, plus a causal chain in which the model's internal verdict and its emitted action are separate stages. The explanation is not a circuit and not a feature dictionary — it says where the model's belief about its own limits lives, that its action is separately decodable, that the two are close to orthogonal at the readout, and therefore that the end-to-end error is a translation failure rather than a perception failure. It predicts something falsifiable and cheap (forcing or masking the tool token with the probe should repair most of the mismatch) and the prediction is tested.

## How they judged success

Held-out MCC for both probes over the full token-layer grid, with MCC ≥ 0.4 treated as the "decent" band and the fraction of grid positions crossing it reported for the 70B appendix. Mismatch rates by category against the behavioural necessity label, with the model-dependent label's own sensitivity to N and T reported as a robustness check. Cosine similarity between probe normal vectors as the alignment measure. Causal validity of the action direction by corrective flip rate under activation steering, in both directions, on the originally misaligned cases. The verbal baseline is scored on the same MCC and on how much it *changes* behaviour. Mitigation is scored as misaligned rate before and after on a held-out test split.

## What they found

Mismatch between what a model needs and what it does is large: 26.5–54.0% on arithmetic and 30.8–41.8% on TruthfulQA, with the direction of the error flipping by model *and* by domain — Qwen3-8B overuses tools on arithmetic (38.2% unnecessary-calls against 3.5% missed-necessary) and underuses them on TruthfulQA, while Qwen3-4B does the reverse. The model-dependent labels visibly disagree across models when the same items are sorted recursively, which is the empirical case against a global necessity annotation. Both signals are linearly decodable: the action probe crosses MCC 0.4 over broad regions of nearly every model and both datasets; the necessity probe crosses it over broad mid-to-late-layer regions on arithmetic but much more sparsely on TruthfulQA, and within a family the two heatmaps look alike. The two directions have partial alignment in intermediate positions — noticeably so for the two Qwen models on arithmetic — but alignment collapses in the late-layer, last-token corner where the next-token decision is made. At 70B the same geometry holds with the sharpest numbers in the paper: cosine similarity −0.018 on arithmetic and 0.026 on TruthfulQA at final token and layer (−0.041 and 0.078 averaged over layers 61–80), while necessity reaches MCC ≥ 0.4 at 86.25% of positions on arithmetic and 39.50% on TruthfulQA. Tracing the trajectories attributes the bulk of the error to the cognition-to-action step rather than to cognition, and the mismatch is not concentrated near probe uncertainty — many misaligned items sit where the cognition probe is confidently near 0 or near 1. Steering along the action direction flips decisions almost completely (94.5–100% in three of four cells; 66.2% for inducing necessary calls on Qwen3-8B / TruthfulQA). Probe-guided decoding on held-out data cuts misaligned use by 77.35% and 34.67% relative for Qwen3-8B on the two datasets and 61.01% and 44.68% for Llama-3.1-8B. The verbal self-assessment arm is poor as a necessity read-out (MCC 0.07–0.23, negative for Llama-3.2-3B on TruthfulQA, undefined where Llama-3.1-8B answers "no" to everything) yet its apparent cognition-execution mismatch is low — because the yes/no answer in context makes the model consistent with its own stated commitment — while it shifts tool-calling behaviour on up to roughly half of samples relative to normal task-oriented prompting.

## Limits and open problems they name

The setup is narrow by design and the authors say so: two domains, one calculator and one search tool, single-turn, and no long-horizon or multi-turn agentic environment, which they name as the obvious extension. The final-token late-layer cosine is read off a heatmap through prose rather than tabulated per cell for the 8B and 3B models. There is no causal intervention on the cognition direction — the authors state plainly that no observable output faithfully represents internal cognition, so the cognition probe remains correlational and only the action direction is validated by steering. The necessity label is itself a behavioural threshold (fail at least one of N runs), so a model that is merely unlucky at temperature 0.7 is labelled necessary, and the reported sensitivity shows the necessary fraction rising with both N and T.

## What we take from it

Three transferable instruments and one framing. (1) **The label was derived from the model, not annotated for it** — necessity is "this model fails this item at least once in N samples", which is a *behavioural* ground truth produced by the same network being explained. Our survey keeps metering probe accuracy against human or judge labels; this is the cheaper and more predictive alternative whenever the property is capability-shaped, because it removes the annotator's disagreement with the model as a confound. (2) **Two probe directions scored by their cosine at the readout site is the cheapest geometry instrument in the folder** — no patching, no SAE, no steering sweep, just two fitted normals and an angle, and the angle is what turns "both signals are decodable" into a diagnosis of where the pipeline loses the information. It is the geometric counterpart of the both-directions inert-patch null (`arxiv-2610.21708`) and it costs one extra probe fit. (3) **The three-node trajectory is the error-attribution format to copy**: ground truth → internal read-out → emitted action, per sample, with the intermediate node's confidence printed against behaviour so that "the model was unsure" can be ruled out. (4) The verbal-report arm is a warning, not a baseline: asking the model to say whether it needs a tool produced a read-out near chance *and* changed its behaviour on a third to a half of items, so a paper that measures "does the model know when it needs a tool" via a decision prompt is measuring a different task.
