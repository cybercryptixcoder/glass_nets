## What they wanted, and why
Bibliographies written from parametric memory look plausible field by field and yet contain falsified entries, and a model has no surface signal separating confident recall from confident invention. The authors want to know whether this failure is visible inside the network before the reference is emitted, whether it is one failure or several, and whether a small set of internal units can be named and pushed on to reduce it. The larger goal is a detection and mitigation route that needs no retrieval and no external checker. The paper is therefore a worked example of a question we care about: can a per-unit account of a behaviour be nominated from activations, survive resampling, and then be confirmed causally.

## What they studied
Nine citation-generating models spanning Qwen2.5 (14B, 32B), Qwen3-30B-A3B (base and instruct), Moonlight-16B-A3B, Mistral-Small-24B and three DeepSeek-R1 distills, prompted for references on 50 computer-science topics under 8 citation styles at 5, 10 and 15 references per prompt (roughly 12,000 references per model, 108,000 in total). Each reference is decomposed into five fields — title, authors, venue, year, DOI — and verification labels each field against OpenAlex plus a web-grounded judge. Probing and neuron localisation run on one model, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, with a Mistral replication of the cross-field heatmap only. The unit of analysis moves through three scopes: whole model (error rates), hidden-state token (linear probes), then individual FFN intermediate neurons (27,648 per layer over 64 layers).

## How they did it
Data: models generate JSON-structured references, and a two-stage verifier labels each field — OpenAlex DOI lookup or top-10 title search first, then a web-search judge for anything marked partial or unsupported; 200 labels were double-annotated by experts with 93% agreement. Probing: each reference is re-serialised with XML-style field tags, character offsets are mapped to token spans, per-token hidden states over the field span become training instances, and an L2 logistic probe is trained per layer per field with topic-level (not reference-level) train/test split and balanced classes. Probes are then crossed: each field's probe is scored on every other field's test set. Localisation: for every (layer, neuron) they compute a normalised contribution score — the neuron's activation magnitude times the norm of its down-projection column, divided by the norm of the layer's FFN output, averaged over the field's tokens — giving a 1.77M-dimensional feature per reference. Sparse candidates come from an elastic-net logistic regression (l1 ratio 0.8, grid-searched strength); stability filtering repeats that fit on 20 bootstrap subsamples drawn at 50% and keeps neurons selected in more than 60% of them, restricted to positive weights. Intervention: a forward pre-hook scales the selected neuron's activation by a factor β (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4) while every other neuron is untouched, and references are regenerated and re-verified under greedy decoding. Three arms: suppression, amplification, and a random-neuron suppression control at the same count over 5 trials.

## What counts as an explanation here
A sparse, field-specific set of named FFN neurons — 224, 78, 129, 51 and 30 positive-weight neurons for title, authors, year, venue and DOI — retained from 1,769,472 candidates per field, i.e. at most 0.013% of the space. The account is a selection with an associated direction (positive weights read as pro-hallucination) rather than a feature dictionary or a circuit; it is explicitly partial and its purpose is prediction rather than reconstruction, namely that amplifying these units makes the error worse and suppressing them makes it rarer. The probe layer profiles (each field's AUC-by-depth trajectory and peak layer) are a second, thinner form of explanation: a statement that each field's hallucination is linearly readable and has its own depth signature.

## How they judged success
Probe AUC per layer, in-field and cross-field; Spearman rank correlation between depth and AUC with Fisher z-tests across field pairs and a permutation test on the dispersion of the correlations; bootstrap intervals on the peak layer; per-field verification accuracy before and after intervention; Friedman-style paired comparisons over the five fields using the one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (n = 5, or n = 4 with the tied Venue pair dropped); a random-neuron suppression control at matched count; a label-permutation run of the whole selection pipeline as a null; the Meinshausen–Bühlmann false-discovery bound for stability selection; and a JSON-schema-validity check to rule out the intervention destroying generation rather than improving it.

## What they found
Field difficulty is strongly ordered and stable: authors is the worst field everywhere (correct-generation rates below 14% at 15 references, hallucination rates 0.86–0.89 across styles), then venue, then title and year roughly tied, with DOI the most model-dependent (53.5% for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct against 17–30% elsewhere). Citation style is irrelevant — the largest spread between any two of eight styles is under 0.04 and no Kruskal–Wallis test over the five fields reaches significance (p from 0.058 to 0.582). Accuracy is highest for the first two references in a prompt and collapses from the third onward. Reasoning-oriented distillation hurts recall badly: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen2.5-14B reaches 0.4% total accuracy at 15 references against its undistilled counterpart's 2.9%. Layer probes separate by field: all fields dip around layers 6–10, authors recovers to peak 0.935 at layer 46, title peaks at the last layer, year declines monotonically (ρ = −0.52) while DOI, authors and title rise (ρ = 0.763, 0.600, 0.554), and a permutation test rejects a shared depth profile (p < 0.0001). Cross-field transfer is near chance: diagonal AUC 0.812–0.922, off-diagonal 0.46–0.59. Amplifying the selected neurons at β = 4 drops accuracy on all five targeted fields (title 76.3% → 4.7%, authors 17.5% → 8.5%, mean −25.5 points, p = 0.031), suppressing them at β = 0 raises title by 6.5 and authors by 6.2 points, and random suppression of equally many neurons degrades every field uniformly (p = 0.031 for that degradation). Specificity of suppression against the random arm is positive on four of five fields with a mean of +8.6 points, reported at p = 0.062.

## Limits and open problems they name
Five fields is the whole effective sample size, so every paired test is a sign test on five (or four) numbers and the specificity claim misses the conventional threshold. Fluency beyond JSON validity is not evaluated. Probing and localisation sit on a single model and 50 computer-science topics, and the only cross-model evidence is a heatmap on Mistral where the field separation is visibly weaker — so the authors themselves print that the degree of modularity is model-dependent while the existence of a decodable signal generalises. The intervention table is run on a two-style, ten-topic, five-reference sample whose baseline differs from the full-run baseline, and the paper attributes this to topic variance rather than re-measuring it on the full set. Two cells cut against the story and are reported honestly: venue suppression matches the random control exactly (35.1% vs 35.2%) because its neurons are scattered over 30 layers, and DOI amplification at β = 4 raises year and venue accuracy while lowering DOI's own.

## What we take from it
Four things, in descending order of transferability. (1) **A stability-selection discipline we should copy wholesale**: nominate candidate units with an elastic net, then keep only units selected in more than 60% of bootstrap subsamples at a 50% subsample ratio, then run the whole pipeline on permuted labels to show that no unit clears the threshold under the null, and quote the false-discovery bound beside the set size. This is the folder's first concrete recipe for the question three previous runs left open — whether a per-unit *set* survives repeated resampling — and it is cheap: the resampling happens on a precomputed 1.77M feature matrix, not on forward passes. (2) **The intervention scale ladder is the right shape**: β ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} with amplification as the necessity-side test and suppression as the sufficiency-side test, plus a matched-count random arm, plus a check that the output format does not collapse under intervention. Their own weak spots are the caution labels: the random arm is matched on **count only**, never on layer or layer-band composition, and venue is the field where that omission bites — a matched-by-band control would have separated "venue neurons are real but scattered" from "venue neurons are indistinguishable from random". (3) **A matched-count random control in a model with 1.77M units is a vanishingly small perturbation, so the control's own uniform degradation is the interesting number**: the random arm moved every field down (title −16.4 points), which says ablation of any 224 FFN neurons is not inert, and a "targeted beats random" claim must therefore be reported as a *difference in direction* (up against down) rather than as a difference in magnitude. (4) **A warning about the corpus side**: topic-level splitting, a 1:1 class balance by downsampling and an emitter that is not one of the evaluated models are the three guards that make the probe honest here, and the paper's own appendix shows why the fourth guard is missing — a two-style, ten-topic intervention sample with a baseline that does not match the full-run baseline is a different population, and per-topic accuracy ranging from near 0% to over 90% means the sample's composition, not the intervention, is a first-order confound. Also useful as a formatting pointer: this is a failure whose field decomposition is a structure the model itself respects in its depth profiles, which is the cleanest recent example of "the sub-units of the behaviour are the sub-spans of the input" — relevant to how we should cut units when a behaviour is composite.
