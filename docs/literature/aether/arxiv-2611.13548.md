# Massive Activations Confound Sparse Autoencoders

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.13548
- **Authors / organisation:** Nathan Delisle, Chenhao Tan — University of Chicago
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13548
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, ~154k raw chars: abstract, §1–§8, references ~50 scanned, Appendices A–M incl. Tables 2–64; figures 1–6 via captions only; accepted at NeurIPS 2026 as a poster)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The paper starts from an anomaly that should not exist. If SAE features are the model's units, the *most* important ones — the ones whose removal most damages the model — ought to be the most reliably rediscovered, since they are the ones the network most depends on. The authors observe the opposite, and ask why, and whether it can be fixed without abandoning the dictionary. Their answer locates the cause in a property of the residual stream rather than of the training algorithm: in most layers of most models a single direction carries almost all the variance, that direction has no unique sparse decomposition, and each training run therefore resolves it differently. The larger point is about measurement: two standard SAE scores, variance explained and ablation damage, are both dominated by a component that carries no feature structure, so both can read near-perfect while the thing they are supposed to measure remains unmeasured. This is a paper about an evaluation instrument that fails in one identifiable direction, and it is written to be usable by anyone already holding a dictionary.

## What they studied

Ten models: GPT-2, Pythia-70M-deduped / 160M / 1.4B / 6.9B, Llama-3.1-8B / 70B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Gemma-2-2B / 9B. Unit of analysis is the SAE **feature** (a decoder direction plus a coefficient), grouped into a second unit the paper invents for the purpose: the **PC1 tile**, a feature whose decoder is partially aligned with the first principal component of the activations and which exists, in that run, to reconstruct part of that component. Activations are residual-stream outputs of the middle block (plus additional layers in appendices) over five million tokens of WikiText-103, with padding excluded, and one document per sequence. Dictionary width equals 4× activation dimension, TopK budget 64, twenty thousand Adam steps, plus cross-checks on BatchTopK, JumpReLU with scheduled thresholds, Gated, ReLU+ and crosscoders, and an extension to DINOv2 patch activations. Released dictionaries (Gemma Scope, Gemma Scope 2, Llama Scope, OpenAI GPT-2, Qwen Scope) are re-scored rather than retrained.

## How they did it

The design is a comparison of rankings across independently seeded runs, with the confound then isolated, then removed. **Measured:** for each model, five seeds (some experiments three); features are ranked by zero-ablation importance, the increase in cross-entropy when the feature is set to zero in the SAE reconstruction; a feature counts as shared between two runs if its decoder's best cosine to any direction in the other run clears 0.7; and the sharing rate of the top fifty is compared against the dictionary-wide sharing rate — a within-model null that removes the need for a synthetic baseline. Sharing is repeated under mean ablation, gradient-times-activation, integrated gradients, and three resample-ablation rules (marginal, support-preserving, position-stratified), so the finding is not an artefact of zeroing. **Isolated:** every feature is placed on an alignment axis against PC1, and sharing is measured within alignment bands; the three bands that emerge are unaligned features, tiles at intermediate alignment, and a rare canonical feature at high alignment. Individual and joint ablations of the tiles' parallel and orthogonal components test whether their damage comes from removing PC1 or from breaking cancellation outside it. **Removed:** the repair trains the SAE on activations with the PC1 projection subtracted and adds that projection back to the reconstruction before splicing, so the model still receives its attention-sink signal; a post-hoc variant simply excludes PC1-aligned features from the ranking pool and leaves the dictionary untouched. **Checked for consequences:** automated interpretability where one instruction-tuned model writes an explanation and another scores whether it predicts firing on held-out contexts (Qwen2.5-32B and Mistral-Small-24B exchanged in both roles); sparse probing on four classification tasks; and a subject–verb-agreement editing task with a matched control edit that changes only a distractor noun, so success requires the donor's verb preference to appear *and* the control's not to. A magnitude-penalty sweep and a canonical-initialisation experiment test whether the objective or the initialisation is responsible, and a held-out content-error comparison tests whether the repair buys reproducibility by damaging reconstruction.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **feature dictionary together with a decomposition of the activation space**, and the paper's own contribution is a claim about what the dictionary is *not* entitled to explain. The confound is stated as a rank-one non-identifiability result: given an isolated rank-one signal, infinitely many TopK dictionaries reconstruct it with zero error (Proposition C.1 constructs them explicitly), so on that component a dictionary is one draw from an equivalence class rather than a description of structure. The repair restores a well-posedness condition rather than producing new explanations — the dominant component is handed to a fixed, single, non-learned coefficient and the dictionary is asked to explain only the residual. Prediction enters at the end, and weakly: the corrected ranking's features are tested as causal handles by whether patching them flips agreement, and the measured answer is that some do and the original ranking's features do not at all. Nothing here claims a mechanism; the claim is about which measurements can bear on one.

## How they judged success

Named statistics, each with a control arm: **top-k feature sharing** at a 0.7 best-match cosine with the dictionary-wide rate as the null, repeated at k = 10, 50, 150, 300 and over all twenty ordered seed pairs, with 95% bootstrap intervals resampling the ranking articles; **content fraction of variance unexplained**, the FVU measured outside PC1 after projecting it out; **the dominance factor**, the theoretical ratio between content and total FVU implied by PC1's variance share, checked against the measured ratio (relative error under 0.2%); **splice cost**, the cross-entropy increase from replacing activations with their reconstruction; **zero-ablation effect** in nats per prediction for the selected top fifty; **feature-detection AUROC** for machine-written explanations against both random inactive contexts and "hard" negatives where a similar decoder fires; **probe accuracy** on SST-2, AG News, Amazon Polarity and Emotion; and **agreement-edit success** with a control-edit pass rate as a precondition. Controls worth naming: a random-direction bypass of the same kind (to separate "removing the dominant direction" from "removing a direction"), matched coefficient budgets (63 sparse plus the PC1 coefficient against 64 sparse), a double-width dictionary and a canonical-initialisation arm against the tiling hypothesis, an exclusion of the first token position, and a per-objective comparison so TopK's behaviour is not mistaken for SAE behaviour.

## What they found

The inversion is real and scales with anisotropy. In the five models where PC1 explains at least 93% of activation variance, the top fifty important features are shared *less* than a random fifty: an average of 43.5–48 of the fifty have no match in another run, where a random draw leaves 19–27 unmatched. Qwen-2.5-7B is worst at 6% top-50 sharing; Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-9B, the two least anisotropic models at 78% and 70% PC1 variance, run at 87% and 92%; the correlation between top-50 sharing and PC1 share is strong and negative (Pearson −0.77, Spearman −0.72). The tiles are about 1% of living features (38–106 per dictionary) but account for 2–66 times their share of ablation importance, and their cross-seed sharing is 0–2.6% against a dictionary rate of roughly 47–66%. Ablating only the orthogonal part of a top-50 feature's contribution reproduces 71–107% of the whole-feature loss increase (Table 12), which is the mechanistic statement: the damage comes from destroying cancellations among components outside PC1, not from removing PC1. The fidelity confound is exact: Qwen's SAEs report 99.96% total variance explained while leaving 33.0% of content variance unexplained, and the reported score understates content error by the dominance factor (891.5% PC1 share there). Cause: TopK constrains only which coefficients fire, so decompositions of the rank-one part are equally free; adding a magnitude penalty moves GPT-2 top-50 sharing from 6.4% to 94.9% with mean active features moving 64.00 → 63.47 and content FVU 0.1394 → 0.1387, and canonical initialisation moves it from 5.5% to 68% at the cost of worse reconstruction (median content FVU 0.149 → 0.165). Across objectives, TopK, BatchTopK and scheduled JumpReLU all show the inversion while ReLU+, which penalises coefficients directly, sits at 95–100% top-50 sharing everywhere. Repair: bypass lifts top-50 sharing from 4–39.5% to 73–99.5% and lowers content FVU by 0.8–33.9%, post-hoc exclusion reaches 77.5–96% sharing with the dictionary's fidelity unchanged, and released Gemma Scope 2 dictionaries show content FVU close to total FVU (median ratio 2.1) while unfiltered Qwen Scope shows 98.8. Behaviour does not fully follow the metric: standard top-50 features yield 0% agreement-edit success in GPT-2, Pythia-1.4B and Qwen while post-hoc selection yields 9.0%, 31.9% and 4.2% (bypass 7.1%, 37.5%, 2.0%), Gemma-2-2B sits at 64–65% under every condition including standard, and sparse probing moves by under one point on average in any model. Automated-interpretability AUROC for top features improves after bypass on GPT-2, Pythia-160M/1.4B/6.9B and Qwen, and *falls* on Llama-3.1-8B (−0.093 with Qwen explaining, −0.097 with Mistral) and slightly on Gemma-2-2B.

## Limits and open problems they name

Scope is stated by the authors as the paper's own boundary. The effect is a property of highly anisotropic models and of middle layers: at Pythia-1.4B's final block, where PC1 is 11% of variance, sharing is already 96% and bypass does nothing. Token-position conventions change the answer drastically — excluding the first position drops PC1 variance in GPT-2 and Llama-3.1-8B from ~96% to ~3%, but Qwen stays at 99.7% — so "massive activations" is not one phenomenon across families. The rank-one result is an existence proof for an isolated component, not a guarantee about a real activation distribution. The bypass does not improve content reconstruction much in unaffected models (0.8–4.9% outside Qwen), and a substantial content error remains everywhere (12–33% at 64 coefficients), so the paper is explicit that linear sparse decomposition is still missing something. Behavioural transfer is weak and heterogeneous: Gemma-2-2B's agreement-edit success is identical at 64–65% across the three learned conditions (standard, post-hoc, bypass) while its random arm is 0%, a pattern the authors record without explaining; IOI success stays in the low single digits for every model; Pythia-6.9B and Pythia-160M show 0% under all arms. Sparse probing and automated interpretability disagree in sign across models, so neither is a clean arbiter. And the paper closes by asking the question it cannot answer: whether other interpretability methods are confounded by the same dominant structure.

## What we take from it

A **measure**, a **null**, and a **warning**, in that order. (1) The dominance factor is the cheapest instrument in the paper and we should compute it for every activation store we probe: the share of variance along the top principal component tells us in advance that any variance-weighted or reconstruction-weighted score we report will be grading us on one direction, and the content-FVU correction (project PC1 out, measure the rest) costs one eigendecomposition. Any probe we fit on raw residual states is exposed to the same confound — the paper's own Table 46 shows PC1 alone achieves R² ≈ 0.9998² against token norm and carries nearly all of the norm signal, and Table 44 shows a probe on PC1 alone sits near chance on four classification tasks, so the direction is information-rich about *position and norm* and information-poor about content. (2) The null for a stability claim is the dictionary-wide sharing rate of the *same* model, not a random-direction or random-feature baseline; the paper's whole finding is that the intuitive baseline (important features should recur more) is wrong, and only the within-dictionary comparison exposes it. This is the run-216 sampling lesson in a second costume: the subset that ranks highest on the statistic you care about is exactly the subset most likely to be an artefact. (3) The warning is for our own white-box ambition: an explanation that reconstructs a rank-one component to five decimal places has explained nothing, and the check is to ask what fraction of the residual it accounts for. Two smaller transfers: the post-hoc variant is a free correction we can apply to any released dictionary without retraining, and the component-wise ablation (parallel versus orthogonal) is a general recipe for locating whether damage from ablating a unit comes from that unit's own contribution or from its role in cancelling others — the same question we would want answered for any probe direction we claim is causally load-bearing.
