# Can Mechanistic Interpretability Help Explain LLM Agent Failures? A Case Study on Web Element Hallucination

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.18514
- **Authors / organisation:** Mohamed Aghzal, Mor Geva, Gregory J. Stein, Ziyu Yao; George Mason University and Tel Aviv University
- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18514
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML v1, §§1–6, appendices A–L; appendix F intervention table read as cells)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Mechanistic interpretability works on narrow tasks with fixed inputs, and its case-study successes share assumptions nobody states — that a computation lives in a few positions and components at one timestep, that a clean baseline exists to patch against, and that one linear direction carries one concept. LLM agents violate all three: the context grows, the environment changes every step, and the failure emerges over a trajectory. The authors ask how far the standard toolkit gets on a real agentic failure, and they pick one that is sharply scorable: a web agent emitting an action whose element selector does not appear in the list of actions it was given. The goal is less to explain this failure than to find where the tools break and what would have to be built instead.

## What they studied
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 28 layers, driving live websites from Online-Mind2Web under a minimal CLICK/SCROLL/TYPE/HOVER action space. 300 episodes and 2,071 actions on which an action list was supplied to the model, of which 32.6% were hallucinated (the chosen selector absent from the list). Unit of analysis varies by tool: attention heads and FFN layers, residual-stream positions (end of prompt, start of the selector, start of the selector value), a linear probe direction, and a pretrained BatchTopK SAE of 131,072 features on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct read at layer 27.

## How they did it
Roll out the agent on the benchmark tasks, log every action, and label it valid or hallucinated against the supplied list. Then, on the valid cases, amplify each layer's FFN output and inject Gaussian noise into each head's projection in turn to see which single components can break grounding; follow with joint interventions that scale sets of attention heads against the attention output and dampen sets of FFNs at the selector-value position, sweeping coefficients, and score each configuration by fix rate on hallucinated cases and break rate on valid ones. Use activation patching with Gaussian-noise corruption over three input spans (selector start, task description, action list) and restore clean residual, attention and FFN states layer by layer to measure recovered probability mass. Train independent linear probes at all 28 layers for three positions on an episode-level 70/15/15 split (1,433 / 282 / 356 actions), then steer with the normalised probe weights in un-normalised activation space over a coefficient sweep, choosing the sign that points toward the valid side. Finally reformat the action list from a JSON array into a numbered markdown list, recollect activations, and repeat the probe and steering evaluation with the same vectors. A logit-lens pass decodes intermediate layers into vocabulary, and a pretrained SAE is applied at layer 27 taking the max activation per feature at the selector-value position and ranking features by class difference.

## What counts as an explanation here
Explicitly a diagnostic of explanation *forms* rather than a delivered explanation. The paper enumerates what each standard artifact would have to give — a circuit (subgraph implementing an algorithm), a feature direction (probe or SAE latent), or a causal attribution to an input span — and then reports, for each, whether it is present, whether it can be acted on, and what property of the agent setting defeats it. Its own positive artifact is small and predictive: a linear direction readable before generation that can be pushed to change the outcome. Its honest summary is that these tools can *detect* the decision before it is made and *steer* away from it, but cannot *explain* how it is made.

## How they judged success
Probe discrimination as held-out ROC-AUC per layer and position on an episode-level split; fix rate (share of confirmed hallucinations that become valid under steering) against break rate (share of valid actions pushed into hallucination), reported per coefficient and per (position, layer) configuration; joint-intervention fix/break and net difference across a large configuration sweep; activation-patching recovery of probability mass against a corrupted baseline; logit-lens token frequencies; SAE top features by class difference with their most-promoted unembedding tokens. Transfer is tested by one out-of-distribution prompt format change, not by a new benchmark. Controls are partial: break rate is the control on all steering, and single-component interventions are the control on the joint ones, but there is no random-direction steering baseline, and no probe retrained on the reformatted list to separate "the direction is format-bound" from "the data shifted".

## What they found
A probe on the pre-generation prompt-end position already separates hallucinated from valid actions at 0.80 AUC in the best layer (18 of 28), which means the commitment is encoded before a token is emitted; signal is stronger earlier in the selector, and present from layer 0 by the selector-value position. Steering with the selector-start layer-17 probe direction fixes 85.4% of hallucinations at its best coefficient while breaking 7.0% of valid actions — the headline number — with a sharply non-monotonic profile: the same vector at one step larger fixes 48.8% and breaks 30.2%, and at the largest it fixes 0% and breaks 96.9%. Under the numbered-list format the same vector drops to 58.1% fix at the same 7.0% break, a 27-point loss attributable to format alone. Circuit-side attempts are weak: the best joint attention/FFN configuration fixes 8.9% of hallucinations at 2.4% break, top-causal and top-correlational head selections give 4.4–6.3% fix, and the configurations that fix more (up to 100%) do so only while breaking everything, so the net-improvement ordering collapses. Single-component interventions never reproduce natural hallucinations — amplified FFNs and noised heads produce malformed selector strings rather than the well-formed but nonexistent elements the model actually emits. Activation patching makes the action list the dominant causal span (corrupted probability mass 0.325 against 0.682 and 0.672 for the other spans; 0.360 recovered by restoring the clean residual at layer 27, against 0.075 for attention and 0.067 for FFN alone), but the authors stress that this does not identify *which* property of the list drives the commitment. Corrupting the action list leaves about half of valid steps unaffected because the agent recovers the value from its own earlier actions. Constructing valid/hallucinated pairs by removing the chosen action yields a hallucination in only 38% of cases and diverges the trajectory within one step (selector agreement ~25%, DOM Jaccard 0.52). Pretrained SAE features separating the classes mostly promote unrelated tokens. Logit lens shows hallucinating trials converging on a generic vocabulary of plausible UI actions (Search, Enter, Find, Explore) at layers 21–27, in one case through the Chinese word for "Search" before the English one — a model resolving what the action *should* be rather than grounding it in the page.

## Limits and open problems they name
One model, one benchmark, one failure mode, one GPU; no agent-specific SAE, no counterfactual construction method that works in stateful environments, no agentic MI benchmark with ground truth for either the failure or its trigger, and no account of when a commitment forms across timesteps. Universality across agent types is left open. The paper's own framing concedes that the probe direction is entangled with the prompt format rather than with the hallucination concept, so its causal role is demonstrated at a position and a format, not for the property.

## What we take from it
*(method, measure, warning)*. The **two-number intervention report** is the reusable measure: every steering result should be `(fix, break)` at each coefficient, because here the same vector is a 85/7 success at one step and a 0/97 catastrophe two steps later, and a paper reporting only the best fix rate would have hidden a cliff inside its own sweep. The **pre-generation probe AUC** is the cheap version of the same claim and belongs in our battery as the default first check for any sequential setting: if a decision is linearly decodable at the end of the prompt, the "explanation" is a state readout and its position tells you how early the commitment formed. The **format swap** is the control we should run by default on any probe we intend to steer with — one syntactic change to the same information, same items, same vector — and the 27-point collapse is exactly the *coupled to the design, not to the concept* failure that a probe-vs-steering comparison without a format arm cannot detect. The **patching result against a corrupted span** is a warning about attribution without a handle: knowing the action list matters recovers a third of the mass but names no property, and the honest report of that is "a causal contributor was located, not a mechanism". Two more carried details: the single-component interventions produce qualitatively *different* failures from the natural ones, which is a general caution that a synthetic intervention reproducing the label is not reproducing the phenomenon; and half the valid steps survive corruption of the action list because the answer is also in the history, so the input span carrying a decision is itself sample-dependent. The roadmap is the useful agenda item for us: feature discovery on interaction data rather than text, and counterfactuals constructed inside a stateful environment.
