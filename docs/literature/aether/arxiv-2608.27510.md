# How Do Linear Probes Emerge? A Circuit-Tracing Framework with Concept-Targeted Attribution

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.27510
- **Authors / organisation:** Vedant Palit and four co-authors (corresponding author Zhijing Jin); Tübingen AI Center, University of Tübingen affiliations acknowledged; EMNLP 2026 Main Conference
- **Date:** 2026-08-27
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27510
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Attribution graphs built on transcoders trace how sparse features push probability onto one specific next token. The authors argue that this makes every such graph a prisoner of the token: a model can register a concept internally and still emit something innocuous, so a logit-rooted graph explains the safe continuation and says nothing about how the toxicity was detected in the first place. Probes have the mirror-image problem — they answer whether a concept is decodable at a layer but not which computations make it decodable, why probe quality varies by layer and prompt, or how a concept relates to the token actually produced. The stated goal is to close that gap by swapping the scalar target of attribution, from a logit to a probe score, so probes stop being diagnostics and become objects that carry circuits. Safety auditing is the motivating application: a probe-based monitor is only as trustworthy as an account of what it is reading.

## What they studied

Two small open models — Gemma-2-2B (25 probed layers) and Llama-3.2-1B (16) — with Cross-Layer Transcoders, over four concept probes: toxicity (ParadeTox pairs, so topic is held fixed), sentiment (SST-2), reasoning (HotpotQA multi-hop against TriviaQA single-hop, both in question-answering form so syntax is controlled), and truthfulness (three balanced Geometry of Truth subsets: counterfactuals, cities, translation). Probes are mass-mean (difference-of-means) directions trained on last-token residual activations at every layer, 5,000 examples per class, filtered to 60 tokens or fewer; a PCA-subspace LDA correction is used for *accuracy scoring only*, while the raw difference-of-means direction is what the attribution targets. Concept directions are Gram-Schmidt orthogonalised against each other and end up near-orthogonal (largest absolute cosine 0.069). The unit of analysis is the transcoder feature node, with edges as local attributions, aggregated into per-prompt graphs.

## How they did it

Each attribution graph is a Jacobian-based propagation through the transcoder's cross-layer decoder vectors and encoder vectors, with normalisation, attention and MLP nonlinearities frozen under stop-gradient, pruned to the features explaining 80% of the final effect and edges explaining 95% of retained edge effect. In the standard setup the target scalar is a next-token logit; in Concept-Targeted Attribution (CTA) it is the probe score, the inner product of a concept direction with the residual stream at a token position — explicitly a per-prompt scalar, not the dataset-level accuracy. Both graph types are built on the same prompts, at a per-concept "belief layer" chosen as the layer of steepest single-step rise in mean positive-class probe score, constrained to sit inside an accuracy plateau within 2-8% of the peak. Causal dissociation then ablates three disjoint node subsets per prompt — probe-exclusive (probe graph minus logit graph), logit-exclusive, and shared — and measures two outcomes: the change in probe score and the top-1 next-token flip rate. Separately, 35 graph-structural features (8 global, 15 node statistics, 12 topological) are averaged over 100 prompts per (concept, layer) pair and used to regress probe validation accuracy, under 5-fold cross-validation whose folds are whole (concept, layer) groups and with a within-concept de-meaned target as a confound check. At the feature level, a binary presence vector over the top 100 activation-ranked transcoder features per prompt is ridge-regressed onto the probe score margin, and a Fisher-exact enrichment test on incorrect-to-correct prompts isolates "rise features". Consistency is measured two ways: presence rate of the top predictive features against a random feature at the belief and best-accuracy layers, and consecutive-layer Jaccard overlap.

## What counts as an explanation here

A probe-specific circuit: a feature-node graph with attribution edges that carries the claim that these sparse components assembled this concept direction for this prompt, paired with a causal partition of that graph into probe-exclusive, logit-exclusive and shared nodes whose ablation effects are reported as numbers. It is a partial explanation in two declared senses — it inherits whatever the Cross-Layer Transcoder conflates or omits, and the pruned graph is a named fraction of the effect. It does predict: an accuracy-across-layers target from structure alone, and a per-prompt probe margin from feature presence. The declared novelty is the target, not the graph machinery, and the authors lean on that heavily — the same tracer, a different scalar at the root.

## How they judged success

Dissociation is judged by a double requirement on the same prompts: probe-exclusive ablation must move the concept score while leaving the top-1 token alone, and logit-exclusive ablation must flip the token while leaving the concept score near baseline — with no ablation ("clean") as the reference point. Prediction quality is judged by Spearman ρ and R² under group-aware 5-fold cross-validation, against a layer-mean baseline that predicts each layer's average accuracy across concepts, plus within-concept de-meaning, a 200-shuffle group-aware permutation test, and a bootstrap 95% interval from 500 out-of-bag group resamples. Feature-level claims lean on presence frequencies compared with a random feature, Fisher exact tests, and per-layer Spearman curves. Stability is judged by consecutive-layer Jaccard and by cross-prompt presence enrichment relative to random. Effect sizes for the high- versus low-accuracy layer separation are reported as Cohen's d per concept.

## What they found

The double dissociation holds on both models. On Gemma-2-2B, ablating probe-exclusive nodes drops the toxicity score from 102.52 to 41.28 with a 0% top-1 flip, while ablating logit-exclusive nodes leaves the score at 101.82 and flips the token 100% of the time; truthfulness follows the same pattern (41.87 to 29.84 with 25% flips on the probe side, 41.84 with 100% flips on the logit side). Reasoning flips 92% rather than 100% under logit-exclusive ablation. On Llama-3.2-1B the pattern replicates with 0% flips on the probe-exclusive arm for all four concepts and 100% on the logit-exclusive arm. Table 2's structure-to-accuracy regression on Gemma reaches ρ = 0.91 and R² = 0.84 for gradient boosting, against ρ = 0.65 for ridge and 0.08 for the layer baseline; the de-meaned target is even stronger at ρ = 0.925, and the permutation null sits at about −0.005 ± 0.128. High-accuracy layers have more concentrated influence (higher Gini), larger maximum feature activation, larger maximum edge weight and higher density, with Cohen's d from +0.88 to +1.68 on toxicity. Llama replicates the raw signal (ρ = 0.808, R² = 0.850) but its de-meaned signal is much weaker (ρ = 0.34, interval straddling zero), and held-out-concept transfer ranges from −0.74 to +0.48. Feature-level predictability is strongly concept-dependent: toxicity keeps ρ above 0.66 at every layer and peaks at 0.86, sentiment's ridge R² collapses to 0.08 at its best layer, reasoning peaks at R² 0.14, truthfulness peaks at ρ 0.66 mid-network. Top predictive features appear 6-21 times more often than a random feature across prompts, and consecutive-layer Jaccard rises to about 0.9 by the early middle layers and stays there.

## Limits and open problems they name

The method inherits the transcoder: anything the Cross-Layer Transcoder conflates, omits, or spreads across polysemantic features is invisible to the graph, and the sparsity-reconstruction trade-off silently decides which features can appear, without any concept-specific tuning. Probes are called a necessary but not sufficient criterion for conceptual validity — a probe trained on a correlated label distribution can learn style rather than the concept, and toxicity's best features fire on slurs and derogatory register, which is exactly that worry. The feature-presence analysis throws away activation magnitude and therefore misses concepts whose signal lives in geometry rather than feature identity; sentiment and reasoning are the measured casualties. They ask for broader cross-architecture replication, finer steering interventions, and richer concept taxonomies. Not raised by them but visible in their own tables: every arm sits at 1-2B parameters, the belief layer is chosen by a score-rise heuristic whose plateau window is set by hand, and the Llama truthfulness probe at layer 1 has roughly 11 exclusive nodes and 55.2% accuracy, so the "0% flip" there is a statement about a circuit too weak to move anything.

## What we take from it

A method, a measure, and a framing. The method: the attribution target is a free parameter, and a read-out you already trust (a probe direction) can be rooted into the same graph machinery as a logit — so a white-box model need not choose between explaining outputs and explaining internals. The measure: probe score change and top-1 flip rate as two outcomes on the same ablated graph, which is the cheapest double dissociation in this folder and the only one that separates "the model knows" from "the model says". The framing warning: a graph partitioned into exclusive and shared nodes exposes how much of a concept's representation is routed through the machinery it shares with generation — truthfulness on Gemma leaks a 93% score drop and a 67% flip into the shared subgraph, and the authors correctly identify the shared component, not the exclusive one, as where the blurring lives. The prediction result is usable and honest about its own brittleness: structure predicts accuracy strongly on one model and barely de-meaned on the other, and fails to transfer across concepts, so it is a within-model, within-concept signal. Also a dataset-level shortcut worth stealing: pairing toxic and detoxified sentences with identical topics is what forces the probe off surface keywords, and anchoring reasoning positives and negatives both in question-answering form is what removes syntax as a confound.
