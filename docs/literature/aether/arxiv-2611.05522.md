# From Activation to Intervention: Interpreting Sparse Visual Features through Reconstruction

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.05522
- **Authors / organisation:** Jianyang Gu, Jacob Beattie, Mridul Khurana, Julia Chae, Xi Xiao, Cheng Zhang, Anuj Karpatne, Wei-Lun Chao, Sara Beery, Tanya Berger-Wolf (The Ohio State University; MIT; Virginia Tech; University of Alabama, Birmingham; Texas A&M University; Boston University — as printed)
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05522
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
A sparse feature in a vision transformer is usually explained by showing the images and the pixels where it fires hardest. The authors point out that this evidence is systematically ambiguous in fine-grained domains: two different dictionary dimensions can fire on nearly the same photographs and the nearly the same regions while encoding different properties, because the properties themselves co-occur — presence versus density of a pattern, a colour versus the pose that shows it. When a feature's top examples all look alike, the activation record says *where the evidence is* and not *which aspect of it* the unit responds to. The larger goal is a feature explanation that is evidence about what the unit controls rather than about what accompanies it, and their route is to make the consequence of an edit visible: intervene on the representation along the feature's own decoder direction and decode the result back into an image, so the change in appearance is the explanation.

## What they studied
Two frozen vision encoders, DINOv3-L/16 and SigLIP2-L/16 (ViT-large, patch 16), each with purpose-trained BatchTopK sparse autoencoders: a *patch* SAE on penultimate-layer patch tokens (1,024-dimensional input, 16,384-dimensional dictionary, k = 32), and — for DINOv3 only, since SigLIP 2 has no conventional [CLS] token — a *[CLS]* SAE on the final-block class token (1,024 in, 4,096 out, k = 32, dead-feature threshold 100,000 presentations). Four datasets: NABirds, CUB, Cars and ImageNet. Images are decoded from patch representations by a pretrained RAEv2 representation decoder (the "dinov3l-k1" and "siglip2l-k1" variants). The unit of analysis is the single sparse dimension; for the [CLS] side the paper adds a second unit, the set of patch dimensions attributed to it.

## How they did it
Two interventions, one per unit. For a *patch* feature the recipe keeps the original token rather than decoding edited SAE codes, and adds only the feature's own share of the change — the edited code minus the original, pushed through the decoder, which collapses to a multiple of the decoder column since everything else cancels. This isolates the edit from SAE reconstruction error across the other 16,383 dimensions and lets the scale factor go below zero, so a feature can be *suppressed* past its original value, not just switched off. The edit is confined to the feature's dominant spatial support: patches are ranked by activation and the smallest set is kept whose activations account for at least 80% of the mass, with a floor of four patches. The edited tokens pass through the remaining encoder blocks and out through the RAE decoder, and the same feature is compared at a few scales from suppression to amplification. For a *[CLS]* feature the decoder is unusable directly — it consumes patch tokens, not the class token — so the target activation is backpropagated to patch gradients, the patch set carrying 80% of gradient-norm mass is selected, each patch-SAE dimension's sensitivity is read off as the alignment between the patch gradient and that dimension's decoder column, features are ranked by activation times absolute sensitivity (absolute so opposite-sign contributions in different locations do not cancel), the top k are kept, and their decoder columns are combined with *signed* sensitivity weights and renormalised per patch so the intervention magnitude is a fraction of the patch tensor's Frobenius norm. That norm-matching is deliberate: the dense baseline — steering along the raw patch gradient on the same patches with the same budget — then differs from the sparse version only in *which* direction is used. Success is read back by re-encoding the reconstructed image through the original encoder and measuring round-trip change in the target [CLS] feature.

Explanation quality is tested behaviourally rather than by similarity: for each selected feature, a paired benchmark of held-out image pairs that are almost identical in dense-embedding space (similarity above the 99th percentile of random pairs) but strongly contrasted in the feature's activation (low/high ratio below 0.15, high image above the 85th activation percentile, and, for a pair to count, different activation distributions as well). A GPT-5.6-Terra judge is given an explanation of the feature plus two test images and has to say which one will activate it more. Five evidence formats are compared — a text description of the top examples; top examples with activation overlays; top plus intervention reconstructions; top plus *bottom* examples; and all three combined — over 1,000 dimensions per dataset-encoder cell.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **causal, image-space demonstration**: the semantic content of a dictionary dimension is identified with the visual change that intervening on that dimension produces in the decoded image, across a range of signed strengths. It is partial by construction — it names a property, not a whole behaviour, and the authors are explicit that intervention reconstruction is a *complementary* signal rather than a replacement for activation evidence, with the two doing different jobs (activation locates the contexts a unit fires in; intervention isolates the property it controls). It is predictive in a narrow sense that matters: an explanation built from it should let a third party forecast the unit's response on unseen inputs, which is exactly what the paired benchmark asks. For the [CLS] side the explanation is also **compositional** — a global class-token feature is accounted for by a short list of patch-level dimensions, each with its own activation profile and its own visual effect.

## How they judged success
Four measurement families. (1) *Explanation accuracy* on the paired benchmark: share of image pairs where the judge's answer matches the higher-activation image, per dataset-encoder cell, plus a text-versus-image format ablation with the same evidence. (2) *Intervention agreement*: directional correctness (fraction of non-zero-strength interventions whose response has the intended sign), trajectory monotonicity (fraction of strength curves non-decreasing, with a margin), and mean Spearman correlation between signed strength and round-trip response, reported separately for patch and [CLS] SAEs. (3) *Selectivity*: absolute target change divided by the mean absolute change of the other [CLS] dimensions, above one meaning the target moves more than an average off-target unit, with a response-matched comparison to dense steering and a sweep over strength and over k. (4) *Control probes*: an attribution faithfulness test (steer top-attributed, random-active and bottom-attributed dimensions), an ablation of the spatial attribution rule (activation-weighted versus sensitivity-only), a contrastive-mean direction, and a round-trip stability trace giving injected and final patch norms, internal versus post-reconstruction target change, and image MSE.

## What they found
On explanation accuracy the ordering is stable across the four datasets and both encoders: top examples beat a text description of the same top examples in all eight cells (62.8 → 66.3 on NABirds-DINOv3, 59.0 → 64.3 on CUB-DINOv3, 61.5 → 67.4 on Cars-DINOv3, 74.1 → 74.7 on ImageNet-DINOv3), adding *bottom* examples adds about two points again (66.3 → 68.2, 64.3 → 65.4, 67.4 → 68.4, 74.7 → 71.4 — the one cell where they hurt), and intervention reconstruction on top of bottom examples helps in seven of eight cells, by 1.8 points on NABirds-DINOv3 (68.2 → 73.0) and 1.6 on Cars-SigLIP 2. The exception is Cars-DINOv3, where the full set lands at 74.3 against 73.2 for top-plus-bottom — still above top-only, so the paper's "seven of eight" is accurate for the *increment*, but it is not a monotone climb: intervention on top of top-only is worth far more than bottom examples on top of top-only in NABirds-DINOv3 (73.0 against 68.2), while in Cars-DINOv3 the two are nearly interchangeable (73.2 against 73.0) and combining them buys only 1.1 more; the largest totals sit in the coarse-grained ImageNet cell, where the benchmark is easiest and intervention adds nothing to DINOv3 (76.9 to 76.2) and almost nothing to SigLIP 2 (73.5 to 73.9). Two negative controls carry as much weight as the positive result: CLIP and IoU scores computed on descriptions generated from the same evidence barely move across the four explanation settings (CLIP 0.204 / 0.202 / 0.202 / 0.202; IoU 0.240 / 0.234 / 0.248 / 0.232), so the field's standard similarity measures are flat where the behavioural test separates the formats by up to eleven points; and comparing images against text descriptions of the *same* evidence shows the gap widening as accuracy rises (63.4 vs 62.9; 65.4 vs 64.7; 69.4 vs 67.3; 72.9 vs 67.1, i.e. from 0.5 to 5.8 points), which the authors read as a description having limited capacity while images give the judge a wider comparison space.

On control of [CLS] features, sparse attributed steering is more consistent than dense: agreement rises from 64.4% directional correctness, 63.1% monotonicity and r = 0.71 (patch) to 87.0%, 73.9% and r = 0.93 ([CLS]), and dense steering collapses early — at the smaller strength its round trip recovers 28.7 of an internal 180.9, and at the larger one the internal change of 342.5 comes back with the *opposite sign* at −18.5 while image MSE is 5.5 × 10⁻² against 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ for the sparse version, with final patch norm 16,024.6 against 1,448.0 — the encoder amplifies the dense perturbation by roughly an order of magnitude more, and the RAE then rejects it. The attribution itself is strongly faithful: steering the top-attributed dimensions moves the target [CLS] feature by 7.70 on average against 0.23 for random active and 0.01 for bottom-attributed dimensions, at a change-per-unit-intervention of 1.94% / 0.20% / 0.02%. Activation-weighted selection beats sensitivity-only at high strength because sensitivity-only keeps choosing units that are not there: at k = 128, 96.0% of sensitivity-only's selected dimensions are inactive throughout the selected patch mask. The decomposition is heterogeneous and that is the point — the eight attributed patch dimensions for one [CLS] feature have wildly different activation and sensitivity levels (the top one, 79.14 attribution score, also ranks third by sensitivity; the second, 24.86, ranks 146th), and the highest-ranked unit is the "elsewhere" concept, which is what marks object presence, so its own visual effect is spread over nearly the whole frame and only the aggregation of several such units gives a directed, localisable change.

## Limits and open problems they name
The agreement numbers are the authors' own evidence that control is inexact — 64.4% directional correctness on patch features means more than a third of interventions move the monitored feature the wrong way, which they attribute to the SAE and the RAE rather than to the attribution and put forward as a training signal for both. The method needs a decoder for the representation under study, which they have because RAEv2 was trained on these encoders; nothing here says what happens for a model without one. The dense-versus-sparse comparison holds budget and patch set fixed, so the paper measures the *direction*, not the benefit of an end-to-end intervention pipeline. Not named by the authors but visible in the tables: descriptive metrics are the reason the benchmark exists, so the headline is a judge's forced choice between two images rather than a semantic label; the [CLS] evidence is a single dataset (NABirds) and a single encoder (DINOv3); "a reusable visual direction" is argued from three illustrative images per feature; and no seed variance or significance testing appears on any table, with 1,000 pairs per cell the only source of resolution.

## What we take from it
- **Method (instrument):** confine an intervention to its own support and inject only the difference. Keeping the original activation and adding the decoder-column difference instead of decoding an edited sparse code is what makes the edit attributable — otherwise every other dimension's reconstruction error is inside the measurement — and the 80%-of-mass patch mask with a four-patch floor is the same idea in space. This is directly portable to our probe and patching work in any modality where a read-out is per-position.
- **Measure:** the paired *similarity-controlled, activation-contrasted* benchmark. Requiring the two test items to be near-duplicates in the dense representation (above the 99th percentile of random pairs) while forcibly opposed in the target feature is what converts "did the explanation help?" into a forced choice with a real chance of failure; without the dense-similarity constraint a judge can win on coarse differences alone.
- **Warning:** standard explanation scores can be flat where behaviour separates. CLIP and IoU moved by at most 0.014 across four evidence formats that the behavioural test separated by up to 11 points. Any of our explanation-quality metrics that is a similarity between a description and its evidence should be reported beside a downstream behavioural test, and the paper's two are the pattern — descriptions scored against images, and images compared directly.
- **Framing:** explanation as *intervention*, i.e. evidence about what a unit controls, offered as complementary to evidence about where it fires rather than as a replacement. The specific form worth copying is the **compositional attribution**: a global read-out grounded in a short, named list of local units, with their activations and their individual effects printed, so the aggregation is inspectable instead of being a dense gradient.
- **Shortcut:** norm-matched budgets for any comparison between a structured direction and a raw gradient. Steer both by the same fraction of the activation tensor's norm and on the same positions, or the comparison measures how much perturbation was injected rather than how well-chosen the direction was — here that matching is what exposes dense steering's sign reversal under reconstruction, which larger nominal strengths had been hiding as "it works".
