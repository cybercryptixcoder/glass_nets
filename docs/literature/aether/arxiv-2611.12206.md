# A Geometric Invariant of Attention Predicts Head Specialization from Weights Alone

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.12206
- **Authors / organisation:** Miguel P. Bento; Feedzai, Lisbon, Portugal (work done independently of the author's role there)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12206
- **Kind:** paper (extended abstract, NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Symmetry and Geometry in Neural Representations; PMLR proceedings)
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, ~13.7k clean chars — the whole submission: abstract, §1 introduction, §2 theory with Proposition 1 and its proof, §3.1–§3.2 with both tables, §4 discussion/limitations, references; code at github.com/MPedraBento/rho-invariant). It is a five-page workshop abstract, so this entry is short by necessity, and **no appendix, no per-head scatter, no figure and no sweep exists to inspect** — every number below is a table cell or an explicit sentence.
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) — it is the folder's first measurement built *entirely* from weights, with no forward pass, no dataset and no intervention in the construction, and it states its own claim in exactly the form a predictive white-box model needs: a capacity bound that is necessary but not sufficient for a behaviour.

## What they wanted, and why
The question is how much can be known about a head before running anything through the network. The author's motivating analogy is from physics: a theory usually has far fewer observables than parameters, so the right move is to find the quantities that survive a change of coordinates and read those. Attention supplies such quantities for free — the query-key composite is a bilinear form, and the reparameterisation that leaves every attention logit unchanged is an explicit group — so a scalar built from that composite is a statement about the head rather than about the basis the trainer happened to land in. The larger goal is a data-independent reading of modern LLMs: rank every head from checkpoint weights alone, then spend forward passes only where the ranking says something is there. The paper also makes one methodological distinction explicit and keeps to it throughout — that its scalar measures what a head's geometry *permits*, not what the head *does* on a given input.

## What they studied
Four bias-free RoPE models taken as published, spanning two orders of magnitude: SmolLM2-135M, TinyLlama-1.1B, SmolLM2-1.7B and Mistral-7B (the 7B model used only for the application experiment). The unit of analysis is a **single attention head's query-key geometry**, not a neuron, feature, layer or circuit — specifically the two matrices `G_QK(0) = W_Qᵀ W_K` (the position-free part of the logit form) and `G_QK(Δ)` (the same form after the rotary rotation at relative offset Δ), from which the positional perturbation `P(Δ) = W_Qᵀ [R(Δ) − I] W_K` is read off. The task variable used to validate the reading is the induction score, measured behaviourally, so the paper compares a weight-only quantity against a data-measured head property.

## How they did it
1. **Fix the symmetry group first.** The static composite `G_QK(0)` is invariant under any invertible change of the head basis, but requiring the whole family `G_QK(Δ)` to stay invariant shrinks that group to a rotation times a positive rescaling acting inside each 2×2 rotary plane. `G_QK` is therefore basis-independent *and* still well-defined in a RoPE model, which is what makes a scalar built from it a property of the head rather than of the weight basis.
2. **Define the scalar.** ρ is the average, over a set of relative positions, of the squared Frobenius norm of the positional perturbation divided by the squared Frobenius norm of the static term (equation 2 in the paper). It is a ratio of "how much the logit form moves with position" to "how big it is without position".
3. **Choose the sample set for a reason, not for convenience.** The offsets are powers of two from 1 up to the context length, because RoPE's per-plane frequencies are themselves geometrically spaced, so uniform spacing in log-offset touches every frequency band up to the horizon. The author states plainly that ρ therefore describes capacity **over the sampled range**, not at arbitrary distance — a scope limit carried in the definition rather than in a limitations paragraph.
4. **Prove the degenerate case.** Proposition 1 shows ρ = 0 (with the static term non-zero) holds exactly when the perturbation vanishes at every sampled offset, in which case the attention logits are provably independent of relative position for *every* input. The proof is one line: a sum of non-negative terms is zero only if each is zero.
5. **Validate against induction.** For each model, compute ρ per head from weights, measure induction scores with repeated random sequences, then correlate across heads (Spearman) and check where the top ten induction heads sit in the ρ distribution.
6. **Spend the invariant on an application.** Apply YaRN context extension to only the upper half of heads by ρ and leave the rest at their original positions, then compare short- and long-context perplexity against uniform application to all heads.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **single basis-invariant scalar per head, computed from weights alone**, plus the claim structure that goes with it: high ρ means the head is geometrically able to couple to position, ρ = 0 means it provably cannot. The explanation is deliberately partial and one-sided — it is a *capacity* statement, so a high ρ says nothing about whether the head uses position on any input, and a low ρ cannot say the head is an induction head, only that it is not excluded from being one. Prediction is the point: the scalar is meant to rank heads before any data exists, and the paper's own read is that the residual correlation is explained by induction being a multi-head circuit, which makes low ρ necessary and not sufficient.

## How they judged success
The metric is Spearman correlation between ρ and a behavioural induction score across heads, per model, with significance. The qualitative companion check is a rank-position statement: where do the ten highest-scoring induction heads land in the ρ distribution. The application experiment is judged by perplexity at two lengths (512 and 4096 tokens) against two reference rows — the unmodified model and uniform YaRN. No random-direction baseline, no permutation null, no init control, no held-out head set, and no per-head uncertainty on ρ itself are reported; the baselines present are the two perplexity rows in the application table, and the comparison in the correlation analysis is across heads within a model rather than against a control arm.

## What they found
**The correlation is negative, moderate and stable across scale.** Spearman r = −0.74 on SmolLM2-135M, −0.71 on TinyLlama-1.1B, −0.55 on SmolLM2-1.7B and −0.59 on Mistral-7B, all with p < 10⁻⁴⁰ — i.e. induction heads sit in the low-ρ tail, as the capacity reading requires, and the relationship does not decay as models grow from 135M to 7B. The author's explanation for the imperfect correlation is that induction is a circuit spanning several heads.

**The top ten are in the tail but the tail is not small.** The ten strongest induction heads fall within the bottom 14% of the ρ distribution for SmolLM2-135M, the bottom 7% for TinyLlama, the bottom 17% for SmolLM2-1.7B, and — the cell worth pausing on — the bottom **30%** for Mistral-7B. So on the largest model, selecting by "low ρ" alone retains nearly a third of all heads, which is a weaker screening instrument than the correlation coefficient suggests. The prose and the table agree on that range, so the spread across models is the finding rather than an inconsistency: the same threshold that identifies induction heads tightly in a 1.1B model retains three times as many heads at 7B, which is what a screening rule's precision looks like when it is quoted without a threshold.

**The application is a genuine but modest win inside the native window.** Short-context perplexity (512) is 7.63 unmodified, 19.37 with YaRN on all heads, and 12.92 with YaRN on the high-ρ half only; long-context (4096) is 5.44, 162.96 and 130.34 respectively. Selective application therefore loses about 5.3 points where uniform application loses about 11.7 at the short length, and about 125 points where uniform loses about 157 at the long length — better on both rows, and the author is explicit that both long-context values are inflated because the evaluation sits near the context limit, that this is a single-model proof of concept at Mistral-7B only, and that evaluation beyond the native window is left to future work.

## Limits and open problems they name
ρ is defined only for RoPE architectures and only over the query-key sector, so nothing about value-output geometry enters the validation. The scalar is invariant to a rescaled query *or* key matrix but **sensitive to the softmax shift that adds a constant to a whole row of logits**, which leaves attention patterns unchanged; the author's mitigation is an argument rather than a check — weight decay discourages large components in the static term that do not affect the output. The sample set is finite, so ρ is capacity over the sampled range. The capacity/usage gap is stated as a feature of the interpretation but is not measured: no head with high ρ is shown ignoring position, and none with low ρ is shown using it. All validation is on one behaviour (induction) with one behavioural estimator, on four models that are all bias-free RoPE, and each model's result is reported without a spread over induction-estimator seeds or sequence sets. The named future work is a fuller invariant signature — the eigenspectrum of the query-key form, the singular values of the value-output form, and the commutator norm between the two — plus a comprehensive study of weight invariants.

## What we take from it
**A framing (and its arithmetic) for reading weights before any data exists.** The construction is three lines and needs no forward pass: form `W_Qᵀ W_K`, form the rotated difference `W_Qᵀ[R(Δ) − I]W_K` at a geometric ladder of offsets, take the ratio of their squared Frobenius norms, average. Anything we build that claims a weight-only prior should be able to write its observable this compactly, and this is the cleanest example in the folder of doing so with the invariance argument stated first — the claim that a scalar is "about the head" is *earned* by naming the group that leaves the logits fixed, then showing the group that survives RoPE, rather than by hoping the units cancel.

**The reporting form: a capacity bound is not a prediction, and the difference shows up in the tail fraction.** A correlation of −0.59 and a bottom-30% containment are the same result stated two ways, and only the second tells you how much work remains after the screen. Any weight-derived prior we report should come with the containment fraction at the decision threshold, not just the rank correlation, because that is what converts a coefficient into a filter with a known false-positive budget.

**A warning about the ceiling of a monotone screening score.** Every one of these models shows the relationship, and the relationship is nonetheless too weak to identify a head. The paper's own resolution — the behaviour is implemented by a circuit, so a single-head read is a necessary condition — is the honest and useful version, and it is the same partition this folder keeps meeting from the activation side: a quantity that bounds participation is a different object from a quantity that predicts the behaviour, and read beside the folder's decodability-versus-causation results it says that a weights-only invariant is best treated as an eligibility mask for expensive interventions rather than as an attribution on its own.

**A small but real lesson from the application table.** Selective application of a global edit was better than uniform application on *both* rows, which is the cheapest available evidence that the ranking carries information independent of the edit — the property we should ask of any score we intend to steer with. What the experiment does not supply is the obvious control: applying the same edit to the bottom half, or to a random half at matched size — so the win is consistent with the ranking being good and also with "edit fewer heads" being the whole story. That arm costs one run and would have settled it.
