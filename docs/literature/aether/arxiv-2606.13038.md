# Nous: An Attempt to Extract and Inject the Cognition Behind Prediction-Market Behavior

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.13038
- **Authors / organisation:** Haowei Qian — Independent Researcher (listed affiliation; a Fudan University alumni email address is printed)
- **Date:** 2026-06-11
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13038
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Agents built on shared foundation models fail together: independently developed frontier models produce forecasting errors correlated at roughly 0.77, so a large ensemble can be a handful of reasoning pathways wearing many names. The authors ask whether human cognitive diversity can be clawed back from observed behaviour — real prediction-market trading records — and handed to an LLM agent population through prompts, so that a thousand copies of one model stop being a monoculture. The framing is crowd-wisdom: diversity of opinion and independence are prerequisites for collective accuracy, and architectural multiplicity is the only diversity current ensembles have. The paper reports three falsifiable claims in sequence — that structure can be recovered from behaviour, that it can be injected, and that injected diversity helps — and its central result is that the first partly holds while the second two do not.

## What they studied
The behaviour of 100 Polymarket wallets whose on-chain histories survive a filter that removes market makers and arbitrage bots, with a median of roughly two hundred closed positions each. An eight-dimension "Nous Schema" partitions a trader's profile into three layers by mutability — core (risk perception, time-scale preference, cognitive style), shell (attention allocation, belief-update inertia, domain confidence) and membrane (independence, loss response) — giving fourteen continuous parameters, each with a behavioural-economics anchor and a minimum event count. Injection is measured on four base models: DeepSeek-R1-32B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-32B and Qwen3.5-122B, with Qwen3.5-122B carrying the ensemble experiment. The unit of analysis is a parameter vector per trader, translated into prose and installed in a system prompt.

## How they did it
Extraction runs ten event types through eight stateless extractors, with a confidence score per dimension and fallback to population priors below a threshold. Because the blockchain exposes only executed trades and resolutions, an on-chain adapter flags dimensions whose designed inputs are missing ("degraded"), which lets the paper test whether input completeness predicts reliability. Recoverability is split into three pre-registered tests: **A**, split-half intraclass correlation across trade-index halves and a position-aware split; **B**, separability, run once with an invalid null, audited, and re-run with a column-wise permutation null and a trade-level shuffle null plus a nearest-neighbour retrieval test; **C**, predictive validity, comparing top and bottom PnL quartiles on four pre-specified dimensions with a Bonferroni threshold, then repeating on a temporal hold-out where the profile is fitted to the first 60 per cent of closes and PnL measured on the last 40. Injection is a prompt ablation across translators, a handwritten persona, a length-matched filler, a persona-mismatch swap and dimension-drop arms, with diversity regressed on condition plus prompt length. Usefulness is a four-group ensemble design — identical seed, varying seed, length-matched placebo, treatment — sharing seeds between placebo and treatment so that cognitive content is separated from length.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanistic account of any model. The explanation offered is a **behavioural parameterisation of a person** plus a measured inventory of which parts of that parameterisation survive each stage of a pipeline: extraction from raw trades, translation into prose, and conditioning of a model on the prose. The paper is explicit that these are behavioural proxies rather than cognitive structure — the term is chosen to mark the gap — and its contribution is the measurement of where in the pipeline the signal is lost, not a claim about the network's internals. It does predict something, but only negative things: what a prompt-borne profile will not do.

## How they judged success
Reliability by ICC with bootstrap confidence intervals and fallback rates printed per parameter; separability by silhouette against two permutation nulls and by top-1/top-5 retrieval against chance; predictive validity by Welch tests with Cohen's d, a Bonferroni threshold, and out-of-sample Spearman correlations, with a Sharpe-based robustness check. Injection by OLS coefficients on three diversity metrics with prompt length partialled out, the semantic embedding metric pre-designated as primary and the lexical one demoted to a sensitivity check. Usefulness by Jensen–Shannon divergence among agent forecasts, ensemble Brier and pairwise error covariance, each with a bootstrap interval and a Welch p, plus temperature, profile-diversity and difficulty robustness sweeps and a printed power calculation.

## What they found
Eight of fourteen parameters are temporally stable with confidence-interval lower bounds above 0.3, led by a contrarian score at 0.90 ICC and a cluster near 0.75 (attention entropy, crowd sensitivity, horizon bias, analytical ratio); the three prospect-theory parameters, despite having the cleanest theory, are the least reliable. Reliability tracks the averaging robustness of the estimator, not the pedigree of the construct, and degraded on-chain input does not predict low reliability. Population structure is real (column-wise null rejected at p below 0.001 in every subset) while individual separability is fragile, and wallet re-identification by nearest-neighbour retrieval reaches 17–22 per cent top-1 and 44–49 per cent top-5 against 1 and 5 per cent chance. Three of four pre-specified dimensions separate high- and low-profit wallets in the predicted direction with large effects; out of sample, only contrarian score (ρ = 0.58) and analytical ratio (ρ = 0.43) survive correction, and neither survives a regression controlling for trade count, capital and holding duration. On injection, injected personas do change outputs relative to no instruction, but no model shows a significant advantage of structured prompts over the length-matched filler on the semantic metric — the favourable reading exists only on the trigram metric and never reaches significance — and a mismatched persona is at least as effective as the correct one, so conditioning on the right profile cannot be claimed. On usefulness, length contributes nothing (−0.0001 JSD, p = 0.93) while cognitive content raises forecast divergence by 0.0035 (p = 0.010) and leaves ensemble Brier and error covariance untouched (both p = 0.95), with agent-error correlation falling only from 0.991 to 0.984. Measuring the prompts explains why: the translator's outputs are semantically near-uniform, and a synthetic population spread 46 per cent wider in parameter space produces prompts no more spread out (0.0162 against 0.0160, permutation p = 0.93), with profile-to-prompt rank correlation around 0.35.

## Limits and open problems they name
The separability test was re-run after a structurally invalid null was found, the pass criterion was tightened after seeing the full-vector failure, and the null was computed only at the silhouette-maximising k — the authors state all three as HARKing risks. The predictive-validity instrument was repaired after an unfavourable direction reversal turned out to be held-to-resolution positions scored at zero PnL, so it is a repaired pre-specified test rather than a pristine one, and one of the most reliable dimensions was dropped from it by a wiring bug. The injection experiment rests on three personas and five scenarios, with no metric pre-specified at first and a filler that is not perfectly content-neutral. The ensemble experiment survives at N = 28 with 80 per cent power only for an effect large enough to remove most of the ensemble error, the retained set is biased toward easier questions by listwise deletion, the behavioural exemplars inside the prompts are synthetic rather than extracted, and a strict correction across all twelve tests would drop the one significant result to suggestive. Four alternative explanations for the null are left open: the evaluation set has little decorrelatable error, the injection channel is lossy, a single heavily aligned base model has a low ceiling, and the aggregation may understate diversity's value.

## What we take from it
A **framing, a warning, and a measure**. The framing is that a pipeline has stages and a null result must be attributed to one of them: this paper does not claim diversity is useless, it claims the prompt is the lossy stage, and it earns that attribution by measuring the intermediate artefact — the translated prompts — and showing that spreading the inputs does not spread them. That is the "how far does the signal survive" question our own account needs to ask of every read-out chain it builds, from feature to description to intervention. The warning is the one this folder keeps re-learning and this paper states most plainly: the same experiment produced a negative, a positive and a retracted narrative depending on the metric, and the authors' response — pre-designate the semantic metric, print the trigram reading beside it and refuse to advance it — is the discipline to copy rather than the numbers. The measure is the four-group placebo design with shared seeds, which is the cheapest way to price prompt length, and the retrieval test, which beats silhouette as an individual-identifiability instrument at small N. The honest summary of the paper is also its most useful transferable lesson: partial recoverability plus zero transmissibility is a coherent result, and reporting both halves is what makes a negative finding informative.
