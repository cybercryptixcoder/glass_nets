## What they wanted, and why
Metaphor is pervasive in language and plausibly part of how concepts are formed, so the authors want to know whether a model's treatment of it is a localisable internal variable rather than an emergent property of prompting. The template they follow is the "truth direction" line of work: find a direction, show it is causally effective, and use it as a dial. Their stated complaint about prior metaphor work is that it is behavioural — accuracy on identification and interpretation tasks — so nothing is known about the internal object. The paper therefore asks three questions in sequence: are metaphorical and literal sentences linearly separable in activation space; can a learned subspace flip the model's output in both directions; and is the same subspace shared across different metaphor datasets.

## What they studied
Seven instruction-tuned models from four families across two size bands — Gemma-2-9B-IT, LLaMA-3.1-8B-IT, LLaMA-3.1-70B-IT, Mistral-7B-IT, Mixtral-8x7B-IT, Qwen-2.5-7B-IT, Qwen-2.5-72B-IT — on three templated datasets: a rewritten Fig-QA noun set of 130 sentence tuples in the form "the N1 is a N2", a 100-pair adjective set reformatted as "the N is ADJ", and a domain-labelled adjective set over eight semantic domains (clarity, depth, light, strength, substance, taste, temperature, texture) with 100 held-out items per direction. Tasks are identification (literal vs metaphorical, single token), interpretation (is this paraphrase apt, single token) and free-form generation (explain the meaning of the adjective). Unit of analysis is the residual-stream vector at one token position in one layer, and then a learned k-dimensional subspace inside it.

## How they did it
Full-vector activation patching first: a literal prompt and its metaphorical twin are formed by swapping one word, activations are cached per layer and position for one, patched into the other, and the change in the log-probability difference between the two label tokens is recorded. The patching map is read for three bands of positions — the swapped word, the sentence-final full stop plus the token before it, and the trailing prompt colon — and the model's *final stop* is taken as the candidate site, at whichever layer shows the largest effect (layer 11 for LLaMA-3.1-8B, layer 29 for the 70B). Distributed alignment search is then fitted at that site: a rotation with orthonormal columns, k = 16 or k = 32, trained for 1,000 Adam steps on the frozen model with the intervention direction alternating each step (literal into metaphor on even steps, the reverse on odd), maximising interchange-intervention accuracy. Two steering arms follow: full-vector mass-mean steering using the difference of class means, and steering only inside the DAS subspace with the subspace's class means. Both are evaluated on items the model got wrong, and then on generation, where the intervention is injected at every generated token and a judge model scores whether the first sentence of the explanation moved to the other reading.

## What counts as an explanation here
A direction and a low-rank subspace in the residual stream at one position and layer, plus the causal-necessity-and-sufficiency argument that the standard DAS protocol supplies: the subspace is held to *be* the metaphoricity variable if patching it is enough to flip the label in both directions. Nothing about which heads or neurons write that subspace is claimed, and the explanation is explicitly partial — the authors say the moderate interchange accuracy implies a proportion of the information is captured elsewhere.

## How they judged success
Baseline 4-shot accuracy per model and dataset; interchange-intervention accuracy on held-out items against a chance and a domain-specific reference; PCA plots of the first two principal components as a descriptive separability check; normalised indirect effect of an intervention, computed from the shift in the average label-probability difference and normalised so 0 is no effect and 1 is a complete shift; flip rate on initially-incorrect items for the two steering arms; generation flip rate at three to four steering scales with a judge model over the first sentence only, degenerate outputs (any word repeated three times) scored as failures and only baseline-correct items counted; a matched-dataset-size retrain of the global subspace as a confound control; and a human annotation of 15% of one model's generations against the judge, reported as raw agreement and Cohen's kappa.

## What they found
- **Baselines are strong enough for the intervention to be meaningful (Table 2):** identification accuracy runs 0.645 (Mistral-7B on nouns) to 0.930 (Qwen-72B on adjectives; Gemma-9B on adjectives), and interpretation 0.696 to 0.862. Gemma-2-27B is excluded from the domain dataset at 0.464, which the authors treat as a floor for probing.
- **The patching map has three peaks** — the swapped word (expected, since it is the manipulation), the sentence-final full stop and the token before it (the candidate site), and the trailing prompt colon (expected, since it predicts the next token). The interesting claim is entirely about the middle peak.
- **Separability:** the first two principal components of the final-stop representation separate metaphorical from literal and apt from inapt across all three datasets, described from the plots rather than from a separability statistic.
- **Global beats domain-specific subspaces (Table 4, LLaMA-8B, held-out domains):** the subspace trained on all eight domains transfers better than the eight per-domain subspaces. The authors name the obvious confound — the global matrix sees more data — and add a matched-n control in which the global matrix is retrained on only as many samples per domain as the smallest domain supplies; it still matches or beats the domain-specific matrices in 6 of 8 transfer domains for Gemma-9B (Table 15).
- **Mass-mean beats a fitted linear probe as a causal operator (Table 5, LLaMA-8B):** normalised indirect effects for mass-mean are 0.195–0.355 for the literal→metaphor direction against 0.011–0.038 for logistic regression on the same representations, and 0.254–0.666 against 0.014–0.051 for the reverse. The pattern holds in both directions across models (Table 16/17), with Gemma-9B the frequent exception.
- **Repair of wrong answers (Table 6):** on initially-incorrect identification items, the mass-mean arm flips 54–88% and the subspace arm 45–91%; the subspace arm wins on Gemma-9B (86% vs 75%), LLaMA-8B (71% vs 54%), LLaMA-70B (65% vs 57%) and Qwen-72B (91% vs 88%) but loses on Mistral-7B (45% vs 64%), Mixtral (61% vs 72%) and Qwen-7B (61% vs 62%).
- **The subspace does not transfer to generation (Table 7):** generation flip rates are far below the classification rates, 0.03–0.52 for the full-vector arm and 0.09–0.47 for the subspace arm, and the ranking inverts — the full-vector arm leads in five of seven models in the literal→metaphor direction. The authors scale the subspace signal up to get any effect at all, and say plainly that the classification concept may be adapted to the classification task, with a separate concept for generation.
- **Judge reliability is measured, not assumed:** 100 items (15%) of Gemma-9B output annotated by an author gives raw agreement 0.79 and Cohen's kappa 0.60 against the judge.

## Limits and open problems they name
English only. The datasets are deliberately templated — one metaphorical target per sentence, one syntactic frame per set — because localisation needs controlled inputs, and the authors say this limits external validity and leaves multi-sentence and discourse metaphor untested. Closed models are excluded. Interchange accuracy is moderate and variable and a perfect score is described as not achievable in practice, so the subspace is a partial variable by construction. The generation result requires an enlarged steering scale and does not reach the classification effect size. A verb dataset was prepared and dropped because baseline performance was too low to probe.

## What we take from it
- **Mass-mean beating logistic regression as a *causal* operator is the reusable finding.** The same representations, the same labels, two directions; the mean-difference one moves the output roughly an order of magnitude more than the fitted one in this paper's own NIE units. For our battery this is another instance of the folder's rule that a probe's fit quality and its causal reach are different measurements — and it argues for carrying a mean-difference arm alongside every fitted probe we report.
- **The floor on the task is a real control, and it is being used correctly.** A model at 0.464 on the domain dataset is dropped rather than reported, with the reason given as a requirement for probing. That is the check our own write-ups should state: what accuracy does the model need before an internal localisation means anything.
- **The generation gap is the most instructive result, and the paper reads it against itself.** Flip rates collapse from 0.54–0.88 in classification to 0.03–0.52 in generation, the steering scale has to be raised to get any effect, and the between-arm ranking inverts. The honest reading they offer — the classification concept may be task-adapted, with a different concept for generation — is exactly the distinction this folder keeps meeting between a variable that is decodable at a site and the computation that actually uses it.
- **A confound named and then controlled in one appendix.** The global subspace sees more data than each domain subspace; the matched-n retrain is the right arm, and reporting 6 of 8 cells rather than an average is the right granularity.
- **A delivery warning worth carrying into the ledger:** the served HTML for this paper has blank table bodies beyond the first few columns for Tables 4, 9–13 and half of the NIE tables. A reader who greps for a value finds nothing, and a summariser that trusts the table prose would attribute numbers to the paper that never appeared on the page. The per-domain DAS comparison — the paper's second main claim — is therefore only quotable through its prose summary here.
