# From Decorative to Load-Bearing: Task Difficulty Shapes the Causal Role of Chain-of-Thought

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.25366
- **Authors / organisation:** Renee Jia, Di Mu; R2M AI (with Cornell University and Carnegie Mellon University affiliations)
- **Date:** 2026-09-21 (v1; cs.AI; CC BY 4.0; reviewed on OpenReview)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25366
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
A monitor that reads a chain-of-thought is only worth its cost if the written reasoning actually constrains the answer that follows. The authors want to measure that constraint directly and at scale, rather than infer unfaithfulness from surface behaviour (rationalisation, unverbalised hint use) or prove it example by example with circuit tracing. Their framing is deliberately narrow: they score the chain-to-answer link, not the inverse question of whether the chain reflects internal computation, and they say so in the abstract and again in the conclusion. The motivation is safety deployment — injected tool outputs, retrieved documents, sub-agent traces and edited reasoning all enter a model mid-chain, so whether a written step binds the continuation is exactly whether such injections can steer the output. A second goal is to find out whether the resulting behavioural types are readable from hidden states and, if readable, steerable.

## What they studied
Three instruction-tuned models — Gemma-2-9B-IT (42 layers, 3584-wide), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers, 4096-wide), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (28 layers) as a reasoning-trained contrast — on GSM8K, MMLU and BIG-Bench Hard. Units of analysis are three: the behavioural type of a continuation, the residual-stream state at three token positions (before, at, and after the injected step) across all layers, and a single direction plus a low-rank basis extracted from those states. Only examples the model answered correctly with at least three reasoning steps are kept (7,691 correct multi-step baselines out of 11,390 questions), so every measured effect is conditioned on competence.

## How they did it
They parse each baseline chain into steps, perturb exactly one step, truncate the chain right after it, and make the model continue from the corrupted prefix — no fresh turn, so the model cannot re-derive from the question. Seven perturbation strategies are used: arithmetic change and operation swap on the numerical side, and confidence injection, wrong elimination, reversed logic, false analogy and premise contradiction on the textual side, each applied at 25%, 50% and 75% chain position. A continuation is then labelled into one of three types: silent bypass (correct answer, perturbation ignored), self-correction (the model notices and repairs), or error propagation (keeps the corrupted step, wrong answer). Labels come from an LLM judge after a keyword rule failed two systematic ways, and the judge's labels are bounded by a four-variant prompt-sensitivity study plus a 500-row, two-annotator blind human study. To separate perturbation type from task difficulty they add the off-diagonal cells of a two-by-two: textual perturbations rewritten onto GSM8K numbers, and numerical perturbations on hard arithmetic subtasks, then fit a logistic model of error propagation on type by a four-level difficulty ordinal over the pooled continuations and partition deviance. Two perturbation-magnitude variables are swept as a competence-floor check. On the interpretability side they extract hidden states at three positions over every layer, concatenate and PCA-reduce, and train linear and small MLP probes under question-grouped five-fold cross-validation for three label tasks; then they take the logistic probe's weight vector at one layer and add scaled multiples of it to the residual stream during generation, with dose, layer, cross-dataset and random-direction controls, followed by a low-rank multi-direction basis run over rank in a small grid and a strength grid.

## What counts as an explanation here
A behavioural, causal notion of load-bearingness, not a mechanistic one: a type label attached to a continuation, given operational definitions and a validated annotator protocol. The hidden-state side adds a predictive readout — probe accuracy per layer per label task — and explicitly denies that it is an explanation of why a continuation went one way, calling the probes readouts rather than mechanisms. The paper is careful about the direction of its claim: it measures whether chain tokens constrain the output, is silent on whether they reflect internal computation, and warns that a high bypass rate is equally consistent with faithful internal shortcuts that simply do not appear in the written chain. The steering results are given as a negative on controllability within the additive-intervention class, with richer interventions named as the open route.

## How they judged success
Three behavioural rates (bypass, self-correction, error propagation) with Wilson intervals per cell and Cohen's kappa between two blind annotators plus agreement between the human consensus and the paper's production labels. For the difficulty claim: within-condition contrasts that hold perturbation strategy fixed across datasets, a within-MMLU subject-level spread at fixed format with a subject-rank slope, a two-by-two with matched off-diagonal cells, and a sequential deviance partition with per-term deviance, degrees of freedom and p-values, plus a question-clustered refit and a re-ordering of the entry terms as a suppressor check. For the probes: grouped five-fold cross-validation, majority baselines, a balanced subsample, token-position ablation, sample-size stability, and — the sharpest of them — re-fitting identical architecture and features on rule labels versus judge labels, plus agreement evaluated against human labels directly. For the steering: a per-type flip-rate table across strength and layer with exact binomial and Wilson intervals, four independent random directions, two basis constructions, and a per-type breakdown because the pooled rate was an artefact.

## What they found
Errors propagate far more on hard tasks than on easy ones and the gap is about difficulty rather than about how the error was written. On Gemma, propagation runs 3.9% on GSM8K, 22.3% on MMLU and 40.9% on BBH, and because GSM8K is the only dataset with numerical perturbations, the paper's primary evidence is internal: the same five textual strategies give 22.3% on MMLU and 40.9% on BBH with a per-strategy rise of 13 to 24 points, and across the 29 MMLU subjects at identical format propagation spans 7.5% to 53.7%, a 7x spread at a subject-rank slope of 0.069. The matched cells make the axis ordering explicit: textual perturbations on GSM8K propagate at 6.5%, numerical ones at 3.9%; numerical perturbations on BBH multistep arithmetic reach 64.5%, textual ones on the same subtask 18.6%. A deviance partition over 28,584 continuations assigns 98.8% of explained deviance to difficulty, 0.8% to perturbation type and 0.4% to the interaction, and the type share rises only to 2.9% when difficulty is entered first, which the authors read as a suppressor pattern that leaves the ordering intact. A magnitude sweep rules out the competence-floor reading: within-task propagation varies by at most four points across subtle, moderate and blatant perturbations, while the between-task gap at fixed magnitude exceeds 60 points. Position also matters but less: 33.5% at early position, 27.4% at middle, 22.5% at late, with remaining-step count as a mechanical confound and a Simpson's-paradox departure at middle position inside the three-to-four-remaining-steps bin, driven by hard chains. Llama replicates — 62.8% and 65.5% propagation on MMLU and BBH at much lower base accuracy, and a 6x within-numerical rise against Gemma's 16x — while DeepSeek-R1-Distill is qualitatively different, with uniformly low propagation (2.7% on MMLU, 16.8% on BBH) and a higher share of self-correction, and with its dataset ordering non-monotonic for reasons the authors attribute to a step-count filter plus RL-induced self-verification. The interpretability results are the folder's cleanest instance of readable-but-not-controllable: probes predict type well above majority on every task (79.4% for bypass at layer 10, 75.3% for the three-way at layer 23, 86.2% for propagation at layer 28), a logit-lens baseline separates nothing (a mean gap of 0.017), the same-features rule-labelled probe hits 99.9% until the labels are corrected to human-verified ones and drops to 75.0%, and steering on the propagation direction across 11,556 generations leaves bypass at zero flips out of 726 (upper bound 0.41%) and propagation at ceiling, with probe and random directions indistinguishable. The rank-eight probe basis moves only 24.6% of propagation cases at the strongest setting, against 5% or less for any single direction, with a wide interval the authors tell us to read as evidence of partial controllability rather than a magnitude.

## Limits and open problems they name
Scale is the first limit: both main models are eight to nine billion parameters, a sweep at seventy billion and above exceeds a single-GPU budget, and the base-versus-instruction-tuned comparison at fixed architecture is unusable because base Gemma rarely produces the numbered-step format the pipeline requires, so post-training and format elicitation are confounded. Only one step is perturbed at a time, and multi-step internally consistent perturbations are named as the natural next experiment. Label noise is the second: the bypass-versus-self-correction boundary moves by an order of magnitude across judge prompts, the human consensus agrees with the production label on only 76.0% of rows for the three-way label because the implicit-correction clause over-counts self-correction, and the rule's wrong-answer heuristic over-counts propagation on BBH, where human agreement with flagged propagation rows is 60%, all flips being answer-scoring failures. The matched MMLU-versus-BBH gap would compress under a worst-case adjustment even though its ordering survives. The probes report what a continuation did, not why; the steering null covers only additive edits at the layers tested; and the authors explicitly cannot separate "faithfulness is unsteerable" from "unsteerable by additive perturbation at the layers we probed".

## What we take from it
**A measurement design, a labelling lesson, and one pooling warning — plus a rare explicit statement of what a null does not cover.** The design to copy is the forced-continuation patch: perturb one step, truncate, and make the model finish from the corrupted prefix, because asking for the final answer in a new turn lets it re-derive from the question and quietly deletes the effect. That is the cheapest interventional test the folder has for "do these tokens carry the decision", and it is strictly better than re-prompting for any question of the form "does part X constrain output Y". The labelling lesson is the 99.9%-to-75.0% collapse from swapping rule labels for human-verified ones at fixed features and fixed architecture: a behaviourally labelled probe is bounded by its labels, and the honest report of a probe score is a pair — score against the automatic label, score against the human-calibrated subset, with the trivial baseline printed beside both. Here the layer-10 bypass probe reaches 66.7% against an always-A baseline of 60.6%, so the headline 79% was mostly label agreement. The pooling warning is a reusable arithmetic fact: pooling fixed-type rates in a balanced design averages to a constant (66 at 0%, 66 at 0%, 66 at 94% averages to about 31% whatever the intervention does), so an aggregate rate can look like a treatment effect when nothing moved; our intervention tables print per-type rows and refuse aggregate-only claims. For intervention scope, the three-tier result — single directions inert, a rank-eight basis moving a quarter of one type, bypass immovable at 8.3% or below across all 66 cells — is the folder's strongest evidence that a readable direction is not automatically a control knob, and it arrives with the required caveat that the claim is about additive steering at one layer. For our white-box model the concrete inheritance is: pair every probe with a non-additive intervention attempt before calling a state a lever; print per-type (per-stratum) rates with the pooled row marked as an average of fixed strata; and treat the difficulty axis as a conditioning variable, since the same readout is informative in one regime and decorative in another.
