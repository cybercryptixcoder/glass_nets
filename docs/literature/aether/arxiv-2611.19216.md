# Operating-Point Failure of Frozen Guardrail Probes Under Backbone Updates

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.19216
- **Authors / organisation:** Yusuf Khalid Shire, Sang-Chul Kim — School of Computer Science, Kookmin University, Seoul
- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19216
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1; main text §§I–VII, Tables I–V, Figures 1 and the layer-sweep figure via captions only; the 137-row registry lives in the supplement and is reported here only through the main text's counts)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

A deployed guardrail is not a classifier but a *threshold*: a linear probe on hidden states, a cut `T` set so that one percent of benign prompts are flagged. The authors ask a narrower and more operational question than the "do probes survive fine-tuning" literature they cite: not whether the probe still *ranks* harmful above benign, but whether the *operating point* — the actual false-positive and true-positive rates at the frozen `T` — survives a backbone update that the deployer does not control. Their observation is that this is a different quantity, because a shift that moves every score by the same amount leaves AUROC untouched and still walks benign prompts across the cut, so a staleness check built on AUROC can pass a guardrail that is now flagging fifteen benign prompts per hundred instead of one. They also ask what a deployer can do with no new labels, and what an attacker who *supplies* the update can do to those repairs.

## What they studied

Sixteen public open-weight checkpoints in 18 headline cells (a cell = one checkpoint pair, one dataset, one layer), all ≤ 9B: base→instruct releases of Mistral-7B v0.1, Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, OLMo-2-1124-7B, plus a base-model version increment (Llama-3-8B → Llama-3.1-8B) in Phase 1, and instruct-side releases and post-training steps (OLMo-2 base → SFT → DPO → Instruct; Tulu-3 SFT → DPO → final) in Phase 2. The unit of analysis is a **layer cell** of a probe's operating point: probe direction, selected layer `L*`, threshold `T`, and the realised rates at that threshold. Probes are ℓ2-regularised logistic regressions on mean-pooled hidden states, 4000 prompts (2000/2000), five seeds; data are Aegis 2.0 and WildGuardMix, with XSTest for the over-flagging measurement, and LoRA fine-tunes of one Mistral checkpoint on Alpaca, HH-harmless and PKU-SafeRLHF at three doses.

## How they did it

Freeze probe, standardisation statistics, layer and threshold on the pre-update model, then apply all of them unchanged to the post-update model; nothing refits. Because `T` at a 1% target on 1000 negatives sits near the tenth-highest negative score, the operating point depends on which negatives a split puts in the calibration half, so they re-partition the pooled negatives 1000 times at their original sizes, re-set `T` in each draw and average. The primary metric is a **paired ratio**: false-positive rate at the frozen `T` after the update divided by the same rate before it, on the *same* evaluation negatives; ≥ 2 is "fails closed", ≤ 0.5 "fails open". They report AUROC change beside it as the check a deployer would plausibly run, implemented from a recent monitor-staleness benchmark's three criteria. Then two label-free checks reading only the benign scores' first two moments (mean and standard deviation): an **estimated-rate check** that compares Gaussian tail mass at `T` using calibration negatives only (out of sample), and a **two-moment model** that maps pre-update scores through the post-update mean and spread to explain the change. Repairs use the stored calibration negatives: threshold re-set, quantile transfer on unlabelled traffic, restandardisation, and ridge/Procrustes realignment of states. Attacks: an additive translation along the probe direction (which cannot move AUROC by construction), a rank-1 LoRA that realises it, a category-targeted version under a KL stealth bound scored only against label-free checks, and poisoning of each repair's reference pool. A post hoc canary of held-out labelled harmful prompts is the last line of defence.

## What counts as an explanation here

A single diagnostic statement of the form *"the update moved the benign score distribution's mean and spread, and that is what moved the false-positive rate"* — a two-moment account of a failure, measurable without labels and applied to the repair question. It is explicitly partial: it explains the change on the same negatives whose rate it gives (in-sample), and the authors say so rather than claiming a forecast. The richer object — which update, at which layer, moves benign prompts toward the harmful side — is offered only as an interpretation, backed by the signed cosine between the negatives' mean shift and the probe direction.

## How they judged success

Paired ratio with 2.5th/97.5th percentile bands over 1000 re-partitions; AUROC change with a 5000-replicate paired item bootstrap; the three-criteria AUC rule for "stale"; per-cell bands of the estimated-rate ratio against the realised one; sensitivity and specificity of each label-free check over damaged and undamaged layer cells; the two-moment model's factor-of-1.5 agreement rate and regression slope of log-realised on log-modelled; XSTest false-flag ratio for over-flagging; true-positive rate at `T` for repairs; and a 137-entry pre-registered prediction registry reported in full with HELD / FAILED / NOT SUPPORTED / REFUTED verdicts, including a hardware control (same weights, V100 vs A100) to bound extraction noise.

## What they found

The operating point moves while the ranking does not. At a 1% target the frozen threshold fails closed in 8 of 9 Phase 1 cells and closed-or-open in 3 of 9 Phase 2 cells; the Mistral base→instruct update takes the realised false-positive rate from 1.01% to 15.12% while AUROC falls 0.0058, and no headline cell's AUROC moves by more than that in either direction, so the benchmark's staleness criteria call none of the 18 cells stale. Over 156 Phase 1 layer cells the AUC rule flags 0.019 of those damaged on the average draw, at specificity 1.000. One Phase 2 cell fails the other way (0.738 → 0.365 true-positive rate at the drawn threshold, AUROC 0.9776 → 0.9733), and representation similarity does not predict which: minimum CKA over layers is 0.99302 on the Llama-3→3.1 pair whose paired ratio band is [2.46, 4.71]. Fragility also depends on the target rate — at 5%, five of the eleven damaged cells fall back inside the cut, all of them between 2.08 and 3.22 at 1% and 1.56 and 1.83 at 5%. The label-free estimated-rate check, using calibration moments only, flags 0.918 of damaged cells and clears 0.898 of undamaged ones, against a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test at sensitivity 0.966 but specificity 0.543 and average-thresholded-confidence at 0.479 sensitivity. The two-moment model accounts for the change within a factor of 1.5 in 0.905 of the 63 Phase 2 cells whose predictions were registered before any Phase 2 score existed, and in 0.773 of the 22 cells that actually moved, against 0.409 for translation alone. Re-setting `T` on 1000 stored benign prompts returns the rate to 0.96–1.02 of target in all nine Phase 1 cells without labels, but repairs that read live traffic invert under prevalence drift (0.12–0.31 of target at 5% harmful traffic) and restandardisation on mostly benign traffic overshoots five- to tenfold; poisoning 1% of the repair's reference with the highest-scoring harmful prompts drops the re-set's true-positive rate by 10 points and leaves it flagging 0.006–0.017 of harmful prompts. An attacker who controls the update moved one target category's rates past both label-free checks in 3 of 5 seeds under a KL bound tied to clean seed-to-seed variation (0.08644 nats per token), refuting the authors' hypothesis that no single attacker evades both; a post hoc canary on 47–99 held-out labelled prompts per category caught every blinded cell. Their own reporting is unusually candid about the direction of their misses: at least 16 of 24 failed predictions failed by predicting a *larger* effect than the data showed, and Phase 1 — which holds 8 of the 10 fail-closed cells — was selected by no registered rule.

## Limits and open problems they name

The read-out, the kind of update and the phase are aliased in Phase 1 (instruct models read raw prompts there, chat templates in Phase 2), so the phase difference cannot be credited to the update type; three Phase 2 pairs change the rendered input together with the weights and are not separated; seven of the 18 pairs change model configuration (RoPE base, scaling, window, max positions, vocabulary) as well as weights. HH-harmless training conversations contained frozen evaluation items in non-prompt turns (763 distinct items at 400 steps), so that arm is an exposed safety corpus and PKU-SafeRLHF is treated as the clean one. Only open-weight models ≤ 9B, one checkpoint for the controlled LoRA work and one backbone for the attacks, two probe-training datasets, one over-flagging set; the hardware control fails its registered bit-identity rule at all but 1 of 17 layers and is run on one model only; "no stale cell" rests on a 0.003 AUROC margin and a seed mean; damage is not dose-monotone (largest at 25 steps in every arm); adaptive attackers are out of scope; and realignment kept AUROC by a criterion the frozen probe already met, so by that criterion there was nothing to repair.

## What we take from it

The measurement, first: **a probe's deployed quantity is its operating point, and the rate at a fixed threshold must be reported beside any AUROC we publish.** The pairing is cheap — score the same negatives before and after whatever changed, at the frozen cut, and print the ratio with the AUROC delta — and it converts a ranked claim into a safety claim. The **paired ratio with a hard class cut** (2 and 0.5) is a better summary than a raw accuracy drop because it says which direction and how far in units a deployer cares about, and it makes the target-rate dependence visible, which is a warning for our own threshold-setting: a monitor that looks fine at 5% may be failing at 1%. The second gift is the cheapest possible label-free health check: the **estimated-rate check** needs nothing but stored benign prompts and the updated model, computes Gaussian tail mass at the frozen threshold from calibration moments only, and catches 0.918 of what an AUROC rule misses at 0.898 specificity — worth lifting wholesale, along with its honest comparison to KS and ATC, whose ranking is better than their thresholding. The third is a general warning about repairs: re-centring on live traffic is a bet on prevalence and on traffic you do not control, and an attacker who can write to the reference set can quietly deflate detection toward zero without moving any label-free statistic. And the fourth is a design rule about our own writeups — this paper's registry counts held and failed predictions with equal prominence, and notes that most of its failures were over-predictions, which is the failure mode a pre-registration is there to expose.
