# Persona Perspectives: Adopting Viewpoints Reorganizes Representations in LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.06848
- **Authors / organisation:** Austin C. Kozlowski (Knowledge Lab), Avital Mintz, Madison Lee; University of Chicago
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06848
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML build, four sections plus references and Appendices A–E; the nine figures were not inspected, so every number below is a prose or table figure and none is read off an axis)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Probes and sparse-autoencoder classifiers are usually built on one assumption that is rarely stated: the concept they read and the inputs they read it from keep the same representation no matter what the context is. The authors' question is what happens to that assumption when the context is a *persona* — one sentence of system prompt telling the model which perspective to answer from. Their reason for asking is not descriptive. If a perspective reorganises how the model's concepts relate to one another, then a monitor trained under one perspective is a monitor of *that* perspective, and a model answering from a sympathetic standpoint could carry the same statement into a region of representation space where the frozen probe reads it as benign. The paper is a step toward knowing which part of an interpretability claim is about the network and which part is about the frame the network was asked to occupy. They choose political personas because that divide is well documented in language and because the two sides are expected to differ on exactly the two judgements an alignment monitor cares about, truth and morality.

## What they studied
Eight instruction-tuned models from four families — Llama 3.1 8B (the main subject) and 70B, Gemma 3 4B and 27B, Qwen 3 8B and 32B, Ministral 3 8B and 14B — with Llama 3.1 8B analysed at layer 24, roughly three-quarter depth, and every other model at a comparable depth. The unit of analysis is a **word representation** and, above it, the **angle between two semantic axes**: a bank of word vectors built from the 10,000 most frequent English words (9,493 after stop-word and anchor removal), and 33 contrastive semantic axes such as liberal–conservative, good–bad, soft–hard, feminine–masculine and collective–individual, each built from ten anchor pairs. Four system-prompt conditions: none, a helpful-assistant prompt, respond as an American liberal Democrat, respond as an American conservative Republican. Two further objects are studied downstream — a steering intervention on the residual stream and a bank of truth and morality classifiers.

## How they did it
Each word is placed in five question templates and the final-token residual activation is read and averaged, separately inside every persona condition, so a word's vector and the axes it is projected onto are both *re-extracted under each persona* rather than shared across conditions. All 528 axis pairs are then scored by cosine similarity within each condition, giving a 33×33 alignment matrix per persona; the analytic object is the liberal-minus-conservative difference between those matrices, correlated against the same difference computed for word2vec models trained from scratch on matched 69-million-token liberal and conservative corpora (partisan news, political podcasts, campaign email, token counts balanced by year and month), across 528 pairs. Next, behaviour: a forward hook at the final prompt token adds ±0.33 times the local residual norm along one axis while the model rates a word on a *different* axis, and the off-target log-odds shift is measured over 1,000 random words for the 1,056 directed axis pairs that are not an axis's effect on itself, under each persona. The test is whether a persona's spillovers are better predicted by its own alignments than by the other persona's or the assistant's. Finally, three kinds of classifier — a full-width logistic probe on the residual stream, a probe on eight pretrained-SAE features at layer 19, and a probe over manually chosen anchor-word vectors for true/false and moral/immoral — are trained with no system prompt on deliberately noncontroversial statements (9,192 Wikipedia-derived true/false statements, 8,000 first-person actions in matched pairs, complete topics held out) and then applied, frozen, to 360 controversial truth claims and 300 controversial actions whose partisan endorsement is a hypothesis generated by another model rather than ground truth.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **geometric** one, at the level of relations rather than components: a persona does not add a direction to a fixed space, it re-angles the space, and the evidence for the specific organisation is the pattern of axis-to-axis alignments. The claim is partial by design — no circuit and no mechanism for *how* the prompt does the re-angling is offered — and its strength rests on three convergences the authors line up: the reorganisation matches prior findings in political psychology (partisan affect first, then egalitarianism-versus-individualism, then the gender and soft–hard axes), it agrees across eight independent models, and it agrees with embeddings trained on partisan text. The second half of the paper turns geometry into a prediction: if the alignments are the real relations, they should predict how one axis's intervention bleeds into another's rating, and they do, better under the persona's own geometry than under a rival's.

## How they judged success
Pearson correlations between difference matrices across 528 axis pairs; the same correlation across the 28 model pairs; correlation between geometry-predicted and observed off-target spillovers, compared across three predictors (own persona, helpful assistant, opposite persona) — a deliberately ordered control ladder rather than a single baseline; classifier accuracy on held-out ordinary statements with bootstrap intervals; and, for the controversial statements, a topic-level sign-flip permutation test (exhaustive over 2^12 topic assignments for truth, 99,999 random assignments for morality) with Holm correction over a family of 140 comparisons. Model behaviour on the same controversial items is collected separately, in an open format rated by a different model and a constrained yes/no format, so the probe readings can be compared against what the model actually says.

## What they found
The re-angling is large. With no system prompt the liberal–conservative and good–bad projections of the word bank are unrelated (r = .02), and under the assistant prompt barely related (r = .21); under the liberal persona words toward "liberal" fall toward "good" (r = .75) and under the conservative persona the relation flips so that words toward "conservative" fall toward "good" (r = −.79). Among the axes, liberal–conservative changes most: under the liberal persona it aligns with us (.52), good (.39), successful (.40), strong (.37) and wise (.39), and under the conservative persona those same five cosines reverse to −.48, −.47, −.45, −.46 and −.37, with all five near zero (−.03 to .10) without a prompt. Next largest are collective–individual, soft–hard and feminine–masculine; collective aligns with strong (.35) and us (.34) under the liberal persona and is near-orthogonal to them (.02–.07) under the conservative one, and hard aligns with conservative (.39 against .08) and with good (−.29 against .02) in the conservative condition. The pattern is shared: the difference matrices of the 28 model pairs correlate at a mean r = .84 at three-quarter depth, and against the partisan word2vec difference matrices at a mean r = .64 (.67 for Llama 3.1 8B). Reorganisation is absent in early layers and emerges by roughly half depth. Behaviour follows the geometry: spillovers are predicted at r = .59 (liberal) and .74 (conservative) by the persona's own alignments, .54 and .64 by the assistant's, .44 and .57 by the opposite persona's, and the *difference* between personas' alignments predicts the difference in their spillovers at r = .69 across the 1,056 pairs. On ordinary items the frozen probes still work — 94–99% on true/false and about 99% on moral/immoral, holding under both personas except the SAE-feature truth probe, which falls from 97% to 88% under the conservative persona. On controversial items the same frozen probes part company: liberal-endorsed truth claims have a median probability of "true" of 98% under the liberal persona and well below 50% under the conservative one for all three probe types; conservative-endorsed moral actions sit at 3–21% "acceptable" under the liberal persona and 67–97% under the conservative one. The paper is explicit that all three probes point the same way in the latent space in all three conditions — the statements moved, not the read-out direction.

## Limits and open problems they name
Endorsement labels for the controversial statements are model-generated hypotheses, not verified ground truth, and many conservative claims are rated untrue even under the conservative persona, which the authors read as matching the model's own stated behaviour rather than as a probe failure. The word2vec comparison rests on outlet- and show-level ideology labels as proxies, and its hyperparameters were chosen after exploration and not preregistered. Probe layers for the eight-model replication were picked on the ordinary test set that is then reported, so those accuracies are not independent validation. SAE-feature probes exist only for Llama 3.1 8B because the SAE is model-specific. There is no control persona drawn from an unrelated axis — the helpful assistant is the only non-political condition, so the paper can show the effect is *directed* by ideology but cannot fully separate it from a generic effect of having any system prompt. Two political personas in one country is also a narrow sample of "perspective", and the paper offers no mechanism for how a one-sentence prompt re-angles 528 relations.

## What we take from it
(1) **The control ladder is the reusable part.** Testing the geometry against three ordered predictors — own persona, neutral persona, opposite persona — turns a correlation into a graded claim, because the ordering is predicted in advance and the numbers respect it. Every time we argue that a read-out tracks a mechanism rather than a context, the same ladder applies: the mechanism's own geometry should beat a neutral frame and beat an opposing frame, and a flat ordering is evidence the claim is about the frame. (2) **A frozen probe is a measurement conditional on its extraction context, and the paper supplies the number that makes this concrete** — accuracy unchanged on ordinary items while the same frozen directions read the same controversial statement as true under one persona and untrue under another. Our probe evaluations are almost entirely on ordinary, uncontroversial items, which is exactly the regime where this failure is invisible; the diagnostic to add is a set of items whose label is expected to *move* with the frame, checked for movement. (3) **Re-extraction, not reuse, is the methodological detail worth copying**: the word vectors and the axes are both rebuilt inside each condition, so the difference matrix is not contaminated by one condition's vocabulary — the same discipline any cross-condition geometric comparison of ours needs. (4) **Warning:** the strongest single number here, the r = .84 agreement across eight models, is a correlation of *differences*, and the paper says so explicitly; it licenses a statement about shared reorganisation, not about shared baseline geometry. (5) **The behavioural cross-check is sets of items, not scores in isolation** — probe-assigned probability is always shown beside the model's actual acceptance rate, which is how the authors can say a low reading under the conservative persona tracks behaviour rather than the probe breaking.
