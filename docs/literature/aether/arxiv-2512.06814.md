# CAuSE: Decoding Multimodal Classifiers using Faithful Natural Language Explanation

## What they wanted, and why
Input-attribution methods say which pixels or words mattered but do not produce sentences, and where a multimodal language model is asked to narrate an explanation on its own behalf it tends to produce something plausible that describes a decision the classifier never made. The authors want both at once for a frozen classifier: a natural-language explanation that a reader can follow, and a guarantee that the sentence is bound to the classifier's actual decision rule rather than to what a human annotator would have said. Their route is the causal-abstraction framework — make a small generator into a model of the classifier's internals, not just a mimic of its outputs — and their own framing is that this shows how interchange-intervention techniques, usually demonstrated on toy networks, carry over to transformer classifiers. The larger goal is trust in deployed discriminative systems, which cannot narrate themselves the way a generative model can.

## What they studied
Four frozen multimodal classifiers spanning four fusion styles: CLIP+MFB (late fusion), VisualBERT (early fusion), FLAVA, and Qwen2-VL-2B with its language-model head replaced by a multi-layer perceptron so that it emits a class label. Three annotated corpora: e-SNLI-VE (image premise plus text hypothesis, three-way entailment with human explanations, 9,000 train / 1,000 test), Facebook Hateful Memes (offensive or not, 6,997 / 1,000), and VQA-X (visual question answering with human attention-and-answer explanations, 5,997 / 960). The unit of analysis is not a neuron but a *frozen encoder state*: the joint text-image representation that feeds the classifier head, which is the object the explainer is trained to reproduce.

## How they did it
Split the classifier into encoder and head and freeze both. Build a small Explainer with four parts: a perceptron that projects the encoder state into a language model's embedding width, a fine-tuned GPT-2-small that generates the explanation (its first token is that projection, so the sentence is conditioned on the classifier's internal state rather than on the raw input), an aggregator that sums the generator's logits over the sequence and maps them into the head's input width, and a replica classifier which is trained to match the original head. Four losses are added: ordinary next-token loss on human explanations; a teacher–student KL between the original head's and the replica's output distributions; a Frobenius-norm penalty pulling the replica's weights towards the original head's; and an interchange-intervention KL, in which 20 per cent of the head neurons are sampled uniformly and their activations from a base pass are frozen into a source pass, in both heads, and the two post-intervention output distributions are matched. An appendix bounds the sampling: with 557,440 neurons, a 20 per cent draw and a failure probability of one in a hundred thousand, roughly 111 repetitions suffice, which a single epoch of the training sets exceeds. At inference only the projector and the generator are used. Reported training footprint is about 450M parameters and inference about 270M, on a single P100. Two design choices matter for reading the results: e-SNLI-VE and VQA-X supply human explanations, while Hateful Memes has none, so explanations for the offensive class were generated by GPT-4o from a caption, meme text and retrieved visual entities, then audited by three annotators on 200 memes (relevance mean 2.34 on a three-point scale, fluency 0.94, Cohen's kappa 0.44, described as fair) and then hand-edited for the offensive examples — 297 of 596 test-set offensive items were post-edited, and the authors report that this covered all of the not-relevant items and nearly all of the partially-relevant ones. Second, training data are filtered by the classifier's own correctness: where the frozen classifier predicts the gold label, the human explanation is kept, and where it does not, the example is retained with the classifier's prediction and no explanation, so the explainer is taught the classifier's answer rather than the annotator's. Faithfulness is scored by F1 between the explainer's predicted class and the classifier's, and by a new counterfactual measure; plausibility by BLEU-1..4 and BERTScore against the human explanation.

## What counts as an explanation here
A short natural-language sentence, generated post hoc by a small language model, whose input is the frozen classifier's encoder state. The claim is strong and formal rather than hedged: the generator-plus-replica pipeline is asserted to be a *causal abstraction* of the classifier, meaning that interventions on the classifier's neurons and on the replica's corresponding neurons produce the same output distribution. The artefact is partial in the sense that no mechanism is catalogued — there is no circuit, no feature list, no direction — and it is meant to be predictive in exactly one respect: given the same input the pipeline reproduces the classifier's label, and given a counterfactual input it ideates the correspondingly changed label.

## How they judged success
Faithfulness: label F1 against the classifier; a new Counterfactual Consistency via Multimodal Representation (CCMR) score; the share of generations that are feasible at all; and a composite which is the harmonic mean of the last two. Plausibility: BLEU-1 to BLEU-4 and BERTScore against the human explanation, with the em dash that the authors draw explicitly — these are the same quantities being asked to point in opposite directions, so a faithfulness–plausibility trade-off is expected and reported rather than hidden. Ablations remove one loss at a time (human-explanation loss only, plus teacher–student, plus the causal pair) across all four classifiers and three datasets, giving twelve cells. External baselines are prompted PaLiGemma and LLaVA at zero and two shots, and both fine-tuned on the task. A human study has two annotators score 150 sampled explanations per condition per dataset on a five-point relatedness scale (coherent justification of the classifier's prediction, ground-truth explanations hidden) and a fluency scale, with Cohen's kappa 0.68 and 0.76. A separate model-as-judge tournament shows a multimodal judge the input, the classifier's label and LIME attributions and asks it to rank the three ablations, with a scoring scheme that awards 2/1/0 for a strict ordering and splits ties.

## What they found
Faithfulness and plausibility do come apart, and the paper's own worked examples are its clearest statement: a VQA-X model answers yes to a question about a storm and the plain-generator ablation says the answer is up in the clouds, which matches the human explanation and hides the fact that the classifier is reacting to the image being black and white — the causally-oriented variant reports the shortcut instead. Across the ablation grid the full loss tends to win, but the size of the win is the part to read carefully: on CLIP+MFB the CCMR column runs 83.00 for the generator-only arm, 67.86 with the teacher–student loss, and 55.40 with the full loss — the causal loss scores *below* the bare generator on that cell, and the composite recovers only because feasibility rises from 10.20 through 5.71 to 50.10 per cent, taking the composite from 18.16 to 52.65. The same pattern repeats with Qwen-VL on Hateful Memes (CCMR 38.49, 48.79, 49.28 with feasibility 99.03, 99.87, 100.00) and with VisualBERT on e-SNLI-VE (69.20, 69.40 for the two generator arms, feasibility 51.65 and 59.09). Where the causal arms do earn their place the mechanism is feasibility rather than agreement: the ablations with insufficient conditioning frequently generate a sentence that names no class at all, so they score high CCMR on the few generations that are feasible and collapse to a low composite. The plain-generator arm's label F1 stays high nearly everywhere (99.00, 93.69, 84.16 for CLIP+MFB on the three datasets; 68.60 with Qwen-VL on Hateful Memes), which means label-matching is largely satisfied by a model that only ever sees the encoder state being imitated, and the counterfactual test is doing the discriminating. Plausibility runs the other way as predicted — the generator-only arm has the best BLEU-4 in most rows (0.59 against 0.58 on Hateful Memes with CLIP+MFB; 0.24 against 0.24 on e-SNLI-VE; 0.25 against 0.12 on VQA-X) and the FLAVA/Qwen-VL VQA-X cells are the extremes, with the full loss dropping BLEU-4 to 0.07 and 0.07 against 0.45 and 0.10. Human relatedness is near parity (averages quoted as 2.49 against 2.29, with fluency slightly lower at 2.79 against 3.09), and the judge tournament puts the full loss ahead on all three datasets (1.08, 1.15, 1.01 against 0.98 / 0.96 / 0.87 and 0.93 / 0.67 / 0.85), but every cell sits near the 1.0 that identical explanations would score. The failure section is unusually concrete: with an implicitly offensive meme the full-loss variant produces nothing beyond the class word where the ablations at least name stereotyping; with an ambiguous image the classifier's own uncertainty leaks into the sentence as the word either followed by an incoherent continuation; and one case is fluent-sounding confabulation with broken grammar. The generic error analysis names two causes it cannot fix — the encoder state lacks object-level detail (the explanation omits the juggling, or the bathtub that is barely visible), and the offence in some memes is only derivable from cultural context absent from both channels.

## Limits and open problems they name
No separate limitations section; the paper carries limits in §5.5.3, §5.6 and §7. Named: fine-grained object-level representations are missing, so explanations under-specify; implicit cultural knowledge cannot be recovered from image plus text at all; and the framework is built for discriminative classifiers and not adapted to generative models, video or audio. Also stated plainly that oracle explanations do not exist and human explanations stand in as a proxy, which is the root of the trade-off. Unnamed but visible to a reader: the Hateful Memes explanation corpus is machine-written and then hand-edited for 49.8 per cent of offensive test items, which means the plausibility ceiling for that dataset is a measure of agreement with edited machine text; CCMR's counterfactual is constructed by an optimised representation perturbation rather than a real input pair, so the metric inherits whatever that optimisation can find; the "identical networks" definition requires matching weights, which in practice is a soft penalty with no reported magnitude; and the abstraction theorem is proved on a two-neuron linear case, with the encoder's injectivity assumed.

## What we take from it
A framing and a measure. The framing: a genuine causal abstraction can be *built into* a narration pipeline rather than tested after the fact, by giving the narrator a replica of the classifier's head and training the replica under interchange interventions and weight matching, and the abstraction claim is then about the pipeline's internals rather than its prose style. The measure: a counterfactual consistency score for an artefact that emits a decision — take each test item, find the nearest test neighbour the classifier labels differently, find a representation-space perturbation that reproduces that class change for the classifier alone, feed the perturbed state to the explainer, and score F1 between the two predicted classes; then report the share of feasible generations beside it and the harmonic mean of the two. That composite is our folder's cleanest example of a number that refuses to reward an artefact which is accurate only when it is silent. The warning is arithmetic and belongs next to it: in this paper the causal loss lowers the counterfactual agreement column in several cells and only wins once feasibility is folded in, so the composite is doing real work but the component columns must be printed or the headline is unreadable. Absorption: run one input through the pipeline and read both numbers; a high score on a minority of feasible items is not a result. Cross-reference run 603's gated-metric artefact, run 594's frozen-instrument rule and run 610's "the direction does X, the label says Y": this paper's version is "the sentence says Y, the causal score says X", resolved by reporting agreement and feasibility as two numbers rather than as one ranking.
