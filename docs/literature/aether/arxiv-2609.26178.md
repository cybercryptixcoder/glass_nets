# Unread or Unenforced? Separating Representation from Enforcement Failure in Content Guards

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.26178
- **Authors / organisation:** Haoyu Zhang, Yi Feng, Shibo Zheng, Hanwen Liu, Haowen Xu, Xiao Luo, Zhuoxi Wang, Mohammad Zandsalimy, Shanu Sushmita; Northeastern University
- **Date:** 2026-08-12 (v1; v2 2026-09-24 read)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26178
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (abstract, SS1-S7, supplementary Appendices A-C with Tables 1-5)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

A safety classifier that lets an obfuscated harmful request through has failed in one of two ways, and the field's single attack-success number cannot tell them apart. Either the classifier never represented the harmful meaning at all - a capability limit - or it represented the meaning and returned its safe verdict anyway, which is a policy limit. The two point at disjoint engineering programmes: more safety training over verdicts cannot repair a state that never held the content, while it is exactly the right repair for a content-to-verdict mapping that is wrong. The authors want an audit that assigns each leak to one of the two, cheaply, from inside the defence. The larger goal is an evaluation of a guard that reports a cause per cell rather than a composed rate - the same shift from "what came out" to "what was inside" that our own white-box goal is built on.

## What they studied

Two open-weight content guards as whole systems, Llama Guard 3 8B and WildGuard 7B (both 32 layers), plus each guard's base model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) used only to calibrate ability. The unit of analysis is the guard's own residual stream captured at the input to every one of the 32 layers, at two positions - the last token of the payload and the last token of the rendered prompt - a 64-cell grid per condition, read by a logistic probe. The corpus is JailbreakBench: 100 harmful behaviours with their topic-matched benign counterparts, so every cell count is out of 100 and every AUROC on 200 examples. The attack surface is a ladder of 19 exactly invertible transforms applied to the payload alone: twelve byte and character ciphers (base64 through morse), two order transforms, and five surface transforms (zero-width characters, tag blocks, homoglyphs, fullwidth, combining marks), each placed in a wrapper that names the encoding but does not ask for decoding.

## How they did it

One deterministic forward pass per prompt: no sampling, no generation, no judge model. Two readings come off that pass. The decode reading is a logistic probe (L2, C=1) fitted on plaintext harmful-versus-benign activations and evaluated on the encoded condition without refitting - transfer rather than refit, because a probe trained on encoded data can separate the two corpora on any feature and prove nothing. The verdict reading is taken from the logits at the position where the guard's verdict token falls, located by the guard's own two-newline or label-string prefix, with the leading whitespace on the label tokens; every run records the mean probability mass on the two label tokens and treats a departure from one as a format failure. Crossing the two gives four cells - blocked on content, decoded but not blocked, blocked without decoding, never decoded - plus a fifth state, unmeasured, used where the decode read is not licensed; the authors hold that keeping "unmeasured" distinct from "did not decode" is the instrument's most consequential design choice. Licence to read decode at all is granted by a permutation null of 200 draws over the maximum of the grid: the evaluation labels are permuted with the fitted direction held fixed (permuting the training labels instead collapses the null and makes it far too tight), stratified by character length because the harmful and benign sets differ in length by a raw-length AUROC of 0.654 that every encoder carries through. Above the null sits a control floor adapted from the control-task literature: the same probe's transferred AUROC on conditions the guard's base model restates at rate exactly zero, floored at mean plus two standard deviations over 12 and 14 controls respectively. Above the floor sits an item-level holdout: the probe is fitted on one half of the plaintext items and read on the encoded versions of the other half, 200 random splits, so the fit cannot recognise the item it was told was harmful. The block axis gets its own screen, a wrapper arm running plaintext content inside each condition's own wrapper, with the floor taken from that wrapper's benign arm - and a length bound that gives the guard's actual blocking budget to the best monotone length-only rule, computed in characters and in the guard's own tokens. Per-prompt decode is thresholded at the 75th percentile of the same condition's benign score distribution, swept over six operating points.

## What counts as an explanation here

A two-axis cell attribution per (guard, condition) pair: for each encoding, whether harmful content was linearly recoverable from the guard's residual stream (decode: yes, no, or unmeasured) crossed with whether the guard blocked. The explanation is a partial, per-cell diagnosis of failure type, not a mechanism, not a circuit, and explicitly not causal - the authors state that establishing the guard could have used the content it did not would require ablating the recovered direction and watching the verdict, which they specify and do not run. What it does predict is which remedial programme applies to each measured leak, and what it correctly refuses to predict is the state behind any condition where its linear read has no licence.

## How they judged success

Success is a conservative count, measured four ways. First, licensing counts at each screen on a 19-condition ladder: free permutation, length-matched null, plus control floor, plus item-level holdout. Second, an ability criterion that is independent of every screen in the paper - a condition the base model decodes at rate zero cannot have been decoded, so any licence there is a false positive by a criterion that never consults the probe - giving a ceiling on true positives of 7 conditions for one guard and 5 for the other. Third, stability of licensing under 5, 10 and 20 length strata, checked as a screen that shares no input with the floor. Fourth, controls on the block axis: a benign arm in the same encoding, a wrapper factorial over bare, wrapped and encoded conditions, a plaintext baseline (the two guards differ by 22 points in benign block rate before any encoding), and a monotone length bound at matched blocking budget. Counts are reported as 95 percent Wilson intervals for block rates and as 200-split spreads for decode counts, which the authors note is spread across splits rather than a confidence interval.

## What they found

The headline is a negative result about method. Free permutation licensing admits 17 of 19 conditions on Llama Guard and 12 of 19 on WildGuard; a length-matched null cuts this to 6 and 7; the control floor to 4 and 4; the item-level holdout to 3 and 4. Twelve of the nineteen conditions are undecodable by the first guard's base model and fourteen by the second's, so at least 10 and 7 of the free-null licences were false positives before any screen ran. The largest result in the first analysis - a Caesar-shift condition licensed at the smallest p-value 200 draws can produce with an apparent policy-failure rate of 0.77 - sits on an encoding its base model decodes at 0.00, as do three WildGuard cells at 0.76, 0.69 and 0.66; the authors name high apparent decode with a block rate of exactly zero as the signature of the artefact, and note it is also what the most publishable finding looks like. The item holdout moves the largest transfer AUROC from 0.985 to 0.845 and shows the leak is ability-dependent: item memory is 0.147 to 0.239 of AUROC on decodable conditions and between -0.011 and 0.017 on the undecodable controls, which is why a floor calibrated where the confound is weakest is structurally blind to it. What survives is homoglyph, zero-width and word reversal on both guards, with held-out AUROCs 0.845, 0.767 and 0.758 (Llama Guard) against floors of 0.7066 and 0.6605, and the policy failure is populated on two of the four non-control conditions at 8 and 7 per 100 harmful prompts, against 17 and 23 when the probe has seen the item it scores. The wrapper screen removes the largest remaining WildGuard cell: the guard blocks 44 per 100 of benign plaintext wearing that wrapper and 25 per 100 of the encoded attack. Blocked without decoding is at most 1 per 100 on every reported pair and exactly 0 on 26 of 29 measurable pairs, with the nine unlicensed pairs settled by containment (block rate 0.00). Every genuine cipher sits at or near 0 per 100 blocked and unmeasured on decode, so the capability failure dominates the cipher band as an absence of instrument rather than a positive finding. The two guards dissociate completely on fullwidth - 85 per 100 blocked by one, 0 by the other - and neither decode measurement there survives, so only the behaviour is reported.

## Limits and open problems they name

The measurement is correlational; the causal test is named as the next experiment. A negative decode reading is evidence about a linear probe at one selected layer and position, not about the guard's computation. Control-set membership is inherited from the base model by proxy, since a format-locked classifier cannot be asked to restate anything. The reported cell is structurally the most vulnerable - it is approximately decode rate times one minus block rate, so false positives concentrate exactly where the guard blocks least. Surviving margins are thin, 0.052 to 0.139 over a floor, and the floor's multiplier of 2.0 lies below its no-verdict-change window on one guard. The format-detector cell is read from the uncontrolled probe and its held-out counterpart is not run; the authors state a held-out bound near zero would support the claim more strongly than the number they print. Two checkpoints and one English corpus. Unpinned: guard checkpoints are referenced by repository identifier, not revision hash.

## What we take from it

Three things. (1) **A verdict is a composition and must be decomposed before it is reported.** Their four-cell crossing is the pattern: for every failure our white-box model explains, name whether the quantity was present at the site we read and separately whether the downstream decision used it, and report "unmeasured" as a third value rather than collapsing it into absence - a negative read at one layer and position is about the instrument, and their software returns no value rather than False so that unmeasured cannot silently become did-not-do. (2) **A licence to read is a stack of screens, and the strongest one is an ability criterion that never consults the probe.** The construction to copy is the ladder: permutation null (with the maximum of the selection inside the null), a length-matched null against a corpus-level confound, a control floor on conditions where the target provably cannot exist, and an item-level holdout that stops the fit recognising the example. Each removed more than the last and the sum removed most of the map. (3) **A weakened instrument moves different cells in opposite directions, so which bound a screen produces has to be decided per cell before the result can be called.** Their Appendix C general form is the reusable rule: a cell whose finding is a positive count becomes a lower bound under a stricter instrument and can still carry a claim, while a cell whose finding is emptiness becomes an upper bound that is informative only if it is small. Our own audits should carry that per-cell direction, and the leftover lesson of this paper is the harder one: the authors' most striking number was an artefact, and they report it for calibration.
