## What they wanted, and why
Steering a model by adding one vector — the difference of class means (DiM) — is the default intervention in this literature, and it is a *single* direction applied identically to every position, whether or not the position carries the concept. A recent method, CHaRS, replaces that one vector with a whole family: cluster both classes' activations, solve an entropic optimal-transport problem between the clusters, and move each hidden state along a mixture of cluster displacements weighted toward its nearest clusters. The paper asks a deflationary question the original method did not: given that the plan is a coupling of the same two clouds, how much of what the map does is still DiM, and what does the transport plan actually add? A second question is deployment-shaped: steering maps are fitted on full-precision activations, but models ship quantized, which moves the activations the map was fitted on and applied to. The aim is to make the *difference* between a multi-directional map and a single direction a measurable, provable quantity rather than an implicit claim.

## What they studied
Base (not instruction-tuned) Gemma-2-2B (26 layers) and Granite-3.1-2B (40 layers) in bfloat16, steered in the residual stream at half depth (layer 13 and layer 20) with robustness checks at quarter and three-quarter depth. The behaviour is toxicity mitigation; fitting data is 2,000 toxic and 2,000 unlabelled Jigsaw comments truncated to 128 tokens, after dropping each text's first position (an attention sink on Granite) and any position with extreme norm. The unit of analysis is the **map**: a token-level or text-level push vector, whose parts are the clustering, the transport plan, the routing weights and the steering strength. Evaluation is on 3,000 RealToxicityPrompts of prompt toxicity ≥ 0.5 (the toxic slice) plus 3,000 below 0.5 as a control (the clean slice), greedy decoding up to 50 tokens.

## How they did it
Five settings define the family: the pooling of the fitting texts (per-text mean, individual tokens, or last token), the number of clusters, the entropic regularizer (sharp versus blurred plan), the routing bandwidth (narrow versus wide kernel), and whether routing weights are multiplied by cluster occupancy. Three points in that family are compared throughout: the **CHaRS code defaults** (mean pooling, 4 clusters, near-flat kernel, occupancy on), a **selective** map (token-level points, 32 clusters, sharp plan, narrow routing, occupancy on) and a **no-occupancy** map (token-level, 32 clusters, sharp plan, wide routing, occupancy off). Each map is compared with DiM of the *same* pooling at equal steering strength and again at matched toxicity, since a map that pushes harder reaches lower toxicity by degrading fluency. Then quantization: every model is re-instantiated under bitsandbytes INT8 (LLM.int8) and NF4, and each map is either **transferred** (fitted at full precision, applied to the quantized model) or **refitted** on the quantized model's own activations. To separate quantization's effect on the map from the noise of fitting it at all, the authors refit at full precision with four other k-means seeds and on four 80% subsamples of the fitting points, and take the larger median shift as the *refitting noise* floor. Geometrically, two quantities are measured on 4,000 unsteered prompt states without generating any text: **input dependence** (average distance of a token's push from the map's average push, relative to the length of that average, so it is scale-free and zero for DiM) and **offset** (how far the average push lies from the DiM vector).

## What counts as an explanation here
A **theorem plus a diagnostic**, not a description. The explanation is an identity: for any coupling whatsoever, the cluster displacements averaged with the cluster occupancies as weights reconstruct the difference of means exactly, so the transport plan only splits one mean displacement among the clusters and cannot change their weighted average. Everything else follows as a corollary — a clustered map can differ from DiM only for tokens whose *routing* weights depart from the occupancy weights, and if routing is flat under occupancy weighting the map is DiM whatever the plan. It is a complete account of one quantity (the map's average push) and explicitly not an account of which part of the push does the work: the paper's own reading of the selective map's behaviour by word class is labelled descriptive, and the author does not claim optimal transport is *necessary* rather than one sharp pairing among others.

## How they judged success
Toxicity is the mean probability of the toxic label under toxic-bert, with three further judges (an offensive-language RoBERTa, a ToxiGen-trained classifier, and a commercial JEV model) to check the result is not an artifact of a Jigsaw-trained scorer. Fluency is median continuation perplexity under an unrelated Qwen2.5-1.5B; repetition is distinct-4 (share of distinct word 4-grams), which catches degenerate loops that perplexity rewards. All comparisons are paired by prompt, with Holm correction inside a stated family; quality is compared at *matched toxicity* by interpolating DiM's strength-to-toxicity curve (strengths 0 to 6) at the toxicity the map reaches, with 2,000-resample bootstrap intervals. Equivalence is tested by two one-sided tests in interval form, with margins fixed before the run (never registered). A grid of layer, pooling, cluster count, regularizer and bandwidth measures input dependence so the tested configurations are chosen from measurement rather than pilot intuition, and the study reports that this grid drew on the same 3,000 prompts later used for evaluation.

## What they found
The identity holds and it is the paper's main result: whatever the plan, occupancy-weighted displacements average to DiM, so only routing can make a clustered map differ. At the CHaRS code defaults the routing is nearly flat — the largest kernel weight of a token is 1.11 to 1.29 times its smallest, routing weights sit 1.6 to 2.6% from occupancies in total variation, and the average push differs from the mean-difference vector by only 0.7 to 2.7% — and the map behaves exactly like mean-pooled DiM: no significant toxicity difference at strengths 1, 2 and 3 with 95% intervals excluding changes above about 10% of the toxicity DiM leaves, and in the every-layer protocol CHaRS is statistically equivalent to Mean-AcT in all eight pre-specified tests. Narrowing the bandwidth is the single lever that moves input dependence (71 to 90 times), while clusters, regularizer, occupancy and pooling move it by factors of at most about four. The selective map reaches 0.17–0.22 offset on the steered prompts and at strength 3 leaves 37–39% (Gemma) and 51–61% (Granite) less toxicity than DiM over three k-means seeds, and at matched toxicity is more fluent by 3.4–12.5%. But the **no-occupancy control**, a nearly constant global vector built from the same clusters and plan (input dependence below 0.01, offset 0.15–0.18), also leaves 14–30% less toxicity than DiM — so how much of the gain comes from treating tokens differently is not isolated. A strong regularizer with narrow routing destroys the text (distinct-4 falling to 0.53 and 0.63 at strength 3 while perplexity stays low, because a loop of short words is easy to predict). With occupancy weighting, NF4 moves a clustered map's average push by 11–13%, about as far as it moves DiM (within 2%); without occupancy weighting, merely changing the k-means seed moves it by 12–29%, so quantization cannot be told apart from fit noise at all. Steering survives quantization: the selective map removes 88–92% of the unsteered model's toxicity at the same precision, and a full-precision map transferred to NF4 never leaves significantly more toxicity than one refitted there, with intervals bounding any extra toxicity at 14% of what DiM leaves.

## Limits and open problems they name
Two ~2B models, one behaviour, greedy 50-token continuations, and only INT8/NF4 via bitsandbytes. The configurations were chosen using the same 3,000 prompts later used for evaluation — there is no held-out prompt set — and intervals account for prompt sampling but not for the randomness of fitting a map (only two extra k-means seeds check refitting). The maps and the primary judge are both trained on Jigsaw data, so a map may learn to remove exactly what that classifier detects; the gain is weaker on Gemma under the ToxiGen judge, suggesting it may rest largely on profanity and slurs, and there is no human evaluation. The account of what the selective map does differently is descriptive with no component removed or randomized, and the two promising maps differ in *both* bandwidth and occupancy weighting, so the two effects are not separated. Whether optimal transport is needed at all — versus another sharp pairing of the same clusters, or a better single global direction — is explicitly untested; the author also could not reproduce the original method's reported advantage over Linear-AcT on Gemma, and notes steering every layer raised perplexity 3–17 times the published figure under their own decoding and prompts.

## What we take from it
A **proof, a diagnostic pair and a control**, all three directly importable. (1) The identity is the general form of a habit this survey keeps re-learning: whenever we build a richer intervention than a mean difference — clustered, per-token, conditional, mixtures of experts of directions — we should first ask what its *occupancy-weighted average* is, because if the answer is the mean difference then the richness lives entirely in the routing and the whole method's merit reduces to whether routing departs from occupancy. Here the departure is measured directly (total-variation distance from occupancies) and it is 1.6–2.6% at the released defaults, which is why the method is DiM in disguise. (2) The split into **input dependence** (scale-free spread of pushes across tokens) and **offset** (how far the average push sits from DiM) is a two-number diagnostic we can apply to any per-item steering rule before generating a single token, and it cleanly separates "the map is selective" from "the map is a different global direction". (3) The *no-occupancy* control is the constructive lesson: any per-token advantage claim needs a same-clusters, same-plan, nearly-constant sibling, because here that sibling captured 14–30% of the win and left the per-token story unproven. Two further warnings transfer to our own intervention experiments: a compressed cache or quantized model can invalidate a fitted direction more by *fit noise* than by compression (seed change moved the push 12–29% where NF4 moved it 11–13%), so the noise floor must be measured before any "quantization hurt the vector" claim; and a lower toxicity score at higher strength is not necessarily an improvement, since degenerate repetition scores as non-toxic — which is why distinct-4 belongs beside every perplexity figure.
