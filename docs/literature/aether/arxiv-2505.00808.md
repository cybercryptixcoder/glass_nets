## What they wanted, and why

Mechanistic interpretability (MI) asks "how does this network compute its answer" but has never stated what kind of statement would count as the answer, so the field cannot tell a real mechanism from a story that happens to fit behaviour. The authors want a philosophical foundation strong enough to justify the practice and precise enough to bound it: a single criterion that separates MI from other interpretability paradigms, a well-defined target for the word "faithful", and an explicit statement of the assumption that everything depends on. The larger goal is to place interpretability as a natural science of artificial-and-formal objects — a "strange science", because unlike physics we already know the implementation exactly and yet lack understanding of the system — so that its methods can be judged by the standards of explanation rather than by benchmark scores.

## What they studied

No models, no data, no experiments: the objects of study are the *explanations* MI produces. The unit of analysis is the explanation itself, and behind it the notion of a **ur-explanation** — the idealised, "always-already present" account of how a trained network computes over learned representations for a given input distribution. They also analyse the abstract (unnamed) class of generalising neural networks, the concept spaces of a machine versus a human interpreter, and one concrete method family (sparse autoencoders) used as the worked example of hidden theoretical commitment.

## How they did it

A constructive-philosophy argument in seven steps. (1) Adopt the **Explanatory View**: a generalising network contains an implicit explanation in its internal structure, so the internals are a target to be extracted rather than a black box to be fit. (2) Define structure as compressibility, and tie a good explanation to reproducing or predicting the observations from a shorter description. (3) Define **ur-explanations** as the ideal explanation of behaviour in terms of learned internal structure. (4) Define **explanatory faithfulness** as the extent to which the intermediate activations an explanation *prescribes* at each layer match the model's actual activations, and contrast it with behavioural faithfulness, which only asks that end predictions agree. (5) Ground "computation over representations" in Marr's levels — explanation belongs at the algorithmic and computational levels, not the implementation level — and take Harding's three criteria for an activation to be a *representation*: information (a probe can be trained on it), use (removing it by intervention degrades task performance), and misrepresentation (patching in information about a property can make the model wrong about it). (6) Give a four-property definition of MI and use it to derive limits. (7) Apply the No Miracles Argument twice — first to the network as if it were a scientific theory (its predictive success would be miraculous unless its internals correspond to real structure), then to the researcher's own explanation — to justify treating a layer-by-layer predictive explanation as *approximately* the ur-explanation.

## What counts as an explanation here

An answer to a "why" question that compresses behaviour into a shorter, runnable description and, in the MI case, is **Model-level** (about the network in isolation, not the surrounding system), **Ontic** (its entities are held to be real, not merely predictive conveniences), **Causal-Mechanistic** (a gap-free step-by-step causal chain, "explaining why by explaining how"), and **Falsifiable**. The intended form is algorithmic-level pseudocode-like structure locatable in layers, explicitly meant to be partial and interpreter-relative rather than complete: the value-ladenness section argues there is no such thing as interpreting a network, only interpreting it at a chosen scale of precision and against a chosen metric of precision. A trivially complete explanation always exists — the activation trace, which is literally a proof of the output — and is rejected as unilluminating because it does not compress.

## How they judged success

Argumentative and conceptual rather than empirical: distinguish correct from incorrect uses of *explanation* by worked pairs (the pen pushed by an "aether" versus tungsten atoms; ice-cream sales versus shark attacks; a metal rod explained by a thermal-expansion law versus by vibrating atoms), and check that each property in the four-part definition does independent work by exhibiting explanations that satisfy three but not four. The empirical anchor for the faithfulness criterion is Nanda et al.'s modular-arithmetic work, cited as an example of an explanation that predicts intermediate structure layer by layer; the paper names circuit-replacement methods that need non-trivial patching to reproduce behaviour as the contrasting case. No new metric, baseline, or benchmark is proposed.

## What they found

The central positive results are definitional. Explanatory faithfulness is well-defined only once ur-explanations are posited, and under the classical behaviourist view statements about circuit equivalence collapse into statements about behavioural statistics — so the criterion MI is implicitly reaching for cannot even be stated without the Explanatory View. The four-property definition cleanly excludes chain-of-thought readings (system-level), concept-based and propositional interpretability (not causal-mechanistic), and circuit-efficiency metrics of the Varma et al. kind (epistemic rather than ontic). The paper then argues that MI is unavoidably **theory-laden** — the SAE commitment to sparse, linearly accessible features, and the TopK/JumpReLU commitment to a monotonic-importance heuristic, are theory the method cannot declare itself free of — and **value-laden**, since the right granularity depends on whether the interpreter wants crude interventions, guarantees, or scientific satisfaction. Its named limits are the model-versus-system gap (systems that think via search, ensembling or multi-agent structure resist model-level explanation) and the low-abstraction gap (it is not known whether higher-level accounts are derivable from mechanism-level ones). Last, it states the load-bearing assumption it cannot prove: the **Principle of Explanatory Optimism**, strong form "everything important for behaviour is human-understandable", weak form "most is", and asks for it to be formalised or refuted.

## Limits and open problems they name

Explanatory Optimism is offered with no argument, only a call to prove it; the authors acknowledge that if it is false, MI may not be a viable route to its stated safety and science goals. The explanatory-complexity classes and the notion of explanatory universality analogous to the Church–Turing thesis are explicitly left undefined. The No Miracles Argument transfers the well-known objections to scientific realism along with its conclusion, and the paper's own hedge is that layer-by-layer agreement is evidence rather than proof — the residual gap being that a researcher has no a priori access to the ur-explanation they are claimed to be approximating. Alignment with published significance thresholds is doubted in a footnote: conventional test levels are called far too weak to license an explanatory-faithfulness claim. Nothing here is operationalised into a measurement.

## What we take from it

**A criterion, and a warning — both usable directly.** (1) *A framing we should adopt as a report column*: separate behavioural faithfulness (predictions agree) from explanatory faithfulness (the explanation's intermediate stages match the model's), because our whole white-box goal is the second and every patching score we compute only measures the first. The concrete form for our batteries is to score a candidate explanation not just on output agreement but on agreement at each intermediate site it claims, which is exactly the instrumentation `arxiv-2610.02098` shows can diverge. (2) *A definition to test our probes against*: Harding's information / use / misrepresentation triple is a ready-made checklist — a probe establishes information, an ablation establishes use, and a patch that reliably induces the error establishes misrepresentation; a probe that passes only the first should not be called a representation. (3) *A standing caveat for any feature dictionary*: the sparse-and-linear commitment, and the monotonic-importance heuristic behind TopK-style gating, are theory, so our reports should name the architectural prior under which a feature was read out and treat "unsupervised" as a claim never to be made. (4) *A precision warning*: "there is only interpreting a network at a given scale of precision and against a given metric" — so effect-size statements carry more information than significance statements, which is the same conclusion `arxiv-2610.02098` reaches from the measurement side.
