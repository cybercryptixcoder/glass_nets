# SEER: Self-Explainability Enhancement of Large Language Models' Representations

- **Key:** arxiv-2502.05242
- **Authors / organisation:** Guanxu Chen, Dongrui Liu, Tao Luo, Jing Shao; Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory; Shanghai Jiao Tong University (School of Mathematical Sciences / Institute of Natural Sciences and the School of Electronic Information and Electrical Engineering). Chen and Liu are marked equal contribution.
- **Date:** 2025-02-07 (v1 only)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05242
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (abstract, sections 1-5, Tables 1-10, Appendices A-C incl. the proof of Theorem 1, the hyperparameter table and the loss-function comparison)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

The authors start from two complaints that the field shares. Chain-of-thought text is not a faithful account of how the model reached an answer, and the usual remedy — bolt a probe or a classifier onto the side of a frozen model — explains one opaque system with another opaque system. Their proposal is to make the model itself easier to read: train the representation so that examples of a given concept land close together and examples of different concepts land apart, then the concept the model is "considering" can be read off by looking at which cluster a state belongs to. The larger goal is a model whose internals are legible without an external interpreter, on the bet that legibility and trustworthiness-task performance move together rather than trade off. They also want a reason to believe disentangling does not damage general ability, so they give a generalization argument from optimal transport and then check it empirically.

## What they studied

Four instruction-tuned open-weight LLMs at the 7-9B scale: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-v0.3-Instruct and Gemma2-9B-it, plus Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct in the appendix. The unit of analysis is a **hidden state at a fixed layer and a fixed token position** — the layer at 80% of depth (25th for Llama-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B-v0.3, 21st for Qwen2.5-7B, 33rd for Gemma2-9B, counted from layer 0) and the last token of the input (usually the end-of-turn token). Concepts come from three scenario families: seven mathematical branches of MATH, seven knowledge domains of MMLU, and five BeaverTails risk categories plus a safety concept. No neuron, head or circuit is examined; the object is the geometry of a token's vector across a labelled set of inputs.

## How they did it

SEER is a fine-tuning procedure with two terms, applied through LoRA at the target layer. The **disentangle loss** is InfoNCE over pairs: two examples sampled from the same concept form a positive pair, the other concepts sampled in the batch are the negatives, so the loss pulls same-concept normalized embeddings together and pushes different-concept ones apart (temperature 0.1). The **retain loss** is what stops that from destroying the model: an L2 distance between the pre- and post-edit representation at that layer on data from a general-capability retain set, plus KL between the edited and reference model's next-token distributions on the first member of each pair. The total is the disentangle loss plus a weighted retain loss (lambda 0.1, alpha 1, learning rate 0.001, two epochs, LoRA rank and alpha 16, dropout 0.05). The edited checkpoint is then evaluated, not interpreted: five geometric statistics on held-out representations (coding rate and effective rank for within-concept compression, average pairwise L2, average angle, average Hausdorff distance between concept sets), general benchmarks on OpenCompass (GSM8K, MMLU, AGIEval), and downstream tasks where a *separate* detector is trained on the edited representations — a mean-embedding similarity classifier, a linear probe, a Latent Guard cross-attention detector, and supervised fine-tuning on the same data. The detoxification arm scores generations on BeaverTails and SaladBench test sets for safety rate, XSTest for over-refusal, and the same three capability benchmarks. A retain-loss ablation drops each component in turn, and an appendix swaps InfoNCE for four other contrastive objectives.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **geometry**, not a description: an explanation of a decision is the identity of the concept cluster its last-token state falls into, made legible by having been trained to be separable. It is not textual and not causal in the intervention sense — nothing is ablated or patched to show that the cluster did the work — but the authors do give it a predictive role, because a clustering that is clean enough to read is also a clustering that a small supervised head can exploit, so the claim "the concept is linearly present here" is testable by fitting that head. The explanation is partial in the strongest way: it covers only the concept partition the disentangle set defines, and it says nothing about how features compose into a prediction, only which bucket the state sits in. The theory supplies the only general statement, and it is a statement about a surrogate classifier on top of the docked representation rather than about the network.

## How they judged success

Five representation-geometry metrics computed before and after the edit on held-out sets (per-scenario, per-model), with percentage improvement averages over models quoted in the text. General capability via GSM8K, MMLU and AGIEval with an average-gap row, which is the honest framing because the point of the retain loss is that these numbers should not move. Downstream: classification accuracy for binary and multi-risk safety detection, each baseline trained on origin and on SEER representations, so the comparison is a within-baseline before/after rather than a cross-method leaderboard; safety rate on two test sets, refusal rate on XSTest, and capability scores for detoxification. Controls: the retain-loss ablation (both terms, L2 only, neither), five contrastive losses ranked on the same five metrics, an SFT-after-SEER arm, a SEER-after-SFT arm, and a larger 14B model. No seeds, no error bars and no confidence intervals are printed anywhere in the text read.

## What they found

Disentanglement is achieved and measured without a detector: effective rank of the concept subspaces falls by 57.3% on average and coding rate by 8.9%, while average L2 distance between concepts rises by 273.5%, average angle by 109.6% and average Hausdorff distance by 324.5%. General capability is essentially flat — average gaps of +0.07 GSM8K, -0.25 MMLU, +0.42 AGIEval across the twelve model-scenario cells — with the largest single regression Llama-3.1-8B on knowledge (GSM8K 84.5 to 82.0). Detectors trained on SEER representations improve: binary safety classification by 13.4% and multi-risk by 17.0% on average, and the best cells are wide gaps (Llama-3.1-8B linear probe 82.2 to 91.4 on binary, 78.3 to 90.8 on multi-risk; self-similarity 56.6 to 78.7). Not every cell improves — Qwen2.5-7B's linear probe drops from 91.2 to 82.1 on binary, and both SFT cells on Mistral and Gemma2 move by less than a point downward in some columns. Detoxification improves by 7.5% on safety rate on average with over-refusal broadly controlled and capability roughly steady (average capability 67.1 to 65.8 for Llama; 70.6 to 71.0 for Qwen), and applying SEER after SFT lifts that pipeline's safety by 12.9%. NT-Xent, which uses both members of a pair as negatives, adds a further 2.4% on average. InfoNCE ranks best among five contrastive losses at an average rank of 2.21 against 2.32 for NT-Xent, 3.14 for Barlow Twins, 3.17 for triplet and 4.14 for plain contrastive. The retain loss is load-bearing exactly where the concepts overlap general ability: on MATH and MMLU, dropping the KL term (L2 only) collapses MMLU from 69.2 to 5.8 and GSM8K from 46.4 to 2.8 for Llama-3.1-8B on the knowledge scenario, while on the safety scenario it barely matters.

## Limits and open problems they name

The theory has flat assumptions and the authors state them: the margin's Lipschitz constant is taken to be uniform over the hypothesis class, and the step from "better-separated representations" to "a better classifier with a larger margin" is *assumed* rather than shown. Theorem 1 is also not theirs — it is imported from the whitening-of-representations literature and the proof is rephrased from that source. The operational definition of explanation is a clustering, so a wrong-but-confident cluster is indistinguishable from a right one by this instrument. The hyperparameter choice is thin: one layer (80% of depth), one token position (the last), one retain set, two epochs, one LoRA configuration across four very different architectures, and no sweep of the layer or the temperature is reported. Several cells go the wrong way and are printed without comment (the Qwen linear-probe regression, the over-refusal increases in the SFT+SEER arms on Llama and Mistral). No variance is reported anywhere, so a 0.4-point move in a capability benchmark cannot be told apart from run noise, and the paper uses the flat capability row as evidence for the retain loss.

## What we take from it

**A method we can use as an intervention, plus one warning about reading it as an explanation.** The intervention is clean and cheap to copy: an InfoNCE pair loss on a single layer's last-token state, regularised by a representation-space L2 anchor and an output-distribution KL anchor to the unedited model, applied through LoRA. If our white-box model wants a concept axis it can reason about, this is how to build one without training a new architecture — and the metric suite (coding rate, effective rank, which they attribute to the subspace-compression line, plus three between-set distances) is a reusable way to say whether a concept axis is clean.

The warning is the one our recent entries keep circling. **A learned clustering is a way of producing an axis, not a way of licensing a claim about the computation on it.** The same checkpoint reports "the safety concept is now linearly separable at 91.4%" through a fresh linear probe and "the concept is clean" through a coding-rate drop, and neither number can fail in a way that would tell the two apart — the probe is trained on the same labels that the disentangle set was built from, so its gain partly measures label reuse, and the geometry metrics reward exactly what the loss optimises, which is a property of the objective rather than a finding. Mechanical rule for us: **when our model is trained to make a representation readable, its readability is scored on concepts held out of the disentangle set, by a head whose data the disentangling never saw, and next to the unedited model's score on the same head; a gain measured on the training partition of the concept family is reported as reconstruction, not as explanation.** Their own best counterexample is in the table: SFT on safety questions collapses on Qwen2.5-7B to 58.4 BT / 68.8 SB and climbs back to 93.5 / 92.5 with SEER, which is a legitimate capability claim, and it sits in the same table as cells where a probe that started at 91.2 ends at 82.1 — the direction of the effect is set by the arm, not by the concept.

Second, short: **the ablation is the part to imitate.** Dropping the KL term and dropping the retain loss entirely, per scenario, is what shows the retain loss protects exactly the overlapping concepts (Math, Knowledge) and not the disjoint ones (Safety) — a per-scenario ablation that names *where* a regulariser binds is worth more than an aggregate "capability preserved" row, and it tells us that an editing procedure aimed at safety concepts is not the one that needs a capability anchor.
