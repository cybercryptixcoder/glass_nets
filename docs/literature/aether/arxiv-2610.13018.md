# FlyHack: Stress-Testing Mechanistic Claims in a Connectome-Constrained Visual Model

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.13018
- **Authors / organisation:** Aashish Dhawan; Department of Computer & Information Science & Engineering, University of Florida; v1 2026-10-09, q-bio.NC, CC BY 4.0
- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13018

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Connectome-constrained networks put a biological wiring diagram into a model and then train only a small parameter set on task error, which makes every component in the model an intervenable stand-in for a named biological object. The obvious hope is that explaining such a model is easier: you can say "these cell types carry the behaviour" and the statement means something outside the model. The author asks what that hope is actually worth. The question is not whether the wiring constraint is real (it is, by construction) but how far a mechanistic conclusion travels when you change the comparison model, the intervention rule, the granularity at which the circuit is described, or which member of a trained ensemble you happened to pick. Two stages: a model-family comparison on behaviour, then a within-model audit of circuit discovery scored against exhaustive single-component ground truth.

## What they studied
flyvis 1.2.0 - 50 released pretrained instances of a recurrent *Drosophila* optic-lobe model: 64 biological cell types across 721 hexagonal columns, about 45,000 neurons and 1.5 million neuron-level contacts, parameterised by 65 time constants, 65 resting potentials and 604 type-to-type synapse scaling factors (one shared scalar per connection class, not per synapse), plus a trained decoder of 7,427 parameters reading 34 type units to optical flow. Controls are causal temporal CNNs trained on the same task, rendering pipeline, loss and input lattice at three sizes (8,161 parameters, matched to flyvis network plus decoder; 50,002; 988,418) with five seeds each. Unit of analysis: connection class, cell type, and the ensemble member. Behaviours: two decoded-flow readouts (B0 a standard grating, B1 a contrast-inverting grating), a three-level reverse-phi stimulus, a stationary sawtooth luminance gradient, and adversarial direction reversal.

## How they did it
Two stages. The family stage trains the CNN controls and compares them on illusions and on adversarial margin, holding the input lattice identical (zero-padded 31x31 grid) so a per-column perturbation bound means the same thing in both families, and attacking with projected gradient descent on the cosine between decoded flow and nominal direction, bisecting in log space for the minimal bound. The within-model stage computes ground truth by brute force: for each of the 604 connection classes and each of four replacement semantics - zero, same-stimulus time-averaged current (kept only as a leakage diagnostic), held-out mean current from six calibration movies at three directions never including the target direction, and the other behaviour's frame-by-frame current - the effect of removing that one connection is measured on the decoded behaviour. Every intervention is run three times and the median is taken. Circuit-discovery methods (integrated gradients, first-order edge attribution patching, greedy search, top-k sets) are then scored against that exhaustive table. Crucially, a second attribution arm is built to match each intervention path: for held-out mean and counterfactual semantics, all 604 strengths are scaled together along a shared path while the summed replacement-current field is simultaneously removed, so the attribution answers the question the intervention actually asked. Finally the unit of intervention is switched to cell type, and a tuning criterion measured directly on T4/T5 cells with the decoder out of the loop is used so that both granularities can be compared under one target.

## What counts as an explanation here
A circuit: a ranked or selected set of named biological components (connection classes or cell types) that is claimed to carry a behaviour. The paper's contribution is not a new form of explanation but a calibration of this one, and its headline framing is that such claims are indexical - they hold for a stated model family, intervention rule, circuit representation and trained solution. It is explicitly partial: single-component effects do not compose into a description of joint action, and the paper shows that they fail to.

## How they judged success
Continuous-effect agreement between attribution and the exhaustive ground truth (mean full-vector Spearman across ten models); identification quality (AUROC against the ground-truth top 20, precision@20); circuit sufficiency scored by the number of eight clips (standard gratings plus backward-drifting counterparts in four directions) on which the background-ablated model reproduces the full model's direction within 45 degrees, magnitude within a twofold band and bounded activity; the same criterion applied to a decoder-free T4/T5 direction-selectivity score that counts subtypes whose DSI is at least half the full model's and whose preferred direction is within 45 degrees; size-matched random cell-type sets as a control; Wilson intervals; Mann-Whitney comparisons against the release's legacy task-error ranking; and a pre-scripted prediction table (sixteen directional predictions and gates, recorded in the experiment scripts before the runs, explicitly not externally timestamped preregistrations).

## What they found
Attribution effect sizes collapse when the attribution is not built for the intervention, and rankings do not. Zero-aligned first-order edge attribution reaches Spearman 0.190 (B0) and 0.062 (B1) against held-out-mean ground truth, while the path-matched version reaches 0.884 and 0.860 - a 0.872 versus 0.126 average across the twenty model-behaviour pairs, and 0.783 against 0.132 restricted to the union of the ground-truth top-100. Matched integrated gradients reach 0.565 and 0.385 under held-out replacement and 0.638 and 0.118 under counterfactual. But AUROC against the ground-truth top 20 stays above 0.91 in every single row including the mismatched ones, and precision@20 only ranges from 0.44 to 0.66. Substitution for a graded effect without substitution for identification, in one table. The mismatched effect sizes also contaminate downstream reading: under same-stimulus mean ground truth the misses look 56.0 and 63.1 percent inhibitory, and that apparent enrichment disappears under held-out-matched attribution, so the inhibitory story was a leak.

Single-component rankings do not yield a sufficient circuit. Under zero ablation of the complement no greedy set reaches the sufficiency criterion within 60 connections in any of ten models; top-k sets need a median of 320 connections (80 to 604) whether ranked by IG or by the true single-connection effect, and all 604 when ranked by first-order EAP. Under same-stimulus mean replacement greedy search finds 4.5-connection sets in 16 of 20 cases, but only three of those sets contain any directed path from a photoreceptor to a decoder input - the compactness was stimulus leakage.

Cell-type granularity gives the paper its cleanest matched comparison. On ten models with one target, a cell-type search reaches the tuning criterion in 6 of 10, after 4 to 9 additions; expanding each successful type set into its source-active connections (up to 71 to 164 connections) and greedily pruning outside the common core removes 74 to 91 percent of them, leaving 13 to 31 added connections and 197 to 215 in total, all six endpoints one-step irreducible. A post-hoc sensitivity band on the magnitude of the preferred-minus-null response then knocks four of the six below criterion and leaves two. Across all 50 models, silencing T4/T5 output drops the sufficiency score to at most 2 of 8 in 40 of 50 (80.0 percent, interval 67.0 to 88.8), the purely anatomical pathway set meets the tuning criterion in only 4 of 50, size-matched random sets score 0 in 989 of 1,000 draws, and the greedy cell-type search succeeds in 25 of 50 after a median of 7 additions - so half the ensemble admits the criterion-preserving circuit and half does not, with no detectable association to the release's task-error ranking (rank-biserial -0.060 and -0.168, p = .780 and .313).

Behavioural comparisons refuse to isolate wiring. Under the three-level reverse-phi stimulus flyvis reversal counts swing hard with velocity (at a 30-degree period, 14, 46, 25, 32, 37 and 20 of 50 across descending velocity; at 90 degrees, 3, 32, 37, 19, 1 and 3), and task-matched CNNs also reverse in selected bands (all five small and all five medium checkpoints at 90 degrees and 65.7 deg/s; none at 90 degrees and 197.2 deg/s), so the response is neither uniform nor family-specific. The stationary sawtooth prediction - at least 35 of 50 models turning in the fly's direction with magnitude at least 10 percent of a matched moving grating, sign flipping with polarity - failed outright at 3 of 50 per polarity, 0 of 50 for both, and median sustained responses 2.3 to 2.6 percent of the moving grating. Adversarial margin is real but shallow in family terms: flyvis needs 0.21 to 0.27 of contrast to reverse at contrasts 0.05 to 0.5, roughly double the parameter-matched CNN's 0.14 and 0.13, but the medium and large CNNs need 0.15 to 0.25, within a factor of 1.4, and at full contrast no flyvis model reverses under any bound up to 0.5 while three CNN checkpoints do. Transfer rates flip order with normalisation (0.15 versus 0.29 at each source's own bound; 0.61 versus 0.50 at a fixed bound), and input gradients are more similar inside the flyvis ensemble (cosine 0.14) than across same-size CNN seeds (-0.08) without buying a transfer advantage.

The appendix adds three evaluation artefacts that each independently produce a false result: two-level contrast inversion is provably equivalent to a half-period translation (exact for all 209 polarity-flipped frames at the even period, one-pixel defect at the odd one), a one-sided retained-output ratio counted as success above 0.8 is satisfied by two-to-eight-connection sets whose actual ratio ranges from below zero to above three and on some curves past a thousand, while those same sets score 0 to 2 of 8 on the behavioural battery, and same-input mean-current ablation injects stimulus-specific information that lets degenerate sets pass.

## Limits and open problems they name
One architecture and its released ensemble; the launch-time training split and the generator of the packaged task-error scalar are not recoverable, so the shared evaluation is descriptive rather than a verified held-out comparison, and the CNNs trained for 30,000 steps against flyvis's 250,000. Most stimuli are periodic gratings; attacks use one direction and frequency per condition, a single random start and a central-column readout; low-contrast CNN cells rest on one or two seeds. Sawtooth luminances are normalised rather than calibrated to the fly experiment and no eye movements were simulated. Held-out means exclude target movies but reuse the same stimulus families and calibration directions. Output silencing is a model intervention, not cell removal, and the T4/T5 result cannot separate recurrent dependence from loss of direct decoder input. The matched-random comparison uses 20 draws per model. Pruned connection endpoints are greedy and one-step irreducible, not globally minimal, and 455 of 615 pruning steps were numerically ambiguous at the frozen scale. The author notes the predictions were script-recorded, not timestamped, preregistrations.

## What we take from it
The method that matters most for us is the intervention-matched attribution path: if our white-box model scores a coordinate or a circuit by gradient, and we validate it against a replacement intervention, the attribution and the intervention have to share a path or the effect sizes will be wrong by an order of what they should be while the ranking stays intact. That gives a mechanical reporting split - identification quality and effect-size agreement as separate columns - which is the sharpest statement of a divide this folder has been circling for a dozen runs. Second, the paper is the cleanest template on disk for scoring a circuit claim by sufficiency rather than by faithfulness: keep the set, ablate the complement, and require the behaviour to be reproduced on a battery of clips under a two-sided band. Third, its record of failed predictions and its three appendix artefacts are worth copying wholesale as a habit - the reverse-phi phase equivalence, the one-sided ratio and the mean-current leak are all variations of "the evaluation credited set membership to a leak", and each would have been invisible to a fidelity score. The negative result on family-level behaviour is the warning: a predictive claim about a model family needs the cheap control family trained on the same task, because the expensive anatomical constraint bought a factor of two on one adversarial axis and nothing on the illusions.
