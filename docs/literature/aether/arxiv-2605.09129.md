# Data-driven Circuit Discovery for Interpretability of Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.09129
- **Authors / organisation:** Daking Rai, Mor Geva, Ziyu Yao; George Mason University / Tel Aviv University (NSF- and Alon-Scholarship-funded work; AI tools used for code and manuscript polishing, not for ideas or experimental design, per its own disclosure)
- **Date:** 2026-05-09 (arXiv:2605.09129v2)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09129
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit discovery has a workflow-shaped assumption nobody states: define a task as a dataset, average every example's attribution into one vector, return one subgraph, call it the mechanism for the task. That assumption is worth exactly one test — do all examples of the task actually use the same computation? The authors first show that when a dataset deliberately mixes two tasks the model handles differently, the standard pipeline returns a single circuit whose faithfulness is *as high on each task as that task's own dedicated circuit*, while the two dedicated circuits are nearly useless on each other. Faithfulness, in other words, does not detect that a circuit is a splice of two mechanisms. They then show the same heterogeneity exists *within* a single task once you vary surface form, and propose the minimal repair: insert a grouping step before attribution is averaged, and run the unchanged discovery method once per group. The larger goal is a circuit whose scope is set by the model's own computation rather than by a human task label.

## What they studied

Three models spanning two orders of magnitude and two families: GPT-2 Small (124M), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Four tasks: indirect object identification (IOI), entity binding (EB), single-token arithmetic addition, and sequence completion (induction-style n-gram repeat). Thirty-two dataset variants in total (7 IOI, 17 EB, 5 arithmetic, 3 sequence completion) built by varying exactly one axis at a time — complexity (2- vs 3-person, 2- to 8-item lists, 2- vs 3-operand, n-gram length), syntax (ABBA vs BABA vs passive; comma vs period delimiter; three verbal phrasings of arithmetic), or domain (names vs single-letter labels, symbolic vs verbal, colour-box vs comma). Unit of analysis is the **edge** of the computation graph, with circuits taken at a fixed budget of the top 5% of ranked edges. Discovery methods are EAP-IG (default), EAP, and — as an independent check on GPT-2 only — E-Act, which shares no attribution code path because it ablates each edge directly.

## How they did it

Two experiments, then the method. **Experiment 1 (cross-task mixing):** confirm the four base tasks use different mechanisms by discovering a circuit on each and scoring it on the others' test sets; then build six unordered task pairs, construct discovery sets of fixed 600 examples at mixing proportions from 0.0 to 1.0 in steps of 0.1, discover one circuit per set, and evaluate it on each task's own 200-example held-out test set — never on the mixture. Faithfulness is the normalised logit-difference recovery f(C,G;m) = (m(C)−m(∅))/(m(G)−m(∅)), unbounded, so values above 1 (circuit beats full model) and below 0 (circuit worse than full ablation) are printed rather than clipped. Then decompose the mixed circuit's top-5% edges by whether they appear in circuit A, circuit B, both, or neither. **Experiment 2 (cross-variant transfer):** for each pair of variants differing along one axis, discover both circuits and score each on the other's test set; a transfer counts as failed only when the shortfall exceeds the larger of the two variants' bootstrap noise floors. Floors are estimated per variant by drawing 2,000 pairs of independent bootstrap resamples of the 200-prompt test set and taking the 95th percentile of the absolute difference — median floor 5.6 points across the 49 variants, smallest on arithmetic (1.3–3.1), largest on Qwen entity binding (up to 21.9). One EB pair is excluded outright because its floor is ~122 points (the 2-colour-box variant's empty-circuit score nearly equals the full model's, so the normalising denominator vanishes). 137 pairs survive construction, 136 are testable, each producing three possible outcomes (both directions transfer / one / neither). **The DCD change itself is one line of workflow:** a grouping function Π partitions the dataset, then the *existing* discovery method F is applied unchanged to each group — no new attribution, no new selection rule. Their instantiation, DCD-Cluster, represents each example by its per-example edge-attribution vector, binarises it to the smallest top-magnitude edge set holding 99% of that example's attribution mass, reduces to 20 dimensions by PCA (silhouette stable over r ∈ {5,…,50}), clusters by k-means under Jaccard distance, and picks K by the gap statistic on the cross-task mixtures — computed in the reduced space because the gap statistic is degenerate over 10⁴–10⁶ binary dimensions. Everything was run on single A100s; E-Act was intractable on the larger models even at 80GB.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **set of circuits rather than one**, each paired with the subset of examples it explains and offered explicitly as a *hypothesis* about a mechanistic boundary, not as a claim about a definitive mechanism — the authors state this twice and hedge the cluster circuits accordingly. The explanatory object retains the field's standard form (a sparse subgraph plus its faithfulness score), so the contribution is a partition of the dataset, and the claim is that the partition is drawn where the model's computation changes rather than where the task label does. No text descriptions are produced for any circuit; interpreting what a cluster *is* is left as a separate human step, which they name as an open problem along with assigning new examples to clusters at test time.

## How they judged success

Faithfulness at 5% edge budget as above, plus Jaccard overlap between edge sets. Three controls do the real work. (i) **K-Random** — the same number of groups, the same group sizes, discovered by the identical method on random partitions of the same data — isolates how much block structure arises from splitting alone; this is the baseline that keeps the DCD result from being a restatement of "smaller datasets give more specialised circuits". (ii) **Noise floors** for every transfer verdict, so a "transfer failure" is a shortfall larger than resampling variation at that variant, and the verdict is directional. (iii) **Three attribution methods**, one of which (E-Act) shares no code path with the others. Auxiliary checks: best-of-K faithfulness as a coverage sanity check (does the circuit set collectively explain held-out examples as well as one circuit does), and full mixing sweeps at every proportion rather than only the 50/50 point. Reference overlaps anchor the Jaccard scale: two random 5% edge sets give 0.026, two circuits from disjoint halves of the same variant give 0.580.

## What they found

**Faithfulness is blind to mechanism splicing.** In all ten EAP-IG pairs no 50/50 circuit falls below 0.46 on either constituent task, while 13 of the 20 pure-circuit off-task scores fall below 0.30. GPT-2's IOI–SC pair is the sharpest: the pure sequence-completion circuit scores −0.47 on IOI — worse than full ablation — and the pure IOI circuit 0.24 on sequence completion, yet the 50/50 circuit reaches 0.63 on both. The splice is visible only in edge composition: across all settings 88–99% of the mixed circuit's top-5% edges belong to at least one task-specific circuit (in 172 task-pair/mixture/setting combinations the smaller of the two task-exclusive shares is at least 2.8%), and the 50/50 arithmetic–EB circuit is 28% shared, 15% arithmetic-only, 55% EB-only, 2% neither. EAP reproduces the pattern in seven of nine pairs at lower absolute faithfulness, and two Llama pairs do not show it — one where the pure circuits already transfer to each other, one where the mixed circuit effectively chooses a single task (0.71 arithmetic, 0.08 EB) — and both are reported. E-Act, the independent check, reproduces the GPT-2 IOI–SC cell: −0.15 and 0.14 for the pure circuits, 0.84 and 0.46 for the mixture. **Within a single task, mechanisms also diverge.** Of 136 within-axis variant pairs, only 43 transfer in both directions, 86 in one only, and 7 in neither. Asymmetry can be large: the Qwen EB position-P1 circuit falls from 0.93 in-distribution to 0.27 on P8, a 0.66 loss. The seven two-way failures (four IOI, three EB) have median top-5% Jaccard 0.30–0.48, against 0.580 for a same-variant split, and the overlap decays with distance along the axis (Qwen EB Q1–Q8: 0.60–0.76 for neighbouring positions down to 0.41 for P1 vs P8). Sequence completion and arithmetic produced no two-way failures, so surface variation does not always induce a new mechanism. **DCD-Cluster recovers the boundary where it is sharpest.** Cross-task: every cluster contains examples from a single task on all three models, and often a single variant. On the 7 pairs where neither circuit transfers, the two variants are separated in all 7 cases, 100% of IOI examples and 87–99% of EB examples falling in a single-variant-dominated cluster (50% under random assignment), and 11 of 13 variants form their own cluster — the exceptions being Qwen's middle positions P3, P4. Four of the seven show a clean two-block faithfulness pattern, one a weaker asymmetric one, versus K-Random showing a boundary for only two. For the one-way and two-way-transfer pairs, however, DCD-Cluster is **not** consistently more structured than K-Random. **A candid weakness in the evidence they present:** at the 5% budget EAP-IG recovers behaviour in all 69 in-distribution cells (median 0.90) and E-Act likewise (median 0.94), but EAP fails 17 of 59 (median 0.13 on Qwen EB, six of seven GPT-2 IOI cells below 0.3, minimum −0.85) — circuits that do not explain their own data — and they say plainly that cross-variant differences measured there are not evidence about mechanism.

## Limits and open problems they name

The grouping inherits every assumption of the attribution method that produces its features, so DCD-Cluster is only as good as the claim that per-example attribution vectors faithfully track mechanism. K selection is the deepest problem and they are explicit about it: for within-task mixtures the gap statistic over-splits (median K\* of 10 on axes with two or three variants), so **K is fixed to the known number of variants — an oracle choice**, and the within-task results must therefore be read as what the grouping recovers when K is right, not what the pipeline's own rule returns. Characterising what each cluster *means*, assigning new examples at test time, and handling clusters that resist human-legible description are all left open. Arithmetic is only evaluated on Llama because only its tokenizer makes the operands and answers single tokens — on the other two models the label is a subword fragment — and Qwen's arithmetic accuracy (28.6%) excludes it from those pairs. GPT-2 supports only IOI–SC for mixing because it scores 0.0% on every arithmetic variant and 10.6–54.6% on entity binding. E-Act, the only fully independent method check, was affordable only on GPT-2's 32,491 edges.

## What we take from it

**A reporting requirement (central to us).** Any explanation our white-box model emits over a pooled dataset ships with the per-example faithfulness *profile* of its own subgraph — the vector f(C, {x_i}), not its mean — because this paper's central demonstration is that a mean-faithfulness gate cannot distinguish a circuit that implements one mechanism from a circuit that splices several. Read beside the folder's Simpson's-paradox and stratified-AUROC entries, this is the same defect one level down: pooling over examples, where the other entries pool over specification strata or evaluation groups. **A construction we should copy outright.** Grouping before aggregation is a *pre-processing* change that needs no new attribution theory, and it composes with whatever discovery method we already trust — the exact shape of cheap, adoptable method this survey exists to collect. **A measure with a floor attached.** Every transfer or stability verdict here is gated on bootstrap noise floors estimated per variant, which is the cleanest small implementation of the folder's standing "matched drift, not significance" rule, and it degrades gracefully: they exclude the one pair whose floor is unusable rather than reporting it. **A warning that generalises past circuits.** Two of the three methods in their own grid produce circuits that fail to explain their own discovery data (EAP median 0.13 in one cell), so method-robustness claims are only worth making against a method that clears a basic in-distribution bar first — otherwise you are comparing noise. **And the negative to carry:** DCD-Cluster's advantage is confined to the clearest mechanistic boundaries and disappears against a size-matched random partition for subtle ones, so grouping is a hypothesis generator with a measured hit rate (7/7 on the sharp pairs, indeterminate on the rest), not a discovery guarantee.
