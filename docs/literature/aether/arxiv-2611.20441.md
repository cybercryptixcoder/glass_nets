# Abstention Direction in Large Language Models: Mechanistic Interpretability of Abstention Across Query Ambiguity, Knowledge Limits, and Safety Alignment

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.20441
- **Authors / organisation:** Koki Itai, Jun Hirako, Hiroaki Yamagiwa; SB Intuitions Corp. (Japan)
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20441
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Refusal in chat models has been studied almost entirely as a safety phenomenon, with a single "refusal direction" computed from harmful versus harmless prompts. But a model declines for other reasons too — the question is ambiguous, or it is asked about something outside what it knows — and nobody had asked whether those three kinds of abstention live in the same place or in different ones. The authors want to know whether abstention is one internal object with one direction, or several objects that merely overlap, and they want the answer to be causal rather than correlational, because whether one edit moves all three determines how a deployment can safely tune abstention at all. The larger goal is the general one of reading a behaviour out of weights: a behaviour that can be located as a direction can be measured, predicted and intervened on.

## What they studied

Qwen3-4B (36 layers, width 2560, TransformerLens residual stream at the final token position of each layer), in its thinking mode as the primary subject and its non-thinking mode as a comparison, with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct as a second model. The unit of analysis is a **direction in residual-stream activation space**, one per abstention "perspective": query ambiguity (Q), model-knowledge limits (MK), and human-values misalignment (HV), the original safety case. Evaluation is behavioural — the fraction of should-abstain queries actually declined, and the fraction of answerable queries wrongly declined.

## How they did it

The methodological core is a data-construction argument. The classical refusal direction subtracts the mean activation of one independently sourced dataset from another (harmful instructions minus harmless instructions), which leaves every distributional difference between the two sources — topic, register, length, template — inside the vector. The authors instead build **slot-flipped counterfactual pairs**: an LLM (Kimi-K3) rewrites each query into a template in which the abstention-inducing element is a single slot, then fills that slot with a same-type phrase of the opposite polarity, so that the two members of a pair differ only at the slot. For Q, the slot is a qualifier or a topic entity (a template question answered with or without its year; a polysemous title versus a specific one); for MK the slot is a real versus fictional entity of the same type (Natural Questions versus FictionalQA); for HV it is the action and object of an instruction (AdvBench versus benign Alpaca content). Pairs are filtered by rule-based checks then verified by the same LLM, giving 1,199 Q pairs, 767 MK pairs and 377 HV pairs. Each direction is the normalised difference of mean activations, computed from **64 pairs per perspective**, and every layer from 1 to 35 is swept (the final block is excluded to avoid reading output tokens). Intervention is additive steering at one layer, x' = x + m·r̂, with |m| in {2,4,8,16,32} for Qwen3-4B and {0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8} for Llama — chosen because the unnormalised vector norms differ by about an order of magnitude between the two models. Both signs are run. Evaluation uses 128 held-out pairs per perspective (256 queries), three sampled generations per query, and an LLM judge (gpt-oss-120b at temperature 0) labelling only whether the response abstains. Controls: the same directions computed from the *source-independent* (classical) construction, random unit directions drawn from a standard normal and normalised, and the thinking/non-thinking pair. Geometry is then read off two quantities — the cosine between directions from different perspectives at the same layer, and the Fisher discriminant ratio between the two polarities' activation clouds.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **direction**, and specifically a *family* of directions with a shared component: the explanation of any one abstention behaviour is "this perspective's mean-difference vector, at this layer, steered with this sign". It is partial by construction and the authors say so — the vector mediates the behaviour, but what it semantically represents is explicitly left open, following the same caveat the refusal-direction literature carries. It predicts in one direction only: it predicts that adding it raises abstention and subtracting it lowers it, within the layer band where the effect appears, and it says nothing about mechanism beyond the linear-representation hypothesis.

## How they judged success

Abstention rate (AR, recall on the should-abstain polarity) and over-abstention rate (OAR, abstention on the answerable polarity) against the no-intervention baseline, per layer, with mean ± one standard deviation over three generations. The ablation that matters is the **construction ablation**: slot-flipped versus source-independent directions, evaluated on the identical held-out test pairs, so the only thing that varies is how the direction was computed. Supporting checks: random-direction controls, a degeneration filter (layers/cells where more than 10% of responses collapse into repetition are excluded and marked), cosine similarity across perspectives, and Fisher discriminant ratio as a check that the layers where steering works are also the layers where the classes are linearly separated.

## What they found

Abstention directions exist for all three perspectives and steer behaviour in the expected sign, with effects concentrated in the middle layers (roughly 17–23). The construction argument wins its test: slot-flipped directions produce consistently larger effects than source-independent directions for every perspective, both signs, and both thinking modes, and the slot-flipped direction shows a noticeable effect at smaller |m| — the authors' reading is that the classical construction dilutes the vector with topic and register differences. At |m| = 32 on Qwen3-4B the perturbation is itself destructive, with over 10% of responses degenerating, which is why the headline results are reported at |m| = 16. HV abstention is already at AR 1.0 without intervention, so addition has nowhere to go there; the informative cells are subtraction for HV and both signs for Q and MK. Thinking and non-thinking mode behave almost identically (mean Pearson correlation across the six panels of 0.94 for AR and 0.93 for OAR), which the authors take as evidence the direction is not an artefact of the reasoning trace. Geometrically the three directions are **not orthogonal but not the same**: at their most effective layers the cosines are +0.163 (Q20–MK19), +0.190 (MK19–HV17) and +0.092 (Q20–HV17) — a small shared component. Fisher discriminant ratio peaks at 2.65 for HV (layer 21) and 1.15 for MK (layer 23) and shows no comparable rise for Q, and the steering-effective layers coincide with the separation peaks for MK and HV. Cross-perspective effects are **asymmetric**: steering Q moves MK's abstraction rate but leaves HV alone, whereas steering MK or HV moves both of the others. Qualitatively the interactions are not benign — subtracting the HV direction on an MK question fabricates an answer about a nonexistent actress, subtracting Q on an MK question fabricates an anthem's author, and subtracting MK on an ambiguous question invents a newspaper editor. The contrastive pairs also carry an asymmetry in what the intervention looks like when it fires: on a genuinely ambiguous question the added direction moves the model's reasoning toward recognising the ambiguity and asking for clarification, not merely toward a canned refusal.

## Limits and open problems they name

Two models only; generalisation to other families and to larger models untested. The semantic content of the direction is unresolved, by design. The taxonomy is inherited wholesale from a prior paper and each perspective uses only a narrow set of inducing factors, so scenario-level subdivisions are unexamined. Most important for anyone reusing this: **direction computation and evaluation use the same slot-flipped distribution**, and the authors flag explicitly that out-of-distribution generalisation is untested — the claim is existence, not robustness. English only. The judge is an LLM that detects abstention but does not score the quality of post-intervention outputs, so correctness and fluency changes are only examined qualitatively. The pair-construction LLM both generates and verifies the pairs, so its biases can enter twice. Also worth noting from the numbers themselves: the AR 1.0 ceiling for HV and the small baseline OAR leave several cells with no headroom, and the paper does not quantify the effect size in a normalised way, so "consistently larger than the baseline construction" is shown as curves rather than as a single comparable statistic.

## What we take from it

Three things, in order of usefulness.

**A construction, not a corpus, is what makes a direction causal.** The comparison here is clean because both conditions are evaluated on identical test pairs: same 64-pair budget, same normalisation, same models, same held-out set, only the pairing changes. Our own probing pipeline builds contrast sets from whatever corpora are convenient and has never run this ablation — take a property we probe for, mine matched pairs by templating a single slot, and compare the resulting direction's *intervention* effect against the corpus-difference direction on the same held-out items. The predicted outcome, and the reason to run it, is that the corpus-difference version is diluted by every confound in the source datasets and will look like a direction that "works a bit".

**A behavioural readout should be a pair, not a scalar.** AR alone rises trivially when the intervention suppresses answering generally; the paper's real claim rests on the joint behaviour of AR and OAR, and on the observation that the informative cells are where one of them has headroom. Our probe tables report one side of the contrast almost always. Any refusal-, uncertainty- or truthfulness-adjacent readout we publish should be scored on both polarities, with headroom stated.

**The negative geometric result on Q is the most interesting thing here for us.** For MK and HV the slot carries a *semantic* property — a fictional entity, a harmful action — and those become linearly separable in the middle layers. For Q the difference is a missing qualifier, which is a property of the question's *completeness* rather than of any content word, and the Fisher ratio never rises even though steering still works. That dissociation — steering works where separation does not — is a warning against using class separability as a proxy for whether a direction will be causal, and it is the exact confound the folder has recorded from the other side (a direction can decode perfectly and steer nothing).
