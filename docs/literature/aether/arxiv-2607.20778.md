# Toward Mechanistic Interpretability of an AI Foundation Model Fine-Tuned for Atmospheric Chemistry

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.20778
- **Authors / organisation:** Jason Y. Hu (Stanford CS), Ivan Higuera-Mendieta (Stanford Earth System Science), Patrick Obin Sturm (USC Earth Sciences), Makoto M. Kelp (Stanford Earth System Science / University of Utah Atmospheric Sciences)
- **Date:** 2026-07-22
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20778
- **Kind:** paper (preprint submitted to JAMES, not peer reviewed at posting)
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) — an audit of a deployed model whose training objective never contained the mechanism being asked about, with an explicit standard for when an internal feature earns a process name.

## What they wanted, and why
Weather foundation models are now fine-tuned to forecast air pollution, and they match the operational chemistry-transport reference on most targets without containing any chemistry: the composition fields come out of an autoregressive rollout over learned dynamics, not from photolysis rates or kinetic mechanisms. The authors ask the question that forecast skill cannot answer — did the model learn chemical relationships, or does it reproduce correlations in reanalysis data that happen to look right at short lead time? Their larger goal is policy-relevant: these models are being positioned to inform air-quality and regulatory decisions, and the authors argue that skill on historical reanalysis is insufficient evidence of trustworthiness under the emission and wildfire conditions the model has never seen. The paper is framed as the first mechanistic interpretability study of an atmospheric-chemistry foundation model, and as a template for testing whether any end-to-end forecasting system learned the physics it was trained to imitate.

## What they studied
Microsoft's Aurora fine-tuned for air pollution (the public Hugging Face checkpoint), a 1.3-billion-parameter Earth system model pretrained on ERA5, CMIP6, GFS and CAMS data and then LoRA-adapted to predict 12-hour changes in composition. Prognostic species are a short list — carbon monoxide, nitric oxide, nitrogen dioxide, ozone, sulfur dioxide, aerosol and total-column fields — on a 0.4-degree grid with 13 pressure levels from 1000 to 50 hPa. Reference is CAMS: its analyses initialise the forecasts and its forecasts initialised at the same times are the comparison. Units of analysis span three levels: a **species pair** (the NO2 fraction of NOx, and a Leighton-derived photolysis rate), a **spatial field** (perturbation response maps, plume maps, principal components of patch embeddings), and an **internal feature** (one SAE dictionary direction in one of 48 U-Net transformer blocks).

## How they did it
Three stages, each moving one step further inside. (1) **Output-side chemistry diagnostics with no intervention**: compute the NO2-over-NOx ratio and an idealised NO2 photolysis rate from predicted NO, NO2 and ozone at the 12-hour step, mask any cell where a component is non-positive, and keep the masked area's extent as its own indicator of non-physical output rather than reading the undefined values. (2) **Controlled input perturbations**: add a constant offset of +0.5, +5 or +20 ppb to both NO and NO2 across every cell, level and input timestep, roll out five days as ten 12-hour steps, and repeat over every CAMS initialisation from 1 June 2022 to 31 May 2023, comparing against the unperturbed rollout; separately, three wildfire events (Spain, Alaska, central Russia) selected by largest total-column CO enhancement in CAMS, where fire information can enter only through the initial composition because the model has no time-varying emissions. (3) **Internals**: PCA on patch-token embeddings at all four encoder and four decoder latent levels, with the first three components mapped to RGB (single initialisation, one month, one year); then AuroraScope, one SAE per operator block at 8x expansion (an expansion factor of 8) trained 3 epochs on subsampled patch tokens from rollouts across the whole analysis period, features interpreted by per-token variance contribution, and a zero-ablation steering test on the global top ten features ranked by maximum per-token variance contribution.

## What counts as an explanation here
A layered one, and the layering is the point. The output-level explanations are **diagnostics against chemistry the model was never given** — a ratio and a rate that must hold if the three predicted species are mutually consistent, with the masked region treated as a finding in its own right. The representation-level explanation is **a map of what the latent space is organised around** (principal components whose dominant directions are geographic and meteorological). The feature-level explanation is **a sparse dictionary entry plus a causal claim from ablating it**, and the paper is careful that the causal claim and the semantic claim are separate: a feature can be proved to move the forecast and still not be assignable to a process. Explanation is partial by design — the authors say plainly that no output diagnostic alone establishes internal mechanism, and that no feature name alone establishes chemistry.

## How they judged success
Against CAMS at matched initialisation and lead time, using pattern-level comparisons rather than a single score: the sign and spatial coherence of the perturbation response, the fraction of surface cells with reduced ozone, the median shift of the surface ozone distribution, plume persistence over 48 and 72 hours, and the extent of the non-positive mask. For the internals, qualitative structure of the principal components across temporal subsets (single time, month, year) as a control for whether a mode is specific to one initialisation, SAE reconstruction as fraction of variance explained per block with its range, and steering as global mean absolute change in surface ozone and NO2 plus the per-cell extremes and the spatial structure of the response map. No chemistry ground-truth quantity is available, so the success standard throughout is **consistency with known relationships and interpretability of the response**, not accuracy.

## What they found
Skill and mechanism come apart. Aurora reproduces coarse cross-species structure at short lead time but violates the fast-chemistry relation badly: for both the NOx partitioning and the Leighton-derived rate the model's field is noisier than CAMS, carries visible 3x3 patch-scale artefacts and ship-track structures, and shows large masked regions where a predicted concentration is non-positive. On the perturbation test the model has learned the first-order ozone response: at +5 ppb NOx the median surface ozone is 6.1 ppb below baseline at 12 hours and ozone falls in 89% of surface cells, and the +20 ppb case suppresses ozone nearly everywhere — but the perturbed distribution spills into negative ozone. The small +0.5 ppb case is heterogeneous, with local ozone **increases** over tropical land regions that could be VOC-limited production, which the authors explicitly cannot distinguish from geography and meteorology that covary with chemical regime. By 72 hours the +5 ppb median flips to 0.5 ppb **above** baseline: the perturbation not only persists but changes sign, which the authors read as a memory of the disturbance propagating through the rollout rather than a chemical re-equilibration, and they note that because the model conflates emissions and chemistry it is not possible to say whether an initial-condition perturbation was interpreted as an emissions change. Wildfire plumes are smoothed away: by 24 hours the localised CO enhancement near the Galicia fires is weaker than in CAMS and the downwind plume is less distinct, and the pattern repeats in the other two events. Internally, the first three components explain roughly 45-60% of latent variance per level and are organised around geography, topography and circulation (the Andes and Tibetan Plateau are visible in the surface latent level; higher encoder levels look like stacked circulation); the decoder differs somewhat, with one level carrying what may be human-activity or emissions structure and another a sharp day-night boundary, though PCA cannot say whether that mode is photolysis, radiation or boundary-layer meteorology. AuroraScope reconstructs the operator well except in the two all-to-all attention stages: mean fraction of variance explained is 99.7% and 97.4% in the first encoder stages, 77.8% in the deepest encoder stage, 79.0% in the earliest decoder stage, then 93.7% and 99.7% coming out. Features include regional transport plumes, whole-globe patterns, day-night wave patterns, and a spurious latitude-band artefact. Steering the top feature (#2712) moves global mean absolute surface ozone by 0.84 ppb and NO2 by 0.063 ppb, with single cells moving up to +24 and -16 ppb in ozone and +57 and -19 ppb in NO2; the response maps are spatially structured rather than random, so the features are causally load-bearing, but ablating either feature moves ozone, NO2 and other pollutants together and the changes do not align with circulation patterns or known emission hotspots.

## Limits and open problems they name
The categories are entangled: with five prognostic trace gases, no VOCs or radicals, no explicit emissions and no process-level outputs, an ozone response cannot be attributed to chemistry rather than to a correlated input. The Leighton diagnostic is deliberately not read as a photolysis rate because photostationarity breaks down under transport, fresh emissions and at night. PCA cannot assign physical meaning to a component. The authors cannot say why the meteorological organisation survives fine-tuning and offer LoRA's low-rank update as one plausible contributor. They state the diagnostic they would need and did not have — a physically well-behaved forecast to compare internal features against, and independent reference fields for production and loss rates, tested by linear probes and by whether a feature activates when its process dominates — and they say such tests would be far more informative on a model that respects positivity and preserves emission structure. Reproduction is partial: the SAEs are released with loading code, but figure and table code is promised only on publication.

## What we take from it
Four things, all warnings with a method attached. (1) **A positivity-violation mask as a first-class diagnostic**: their move of excluding non-positive cells from the chemistry diagnostic and reporting the excluded area as its own quantity is the cheapest way to find out that a model has no internal constraint — and their follow-on argument is the one worth stealing, that the negative values are the *detectable* failure and chemically inconsistent but positive combinations are the same failure hidden where no positivity check will catch it. (2) **Perturb, then watch the sign over the rollout**: a first-order response that flips sign at longer horizon (here 12 hours to 72 hours) is evidence of a propagated memory rather than an adjustment, so a probe that only checks the immediate response reports a mechanism where the model has a memorised trajectory. (3) **The SAE reconstruction drop localises where sparse dictionaries stop working**: their two all-to-all-attention stages reconstruct at about 78-79% while the locally-attending stages reach 93-99.7%, which is a concrete prediction for our own dictionaries — when reconstruction is stage-dependent, check whether attention span is what changed before blaming the features. (4) **Causality and semantics are two separate claims and must be reported separately**: a feature can pass the ablation test (structured, reproducible, large per-cell effects) and still be unnameable, and this paper's most useful sentence is the authors' admission that interpreting features in a model that already violates basic constraints is harder than naming features in a model that satisfies them — which tells us to check our white-box model's output-side invariants *before* we start naming internals, since a feature dictionary over an inconsistent model inherits the inconsistency.
