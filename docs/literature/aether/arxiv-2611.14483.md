# Mechanistic Interpretability Needs a Logic of Inference

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.14483
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhimin Hu, Chen Jiang, Hanbo Xie, N. Apurva Ratan Murty, Sashank Varma; Georgia Tech (School of Psychology / Neuroscience), Ratan Murty also Emory (acknowledges Guillaume Lajoie, Mila)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14483
- **Kind:** essay / survey-agenda (NeurIPS 2026 Interp4Discovery, Oral — no new experiments)
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Observational tooling has outrun the reasoning that is supposed to connect observations to mechanistic claims. The authors' complaint is not that interpretability results are wrong but that a study routinely reports what a method returned (a neuron, a feature, a circuit, a population geometry, a trajectory) as the mechanism, without stating which of several distinct inferential steps the result actually licenses. Their diagnosis is that the field is repeating a path neuroscience already walked: a sequence of refinements in which each generation of explanation was forced to say something more specific than the one before it. The larger goal is to give MI a shared inference discipline so that findings accumulate rather than being re-litigated per paper, and so that a mechanism found inside a model can be turned into a hypothesis precise enough to test outside it. The paper is therefore aimed exactly at the question our survey exists to answer — what counts as an explanation, and what does a given measurement establish. The recursive framing is their main proposal: the output of one inference step is the input to the next question, not the end of the paper.

## What they studied
No models are trained and no benchmarks run. The unit of analysis is the *inference* itself: the paper surveys five families of explanatory object already in use — individual neurons/units, alternative feature bases (SAEs, steering directions), circuits of interacting components (heads, sparse features), population geometry and low-dimensional structure, and dynamical trajectories — and pairs each with the kind of evidence it typically comes with. The empirical content is borrowed: the neuroscience and ANN results that motivate each principle are cited, not re-run (mouse ALM hemisphere silencing, zebra-finch song-region lesion versus acute silencing, gnostic and face cells, CNN feature visualisation and the unit-selectivity critiques, attention-head self-repair, SAE- and circuit-level stability results).

## How they did it
The method is structural comparison. First, they lay out the five objects of explanation and note what each promises and what it leaves underdetermined — sparse decompositions are explicitly called non-unique, and an individual neuron is admitted as legitimate only when it passes a causal check. Second, they take the neuroscience record where the same questions were faced and extract from it a chain of distinctions that were learned the hard way: selectivity is not use; decodability is not readout; a lesion effect is not a role; a role is not an organisation. Third, they order those distinctions into a recursive sequence of five questions (P1–P5) hanging off a measurement condition (P0), and illustrate every principle with paired examples in which the *same* data support two different conclusions depending on which step the authors claim. Fourth, they run the same comparison against the parallel history in computer vision, where visualisation gave way to population coding gave way to causal manipulation, and read the repetition as evidence that the sequence is not local to one field. Fifth, they apply the machinery to what they call discovery beyond the model: turning an internal mechanism into a domain hypothesis and a set of predictions that could fail independently of the model.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a feature dictionary and not a circuit — the contribution is a *form of argument*, offered as the missing connective tissue between existing artifact types. Concretely they want five named claims, each with its own evidence requirement: that the chosen object is the right level for the phenomenon (treated as a hypothesis, testable in both directions by asking whether adjacent levels are sufficient); that the information it carries is used rather than merely present; that the component's computational role is identified, not just its causal relevance; that the relations among several component roles are established as an organisation; and that the account survives a change of seed, condition, task or implementation, at which point the explanatory object itself may need to be revisited. Measurement is put underneath all five as a dependency rather than a sixth principle, with the explicit position that a measurement choice is part of the evidence. The framework is deliberately partial: it is a specification of what a study has and has not established, and it claims no mechanism of its own.

## How they judged success
By argument and by worked contrast, not by metrics — no numbers, baselines or ablations appear anywhere in the paper. The currency of evidence is the case in which an inference is warranted and the neighbouring case in which the same observation does not warrant it. Their sharpest instances: transient silencing of a song region in zebra finches disrupts song while a permanent lesion produces only a temporary deficit that recovers, which separates the effect of an intervention from the function of the tissue; unilateral versus bilateral silencing of mouse ALM during a delay period, where single-side suppression recovers and bilateral suppression does not, which separates *redundant maintenance* from *local recurrent maintenance* and licenses the organisation claim. On the ANN side they use attention-head ablation followed by partial downstream compensation, whose size varies by input, as the demonstration that an observed change reflects both the target and the system's response to being disturbed, and they cite the finding that circuit-level algorithms persist across training and scale while the specific components change. Success is therefore the reader being able to say, of a given result, which of the five claims it does and does not support.

## What they found
The position's substantive content is a ledger of mismatches, each stated as a claim we can check our own work against. Selectivity does not imply use: unit selectivity is a poor predictor of downstream task performance, and population activity can carry a task variable after the individually selective units are excluded, in both cortex and ANNs — so the explanatory level has to be argued for rather than inherited from the method that found it. Decodability does not imply use: a variable can be read out of a representation without entering computation, and probing accuracy need not track behavioural importance. Causal relevance does not imply role: an intervention shows dependence, and multiple incompatible accounts of what a component does predict the same dependence, so role claims need predictions about where and when an effect appears and which downstream quantities should move. Role does not imply organisation: the dependencies among roles — redundancy, coupling, serial composition — are a separate measurement requiring paired perturbations. And organisation does not imply invariance: an account may hold only at a level that is not the one first identified, and independently trained dictionaries can be stable in their subspaces while their individual features are not. The framing of "MI as a science of discovery" carries a second half worth separating: a mechanism inside a model is a hypothesis about the domain, not a finding about it, because the model may exploit computations that differ from the ones that generated its training data.

## Limits and open problems they name
The paper states its own boundary plainly, twice: evidence beyond the models is ultimately required to establish what a model mechanism reveals about the world, and mechanistic interpretations may be overgeneralised — hence mechanisms are offered as generators of testable hypotheses rather than substitutes for domain validation. Beyond the stated limits, the design has visible costs. No principle is made operational: there are no thresholds, no power analysis, no worked example on a real model where the framework changes a published conclusion rather than a hypothetical one. The five-step order is presented as recursive but not as a schedule, so nothing says how much of the chain a paper must walk before its claim is admissible — which is precisely the question a referee would ask. The historical-parallel argument is suggestive rather than evidential: similarity of sequence between two fields does not by itself establish that the needed distinctions are the same ones. And the responsibility section is a caution, not a procedure.

## What we take from it
Three concrete borrowings for a predictive white-box model. **A claim vocabulary, which we should adopt as section headings.** The folder has been collecting exactly these dissociations case by case — a probe that separates classes and a head that is redundant, a feature that reorganises a manifold and cannot flip a decision, an ablation that localises a span and leaves the property unfound. This paper gives them one place and one set of names, and it makes the discipline cheap: for every result we report, say which of the five claims it establishes and which it leaves open, with the silent default being the weakest. **The "which level is the explanandum" test as a two-directional check.** Whenever we present a component-level account, the adjacent-level sufficiency question is one measurement away in the same battery: does the phenomenon survive at the population level when the units are excluded, and conversely can a localised set reproduce it? That is a better use of an existing arm than another α sweep. **The paired-perturbation recipe for organisation.** Single-component ablations cannot separate redundant from coupled from serial structure; the ALM pattern — suppress one part, ask whether it recovers, then suppress both, ask again — transfers directly to our redundancy controls and costs two interventions per claim instead of one. The warning to carry: our folder's most repeated finding is that a method's output is mistaken for the mechanism, and this paper's contribution is that the mistake has a determinate shape — a skipped question — which makes it checkable at write-up time.
