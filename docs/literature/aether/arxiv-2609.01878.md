# GAPS: Dimension-Level Gates for Conditional Activation Steering

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.01878
- **Authors / organisation:** Moghis Fereidouni, Muhammad Umair Haider, Hassan Sajjad, A.B. Siddique; University of Kentucky / Dalhousie University
- **Date:** 2026-09-01
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01878
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Recent steering methods improved the behaviour-versus-capability trade-off by deciding *when* to intervene — gating or scaling the update on the current context so benign generation is left alone. The authors point out that this fixes only one of two excesses: once any such method fires, it still adds the **full dense vector to all D hidden dimensions**, whether or not a given coordinate carries concept information, and whether or not that coordinate already sits in the desired regime. Steering a neuron that says nothing about the concept cannot suppress the behaviour and can only perturb the representation. So they want a second, orthogonal axis of selectivity — *which neurons* — on top of the existing *when*. The larger goal is the same as the rest of this folder's steering literature: move the behaviour without moving the capability, and make the comparison across methods fair by holding strength fixed and varying only the gate.

## What they studied

Two instruction-tuned models, Gemma-3 4B (hidden width 2560) and Qwen-3 1.7B (2048), with every intervention applied to the residual stream at layer 15 (layer 18 in a robustness check). The unit of analysis is the **single coordinate of the residual stream** — they call it a "neuron" — at one layer, one token position, one generation step. Tasks are toxicity mitigation on RealToxicityPrompts and removal of seven OneSeC concepts (football, cloud, baby, church, book, flower, balloon).

## How they did it

The steering direction is the standard difference of class means between target and source concept activations. On top of this they compose two dimension-level masks with an existing token-level gate, so the update is the token gate times the elementwise product of the two masks times the direction. The first mask is **static and computed once offline**: for each coordinate, the AUROC of that coordinate's activation as a discriminator between source and target concepts, with the Mann–Whitney identity supplying the exact null mean and standard deviation, and the coordinate retained only if its AUROC deviates from chance by more than a threshold number of null standard deviations — a sample-size-aware criterion rather than a fixed cutoff, set to seven sigma, comfortably above a Bonferroni-corrected significance floor. The second mask is **dynamic and evaluated at every generation step**: each coordinate's activation is scored under a Gaussian fitted to the source-concept activations and under a Gaussian fitted to the target-concept activations, and the coordinate is steered only when the source concept is the more likely explanation under equal priors — computed entirely in log space with a log-sum-exp for stability. Both sets of statistics come from the same contrastive activations already collected for the direction, in one pass, so nothing new is trained. They formalise the composition so that setting both masks to one recovers the token-level baselines and setting the token gate to one as well recovers unconditional steering, then compare: the two token-level baselines alone, each combined with the static mask, with the dynamic mask, and with both, plus a **range gate** as a third comparator that fires when an activation falls within a fixed number of standard deviations of the source-concept mean, and every combination of the static mask with that range gate. Strength is swept, so each variant is a curve rather than a point, and all variants are compared at a *fixed capability budget*.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **gate**: a per-coordinate binary decision with an explicit statistical justification, in two flavours — a hypothesis test against a null (the static mask) and a likelihood ratio between two fitted densities (the dynamic mask). It is a *local* claim, not a global one: the dynamic mask does not say what the coordinate represents, only that at this token its value is better explained by one of two corpora. The explanation is partial by construction and the paper is explicit that the two masks answer different questions — "which coordinates can encode the behaviour at all" versus "which are expressing it right now".

## How they judged success

Behaviour versus capability, both swept over intervention strength: toxicity rate (fraction of continuations a RoBERTa classifier labels toxic, ten samples per prompt over 500 prompts) and concept-removal score (an LLM judge with the WordNet definition supplied, 1,000 completions per concept, normalised by the unsteered score and averaged over the seven concepts), against Wikipedia perplexity and MMLU — reported as the **probability mass on the correct option** rather than accuracy, for smoothness. The main comparison is the Pareto front between the behaviour metric and each capability metric. The ablation compares eight settings (two models × two token-level methods × two tasks) at a fixed capability budget (perplexity at most 5% above and MMLU at most 3% below unsteered), and the separability threshold is swept over five values under both that budget and a 15-budget average, with the selected value then reused everywhere.

## What they found

Every dimension-gated variant matches or improves on its token-level counterpart's Pareto front in all eight settings. At the matched capability budget the strongest operating point is the dynamically-gated baseline-plus-both-masks on Gemma-3 toxicity, which takes toxicity from 6.52% unsteered to 0.48%, against 3.52% for the token-level gate alone — a 92.6% relative reduction versus 46.0%. The ablation divides the credit unevenly and the paper says so: in all eight settings the best variant contains the dynamic mask, and in seven of eight the two dynamic-mask variants take both top spots; the static mask alone or the range gate alone never wins. The dynamic mask beats the static mask in seven of eight settings, by up to a 41% further relative reduction. The range gate is beaten by the dynamic mask in all eight settings and by the combined mask everywhere, and the paper gives the mechanism: the range gate only asks whether an activation is near the source-concept mean, so it fires in the region where the two distributions overlap, where the source is not the better explanation. The static mask is described as an essentially free complement — it improves whichever rule it is paired with in a majority of settings, and the headline 0.48% point is reachable only with it, since the dynamic mask alone plateaus at 1.26%. Which capability metric moves depends on the model: on Gemma-3 the gain shows up in perplexity while MMLU is nearly unchanged, and on Qwen-3 the reverse. The separability threshold sweep shows a band, not a peak: toxicity is lowest around seven to eight sigma and degrades on both sides, with a lower threshold admitting weakly informative coordinates and a higher one discarding useful ones.

## Limits and open problems they name

The dynamic gate assumes equal priors over source and target concepts, which is a real deployment choice — a mostly-benign setting should down-weight the source prior and an adversarial one should not — and they leave a systematic study of that to future work. Beyond what they name: the two-density story requires a concept pair with cleanly separable corpora, and the gate is defined only between those two, so a multi-way or compositional behaviour has no stated form here. The Gaussian assumption for each coordinate's conditional density is imported from prior work rather than tested in this paper. The token-level gate is applied before the dimension gates, so the gated variants inherit some of the base gate's damage at strong interventions, which they note explicitly in the Qwen-3 concept-removal case.

## What we take from it

A **method and a measure**. (i) The method: a per-coordinate likelihood ratio between two fitted class-conditional densities is the cheapest defensible "is this coordinate expressing the behaviour right now" test this folder has seen — two stored moments per coordinate per concept, one log-space comparison per token, no training, no forward pass. It is a strictly weaker object than a density model over the whole residual stream, and the paper's own numbers show how much of the benefit survives that weakening: most of it. (ii) The measure: this is the folder's cleanest template for **credit assignment between gates**. Every combination of masks is run, the winner is reported, and the sentence that matters is the one where the combined result is *not* reachable by the stronger mask alone. That is what tells you the weaker, cheaper component is load-bearing rather than decorative. (iii) A **warning** that repeats a pattern this folder keeps finding: a gate stated as "is the value inside the source range" loses to one stated as "which explanation is more likely", for a reason that is entirely about the overlap between the two distributions — and range-style gates are the default in applied work. (iv) A **framing** worth keeping: conditioning in space (which coordinates) composes with conditioning in time (which tokens) without either one having to know about the other, and the paper's composition is literally an elementwise product of masks. For our predictive model that suggests the selectivity dimensions should be kept as independent, separately-ablatable factors rather than folded into one learned rule.
