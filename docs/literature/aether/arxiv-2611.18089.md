# Deep Latents, Long Memory: Latent persistence explains temporal correlations in transformer activations

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.18089
- **Authors / organisation:** Francesco Cagnetta (Simplex), Santiago Acevedo (SISSA)
- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18089
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The standard story is that hidden layers encode ever more abstract representations as depth increases, but "abstract" has no operational definition, so the claim cannot be tested. The authors propose to replace the abstraction question with a measurable one borrowed from the statistics of sequences: the correlations along a sequence carry the signature of the generative process that produced it, so if a layer encodes a variable that stays true across many tokens, its activations at distant positions should be unusually similar. Their goal is an observable that can be computed identically on a synthetic grammar where the true latent variables are known and on pretrained language models where they are not, so that a theory of hierarchical representation learning becomes an empirical measurement rather than a metaphor. The larger aim is to connect the geometry of representations to the structure of the information they encode.

## What they studied

Two settings. Synthetic: the Random Hierarchy Model, sentences of 32 tokens generated top-down through a binary tree of depth 5, vocabulary 32 with 8 production rules per symbol, so every token position has a known chain of latent parents, grandparents and so on, each spanning a known contiguous window. A 5-block GPT-2-style transformer (d = 768, 12 heads) is trained to within 0.033 nats of the optimal per-sentence loss. Natural: five pretrained models with public SAE checkpoints — Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-9B with Gemma Scope JumpReLU residual-stream SAEs, Pythia-160M and Llama-3-8B with EleutherAI top-k SAEs, Qwen3-8B with Qwen-Scope top-k SAEs — evaluated on The Pile and WikiText. The unit of analysis is the layer, and secondarily the individual SAE latent treated as a proxy for a latent variable.

## How they did it

Activations at each position are standardised to zero mean and unit mean squared norm, and the layer's autocorrelation is the expected dot product between the anchor position (the last token of a sample) and the position a lag earlier, summed over lags to give an integrated autocorrelation. In the synthetic setting the 63 tree nodes (32 tokens plus 31 latents) are all given one-hot encodings and regressed jointly on each position's activation, producing a code per variable plus a residual; because the regression is joint, a variable's code is nonzero only if the activation carries something about it beyond what any other single node implies, which the authors distinguish carefully from a probe, since a probe for a parent variable reads positive signal wherever its children are encoded. The per-variable same-node autocorrelation term is then predicted from the codes alone and compared to the measured curve. In pretrained models the autocorrelation and its layer profile are measured directly, SAE latents are summarised by persistence (the number of consecutive tokens on which a latent's code is nonzero, averaged over its firing episodes and over the 1,000 most active latents per layer), and the two curves are correlated across layers. Two controls do most of the work: subtracting each sequence's mean activation to remove the sequence-wide component, and a shuffle that permutes token positions within each sequence, preserving every latent's activation frequency while destroying temporal order. Finally, persistent latents are ablated by projecting the activation onto the decoder direction and subtracting it, over the 500 longest firing episodes of the layer's 400 most persistent latents, with a matched control latent that is active at the episode start but not persistent.

## What counts as an explanation here

A geometric observable — a single function of the raw residual stream, invariant to a common change of orthonormal basis — plus an additive decomposition of that observable into one term per latent variable and a residual. The explanation is that an encoding which is aligned across positions is what produces long-range correlation, so the shape of the autocorrelation curve (where it steps down, and how far the tail extends) is read as the span structure of whatever variables the layer holds. It is predictive in a narrow, quantitative sense: given codes measured at single positions, the same-node terms account for a stated share of each measured curve at each lag. It is deliberately content-free: the organisation of encoded variables is characterised without naming any of them.

## How they judged success

In the synthetic model, agreement between the measured autocorrelation and the sum of predicted same-node terms per lag; agreement between the measured per-sentence loss and the exact per-depth-subtree entropies (which makes each training plateau a known milestone); a sampling-noise floor for the measured curves, quoted below 1e-4 at every lag and layer, with a pure-noise code picking up about 1e-4 of residual variance at the regression sample size; and a stability requirement that every reported code strength change by less than a factor of 2 between two sample sizes where pure noise would change by a factor of 4. In pretrained models, Pearson correlation across layers between integrated autocorrelation and mean or median persistence, per model and per corpus, with the sequence-mean-removed variant reported alongside. For the intervention, the percentage change in autocorrelation under ablation, against a non-persistent-latent control and against reference positions just outside the firing episode.

## What they found

Autocorrelation grows with depth in both the synthetic model and every pretrained model, and in the synthetic model it grows in a staircase: each plateau of the loss curve, one per level of the latent hierarchy learned, is matched by a new step outward in the autocorrelation, at lags 1, 3, 4–7 and 7–15 corresponding to the span ends of the three diagonal ancestor variables. Predicted codes account for 55–95% of the measured autocorrelation at layer 4, the remainder being mostly the non-additive residual and confined to the first plateau. Measured encoding patterns follow a one-level-per-layer construction for layers 1 and 2 but are less clean at layers 3 and 4, where some deeper structure and some extra persistence appear early. In pretrained models, median persistence and integrated autocorrelation both rise roughly monotonically with depth, with persistence flattening or slightly declining in the last quarter of the network; the per-model Pearson correlation across layers runs from 0.5 to 0.95 depending on model, corpus and mean-versus-median, and in most cases improves after removing the sequence mean (Gemma-2-2B on The Pile, over 26 layers: 0.86 to 0.94 for the mean). The shuffle control is decisive: real-order persistence rises from 1.1 at layer 0 to 1.8, shuffled-within-sequence persistence stays between 1.1 and 1.3, and corpus-wide shuffling tracks the independent-token expectation; mean activation frequency of the selected latents stays inside 0.03–0.07 at every layer, so frequency does not explain the depth trend. Ablating one persistent latent cuts autocorrelations by 3.0–13.3% (largest for Pythia-160M), three by 4.4–24.3%, all active latents via the SAE reconstruction by 43.7–68.3%, and the non-persistent control by 0.0–0.7%. A persistent latent also removes a median 1.2% of the squared activation norm against 0.29% for the control, roughly a factor of four, so persistence and projection magnitude are coupled in practice rather than independent.

## Limits and open problems they name

The autocorrelation is a trace, so it counts only variables written along aligned directions at both positions; a variable re-encoded in a rotating basis across the context is invisible, and the authors say the full cross-covariance matrix would be needed to apportion persistence between fixed directions and moving orientation. SAE latents are imperfect proxies whose reconstruction leaves a substantial fraction of the activation norm unrecovered (the paper's own explained-variance panel keeps it above 0.7 in every layer of every model but is computed on uncentred activations, so it is a norm fraction rather than a centred one), which is why removing every active latent still does not zero the correlation. The synthetic results come from one grammar and one training run. The transforms are not innocent either: every natural-language curve is computed on activations clipped to their 5th–95th percentiles, one-token dropout of latents is bridged by default in the persistence figures, and the explained-variance numbers exclude tokens whose squared norm exceeds 20 times the layer median. The final section floats, without testing it, whether the temporal structure of the autocorrelation predicts the lag-resolved response to perturbation, noting that a fluctuation–dissipation relation is not expected to hold outside equilibrium.

## What we take from it

A method and a measure. Lag-resolved autocorrelation is the cheapest instrument available for asking what a representation holds invariant along a sequence, it needs no labels, no probes and no dictionary, and it is computed from the same activations our probes already read — so it can be added to any write-up as a context-control arm with no new data. More importantly it is a template for what a real explanation looks like: a decomposition that is *additive per variable and predicted from single-position measurements*, with the residual printed as a number rather than ignored. The paper's own discipline is the part to copy — every correlation ships with the sequence-mean-removed variant, with a shuffle that holds activation frequency fixed, and with a matched control latent for the intervention, because in each case the naive measurement includes a component produced by something other than the thing being claimed. Two warnings. The probe-versus-joint-regression distinction (a probe for a parent reads positive signal wherever its children are; the joint regression zeroes it exactly) is a ready-made diagnostic for the folder's recurring "the parent variable is decodable" claim. And the sequence-wide component is the same degenerate direction we have met in activation statistics before: a rank-one offset that grows with depth and puts a non-decaying floor under every long-range similarity number, removable in one subtraction.
