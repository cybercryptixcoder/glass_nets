## What they wanted, and why
Skill characterizations for language models are usually external: subject taxonomies, textual capability descriptions, or prompted profiles. The authors argue that when the point is to *intervene* on one specific model — pick its training data, steer its behaviour, or decide which behaviours are under-covered — the characterization should be recovered from that model's own representations instead. They name three desiderata (compositional, compact, grounded in a space that determines behaviour) and observe that together they point at an orthogonal basis in activation space. PCA on sequence-level activations is proposed as the simplest instance, named AutoSkill. The larger goal is a single unsupervised object that supports data selection, inference-time steering and coverage balancing without annotation or contrast pairs.

## What they studied
Two instruction-tuned models in the reasoning case study: Llama3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-3B. Data is the llama-nemotron math SFT pool (50k traces, generated by strong reasoning models such as DeepSeek-R1-671B and QwQ-32B), evaluated on GSM8K, MATH-500 and AMC. The safety case study uses Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, 1,494 jailbreak prompts drawn from 32 attack papers (Nov 2022–Nov 2024), with WildJailbreak and a skill-composed set of 2k queries each as test sets. Unit of analysis: a *sequence*, represented by either the mean of per-token hidden states concatenated across all layers, or a single token position (last prompt token, layer 15) for the safety setting.

## How they did it
Each token is represented by concatenating its hidden states from every layer, so the per-token vector dimension is layers x width. A sequence is then either mean-pooled over tokens (reasoning traces, where the pattern is spread across the whole trace) or reduced to the final position (safety, where the comply/refuse decision is localised at the first generated token). The stacked sequence matrix is centred and decomposed with PCA; principal directions are the skill axes. Interpretation is strictly post hoc: examples are ranked by cosine similarity to each direction, the top and bottom groups are shown to GPT-5, which writes a keyword-pair contrast. For data selection, the 50k pool is ranked by cosine similarity to one direction and the top 25k (or bottom 25k for the negative pole) are used as an SFT set against a random 25k control. Because running full SFT on every candidate direction is expensive, they screen with two proxies — pilot fine-tuning on 1k examples along a direction, or inference-time steering toward it — and take the best-scoring direction. Steering itself is implemented by writing the scaled direction into the unused bias parameters at the end of each layer, so the edited model keeps the original architecture and stays compatible with vLLM. The safety experiment instead projects layer-15 activations into 25 dimensions (73% of variance) and selects adversarial examples by farthest-point sampling (a greedy k-centre approximation), comparing against random selection at budgets of 50–100 prompts, with a fixed 2:1 benign-to-harmful ratio and 20 refusal templates.

## What counts as an explanation here
A compact orthogonal basis recovered from activations, with each axis given a natural-language contrastive label after the fact. It is explicitly partial and explicitly not a human ontology: the axes are meant as intervention handles and as axes of behavioural variation, and the authors concede the labels can be coarse even when the axis works. Nothing in the paper claims the basis is complete or that the axes are the mechanism; the direction is a predictor of which data will help and which edit will move behaviour.

## How they judged success
Downstream Pass@1 and Pass@8 after SFT (16 repetitions, temperature 1.0 by default, math-verify parsing), against random selection, curated sets (s1.1 with Gemini and DeepSeek traces, LIMO), a skill-targeted baseline (STAT-Sel / STAT-Syn), a process reward model (ReasonFlux-PRM), and a battery of heuristic filters (difficulty, correctness, repetition, length, deduplication, outlier removal) each run at the same budget. Proxy quality is measured as Pearson and Spearman correlation between proxy outcome and full-SFT outcome across all 20 directions. Steering is judged on Pass@1, Pass@8 and output entropy against a no-steering baseline over an alpha sweep. Safety is judged by the count of explicit refusals (StrongReject = 0, GPT-4.1 judge) at each adversarial budget. Controls include a text-embedding clustering ablation with a silhouette sweep and cross-model skill transfer.

## What they found
- The two models recover different bases from the same pool: symbolic calculus is the positive pole of Llama's dominant direction but the negative pole of Qwen's, and Llama surfaces reasoning-style and reasoning-quality axes with no Qwen counterpart.
- Selectivity matters more than the number of axes: on Llama at 25k examples, choosing the single best direction gives 49.25 MATH-500 against 41.38 for random (+7.87), with average 45.12 against 40.71; the best heuristic (high difficulty) reaches 45.50 MATH-500 and average 43.77, and PC8- (labelled "speculative, ad hoc reasoning") is the single best direction at average 45.12.
- Proxy screening works: pilot fine-tuning predicts full-SFT outcome at Pearson r = 0.73 (Spearman 0.68), inference-time steering at r = 0.68 (Spearman 0.62) across 20 directions.
- Steering moves behaviour smoothly with alpha and lowers entropy; Pass@8 on MATH-500 peaks at 75.79 at alpha = 0.2 (+4.79 over 71.00 baseline), and Pass@1 peaks at 43.12 (+1.74). At alpha = 0.5 Pass@1 falls below baseline while Pass@8 remains above it.
- Cross-model transfer is near-neutral: Qwen2.5-3B trained on Llama-derived skills scores average 50.78 versus 50.61 on its own skills, with the two winning different benchmarks. The authors report this plainly, including that there is no sign the target model's own skills are better.
- Text-embedding clusters carry no useful structure for this: silhouette scores of 0.047–0.056 across k = 2–10 (below the 0.25 "no substantial structure" threshold), and none of the eight clusters beats a matched random baseline by 1% in average performance.
- In safety, 32 textually distinct jailbreak families overlap heavily in a 2-D projection of layer-15 activations, and latent-coverage selection beats random at every budget, with the advantage largest at the smallest budgets (+16 and +23 of 2,000 at budgets 50 and 70 on WildJailbreak; +25 and +30 on the skill-composed set at budgets 70 and 80).
- The spectrum is sharply decaying: the top 5–10 principal directions cover more than 90% of variance on reasoning traces.

## Limits and open problems they name
PCA may miss non-linear or polysemantic structure, and SAEs are floated as the natural next step. The semantics of an axis are post hoc and sometimes coarse. Only mathematical reasoning and safety were evaluated; domains with subjective success criteria are left open. Also visible in the tables but not discussed as a limit: the negative pole of a direction can be the better training set (PC8- and PC9- both beat PC8+ and PC9+), and different models prefer different directions, so "the best skill" is found by search over a screen rather than by any principle.

## What we take from it
Three things, one of them a warning.
1. **Method: a rechunking trick that buys steering without touching the runtime.** Writing the scaled direction into unused per-layer bias parameters gives the same hidden-state offset with the original architecture retained. For our white-box model this is the cheapest possible way to test a predicted edit end-to-end on a production inference stack, and it removes the usual excuse that a proposal cannot be evaluated at serving speed.
2. **Measure: proxy fidelity is a measured quantity, not an assumption.** They report the rank correlation between a 1k-example pilot and the full 25k run (r = 0.73) and between a single steering run and full SFT (r = 0.68) over 20 candidates. Any cheap screen in our pipeline should ship the same number over the same set of candidates, because a screen at r near 0.7 still mis-ranks roughly a third of the field.
3. **Warning: orthogonality buys compactness and costs interpretability, and the sign of the direction is arbitrary.** Nothing in the construction says which pole of a principal component is "the skill"; the labels are generated afterwards, from whichever pole the data happens to place on the positive side, and the paper's own best Llama direction turns out to be the pole labelled *speculative, ad hoc reasoning*. For us the lesson is that any direction we report inherits an arbitrary sign, so sign-dependent claims (steer toward, steer away, ablate) must be stated relative to a measured pole rather than to the coordinate's positive side.
