## What they wanted, and why

Every activation-probe result in this folder is reported as a number produced under one inference configuration, and then read as a property of the model. But a probe is trained from activations extracted at one batch size and one numerical precision and is then served under whatever batch shape the scheduler assembles and whatever format the serving stack prefers. Common GPU kernels are not batch-invariant (a reduction can be split differently for a different batch shape) and lower-precision formats round differently, so the activations a deployed probe actually sees are not the activations it was fitted on. The question is what that costs, measured for the first time on the *representations* rather than on outputs or on trained weights. The motivation is concrete: probes are now shipped as misuse and deception monitors, so their stability is a safety claim, not a methodological nicety. The larger goal is a vocabulary for the three ways a perturbation can matter — benign, behaviourally consequential, or an outright representation failure — because a single accuracy number cannot tell them apart.

## What they studied

Three models: Llama-3.1-8B (base), Qwen3-8B (post-trained), Gemma-3-4B (instruction-tuned). Three datasets: Rotten Tomatoes sentiment (10,662 sentences, official 8,530/1,066/1,066 split), a de-duplicated SMS Spam collection (5,159 messages, 70/15/15 by content hash), and TruthfulQA (817 questions, activation-level analysis only since it has no binary label under their preprocessing). Grid: batch sizes 4, 8, 16 × precisions fp32, bf16, fp16, four depths (block 3 plus 50%, 75% and 100% of depth) and four token positions (last prompt token, then generated tokens 5, 10, 20 under 20-token greedy decoding). Unit of analysis: the per-row residual-stream activation vector at a fixed layer and position, and on top of it 768 trained probes — logistic regression, one-hidden-layer MLP (256 units), three-hidden-layer MLP — kept after excluding the Gemma fp16 cell.

## How they did it

Extraction is fully crossed: every model × dataset × batch size × precision is run with one seed, fixed row order, deterministic PyTorch algorithms and a pinned software stack, and the residual stream is captured at four blocks and four positions, upcast to fp32 for storage, with output logits saved so each greedy run's realised tokens can be recovered by argmax without re-running the model. Most runs are on one L4; the two 8B models at fp32 do not fit and use an A100, which splits comparisons into a same-GPU class (all batch-size comparisons, all Gemma comparisons, all bf16↔fp16 transfers) and a cross-GPU class that changes GPU and dtype together — every claim is scoped to the same-GPU class where it is precision-specific. Probes are trained at batch size 4 in one precision and then evaluated unchanged on every available (precision, batch size) cell of the test split, giving 5,568 transfer measurements; the reported quantity is the accuracy change against the probe's own matched cell, plus, for a fixed set of checkpoints, per-example agreement between the two runs' verdicts on the same rows. Activations are compared per row by relative ℓ2, cosine, exact bitwise agreement and median ULP distance, and at population level by linear CKA, orthogonal-Procrustes residual, kNN Jaccard overlap, participation ratio and a spectral-alignment statistic. Decode divergence is measured twice — from stored logits (realised tokens) and from a block-3 relative-ℓ2 threshold whose bimodal gap makes it insensitive to the cut (any threshold in [10^-1.5, 10^-0.5] moves the pooled diverged fraction by at most 1.5 pp, and it agrees with the token measurement to 0.95 pp mean absolute difference, r = 0.993). Controls: the whole sweep is re-run from scratch on the same stored activations and reproduces every row's accuracy and macro-F1 exactly, which fixes probe training as deterministic here and pins every difference on the extraction configuration; the noise is also tested for a systematic direction with an additive-distance triangle test, and for depth amplification with a per-block growth factor.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not an explanation of a mechanism — a **reliability envelope** for one class of read-out. The deliverable is a decomposition: for a given perturbation, say whether it moved the representation but no decision (benign), changed a discrete choice and thereby the model's own future input (behaviourally consequential), or produced representations the format cannot hold at all (validity failure). Each is quantified with the right instrument: activation distance and geometry for the first, per-example verdict-flip rate for the second, and non-finiteness counts for the third. It is explicitly partial and is meant as a reporting standard rather than a claim about what probes compute.

## How they judged success

The central instrument choice is the finding: aggregate accuracy is pitted against per-example agreement (`P[ŷ_A ≠ ŷ_B]` over identical rows) and score-level companions — Cohen's κ, correlation of class-1 probabilities, AUROC, TPR at 1% FPR — with flips split by direction and by true label. Activation-level checks are relative ℓ2, cosine, bitwise-identical fraction, ULP distance, CKA, Procrustes residual, kNN Jaccard at several k, participation ratio, and Spearman correlation between data variance and perturbation variance along the top 50 principal components. Divergence attribution uses Spearman ρ between the diverged-row fraction and |Δ accuracy| recomputed within subsets (same-GPU / cross-GPU, within-bf16 / within-fp16, and the strict cell set) to show the correlation is not a hardware or dtype artefact. Symmetry is tested by flip direction counts and by flip rate on positives versus negatives, with a binomial test on the sign balance. There are no human ratings and no downstream-task benchmark; the baseline being beaten is "the number the probe reported yesterday".

## What they found

At the prompt the perturbation is rounding-scale and harmless. Under fp32, Llama and Qwen prefill activations are 99.9–100% bitwise identical across batch sizes; Gemma fp32 differs at ~5×10^-7 relative ℓ2. Under bf16, changing only the batch size perturbs activations ~8× more than the same change in fp16 (median relative ℓ2 1.3×10^-2 vs 1.7×10^-3 for Llama, 1.0×10^-2 vs 1.3×10^-3 for Qwen — a ratio of 7.8 and 7.7, matching fp16's three extra mantissa bits), and for Gemma, where the GPU is held fixed, a bf16 batch-size change (4.9×10^-3) moves the representation further than the entire fp32→bf16 conversion at fixed batch (4.4×10^-3). The noise has no direction: every non-fp32 triple fits an independent-draws model rather than an additive one (224/224 cells; 1.5% median relative error vs 50% for the additive model), prefill growth is flat with depth (median per-block factor 1.01), and it does not scale with input norm or length (median R² 0.05). Global geometry survives (CKA ≥ 0.995) but local neighbourhoods do not — 10-NN overlap 0.95 under bf16, and 5-NN is consistently worse than 50-NN, so a kNN or clustering-based monitor is more exposed than the probes studied. Probes are unaffected: across 1,392 prompt-position transfers accuracy never moves more than 0.47 pp (mean |Δ| 0.04, 74% exactly unchanged, signs balanced, p = 0.68), and only 0.076% of individual verdicts change; under fp32 with only batch size varied, none of 201,960 verdicts flips. During generation the same noise becomes different text: a bf16 batch-size change flips the first generated token for 2.1% of rows and leaves the runs on different tokens in 25% of rows by token 20 (fp16: 0.4% and 3.6%); fp32 diverges nowhere in 5,610 rows per model. Decode-position probe accuracy changes in 69%, 75% and 80% of cells at tokens 5, 10, 20 for a maximum of 2.35 pp, but per-example agreement shows the cause cleanly — rows whose realised tokens matched flip at a flat 0.12–0.15% at every position, while diverged rows flip at 12.9%, and that rate is nearly constant across perturbations (12.5–15.8%), so a perturbation decides how often the text changes, not what changed text does to the probe. Accuracy understates this churn by 2× at the prompt and 9× at token 20 (0.31 pp of accuracy against 2.76% of verdicts). Flips are symmetric (6,671 one way against 6,813 the other), κ stays ≥0.94, score correlation ≥0.96 and AUROC moves ≤0.05 points, so what moves is the set of examples within rounding distance of the boundary and not the ranking. The sharpest number is a margin argument: the same perturbation flips 0.14% of probe verdicts but 2.1% of the model's own next-token argmaxes, a 15× difference, because a binary boundary tolerates the perturbation while an argmax over ~10^5 tokens usually has a near-tie at the top. Gemma-3-4B in fp16 is the validity failure: every hidden state at blocks 17/26/34 and every logit vector is NaN (80% of stored tensors) and the cell is excluded everywhere. The paper's own limitations are unusually candid: one software stack, an unidentified GPU contribution, batch sizes 4–16 only, two binary tasks with well-separated classes, per-example metrics computed only at the final layer for an fp32-trained probe, and **no seed baseline** — probes are trained once, so a 0.47 pp change has no noise floor to be compared against.

## Limits and open problems they name

One software stack and effectively one GPU (the 8B fp32 runs move to an A100, so GPU and dtype are confounded for those models; the same-GPU subset supports every conclusion but the GPU's own contribution is not identified, and fp32 batch-invariance is a property of one kernel set). Batch sizes 4–16 are small for production serving, and larger, ragged, continuously batched or tensor-parallel workloads may select other kernels. Two binary classification tasks on what are by construction well-separated classes; real safety probes operate at low false-positive rates on harder distributions where margins are smaller. No seed baseline, cited as the single outstanding measurement. Only four logged positions, so a divergence that self-corrects in between is invisible — the likeliest source of the residual same-token flip rate. 4B–8B dense models only; quantised weights and larger models untested.

Limits I am adding, marked as mine: (i) **the flip rate is a boundary artefact and the paper does not say so in those terms** — with a well-separated binary task, 2.76% churn means roughly 2.76% of examples had a margin comparable to the perturbation; on a monitor operating at 1% FPR those are exactly the examples whose label the threshold is deciding, so the reassuring pooled number and the operating point are two different questions and only the pooled one is measured. (ii) **There is no matched-random-perturbation control** — no comparison against, say, adding isotropic noise of the same relative norm to the activations, which would separate "numerical noise is benign because it is unstructured" from "the probe is simply insensitive at this task's margin". The triangle test and the spectral-alignment statistic gesture at this but do not run it. (iii) **The prompt-position result is only as general as the feature-extraction site** — activations are read at block 3 and at 50/75/100% depth of the residual stream, never inside an MLP or attention output, so "probes absorb this" is not tested where dictionaries and transcoders read. (iv) **Batch sizes 4–16 on one GPU with a fixed cuBLAS workspace and deterministic algorithms is the most reproducible corner of the space**, so the measured noise is plausibly a floor and not a ceiling for a real serving stack. (v) The pooled effect on a *safety-relevant* probe at its operating threshold is never reported, so the paper bounds the average read-out but not the monitor.

## What we take from it

**A reporting standard, adoptable immediately.** Any probe or monitor result we publish should carry: the serving configuration (batch size, dtype, hardware), per-example verdict churn alongside accuracy, a κ and an AUROC-at-fixed-FPR next to the accuracy delta, and the split of flips by direction and true label. This is cheap (it is the same forward passes, compared pairwise) and it is the only instrument that distinguishes "the probe is stable" from "the errors rearranged".

**The accuracy-is-the-wrong-instrument result generalises past numerics**, and it is the same defect this folder keeps finding under other names: aggregate scores that are invariant to which examples are right. Wherever we report a probe's accuracy across seeds, layers, checkpoints or datasets, the per-example agreement figure is the honest one, and the gap to quote is the ratio between them.

**The margin explanation is the reusable theory.** A read-out is robust to a perturbation in proportion to its decision margin, so an argmax over the vocabulary is the most fragile read-out in the system and a thresholded scalar the most robust — which means our probes can be stable in exactly the regime where the model's own behaviour is not, and "the probe agrees with the model" must specify *which* model run. The 15× number (0.14% of probe verdicts vs 2.1% of argmaxes under one bf16 batch-size change) is a good calibration of how large a serving-side effect is relative to a read-out's sensitivity.

**A validity-failure check we should run before trusting any activation comparison.** Gemma-3-4B in fp16 silently produces NaN hidden states at 80% of extraction sites; a pipeline that does not assert finiteness would report those as a large representational difference. Asserting finiteness on every captured tensor is one line and belongs in our extraction code.

**A warning against carrying the headline to monitors.** The paper is about Probes of a classification task with well-separated classes; production monitors run at low FPR on adversarially chosen inputs where margins are small, which the authors name — and our own write-ups should not cite "0.08% of verdicts change" as evidence that a deployed safety monitor is numerically stable.
