# Faithful Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

- **Key:** arxiv-2301.13379
- **Authors / organisation:** Qing Lyu, Shreya Havaldar, Adam Stein, Li Zhang, Eric Wong, Marianna Apidianaki, Chris Callison-Burch — University of Pennsylvania; Delip Rao — independent (gmail address, no institution line)
- **Date:** 2023-01-31 (v1); 2023-09-20 (v3, this entry)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13379
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Chain-of-thought prompting improves accuracy on reasoning tasks, but the written chain carries no guarantee that it is what produced the answer — the model emits a fluent derivation and then announces a conclusion that need not follow from it. The authors want to remove that freedom by making the answer a *consequence* of the written chain rather than a continuation of it. Their repair is architectural rather than statistical: let the model write a program (or a Datalog query, or a PDDL goal), and let a deterministic solver read the answer off the program. The larger goal is a form of transparency that costs nothing in accuracy — they argue explicitly that faithfulness and capability can be synergistic rather than traded off. The paper is therefore the constructive counterpart to the measurement papers that document unfaithful chains: instead of scoring how often a chain is post-hoc, it changes the interface so that the question cannot arise.

## What they studied
Five OpenAI code-generation models as the *translator* — `code-davinci-001`, `text-davinci-002`, `text-davinci-003`, `code-davinci-002` (the main model for §5) and `gpt-4` — called through the API between September 2022 and January 2023. Ten datasets over four domains: five math-word-problem sets (GSM8K, SVAMP, MultiArith, ASDiv, AQuA), three multi-hop QA sets (StrategyQA, Date Understanding, Sports Understanding from BIG-bench), the SayCan robot-planning set, and CLUTRR relational inference with up to ten intermediate steps. The unit of analysis is not a neuron, feature or layer but a **generated artefact**: a reasoning chain that interleaves natural-language comments with a symbolic program, plus the solver that executes it. Nothing internal to the model is read; the account is a text plus a program.

## How they did it
Two stages. In **Translation**, a few-shot prompt asks the LM to decompose the question into named subquestions and, for each, a symbolic program in a task-appropriate language: Python for math, Python or Datalog for multi-hop QA, a PDDL goal for planning, and a small relational-expression language with transitivity rules for CLUTRR. For math the natural-language commentary carries three structured fields per subquestion — the subquestion itself, a dependency graph saying which earlier subquestions it uses, and rationale quotes drawn from the question or from outside knowledge — and each field is meant to condition the code that follows. In **Problem Solving**, an external deterministic solver runs the program (a Python interpreter, a Datalog engine, a PDDL planner, or a hand-written recursive rule engine) and whatever it returns *is* the answer. Decoding is greedy (temperature 0.0) or self-consistency (temperature 0.4, 40 samples, majority vote), 6–10 exemplars per prompt, `max_tokens` 1000. Baselines are standard prompting, CoT, and Least-to-Most, re-run by the authors wherever the published numbers were missing or the test set was cleaned. Analysis arms remove pieces of the prompt one at a time; a separate arm deletes the solver and lets the model answer itself; a second samples six different exemplar sets; a third perturbs prompt phrasing; a fourth enforces structural constraints on the generated dependency graph and checks the accuracy cost. A Prolific crowd study rates the correctness of 100 chains per domain, but only chains that ended in a correct answer.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **program-with-comments and its execution trace** — formally a two-part object: natural-language subquestions plus symbolic code, with the answer defined as the solver's output. The claim to explanation is one-directional and deliberately narrow: because the answer is computed from the written chain, the chain is a faithful account of how the answer was produced, so no separate faithfulness measurement is needed *for that link*. The authors are explicit that this is partial in two ways. The Translation stage — how the model chose that program from the question — remains opaque and uninterpreted, so the account explains the last step and not the first. And the natural-language commentary is not enforced to correspond to the code: in the main experiments nothing checks that a subquestion's stated dependencies or rationale quotes are the variables the code actually uses. It is a guarantee about the path from chain to answer, not about the path from question to chain.

## How they judged success
Main metric is final-answer accuracy under each task's official scoring rule (integer match, boolean match, plan member of the ground-truth set, string match). They report it against three baselines under both decoding strategies, then repeat the whole comparison across five translator models. Secondary measures: accuracy change when each prompt component is ablated (natural-language comments, rationales, subquestions, the solver itself); spread across six exemplar sets and three prompt phrasings (mean and standard deviation reported); the fraction of generations that violate the structural constraints; and a human-rated plausibility score with inter-annotator agreement per domain. No faithfulness metric is defined, because by construction the solver link is exact; the human study is the measurement of what the guarantee does *not* cover.

## What they found
With `code-davinci-002` and greedy decoding, Faithful CoT beats all three baselines on 8 of 10 datasets, and the gains are largest where the task is hardest: GSM8K 72.3 against CoT's 63.3, CLUTRR 58.9 against 48.5, Date Understanding 81.6 against 59.9, SayCan 89.3 against 86.4, AQuA 47.2 against 42.1. Under self-consistency it leads on 7 of 10, with CLUTRR 71.9 against CoT's 45.7 and AQuA 61.4 against 52.0. It loses to CoT on StrategyQA under both strategies (63.0 against 72.5 greedy; 65.2 against 79.8 self-consistency), which the authors attribute to Datalog's rarity in pretraining — 29% of its StrategyQA errors are syntax errors, and the Datalog prompt also degrades the natural-language subquestion generation. With `gpt-4` the method reaches 95.0 GSM8K, 95.4 SVAMP, 95.6 ASDiv, and the authors claim few-shot state of the art on 7 of the 10 datasets. The solver is the load-bearing component: deleting it and letting the model answer its own program costs −50.8 points on GSM8K, −22.9 on Date Understanding and −19.4 on CLUTRR, and is roughly neutral on SayCan, where the plan already is the chain. Natural-language commentary is nearly free in accuracy terms — removing it costs 0.5 on GSM8K, 1.9 on Date, and is +1.0 on SayCan — but is worth 49.6 points on CLUTRR, where the entire drop comes from a single nudge line that changes the format of the generated code. Six exemplar sets keep the result well clear of the baselines, with the only sub-baseline case being the easiest dataset (SayCan); three prompt phrasings are likewise stable. Enforcing structural constraints on the dependency graph costs accuracy rather than buying it (all three constraints together: −5.0 GSM8K, −5.9 SVAMP, −3.2 ASDiv) and reveals that 1.0–8.9% of generations violate at least one. The human study finds 92.0% of MWP chains rated fully correct, 94.0% planning, 100% Sports, but 66.7% StrategyQA, 87.9% Date and 88.0% CLUTRR, with agreement ranging from 0.455 (StrategyQA) to 0.947 (Planning); the authors' own audit finds annotators mislabel 41.7% of Date chains and 100% of the CLUTRR chains they inspected as wrong when the chain was right.

## Limits and open problems they name
The Translation stage is opaque and they say so twice — the faithfulness guarantee covers only solving, not how the chain was derived. Faithfulness is not correctness: a wrong world-model in the code yields a wrong answer executed faultlessly, and their own worked StrategyQA failure is exactly this (a chain asserting wrong facts about Pakistan's neighbours while answering "True" correctly anyway) — a case where the answer is faithful to the chain and the chain is false about the world. Translation-stage opacity is not repaired: nothing forces the natural-language comments to match the code, and the constraint experiments show that forcing them costs accuracy. The exemplar sets and the Datalog prompt were hand-tuned per dataset, so the method is few-shot prompt engineering per task rather than a general interface. Practically, `code-davinci-002` was withdrawn by OpenAI in March 2023 and the main results are unreproducible without continued access, which they raise as a research-infrastructure complaint. They also note that the human study measures plausibility, not faithfulness, and that a correct answer does not certify its chain.

## What we take from it
A **framing** and a **warning**, both load-bearing for our white-box account. The framing: an explanation can be made faithful by construction — by moving the answer's production into an interpreter the explanation is written for — and the price is that you explain only the last stage. That is precisely the shape of a surrogate-model claim in our setting: if we fit a decoder that maps activations to outputs and report the decoder, we have guaranteed fidelity from activations to prediction and left the mapping from inputs to activations unexplained, exactly as they leave Translation unexplained. So their Limitations section is our Limitations section, and quoting "faithful" about a constructed read-out carries the same obligation to say *faithful to what*. The measurement lesson is sharper. Their ablation table is the folder's clearest demonstration that a component can be **load-bearing for accuracy and empty for interpretability**, or vice versa: the natural-language commentary changes accuracy by under two points on three of four datasets and is the link a human reads, while the nudge line that buys 31.3 points on CLUTRR is pure format steering. Any account we build whose "explanation" strand is decorative and whose arithmetic strand does all the work is this paper's design, and its own numbers say the decorative strand should not be credited with the result. A third transferable habit is cheap: **ablate the interface, not only the content** — deleting the solver is the intervention that reveals how much of the answer was ever being computed from the account, and the −50.8-point drop on GSM8K is the general test we should run on any generated rationale whose answer is supposed to follow from it. Finally their human study is a caution about the evaluation we would otherwise default to: agreement as low as 0.455 and annotator error rates of 41.7% and 100% on inspected cases mean a human plausibility rating is a noisy instrument that measures the reader as much as the chain.
