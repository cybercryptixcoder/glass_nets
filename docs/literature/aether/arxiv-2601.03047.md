# When the Coffee Feature Activates on Coffins: An Analysis of Feature Extraction and Steering for Mechanistic Interpretability

- **Key:** arxiv-2601.03047
- **Authors / organisation:** Raphael Ronge, Markus Maier, Frederick Eberhardt; Munich School of Philosophy (Department of Philosophy of Nature and Technology) + California Institute of Technology
- **Date:** 2026-01-06
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03047
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The paper is a philosophical stress-test of the two claims that carry most of the mechanistic-interpretability programme as practised at Anthropic: that sparse autoencoders yield human-interpretable features, and that those features can be used to steer generation. The authors' worry is not that the demonstrations are false but that they are selected. If a safety argument rests on being able to read and regulate a concept inside a model, then the interesting quantity is not whether some feature somewhere behaves conceptually, but how often a feature's label predicts its behaviour, and the published work does not report that base rate. They also argue that controllability has been used as evidence for correct interpretation, which loads a single experiment with two jobs. Their stated aim is to ask whether the route generalises far enough for the safety use it is being sold for, and to argue that the field's priority should shift from describing internal representation toward predicting and controlling output.

## What they studied
One open model, Llama 3.1 8B, with the publicly released Llama Scope residual-stream SAEs — all 32 layers, each trained with an 8-fold expansion, giving 32,768 features per layer. Feature units are single SAE latents indexed by (layer/feature). The unit of analysis for behaviour is the generated continuation under a clamped coefficient, so the object under test is a feature as a pair of (auto-generated description, observed effect), not a circuit and not a weight. Interpretations were produced for them by an automated pipeline running on o4-mini; the authors did not hand-label the dictionary. Control set for steering: temperature 0.5, 70 generated tokens, frequency penalty 1.0, a fixed seed, and a strength multiplier of 1 so that the printed coefficient is the multiplier applied to the decoded feature vector.

## How they did it
First a replication pass. They pick a feature the auto-pipeline labels as mentions of coffee and related terms (18/9463), verify it activates on and around coffee terms in English, German and Japanese, and reproduce Anthropic's four-level specificity rubric by scoring four hand-chosen sentences per level (unrelated, vaguely related, loosely related, cleanly identifying) and plotting both the maximum and the mean non-zero activation per level, which rise monotonically. They then clamp the same feature at +2 and −2 on two prompts and show the output moving from cocktails to coffee and back. With the mechanics confirmed they switch to a hunt for counter-examples, deliberately small-scale and cherry-picked in the opposite direction: features whose label and behaviour agree are collected as controls (regret and mourning, subjunctive "would have", list-making), and features where label and behaviour disagree are documented one at a time. The disagreement work has four probes. (1) They take the five layer-18 features whose descriptions all mention coffee, choose ten prompt words per feature meant to be specific to that description, rescale each feature's activations per column, sum over the category, and print a 5×5 matrix whose diagonal should dominate if the descriptions discriminate. (2) They select three features sharing the identical description across layers 6, 19 and 29 and compare their activation profiles and their steering at matched coefficients. (3) They strip the surrounding words from a target token and re-run activation with and without a one- or two-word context, isolating context-dependence as its own factor. (4) They look for activations that carry no semantic content at all, by measuring activation density across tokens and by inspecting activation at the beginning-of-sequence token. Finally they sweep the steering coefficient for several features to see whether the response is smooth, and they reconcile steering with Anthropic's own ablation-versus-attribution footnote.

## What counts as an explanation here
A feature's auto-generated description, taken as a hypothesis about what a latent stands for, and the demand that two independent measurements of the same latent agree with it: the activation profile over inputs, and the change in generated text under clamping. The paper's own contribution is not an explanation of any model behaviour but a criterion for when such a description may be called an explanation, borrowed from a philosophical definition of concept representation — a model part represents a concept when it coactivates on instances of the concept and when manipulating it changes the concept's functional role. Their test is therefore conjunctive: a label that predicts activation but not steering, or the reverse, is not an explanation of the latent, and they show latents that satisfy one conjunct and fail the other. Nothing here is complete in the sense of covering a network; it is a per-latent reliability audit of an existing dictionary.

## How they judged success
Replication success is qualitative agreement with the published demonstrations (activation pattern correct, specificity gradient present, steering direction correct in both signs). Generalisation is judged by the authors' own reading of case studies rather than by a metric with a baseline — they say plainly that they did not have the resources to be exhaustive or representative, and justify the small sample with the argument that for a safety-critical claim even a few easily found failures are disqualifying. The quantitative instruments are the normalised 5×5 description-versus-category activation matrix (off-diagonal mass as the failure signal), the activation-density threshold of ninety-plus percent used to flag hyperactive features, the magnitude of beginning-of-sequence activation against normal-token activation, and the coefficient at which a steering effect appears, degenerates into an unrelated topic, or breaks syntax. No significance testing, no held-out set, no human agreement study of the auto-interpretations.

## What they found
The replication holds: a coffee latent activates precisely, generalises across languages, shows the specificity gradient, and steers both ways at coefficients of 2 and −2, and several more abstract latents (regret, subjunctive mood, lists) behave as labelled. The generalisation evidence goes the other way. The five layer-18 coffee features do not discriminate: in the normalised matrix the diagonal is not the largest entry in any row — the row for the feature labelled drinks-particularly-wine-and-coffee peaks on the culturally-significant-coffee category at 3.38 against its own 0.48, the general coffee feature peaks off-diagonal at 4.16, and no column or row shows the separation the descriptions imply. Three latents with the identical description "references to coffee" across layers 6, 19 and 29 activate differently and steer differently at the same coefficient. Labels can also be token-shaped rather than concept-shaped: a layer-29 "references to coffee" latent fires on the token "coff", so it responds to coffins, and clamping it at −20 removes the word coffin from a funeral continuation; a "Morocco" latent instead follows the letters "mor", and clamping it at +15 fills a "My day starts" paragraph with the word morning and no Morocco at all. Context can do all the work: a wine-and-coffee latent fires on the token after "cup of" or "drank" regardless of what follows, so "cup of poison" and "drank poison" activate it while "coffee" alone does not. Spurious structure is common enough to have families: six collected latents have activation density above ninety percent (up to 96.67 percent, including one labelled climate change), and another family's maximum activation sits on the beginning-of-sequence token, where one latent reaches 245.00 against ordinary token values — strong enough that steering is still possible but described as relatively erratic. Steering behaves non-monotonically with magnitude: a tennis latent at coefficient 1 turns "My favorite sport is" from baseball into soccer and basketball before tennis appears at 2; a health-effects-of-coffee latent works at 5, answers with tea at 6, and breaks syntax by 8; a bird latent labelled pigeons and airborne topics produces American Robin at 2, 5 and 10 with no pigeon at any strength. The paper closes with the argument that the field is measuring the input-to-feature relation when the safety-relevant relation is feature-to-output, and cites a footnote in the work it replicates showing that ablation correlates much better with attribution than with activation, i.e. that the quantity used to describe features is a poor predictor of the quantity that matters; it adds that a steering benchmark checking only capability loss between −5 and 5 cannot distinguish a working feature from an inert one.

## Limits and open problems they name
The study is explicitly a first pass and says it is neither exhaustive nor representative: a handful of features per phenomenon, chosen by the authors. It runs on a different model than the one the claims were made about and rests on a weak universality assumption to transfer, so nothing here directly measures Claude. Informally learned feature-magnitude scales — one latent needs 10 and above to move anything while another acts at 2 — are not tabulated, and the authors concede that layer-specific SAEs cannot establish whether two similarly labelled latents in adjacent layers are the same object, with crosscoders cited as the untested alternative. They also cannot separate the sources of a mismatch: a failing latent may be a merged concept, a mis-description by the auto-interpreter, or a steering artefact, and the paper names all three without adjudicating between them. Whether features compose into anything resembling reasoning is left open, as is what a complete or faithful feature set would even mean.

## What we take from it
The most useful thing here for a predictive white-box model is a measurement that costs nothing: report, for every nominated feature, the three-way agreement between its description, its activation profile on inputs chosen to be specific to that description, and its induced output change under clamping. The 5×5 labelled-word matrix is the cheapest instrument in the folder for showing that two features with different names are not functionally different, and the context-stripping probe is a one-line test for whether a "concept" is really a token-trigger plus a collocation. Two framing points are worth importing whole. First, a label is a hypothesis with two independent falsification channels, and a unit that passes one and fails the other must not be filed as understood — this is run 899's significance-versus-suppression rule stated in the SAE vocabulary, and it comes with a concrete case (five coffee latents, all descriptors, none discriminative). Second, for prediction: if attribution tracks ablation while activation does not, then our model's predictions under intervention should be built from causal-importance estimates and checked against real ablations, never from "what makes this feature fire". The paper's robotics-style conclusion — that for safety the deliverable is reliable prediction and control of output, with interpretation as an aid rather than the goal — is the same target the survey exists to reach, and its warning about sweeping a steering coefficient without measuring whether the output moved in the labelled direction is a warning about any knob our model exposes.
