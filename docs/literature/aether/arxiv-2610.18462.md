# Readout Scaling Shapes Sparse Feature Recovery

## What they wanted, and why
A model's final linear readout multiplies some hidden directions far more strongly than others, and a sparse autoencoder trained on the resulting output scores weights its squared error by those multipliers squared. The authors ask whether that weighting — not the information available, which is identical either way — changes which features a dictionary learns and how well it recovers them. Their larger goal is to make the choice of input coordinates an explicit, testable part of dictionary design, and to separate a reconstruction improvement from a genuine improvement in feature quality. It is a measurement-heavy paper: one coordinate transformation, then a grid of studies whose purpose is to show why any single headline number from that transformation would be misleading.

## What they studied
Small synthetic generators with known factors (16 unit directions in 8 dimensions, two to four active per sample), two OpenSpiel game policies (bargaining and seven-card Goofspiel), and three frozen language-model checkpoints — Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen3-0.6B-Base and SmolLM2-360M — mostly on six-label Emotion heads plus full-vocabulary readouts for Qwen2.5. The unit of analysis is the decoder direction learned by a Top-K SAE, scored either against a known generating factor (synthetic only) or by reconstruction of the readout and by single-feature label detection and steering (real models). The coordinate being varied is a family that interpolates between keeping and removing the readout's singular-value gains.

## How they did it
They define readout-canonicalised decision-space coordinates (RC-DS): centre the logits over the legal actions or labels, take the SVD of the resulting readout matrix, and map the hidden state into the retained subspace with the singular values removed. A knob α ∈ {0, 1/2, 1} keeps no gains, square-root gains, or all gains, so logit training and RC-DS training are the two ends of one axis on the same retained information. They prove these coordinates and the retained centred logits are affine images of one another, so a comparison between them isolates training geometry rather than information. Then they run the same SAE architecture in each coordinate system: a coordinate-matching study that moves where the factors were generated and where training happens and scores recovery on and off the diagonal; a condition-number sweep; a loss-only control that reweights the error but still feeds scaled inputs; an exact wrapper that transforms encoder input, decoder output and loss together and is shown to reproduce RC-DS training to numerical precision; correlation and activity sweeps; and a frozen predictor of the recovery gap built only from fitting-data geometry. On real models they score every fit in both a logit space and a gain-free space, compare against hidden-state, standardisation, PCA and ZCA baselines, extend training from 60 to 1,000 epochs and from 6,000 updates to a separate tuned protocol on 360 fresh prompts, and then run label-detection and KL-budgeted steering tests on the same features.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation of a model's computation but an account of an instrument: a statement of when a dictionary's learned features reflect the target and when they reflect the coordinates the optimiser was pointed at, together with a diagnostic that predicts the sign of the difference before any SAE is trained. The paper's form of understanding is a matched-comparison design plus an equivalence proof — the wrapper is the claim that the difference is transportable geometry and nothing else — with the predictor as the small predictive component. It is partial by construction: it explains a gap between two coordinate choices, not the features themselves.

## How they judged success
Signed cosine similarity between learned decoder directions and known generating factors after a one-to-one assignment (recovery), and support micro-F1 between active features and true active factors (the predictor's target). On real models, explained variance in centred-logit space and in gain-free space computed on the same residuals, with a declared primary outcome (semantic label detection) and reconstruction reported as secondary. Paired seeds with bootstrap intervals over seeds, diagonal entries of the matching grid used as a construction check, exact-wrapper replay checked by activation, gradient and parameter agreement, a two-seed or three-seed descriptive count on the reserved checkpoints rather than significance tests, and a fixed-effect-baseline comparison for the predictor.

## What they found
Recovery is highest when training coordinates match the space the factors were generated in, in every row of the matching grid (diagonal 0.8364), and the off-diagonal losses are what the study is about. When factors are generated before readout scaling, mean recovery falls monotonically as the condition number grows (0.9134 at κ=1 to about 0.5063 at κ=100) while RC-DS holds 0.9134 at every κ, and the gap survives adding noise before scaling (0.4034, interval 0.3627 to 0.4445) — the control that rules out "RC-DS just got more information". Changing the generating geometry at a fixed condition number reverses the ordering: the same κ=100, K=2, zero-correlation setting gives +0.4095 F1 for RC-DS in one construction and −0.4460 in the other. The exact wrapper matches RC-DS in every trial (0.8176, maximum discrepancy 1.33e-15) while loss-only reweighting recovers part of the gap (0.6284) and ordinary scaled-input error sits at 0.4841. A predictor built only from fitting-data geometry got the sign right in all 36 frozen forecast pairs (MAE 0.0526 against 0.241 for predicting no effect, missing its own 0.05 target), but ZCA beat RC-DS on support F1 in 34 of those 36 pairs. On real models the reconstruction story splits by metric: RC-DS had higher gain-free explained variance than logit training in all 18 settings and lower logit explained variance in 17 of 18, gains over the ordinary hidden-state SAE were tiny (0.0002 to 0.0124), the advantage shrank from 0.0198 to 0.0016 when training went from 60 to 1,000 epochs, and separate tuning on fresh prompts produced near-ties whose intervals include zero. Raw singular coordinates beat every trained RC-DS label detector in all 18 settings (differences 0.0002 to 0.1838 AUROC), and the geometry predictor failed on the reserved checkpoints — wrong on all four settings outside the ±0.01 band and worse than predicting zero. Steering succeeded in exactly one frozen study (+7.73% of oracle gain, interval 4.37 to 11.18) and failed the rest, including a longer-trained RC-DS-versus-logit comparison at −4.08%.

## Limits and open problems they name
Three fixed checkpoints with only two reserved, and repeated tasks and sparsity levels rather than independent models; reconstruction was elevated to a headline after the results were seen; several fits fail the authors' own loss-stability checks so equal budgets are not equal convergence; known-factor recovery exists only in synthetic data; the equivalence result holds only on the retained subspace at a fixed checkpoint and mask; the effect has not been shown for other SAE architectures; the semantic predictor has three development task groups and is under-determined by its own admission. They also record their failures in place — an encoder-copying trainer branch, a 48-fit recipe audit that selected no recipe, two steering studies whose intervals span zero, and a feature-activity ratio that diverges during generation.

## What we take from it
A warning and a design template. The warning: a dictionary's features are partly a function of which coordinates the reconstruction loss weighed, so two dictionaries over the same information can disagree about what the model contains, and the disagreement is real rather than an artefact of one being worse. The template is the instrumentation — an exact wrapper to show a difference is geometry rather than information, a matched diagonal as a construction check, a known-factor generator to interpret the sign of an effect, and scoring every fit in two metric spaces rather than one — and the pre-registered directional forecast, including its own failure, is the honest version of the prediction claim our white-box model will need to make. The reusability of the lesson is bounded by three things the paper states rather than hides: the advantage shrinks with training, it does not survive a different normalisation (ZCA), and a raw coordinate probe beats trained features on label detection, which is the same "zero-cost baseline wins" result arriving from the dictionary side.
