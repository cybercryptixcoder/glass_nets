## What they wanted, and why
Post-hoc attribution for clinical sequence models returns a heat map: one importance number per (variable, timestamp) cell. Such a map answers "which cells mattered" but not "what clinical pattern was the model using", it cannot be composed into units that recur across patients, and the clinical reading of a cell depends on the cells around it in time and across variables. The authors want a *shared, reusable* concept dictionary for a frozen predictive model: a finite vocabulary in which each entry is a pattern with a temporal extent and a variable composition, so that two patients can be described as being affected by the same concept and a clinician can check the description against the record. The larger goal is the one the field keeps stating and rarely measuring — explanations that are structured rather than per-cell, and structured in the shape of the domain's own concepts.

## What they studied
Two critical-care cohorts: MIMIC-III (48-hour ICU episodes; 14.1% in-hospital mortality) and MIMIC-IV (14.4%), each with three binary tasks (in-hospital mortality, 30-day readmission, length of stay ≥ 7 days) split 64/16/20 by patient. The unit of analysis is a *latent* in a dictionary learned on top of one frozen backbone layer: 1,280 latents, four temporal islands per latent, a selection budget of 128 islands per episode. Five backbones with different event representations are used — a discrete (binned, single-token) transformer with and without value-forecast pretraining, STraTS and HyMaTE (continuous-value embedding), and BAT (grid-native bi-axial attention) — so the dictionary is re-learned per backbone and cross-backbone agreement can be measured. Variables are also grouped into a clinical channel (cardiovascular, renal, respiratory, …) via an ontology mapping.

## How they did it
The backbone is frozen; its per-event embeddings are scattered onto a dense time × variable grid. Stage A learns the dictionary with three structural constraints. Each latent owns a sparse top-k, row-normalised, non-negative variable-routing vector shared between encoder and decoder; the encoder projects the routed variables onto a per-latent direction to get a pre-selection activation trace. An *Island Parameter Network* turns each trace into four Gaussian windows: centres from a softmax over time positions, widths from three EM iterations with no learnable parameters. Islands are then selected by an energy-gated score normalised by effective window length, through differentiable top-k with straight-through estimation, and the selected windows form a temporal mask on the activation; the decoder reconstructs observed grid cells from the masked activations with a shared routing matrix and sparser decoding (top-3). Reconstitution is scored by per-cell normalised MSE on observed cells, with four regularisers: dead-latent revival (unselected latents reconstruct the residual), centre repulsion within a latent, decoder-row diversity, mask coverage, and channel purity. Stage B freezes the dictionary and trains a *prediction selector* that scores island features (centre, width, energy, peak, gate, gradient prior, routing weights, coverage) against a patient query, selecting soft top-k and matching both the full-island and complement-island logits to the backbone's teacher logit under a hinge margin. Evaluation uses comprehensiveness/sufficiency at matched top-k fractions under three perturbation regimes (deletion, marginal replacement, nearest-observed-value in the same variable), summarised by AOPC, with neighbourhood-based perturbation primary; clinical plausibility is measured against SOFA-derived organ-severity scores; and verbalised explanations are compared in a pairwise LLM-judge protocol (judge model different from the verbaliser) plus a four-clinician panel.

## What counts as an explanation here
A dictionary entry with three readable parts: *when* (learned Gaussian windows), *what* (a sparse variable-composition vector, ideally within one clinical channel), and *how much this patient relies on it* (a selector weight). It is partial by construction — the dictionary is not claimed to span the representation, and the paper reports what fraction of it is never used — and it is meant to predict: the selector's ranking is validated by knocking concepts out and watching the prediction move more than under random removal. Labels are post-hoc decoration supplied by an LLM from exemplar episodes, not part of the concept definition; the authors are explicit that the label is validation rather than definition.

## How they judged success
Faithfulness by AOPC on comprehensiveness curves with sufficiency at 10% reported alongside, averaged over three tasks and two cohorts, with bootstrap standard deviations over 2,000 resamples and a random-attribution floor. Plausibility by AUROC/AUPRC of top-attributed cells against a SOFA-derived severity prior. Faithfulness matched against eleven baselines at equal top-k: attention, Integrated Gradients (in embedding space, median-reference baseline), TSR, DynaMask, TimeSHAP, FIT, WinIT, Cluster-TCAV (ACE clusters + TCAV directional derivatives with a random-direction significance test), TimeX/TimeX++/ExtremalMask (adapted, near-random, excluded from the main table), and random. Controls include structural ablations (remove islands, remove channel purity, remove variable routing, replace the whole thing with an event-level top-k SAE, and train the selector on raw hidden states with no dictionary), a concept zero-ablation curve against random-order removal, and seed reproducibility over three to five seeds with a *matched* random null that searches the same candidate sets. Verbalised explanations are judged pairwise on three separate dimensions with A/B order randomised, with the label contribution isolated in a separate ablation. A cross-dataset transfer test to a disjoint MIMIC-IV cohort (2014–2019 admission bands) and a cross-backbone dictionary comparison complete the set.

## What they found
Faithfulness (AOPC, averaged over tasks and datasets): TI-SAE 0.086 / 0.076 / 0.097 / 0.043 / 0.016 across the discrete, pretrained-discrete, HyMaTE, BAT and STraTS backbones against IG 0.060 / 0.051 / 0.049 / 0.031 / 0.067 and TSR 0.063 / 0.054 / 0.062 / 0.037 / 0.071 — best on four of five, with STraTS the exception (0.016 against IG's 0.067). Plausibility against SOFA is best or near-best throughout, 0.588–0.614 AUROC on the discrete variants and HyMaTE. The standard event-level top-k SAE scores 0.023 by activation (0.155 when a modified selector is allowed to inject variable-identity information); the selector trained on raw hidden states without a dictionary collapses to 0.038; removing variable routing costs 58–75% of AOPC, while removing the islands costs essentially nothing on faithfulness (0.149 → 0.145) but collapses temporal localisation (activation entropy 0.957 across 48 bins) and channel coherence (91.9% → 36.9%). Dictionary side: reconstruction cosine is reported per backbone (the HTML renders the values blank, so the entry does not quote them), island identity under 5% event dropout has Jaccard 0.837–0.903; of 479 active latents, 385 (80%) have an outcome odds-ratio interval excluding 1 (223 high-risk, 162 protective); mean absolute pairwise activation correlation 0.125 with 1.4% of pairs above 0.5; four clinicians rate 30 labels at 4.07–4.40 mean Likert. Reproducibility: full-pipeline retraining gives decoder cosine 0.724 and 77% of matched latents sharing two of three decoder variables (1.2% random); five seeds retraining only the dictionary give cosine 0.735 and 82% overlap; the selector on a fixed dictionary has Spearman 0.87 and top-64 Jaccard 0.708. Cross-backbone the variable groupings agree well (decoder cosine 0.712–0.801, 77.5–88.2% variable overlap) but energy correlation is 0.108–0.246 and selector NDCG 0.089–0.163 — the same variables, different patients and different importance. Verbalised explanations win concept and actionability against every baseline; with the label stripped the concept lead vanishes (24–23 against IG), and a per-episode LLM summary of the baseline's cells beats TI-SAE outright (11–39 against IG), while a matched channel-grouped, label-free baseline loses (27–21). Per-item clinician agreement is low (Fleiss κ values small across all dimensions) and the paper reports aggregate marginals instead.

## Limits and open problems they name
Fixed 48-hour time × variable grid; variable-length episodes unvalidated. The variable-routing encoder needs representations that keep per-variable locality, and not every layer (or residual scheme) does — STraTS's averaging residual is diagnosed as the cause of its failure, and the authors repair it only in an ablation. Concept identity rests on post-hoc validation. SOFA is a coarse organ-independent proxy. Single-token-per-event assumption. The verbalisation study is mediated by an LLM whose clinical priors can make any input read fluently. Beyond their list, the paper's own ablations show the structural novelty splitting into two halves: islands and channel organisation buy readability, while the faithfulness advantage is carried by sparse variable routing and would survive a much simpler dictionary; and the "full TI-SAE" attribution is produced by a *separately trained selector*, whose gain over raw hidden states is measured (.149 vs .038) but whose gain over a cell-level selector given the same structural information is only inferred.

## What we take from it
Four things. (1) **A methodological split to copy**: separate the dictionary's fidelity from its readability metric, then report the ablation that keeps one and destroys the other — here islands cost nothing on AOPC and destroy temporal localisation, channel purity costs AOPC and buys 91.9% coverage. Our read-outs should be scored twice for the same reason. (2) **Matched random nulls, not raw ones**: seed-reproducibility numbers are only meaningful because the null searches the same candidate pool with the same search procedure (77% variable overlap against a 1.2% matched null), which is the discipline we keep re-deriving. (3) **A warning about label-mediated explanation metrics**: the verbalised advantage is carried almost entirely by LLM-written concept labels, and a per-episode LLM grouping of the *baseline* beats the whole dictionary — so any judge-based comparison where one arm gets labels is measuring the label. (4) **A cheap structural shape for our own dictionaries**: bind each feature to an explicit support (when) and an explicit sparse input composition (what), and then claim interpretability from the support rather than from the reconstruction — with the caveat this paper states honestly, that sparse decoder atoms were near-orthogonal by construction so activation correlation, not decoder cosine, was the only informative separability measure.
