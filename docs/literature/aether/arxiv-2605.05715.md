# Decodable but Not Corrected by Fixed Residual-Stream Linear Steering: Evidence from Medical LLM Failure Regimes

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.05715
- **Authors / organisation:** Ming Liu (sole author); Amazon
- **Date:** 2026-05-07
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05715
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1; abstract, §1-§7, Limitations, Ethics, Reproducibility, Appendices A-M, references skimmed)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The paper asks a single question about the inference we habitually make: if a failure is linearly readable from a hidden state, can a direction fitted to that failure then be added to the residual stream to stop the model failing? The motivating case is "overthinking" in medical multiple-choice answering — a regime where the model gets the question right most of the time under sampling yet produces a wrong answer in long chain-of-thought traces, so the failure is neither a knowledge gap nor a question-difficulty effect but a property of the generation. The larger goal is to test whether probing findings transfer into control, because a great deal of interpretability work treats a successful decoder as licence to steer with the decoded direction. The author's stance is that the field has been documenting this gap case by case and none of the documentations explains it, so the contribution claimed here is a *mechanistic* account — a geometry that says in advance which directions will steer and which will not. A secondary aim, stated as the operational upside, is that the same decoder that cannot repair a trace can at least tell you afterwards that the trace is untrustworthy.

## What they studied

Two instruction-tuned 7-8B models: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers, 4096 hidden) and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (28 layers, 3584 hidden). Data is MedQA — 10,178 training and 1,273 test questions, ten sampled traces per training question at temperature 0.8 — plus a 300-question MMLU-STEM replication across ten traces each. The unit of analysis is the **direction**: a per-layer mean-difference contrastive vector between correct and failing traces, a linear probe weight vector, a low-rank subspace basis, a concept-erasure projector, and (as a control) a learned non-linear residual adapter. The behaviour being read is not an internal feature but an observational construct the author defines from the outside — a question where at least 60% of ten sampled traces are correct while the incorrect traces run over 200 tokens.

## How they did it

Failures are first labeled by a two-phase pipeline: a heuristic over the ten traces tags overthinking (OT), then remaining failures are split into knowledge deficit (KD) and reasoning-chain break (RCB) by an LLM judge, with a 500-trace expert-annotated gold set for validation. For 12,000 training traces they take the last-token hidden state at every layer in fp32, 2,000 per failure mode plus 6,000 correct traces matched to the same questions, and fit both a PCA-50 logistic-regression probe and per-layer contrastive vectors. They then measure the geometry: classification accuracy per layer, pairwise cosines between mode vectors, a *specificity ratio* measuring how much of each mode vector survives projection onto the mean of all mode vectors (the "shared" incorrect-vs-correct axis), within-class spread relative to the centroid gap, and a permutation null for the specificity value. A cross-validated version of direction ablation is run — directions fit on one split, ablated on another — to separate probe overfitting from genuine necessity.

The intervention sweep is where most of the work sits: five families of fixed linear additive steering (29 configurations) applied by forward hook to the post-layer residual stream during generation on the full test set — mode-specific and uniform contrastive vectors at amplitudes 0.5 to 3.0, probe-weight vectors, multi-layer application at 1/3/5 layers, rank-k subspace projections built by SVD over stacked probe and contrastive matrices, and prompt-end vectors extracted before generation began. Alongside these they run mean-difference concept erasure (projecting the OT direction out, plus a whitened variant), ten prompt-level baselines including self-refinement and verbalized confidence, and a learned 4096-64-4096 residual MLP trained on the same correct/OT supervision. Nine named threats to the null — broken hooks, wrong vector position, rank too low, temperature mismatch, metric insensitivity, non-linear capacity, random artifacts, prompt fixability, single-layer limitation — each get a dedicated control, and a refusal-steering positive control on 50 borderline prompts checks that the identical code path can move behaviour at all.

## What counts as an explanation here

The explanation is geometric and diagnostic rather than mechanism-in-the-circuit sense: a pair of scalar quantities (specificity ratio, within-class spread over centroid gap) plus a two-point calibration between a direction that steers and a direction that does not, offered as a pre-intervention test of whether an additive linear intervention can work at all. It is explicitly partial — the author calls the four correlates "compatible explanations, not causally identified mechanisms" and states that the data cannot distinguish superposition-with-task-computation from the alternative that the OT direction is itself required for correct reasoning. It predicts something real and falsifiable, though: directions above a specificity of roughly 0.99 were steerable in this setting and directions at or below about 0.21 were not, across 29 configurations. A second, independent diagnostic (a linear-accessibility profile comparing logit-lens accuracy against probe accuracy) is used as a cross-check rather than a rival explanation.

## How they judged success

Accuracy change against a run-matched baseline on the full test set, with McNemar tests two-sided and every delta computed within its own run's paired baseline because sampling at temperature 0.1 gives baselines ranging 64.0-67.4%. The null is tested as an *equivalence* claim rather than merely a failure to reject: a TOST at an equivalence margin of plus or minus 2.5 points, chosen a priori as half of a 5-point clinically meaningful difference, with the note that power per individual test is only about 20% and that the joint evidence across all 29 configurations is what carries the negative. Correction and damage counts are reported separately for every configuration so the two can be seen offsetting. Abstention quality is judged by AUROC against five single-forward-pass uncertainty baselines with paired bootstrap and Holm correction. Robustness is checked by five-fold stratified cross-validation, GroupKFold to rule out question-identity leakage, random-label and permutation nulls, length regression, threshold sweeps with Jaccard overlap, and 37% simulated label noise on the poorly-agreed sub-taxonomy.

## What they found

The failure regime is real and reproducible: 94% expert agreement on the OT label, a 60%-correct threshold whose perturbation leaves Jaccard at 0.81 or above, and probes that read it at 71.6% binary accuracy (balanced accuracy 62.3%, AUROC 0.672) — modest over the 66.7% majority baseline but at p around 1e-16, and driven by 90% recall on non-OT against only 34% on OT. An important sanity result sits next to it: probing the *question* representation before generation gives only 54.4%, so what is being decoded is a property of the trace, not of the question.

The steering null is comprehensive. All five method families at all tested amplitudes land near zero — mode-specific contrastive steering gives minus 0.2 points at alpha 1.5 and plus 0.2 at alpha 3.0, and the strongest probe (81.5% correct-vs-OT) gives plus 1.5 points at p = 0.296 and turns to minus 3.8 at p = 0.010 when the amplitude is tripled. Rank-k subspaces do not help (all per-mode ranks equivalent to zero within 3 points, while uniform correctness-direction steering damages at every rank between minus 2.6 and minus 6.2). Prompt-end vectors, near-orthogonal to the last-token vectors at cosines of 0.17 or below, also do nothing. Qwen's nine configurations span minus 0.9 to plus 0.8. Mean-difference erasure of the OT direction at layer 16 *hurts*, minus 3.6 points at p = 0.010, against ten random rank-1 erasures at plus 0.3 with SD 1.8. And uniform steering along the shared incorrect-vs-correct axis costs 12.1 points. Re-running three conditions with full text shows the perturbation is not inert — 38% to 56% of answers change, corrections run roughly 2 to 3.6 against damages, and the answer distribution collapses toward one dominant option at 33% to 52%, which is the signature of a directional bias rather than targeted repair.

The geometric account is the paper's sharpest instrument. The OT vector's specificity ratio is 0.119 on Llama and 0.152 on Qwen — that is, roughly 85 to 88% of it is the shared incorrect-vs-correct axis — against a permutation null mean of 0.370 with the observed value below all 10,000 shuffles. The circularity objection is pre-empted by fitting an OT-vs-KD direction that never touches correct traces and finding it still at cosine 0.86 with the correctness direction. The refusal control direction, computed the same way on the same code path, scores 0.999 specificity and is near-orthogonal to the failure directions (minus 0.008 and 0.014), and refusal steering works in both directions with 7 prompts moving one way and 0 the other, sign test p = 0.008. Geometric independence also explains why probe and contrastive vectors achieve the same classification accuracy from directions at cosines of only 0.23 to 0.54: decodability does not single out one intervention target.

The headline positive is elsewhere. The same decoder supports selective abstention at held-out test AUROC 0.610, beating the best of five uncertainty baselines by 0.041 at p = 0.009, and on balanced training traces the probe reaches 0.716 and buys plus 14.0 points of accuracy at 60% coverage. A learned non-linear MLP closes part of the correction gap — plus 2.8 points at p = 0.025, cutting centroid distance by 50.6% while changing only 25% of answers — which the author reads as evidence that the entanglement is a matter of degree. And the honest cost comparison: majority vote over ten traces gets 72.7%, the probe-based best-of-N at k=10 gets 70.7%, so the regime is solvable at ten times the sampling cost but not by any single-pass intervention tested. Two smaller diagnostics round it out: the logit-lens classification accuracy of the OT direction peaks at 58.6% against probe accuracy of 81.5%, and its top projected tokens are semantically incoherent at every layer; and steering applied only to the first half of the tokens is the most damaging condition (minus 11.0, p = 0.022) while same-temperature evaluation is worse than the temperature-mismatched one (minus 6.7), falsifying the mismatch explanation.

## Limits and open problems they name

Two 7-8B models on one domain with a 300-question cross-domain check is thin, and Qwen never gets the erasure experiment, so whether its much higher average specificity (0.414 against Llama's 0.119) reflects a different kind of encoding is unknown. The negative result covers *fixed linear* interventions only: the full oblique LEACE projector is never run (the whitened mean-difference variant is close), DAS, ReFT and instance-adaptive methods are untested, and the probe-gated dynamic variant degenerates because the probe reports low confidence on 93% of intermediate tokens. The KD/RCB sub-taxonomy has only moderate agreement (kappa 0.30) — the paper is careful to note the core claims bypass it. Most consequentially, the author states outright that the specificity ratio is a *two-point* calibration, not a validated predictor, and that the data cannot choose between entanglement-with-task-computation and the OT direction being causally required for correct reasoning. The "100% of OT questions corrected by majority vote" figure is definitional given the 60% threshold, and the paper says so.

## What we take from it

- **Framing.** This is the entry that gives our white-box model its most important negative obligation: a direction that decodes a behavioural difference is not thereby a handle on it, and the difference between the two is measurable *before* you try. Any probe our model emits should be labelled as read-only until a specificity-style test has been run on it.
- **Method.** The specificity ratio is directly reusable and cheap: project a mode direction onto the mean of all mode directions and report the surviving fraction, with a permutation null over label shuffles so that a subset structure cannot manufacture the result. Pair it with the within-class-spread statistic (standard deviation over centroid distance), which is the quantity that says whether a single additive nudge can move a sample across the boundary at all.
- **Method.** The direction-ablation control — fit on one split, ablate on another — is the cheapest known separator of a genuinely necessary subspace from probe overfitting, and here it overturned the in-sample reading by going from 51.4% down to 25.8% in-sample against minus 0.2 points cross-validated. Our model's feature-importance numbers need this arm.
- **Measure.** Reporting corrections and damages as separate counts, and reading the answer-distribution concentration (33% to 52% on one option) as a diagnostic of bias rather than change, is the right way to report an intervention that "works" in a generation task.
- **Warning.** The 12.1-point damage from steering along the *shared* component is the concrete cost of failing to check specificity, and it is the failure mode a naive "add the mean difference" pipeline walks straight into.
- **Datapoint for our prediction goal.** The refusal-versus-overthinking contrast (0.999 versus 0.119, and 0.152 on a second architecture) is the best available evidence that specificity is architecture-stable where the surrounding geometry is not — exactly the kind of quantity a predictive white-box model should be built on, and the paper hands us its strongest caveat with it.
