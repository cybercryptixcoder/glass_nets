# Right Answer, Wrong Mechanism: Detecting Pernicious Divergence in Causal Interventions

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.39243
- **Authors / organisation:** Beiming Liu, Minjie Chen (equal contribution); China Financial Certification Authority (CFCA) and PetroChina Southwest Oil and Gasfield Company, China
- **Date:** 2026-09 (v1 2026-09-30; v2 2026-10-04)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39243
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v2 HTML; §1–§8, Appendices A–C including Tables 1–13)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Every mechanistic claim about a network rests on one move: change an internal representation and watch the output change. That move is only evidence about the model's natural computation if the state being installed is one the model could plausibly have been in on its own. Earlier work showed the assumption fails — common interventions (patching, DAS, SAE reconstruction) push representations off the data manifold — and split the resulting divergence into harmless (pointing where downstream computation ignores it) and pernicious (waking up dormant paths that deliver the expected answer through a mechanism the model never uses). What was missing was any way to tell the two apart on a live run, since a successful intervention looks identical either way. The authors' goal is to make that distinction measurable rather than argued: supply ground truth for which interventions are pernicious, then score candidate detectors against it. The larger aim is to say when a causal result may be read as a claim about the natural mechanism at all.

## What they studied

Four decoder-only language models at four scales — GPT-2 small, Pythia-1.4B, Pythia-6.9B, Qwen2.5-7B — under TransformerLens at fixed revisions. Two binary tasks with fixed answer tokens (subject–verb agreement and gendered pronouns; indirect-object identification only in the unmodified-model analysis), with prompt pairs that differ in exactly one causal variable and are filtered to those the model answers with a comfortably positive logit margin. Unit of analysis: a residual-stream site (one layer × one position) that is overwritten, and the MLP neurons downstream of it, treated as individual units with per-class natural firing ranges.

## How they did it

They plant known "hidden pathways" inside the pretrained models. A pathway unit is a ReLU on a fixed random direction through the normalised MLP input at a later layer, with its threshold set just above the maximum that direction reaches on the benchmark prompts, so the unit is silent on every prompt by construction and the model's natural behaviour is asserted unchanged. 24 units are planted per configuration in one of four arrangements: brand-new units writing to the logit difference or to a random token's unembedding; extra drive into one existing MLP neuron with a large downstream effect; the same drive spread over 64 neurons; or the same drive spread over 128 neurons, each clipped to stay strictly inside its natural range (so only the *combination* is anomalous). Half the directions are reachable by overshooting natural directions, half only by moving off-manifold. Ground truth is obtained by simply switching the pathways off: an intervention is *pathway-dominated* if removing them takes away more than half of its decision margin, and a *wrong-mechanism success* if it flips when they are removed. Against that, 1,500 interventions per configuration are drawn from held-out pairs — full activation patch, mean-difference patch at several scales, DAS trained on the clean model, DAS blended with random natural directions, DAS trained on the pathway-equipped model, and a deliberately *restricted* DAS that is forbidden the top principal components of the site and the genuine DAS direction, which makes any success it reports structurally suspect. Detectors are then scored: site-level distances to the natural distribution (nearest neighbour, Mahalanobis, local-PCA residual, and a reimplementation of the earlier off-manifold component test), plus their own downstream measure, which clamps every MLP neuron after the site to the range it occupies in natural runs with the *same* output and asks how much of the margin disappears.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not an explanation of a model — a *validity test* for explanations. The deliverable is a detector, a scalar per intervention that says whether the decision arrived through natural computation or through machinery natural inputs never engage, plus a benchmark that makes the detector's own errors visible. It is deliberately partial: it certifies one claim only (that the intervened representation is used by the model's own mechanism), and the authors are explicit that harm is relative to the claim, so out-of-regime computation may be perfectly acceptable if the claim is merely that a subspace suffices to steer behaviour. Their detector is causal — it intervenes downstream and measures the lost margin — on the argument that "unusual" and "used for the decision" are different properties and distance measures conflate them.

## How they judged success

AUROC under two labels: pathway-dominated among all interventions, and wrong-mechanism among successful ones (the label that matters for interpretation). Means are taken over configurations (sites × pathway layers × seeds), with per-seed spreads printed separately and pooled rank-normalised AUROCs with bootstrap intervals. Controls: a reimplementation of the prior off-manifold test and its local-PCA statistic as the comparison; a synthetic setting adapted from the earlier divergence paper as a sanity check with a harmless-divergence false-positive rate; a *positive control* table bounding what the natural-model statistic can detect; three seeds for every larger-model configuration; a cross-hardware reproduction run; a sweep over how many components restricted DAS is denied; and an exploratory base-versus-instruction-tuned comparison.

## What they found

Site-level distance fails exactly where it is needed. On the gender task, nearest-neighbour and local-PCA distances among successful interventions are *below chance* for every model and pathway family (reported range 0.21–0.46), and never exceed 0.62 on any task. Mahalanobis and the earlier component test are inconsistent across models, tasks and families. Their own downstream-but-non-causal measure helps, and adding the causal clamp helps most: for pathways visible as unit-level out-of-range activity, the loose clamp reaches 0.82–1.00 among successful interventions on every model, against 0.35–0.62 for local PCA; per seed the gap is at least 0.26. Pathways hiding in *in-range combinations* defeat range clamping (0.59–0.70 on the three larger models; 0.39 on Qwen2.5-7B gender), and first projecting onto the local natural manifold only partly closes it (0.83–0.85 Pythia-1.4B, 0.73–0.75 Pythia-6.9B, 0.85 Qwen2.5-7B SVA, but 0.48 on Qwen2.5-7B gender and 0.57 on GPT-2 gender).

The second result is the one that should reorder priorities: optimised interventions *seek* pathways. DAS trained on a pathway-equipped model routes most of its gender successes through them at every scale (roughly 86–100% across models in the reported table's pathway-dominated share), and even clean-model DAS applied with overshoot lands a large share there. The restricted DAS probe — the genuine subspace removed — still reports success, and essentially all of those successes are pathway-dominated; the authors read this as the earlier interpretability-illusion critique reproduced as a controlled measurement at four scales. Adding a differentiable on-manifold penalty downstream cuts the pathway-dominated share of unrestricted DAS successes to under a twentieth at a modest success-rate cost, but does *not* clean up the restricted variant — whose residual successes stay almost entirely pathway-dominated. For us the practical line is blunt: a restricted-subspace result should not be trusted even with the penalty.

Finally, unmodified models are mostly innocent on this particular signature. Across full patches, mean-difference patches, plain DAS and random-blended DAS on GPT-2 and both Pythias, at most a fraction of a percent of successful interventions depend on out-of-range downstream activity — and none of the 2,232 restricted-DAS successes in Pythia-6.9B with a single component excluded. Site divergence and the causal measure correlate only weakly. The exception is Qwen2.5-7B with restricted DAS at the final token, where roughly three in ten SVA successes carry the same downstream signature as a planted unit-visible pathway; the authors label these candidates, not findings, because clamping can overstate dependence when later layers compensate and because there is no planted truth to confirm against.

## Limits and open problems they name

Planted pathways show what a detector can see, not how often such pathways arise naturally — and three of four families are visible by construction, so their near-perfect scores are an upper bound. In-range pathways remain the open problem, with one model–task pair (Qwen2.5-7B gender) where nothing tested beats chance reliably. Clamping ignores downstream self-repair, so the lost margin is an imperfect estimate. Natural ranges are estimated on the same templated prompts that are then intervened on, rather than a held-out distribution; several constants (pathway gain, the clamp threshold) are fixed rather than tuned; the tables average over configurations that share prompts and are not independent replicates. Largest model is 7B; the two largest are analysed on every second downstream MLP layer in bfloat16; the detector covers MLP neurons at the final position only, so attention-mediated or other-position pathways are out of scope; the mitigation is evaluated on GPT-2 alone. Same-seed runs are not bit-reproducible across hardware — re-running one configuration on a different GPU moved several AUROCs by up to about a fifth and changed the count of wrong-mechanism successes by a factor of several.

## What we take from it

A method: **evaluate a causal claim downstream, not at the address you edited.** Distance-from-natural at the intervention site is a property of the edit; whether the model used the edit for the decision is a property of what the downstream network did with it, and only the second can separate harmless from pernicious divergence. The specific instrument — clamp downstream units to the regime they occupy in natural runs *with the same output*, then measure the margin that vanishes — is cheap, label-free, and reusable on our own patches.

A measure and a discipline: **the label should be "does the decision flip when the suspect machinery is removed", and the benchmark should be built by planting the machinery.** More generally, this is the strongest template in the folder for making an interpretability artefact testable: instead of arguing about whether divergence is harmful, construct a model in which you know the answer and score every candidate detector against it — including a *positive control* that says what your detector cannot see.

A warning, and the sharpest one: **any search that is free to choose its intervention direction will find the shortest path to the target answer, and the shortest path may be a path the model never walks.** A fit that scores well after being denied the genuine subspace is reporting a property of the optimiser, not of the model. The mechanical consequence for us: any fitted alignment, subspace or steering result ships with (i) a restricted variant that removes the subspace you claim, (ii) the pathway-share of its successes, and (iii) a post-hoc "did the right thing for the right reason" check downstream. And an honest caveat from this paper's own prevalence section: a bad detector plus a real effect looks like innocence, so a null result from such a check is only as strong as the detector's sensitivity bound.

A last point of framing worth keeping: harm is relative to the claim being made. Our white-box model should therefore carry, per explanation, the claim it licenses — natural-mechanism, sufficiency-to-control, or prediction-only — because the same intervention can be innocent under one and pernicious under another.
