# A Bag of Concepts? Testing Role-Filler Binding in the Verbalizable Workspace

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.19176
- **Authors / organisation:** Reya Dawlah (UT Dallas), Elizabeth McMillin (MiraCosta College), Michael Shihong Zhang (Waterloo), Badrish Ananth (UC Irvine), Rishi Adi Ruia (University of Washington), Ane Zuñiga (MIT), Thomas Jiralerspong (Mila / Université de Montréal); workshop paper, "Interpretability as a Science"

## What they wanted, and why
Runtime alignment monitors have been proposed that read a "verbalizable workspace" — a small, vocabulary-indexed subspace of the residual stream proposed as the model's global workspace — and project it back into words to surface silent cognition before any output is produced. The authors point at a gap in that programme: nearly every safety-relevant proposition is *relational*, not lexical. "The model plans to deceive the user" and "the user plans to deceive the model" contain the same four concepts and differ only in who fills which argument slot, so a monitor that reads a set of active concepts without their bindings cannot tell a benign sentence from a dangerous one. Their question is whether the workspace carries that binding structure at all, or only a bag of concepts, and they test it directly rather than assuming it from the workspace's other successes.

## What they studied
Three dense open models — Qwen3.6-27B (64 layers, width 5,120), Gemma-3-12B base (48 layers), Gemma-3-27B-IT (62 layers, the only instruction-tuned one) — under two readout tools (the J-lens and the R-lens, plus a matched J-lens fitted on the same corpus so the lens comparison is not confounded by provenance) and two estimators for a "role direction" (difference of means, and a readout-gradient estimator in the Linear Relational Embedding style). Unit of analysis: the residual stream **and** its decomposition into the workspace slice (top-16 J-lens atoms recovered by non-negative matching pursuit), the orthogonal remainder the lens discards, and a matched-rank random subspace that serves as the capacity control; plus fitted per-entity directions at a read layer near 75% depth (L48 / L36 / L46) and near the entity token or the sentence-final token.

## How they did it
A stimulus set of minimal pairs: three concept pairs (doctor/nurse, teacher/student, driver/passenger) crossed with four constructions (active-passive, cleft, relative clause, dative) and the four role × linear-position cells, giving 50 families per (pair, construction) cell and 600 families / 2,400 sentences per model; a six-pair version with half the families per cell keeps the item budget constant. Each family is read with one fixed probe, the probability of the target entity against its partner is turned into log-odds, and averaging the two surface positions inside a family cancels word order, so any surviving contrast is role. **(1) Decodability:** a closed-form ridge probe to ±1 role labels, leave-one-pair-out, run on all four activation sources, with a quadratic probe, a random-Fourier-feature probe and a seven-order ridge sweep as capacity controls and a second leave-one-pair-out cross-pair transfer test. **(2) Intervention:** fit a unit role direction per entity per site, push the residual by a calibrated coefficient with sign, and score a **binding score** — the difference of differences of the agent-minus-patient log-odds gap, averaged over a toward-agent and a toward-patient push, so a workspace that binds shrinks the natural gap from both sides while a bag of concepts leaves it flat. Controls share the push magnitude and mechanism but push the direction of an entity absent from the sentence, a seeded random vector, or a shuffled-label refit; the coefficient is calibrated on a disjoint corpus to a pre-registered 0.5-log-odds floor, re-verified at analysis time, and a failure produces an "uninterpretable strength failure" verdict rather than a null. **(3) Ablation:** zero the stream's projection onto the top-10 J-lens atoms during the forward pass and measure a binding-specific deficit — the binding margin drop as a share of its own baseline minus the same share for a recall control — against a fresh matched-rank random-subspace ablation, with a difficulty-matching precondition on the two tasks' baseline scores.

## What counts as an explanation here
The object under test is a *readout cheap enough to run as a monitor*, so the paper's answer is a bounded yes/no about a substrate rather than a description of a mechanism: the workspace carries a direction that steers what the model says about roles but not the representation the model uses to assign them. Formally the explanation they deliver is a **dissociation** — readout versus computation, where readout is judged by a signed, reversible shift in the model's own report and computation by decodability from the substrate and by causal necessity under ablation. It is deliberately partial and it is predictive in a narrow sense: the paper claims a workspace-built monitor will respond similarly to propositions that share a concept set and differ in binding, and that claim is testable on any new model by the three tests it defines.

## How they judged success
Four pre-registered verdicts replace a significance star: positive binding (significant pooled test, Cohen's d ≥ 0.5, pooled mean above a null band taken from the 95th percentile of the control scores, consistency across at least two of three person-role constructions, random-direction control inside the band); indistinguishable from controls; clean negative (not significant with a d-CI width under 1.0, read as evidence for the bag answer rather than absence of evidence); and uninterpretable strength failure. Statistics are at the family level — bootstrap CIs (10,000 resamples), a two-sided sign-flip permutation test that is exactly null because agent and patient labels are exchangeable within a family — with Holm-Bonferroni across constructions and pairs. Direction fits must clear 0.8 mean cosine over 200 bootstrap resamples before being used. Ablation claims need a deficit above a 0.05 pre-registered floor, above the random-subspace control, a CI excluding zero, matched edit magnitude, and the difficulty-match on baselines.

## What they found
Role is decodable from the full residual at 0.989 / 0.975 / 0.978 (Qwen / Gemma-12B / Gemma-27B-IT) and from the discarded remainder at 0.984 / 0.949 / 0.974 — but from the workspace slice only 0.587 / 0.573 / 0.574, at or below a matched-rank random subspace (0.643 / 0.589 / 0.590). Nonlinear probes, the ridge sweep (workspace ceiling 0.645 against 0.678–0.724 for the random control) and layer/seed variation do not close the gap. Steering separates cleanly into a real readout effect and no binding effect: the LRE direction is stable (mean cosine 0.967–0.999) and moves the model's own role report by +0.94 log-odds under agent-push and −0.99 under patient-push, while the binding score stays indistinguishable from controls on all three models — on Gemma-3-27B-IT the pooled effect grows more than tenfold as the coefficient goes from 64 to 256 (−0.0134 → −0.1809) but the non-participant control grows with it (ratio 0.83 / 1.01 / 0.84, pooled mean inside the null band throughout). Ablating the workspace damages binding on one model of three: +0.275 binding-specific deficit on Gemma-3-27B-IT at the entity token (CI 0.262–0.288), replicating across three lens variants (0.284 / 0.334 / 0.297), while Qwen and Gemma-3-12B show nothing at either site even where the workspace edit perturbs the residual about five times harder than the random control. Cross-pair transfer at the entity token gives every source above chance (residual 0.594–0.644, remainder 0.603–0.626, random 0.575–0.713) except the workspace (0.510–0.524), and at the sentence-final position every source *inverts* below chance — which the authors flag as a property of the probing setup, and which is why within-pair decoding is their primary test.

## Limits and open problems they name
The identity-swap strength check on the editing machinery failed on all three models (counterpart-probability shifts 0.0001–0.0048 against a 0.05 floor), so the steering null is compatible both with the direction being irrelevant to binding and with the edit being too weak to reach it — they propose patching in the opposite-role sentence's residual as the missing positive control. The single positive causal result is confounded between instruction tuning and scale, and a base Gemma-3-27B ablation would separate them. All tests are on dense English models, one layer each, agent-patient stimuli; coreference, temporal relations and multi-entity planning are untested, as is whether the dissociation is specific to the workspace-lens family or general to any verbalizable readout. Two of five seeds on Qwen are still outstanding.

## What we take from it
A **measure** and a **warning**, and the most useful item in the folder for anyone who wants to read a subspace and call it a monitor. The measure is the binding score: a two-sign difference of differences over natural pairs, averaged over two surface positions, with nulls from three strength-matched control directions and a verdict taxonomy in which a failed strength check is reported as uninterpretable rather than as a null. It costs nothing extra on top of any push experiment we already run and it directly prices the thing our own probe battery has never priced — whether a representation distinguishes *who* from *whom*, not just which concepts are present. The warning is sharper: the workspace slice decodes role *worse than a random subspace of the same rank*, so the correct control for a low-dimensional verbalizable readout is a rank-matched random projection, not a shuffled-label or ablation null; without it the workspace looks like it is doing something. Two operational consequences. First, when we project states into a vocabulary or concept basis we must run the same probe on the full stream, on the discarded remainder and on a matched random basis, because "the readable part is where the information is" is an assumption with a measured counterexample here. Second, a monitor whose target is a readout of the model's own report is measuring the report; our safety-facing claims should say which of the two they are about, and near-ceiling residual decodability is not evidence that a cheap readable slice carries the same information.
