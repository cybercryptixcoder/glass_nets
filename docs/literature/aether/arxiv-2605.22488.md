## What they wanted, and why

Mechanistic interpretation usually starts by asking whether a model's internals look like a proposed algorithm: does a stream carry a decodable quantity that the algorithm would need at that point? The authors argue that this question conflates three separate ones — can the model do the task, are the algorithm's quantities represented, and are those quantities the causal intermediates actually used — and that natural language and vision make the third hard to settle because nobody can write down the candidate algorithm precisely enough. They pick a synthetic arithmetic task where the input-output function is known in closed form, so a candidate internal algorithm can be stated exactly and then falsified. The larger goal is methodological: a case where decodability and causal use can be put in direct opposition and the opposition measured rather than warned about. The lesson they want to leave is not that probing is pointless but that a probe result is a hypothesis generator, and that the hypothesis has to be handed to an intervention study.

## What they studied

Base-digit extraction: the prompt gives a decimal integer N, a base B and a digit position D, and the model must emit the coefficient of B^D in the base-B expansion of N as a two-digit decimal string. The closed form suggests a staged computation — form B^D, divide N by it, take the floor, reduce modulo B — and the four quantities in that chain are the candidate intermediates. The models are 10-layer decoder-only Transformers (width 384, 12 heads, FFN 1536, 13-token context) trained from scratch in three seeds, plus a 5-layer companion in one seed. The unit of analysis is a named token position's residual stream across layers (number digits, base digits, the digit-position token, and the two output-prompt streams), so the objects are streams, attention routes between streams, and layer-edges of those routes — not individual neurons or heads.

## How they did it

Training covers N from 0 to 999, B from 2 to 30, and every in-range digit position plus one out-of-range query answered 0; each example is expanded across all six field-order permutations so the model must use field tags rather than fixed positions. Evaluation is strictly autoregressive and held out by *number–base intersection* — test prompts pair test N values with test B values, so the pairs were never seen — and exact-answer accuracy requires both digits. Step two is a bank of ordinary least-squares linear probes on frozen activations for the four closed-form targets, cross-validated within the pooled held-out set, with two controls: a regression of each target on the raw N, B and D scalars (to check that correlation alone does not explain the probes), and a re-instantiation of each seed's architecture at initialisation with the same split (to check whether decodability predates learning). Step three is a route-specific ablation: attention from the output-query rows to the digit-position token is killed by setting its pre-softmax logits to a large negative number, all other edges untouched, run as cumulative shallow-to-deep and deep-to-shallow sweeps to separate early from late dependence. Step four is key/value patching along that same route, first at layer 1 only, then at every layer, with donors differing from the source in exactly one of N, B or D and the same source set reused across the three donor conditions. Step five is a greedy right-to-left sparse circuit search over attention relations, scored fully autoregressively on validation, frozen, then evaluated on test, with a sweep over retention thresholds and a cross-seed intersection.

## What counts as an explanation here

A causal one, and deliberately partial: the explanation is the set of attention routes that carry the task-relevant information into the output streams, together with a content claim about what each route transmits, established by a matched donor swap. The closed-form staged algorithm is treated as a *candidate* explanation and rejected on evidence, not replaced with a complete account — the authors explicitly decline to decompose what the output streams do with the three factorised routes once they arrive, so their object is a sufficient circuit plus a dissociation, and they say outright that the search is not a proof of minimality or uniqueness. In causal-abstraction vocabulary they say the hypothesised intermediates are not faithfully implemented by the localised route, which is a claim of infidelity rather than a replacement abstraction.

## How they judged success

Held-out exact-answer accuracy on number–base intersections as the behavioural ceiling; probe fit as 5-fold cross-validated R² per stream and layer, with raw-scalar regression R² and initialisation-model R² as the two controls, and a training-added-gain figure measuring the fraction of the initialisation-to-ceiling R² gap closed. Causal strength comes from accuracy under cumulative route ablation (reported as seed means with percentile-bootstrap confidence intervals) and from source-exact versus donor-exact accuracy after patching, where the key comparison is across donor conditions rather than against a random donor. Circuit quality is reported as kept-only held-out accuracy alongside the retained fraction of candidate source-target relations and of candidate layer-edges, plus intersection-over-union of the retained relations across the three seeds and overlap across a three-by-three threshold sweep.

## What they found

The models solve the task on held-out intersections at essentially ceiling (mean 99.83% exact across three seeds, with one seed slightly below perfect). All four closed-form quantities are strongly decodable and, awkwardly for a training story, in the streams the staged algorithm would predict: B^D reaches R² = 1.00 in the digit-position stream already at layer 0, both quotient-like quantities reach about 0.96 in the same stream at layer 2, and the final answer quantity reaches about 0.94 in the late output stream. The raw-scalar control reaches only 0.25–0.50, so the streams genuinely add accessibility; but the initialisation control shows a large share of the accessibility is there before any training, with training mostly closing the last 2% of the gap for B^D and contributing more (roughly a third of the gap, peaking at layer 6) for the quotient-like quantities. Causally, the picture inverts: killing the output-to-digit-position read at layer 0 alone changes nothing, killing layers 0–1 drops accuracy to about 73%, and killing the whole route to about 32%; in the reverse sweep the deepest six layers can be removed with essentially no cost while including layer 1 collapses performance to the full-ablation level. Patching that route at layer 1 alone shifts source-exact accuracy down to 25% and donor-exact up to 68%, and full-route patching is sharply selective: donors differing only in N or only in B leave the model 100% source-exact, while donors differing only in D flip it to 0% source-exact and about 99.8% donor-exact. The sparse circuit that survives across seeds retains about 28% of candidate relations and 20% of layer-edges while reaching 93% held-out accuracy, is 17-of-18 stable across seeds and 93% stable across thresholds, and its structure is factorised: local N, B and D scaffolds that run mostly separately into the output streams, with no stable prompt-side relation joining them, plus one retained coupling between the two output positions.

## Limits and open problems they name

The central limit is stated plainly: the output-side computation is not decomposed, so the authors cannot rule out that the answer is formed by a structured arithmetic integration happening *after* the three routes converge — they claim only that the closed-form quantities are not the main causal intermediates carried by the routes they identified. The circuit search is sufficient, not minimal or unique, so a different search could in principle find other routes. The scale is one toy task on models trained from scratch at 10 layers, and generalisation is bounded to held-out number–base combinations within the trained numeric ranges; nothing here tests a pretrained language model. Layer-1 patching transfers only partially (donor-exact about 68% against near-ceiling for full-route patching), which the authors read as distributed control along the route rather than a single bottleneck, and therefore as a weaker localisation than an ablation alone would suggest.

## What we take from it

A **warning with a procedure attached**, which is the rarer of the two. The procedure is the one we should copy wholesale: a probe result is admissible only as a hypothesis, and the hypothesis is discharged by a three-donor swap along the route the probe implicates, with the control being the *same route and same sources* under a donor that differs in a different factor. That design turns "what does this component carry?" from a specificity claim into a content claim, and the 100% / 100% / 0% pattern is what a content claim looks like when it is true. Second, the **initialisation control on any decodability claim** is cheap and, here, devastating — a large part of the probe signal existed before training, so a white-box model that attributes decodability to learning needs the untrained instantiation measured against the same split. Third, the finding itself is a **correction for our own architecture choices**: our predictions must be stated as predictions about what a route *transmits*, not about what a stream *contains*, because the two came apart in the cleanest possible setting with an explicit known algorithm available. Fourth, a **measure**: kept-only accuracy reported alongside the retained fraction of relations and of layer-edges, so sparsity is never traded silently for performance.
