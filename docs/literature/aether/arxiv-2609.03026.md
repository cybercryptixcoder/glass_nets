## What they wanted, and why

Interpretability estimates now select actions — which steering direction to apply, which heads to ablate, which request to escalate — and the field grades those estimates on average accuracy. The author's claim is that this is the wrong object: an estimate that is accurate on average can still pick a poor action, because the decision uses only part of the estimate and weights errors by consequence. The paper builds a benchmark framework in which a model, an information boundary, an action set, a decision rule, a loss and held-out cases are all frozen by the task, so that an estimator is scored on both what it predicts and what its chosen action costs. The framing is explicitly operational rather than mechanistic: it does not ask how to recover a mechanism, it asks whether a proposed estimate is adequate for a particular intervention, control loop or safety policy. For us this is the missing evaluation layer around a predictive white-box model — we intend to emit predictions that guide interventions, and this is the first entry in the folder whose whole subject is that the prediction and the decision must be scored separately. The paper also names a companion paper of the author's (mechanistic tomography, arXiv:2608.19338) as the measurement-side counterpart, which is a useful pointer for a later run.

## What they studied

Three operational modes, each on its own surfaces. (i) **Closed-loop control**: twelve independently trained two-block Transformers on sequences with two binary features and a target that mixes an additive part with a `γ`-scaled product term, with edits injected into the residual after block 0 so every edit passes through a learned nonlinear suffix; plus an exact affine analysis. (ii) **Effect prediction**: GPT-2-small on indirect object identification (eight prompt templates, 64 names, separate name banks for reference/train/validation/test, the published primary/backup/negative name-mover head groups) and Qwen2.5-7B on an induction-copy task (three key–value pairs, one key repeated, distractors scored by a log-mean-exp margin), with an eight-head panel confirmed by held-out ablation. (iii) **Safety triage**: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on a non-executing authorization-interlock task (paired prompts granting scope to the requested resource versus elsewhere, consequence set by operation type), and the ControlArena APPS backdoor-audit setting (1,444 honest/backdoor pairs split by problem ID) replicated on Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B-it and Qwen3.5-9B. The **unit of analysis is the observer** — a function from declared measurements to a state estimate, an effect prediction, or a risk score — not a component, feature or circuit. Every comparison holds the controller, action set, budget and loss fixed and varies only what the observer reads and what it predicts.

## How they did it

Each task is written as a *TaskCard* fixing the target, the information and integrity boundary (what the observer may read, and who could alter, omit, delay or spoof it), the action space, the decision rule and loss, and the operating regime; each submission is an *ObserverCard* declaring what it reads, how it was fit, what access it needs, and — for control — whether it replaces the estimate, the direction, or both. To make the framework fair, the paper separates construction from evaluation, requires a *clean stop gate* (the unedited model must pass the base task before any intervention is scored), requires that the measurement design actually exercise the effect being compared, and requires that the task score the quantity used to choose the action rather than a convenient proxy. Two submission routes exist: a local implementation that may run the model, or a table of predictions for released cases, with held-out evaluator answers sealed behind a cryptographic hash so scoring is checked without disclosing labels. For control, the paper proves a reachable-subspace adequacy proposition for affine readouts (the observer must match at the starting state and along every direction the allowed interventions span) and derives an exact finite-horizon identity decomposing final tracking error into the observer's own remaining reported error, a starting-state mismatch, and a response-mismatch term scaled by the ratio of the target's and observer's responses along the edit direction. That certificate is then tested on the twelve trained Transformers with the nonlinear suffix left in place, varying response mismatch, initial bias and target offset. For effect prediction, four nested ridge designs (per-head additive, count-additive, capacity-matched pairwise term, all group-pair interactions) predict either a mask's mean ablation effect or its prompt-level absolute action loss, with the action frozen per pool and target before held-out effects are opened; the paper also derives an exact decomposition of action loss into distance of the predicted mean from the target plus a nonnegative dispersion penalty capturing prompt-to-prompt variation. For safety, the fixed policy blocks the top 10% and escalates the next 10% under a consequence-weighted loss, and monitors are compared as averaged over 2,000 resampled populations at attack rates from 1% to 50% and audit budgets from 0.5% to 10%. Throughout, controls are layered: text baselines, output-distribution readouts, neutral-context activations, exact oracles, exact no-op actions, and matched control heads chosen on attention specificity and output-norm distance rather than causal outcome.

## What counts as an explanation here

An **observer score plus a decision policy**, evaluated in the action space the score is meant to guide. There is no circuit, dictionary or description to be found — the explanatory object is an estimator that is adequate for a declared task, and adequacy is three separate properties the paper keeps apart: the observer must classify the state, rank it by consequence, and calibrate its scores across deployment groups. The paper is explicit that it takes the mechanism claims of prior work (the IOI circuit, induction heads, Hydra-style self-repair, copy suppression) as given and uses them only to define intervention surfaces, so it makes no new mechanism claim at all. Predictions are partial in a stronger sense than usual: they are claimed only for the declared masks, prompts, targets and budget, and the container ships versioned contract identifiers precisely so a scorecard from one contract cannot be silently compared with another.

## How they judged success

Two outcomes reported side by side for every observer: **estimation accuracy** (held-out MAE, R², rank correlation, AUROC) and **action performance** (absolute prompt-level action loss of the frozen selected mask, or mean and tail policy loss and realized violations under the audit controller), plus the access or measurement cost. Statistics are paired bootstrap intervals over the appropriate resampling unit — training seeds, name-pair clusters crossed with action pools, or prompts — with the paper noting explicitly that uncertainty is measured across the twelve trained models rather than across the many within-model conditions. Pre-registration is honestly qualified throughout: several analyses are described as pilot-informed and locally sealed, with the paper stating that a record of data-access order is not an independent preregistration; two runs were stopped before outcomes were inspected (one on a provenance-seal omission, one on a failed clean gate at 92.97% against a frozen 95% threshold) and restarted under fresh frozen protocols. Failed checks are reported rather than removed: the IOI pilot's clean gate failed (0.867 overall against 0.900), the exact no-op was absent from its action pools, and both are printed in the results table.

## What they found

**Control.** The affine certificate stays predictive through the learned nonlinear suffix: correlation 0.952 with actual final error, sign correct in 96.0% of conditions, residual MAE 16.5% of mean absolute actual error, and leave-one-seed-out MAE 0.063 against 0.302 for a bias-only model and 0.367 for an observer-pole-only model, winning on every held-out seed — while the suffix's own deviation from an affine prediction reaches 16.1% of output movement at the largest offsets. The interaction coordinate helps only where the interaction exists: integrated squared error 2.632 → 1.870 at `γ = 1.15` (11 of 12 seeds) and 1.693 → 1.662 at `γ = 0` (6 of 12). The mismatched estimator–direction cells at `γ = 1.15` fail loop conditioning in 10 of 12 models, so the gain is attributed to the pair, not to the estimator. **Prediction versus decision.** On GPT-2 IOI at 160 measurements, all-pairs interaction terms cut candidate-mask mean-effect MAE from 0.486 (per-head) and 0.473 (count-additive) to 0.395, yet the fixed-action reductions are 0.10% and 2.34% with intervals crossing zero, because the better-averaging masks also disperse more across prompts: even exact knowledge of each mask's test-set mean picks the lowest-loss mask in only 3 of 30 pool–target cells. Training the read-out on the realized action loss instead cuts loss from 1.076 to 0.833 (22.6%) at a worse mean match (0.550 versus 0.433) and a much lower dispersion term (0.283 versus 0.643); in the confirmation at a one-logit target it beats the same-feature mean observer by 18.5% (all eight templates) and exact no-op by 10.9% (six of eight) — but exact no-op's loss is exactly `t`, so at the smallest target the direct-loss observer is 46.2% *worse* than not acting at all. On Qwen2.5-7B the pattern transfers on a bounded prompt set: pairwise terms at 128 measurements cut MAE 0.121 → 0.040, direct loss cuts aggregate action loss 8.9% against natural mean and 49.6% against no-op, and ties natural mean at the largest target. **Safety.** In the authorization interlock a label observer with AUROC 1.000 scores 3.334 mean loss; multiplying by severity drops AUROC to 0.954 and loss to 2.155, closing 71.1% of the action-only-to-oracle gap — and the same observer has AUROC 1.000 inside every operation–format subgroup but allocates 63.2% of interventions to held-out deletes in seen forms and only 18.4% to the doubly held-out subgroup, where activation loss (5.007) is worse than action-only (3.059). Qwen's own block-minus-allow output-score difference times severity reaches 1.744 mean and 10.078 tail loss against an oracle at 1.740 and 10.039, i.e. the activation probe is unnecessary when that output is available. On APPS the best measurement context changes by model: neutral residual wins on Qwen2.5 (AUROC 0.979, exceeding verdict-adjacent by 0.026), verdict-adjacent wins on Gemma (0.969 against neutral 0.944) and decisively on Qwen3.5 (mean violations 1.976 against 6.601, verdict better in 99.15% of resampled populations). AUROC and realized violations also reorder monitors: on Gemma the detailed prompt has AUROC 0.893 against 0.870 for the official Gemma Scope SAE probe but 10.803 versus 9.070 mean violations; on Qwen3.5 the basic prompt has AUROC 0.876 against 0.868 for supervised text but 12.218 versus 9.054 violations. The official Qwen-Scope SAE probe trails a dense residual (12.725 versus 6.601, dense better in 99.65% of populations), and a post-outcome sparse-probing diagnostic keeps 127 coefficients instead of 3,584 but misses 4.67 attacks instead of 2.05.

## Limits and open problems they name

The paper lists scope and next-test pairs for each mode, and they are unusually candid. The control result assumes affine readouts, one fixed direction, proportional control and no clipping, and its nonlinear test is a small model whose underlying features are known by construction — so it does not establish token-level or large-model control. The pretrained-model studies use pinned models, selected head sets and narrow prompt distributions; the Qwen task keeps only eligible prompts (85.03% of fresh prompts) and the unfiltered model produces the desired next token only 24.45% of the time; the direct-loss observer ties the mean observer at the largest target. The authorization task asks the model directly for a verdict, so it cannot establish a uniquely hidden safety state, and its holding-out failure cannot be repaired by this split because calibration contains no held-out case. The APPS studies use one stored-backdoor task rather than attacks adapted to each monitor, all scoring is offline, the Gemma and Qwen3.5 runs were frozen after earlier outcomes were known, the official Qwen-Scope SAE was trained on a base checkpoint and applied to a post-trained one, and neither study tests deception or an adaptive attacker. Mine, marked: (a) the "prediction need not improve decisions" result is demonstrated mainly on one GPT-2 action surface where the competing observers differ by a handful of features, and the decomposition that explains it (Eq. 2) is exact arithmetic rather than an empirical finding — the finding is that the dispersion term is large, not that prediction and decision *can* diverge, which was known; (b) the safety budget result on a balanced 50%-attack panel cannot resolve the differences it claims to care about, and the paper says so, but the headline "activation observers lead on average" then rests on the rarest operating points where 29 audits are resampled — the same 29-audit cell the Appendix calls descriptive; (c) no figure was visually inspected — Figures 1–4 are the certificate scatter, the subgroup risk plots, the prevalence sweep and the family/quartile allocation panels, so every number here is a table cell or an explicit sentence, and the sweep results are recorded as the text states them; (d) the author is solo and independent, so there is no independent reproduction, and the several pilot-informed choices are sealed by the same person who ran them.

## What we take from it

Six things, and this is the most directly adoptable entry in the folder for our stated deliverable. (i) **Score the decision, not the estimate** — the folder's actionability entry argued this from outside interpretability; this is the first entry that *measures* the gap on interpretable objects, and the measured gap is large and one-signed: better mean-effect prediction moved action loss by ~0–2% while retargeting the same basis at realized loss moved it 18.5%. Any predictor we ship publishes both numbers or it publishes an intention. (ii) **Target the loss, not the mean** — the exact decomposition `R_t(m) = |μ(m) − t| + J_t(m)` is the cheapest diagnostic in this folder: it says why a better estimator loses, and it converts "our predictor is accurate" into "our predictor is accurate about the quantity the controller uses". (iii) **The no-op belongs in every action set.** Exact no-op's loss is exactly the target, so it turns an action-selection scorecard into a test of whether intervening was warranted at all — and the paper's own sign change (direct loss beats no-op at `t = 1` and `1.5`, loses at `t = 0.5`) is the cleanest statement of why "our method beats the baseline" without a null action is an incomplete claim. (iv) **Reachability narrows what a predictor must get right**: for a linear read-out, adequacy for control requires matching at the initial state and along the span of allowed edits, nothing else — so the correct denominator for a steering-related prediction is the edit subspace, not the whole activation space, and a wrong component outside it costs nothing. (v) **The four-question pre-flight** for any internal safety signal is a reusable checklist and generalises past safety: does the output already suffice, does the input already suffice, does the signal survive a neutral (unprompted) context, does it work at the intended rate and budget. Applied to this folder's probe results, several would fail at question two. (vi) **A warning about compression claims**: the sparse SAE readout used 127 coefficients instead of 3,584 and missed more than twice as many attacks — the folder's recurring "compression is not a decision" lesson with a safety cost attached, and a reminder that the disclosed activation-density mismatch (`L0` 115 observed against 240 reported) is the kind of distribution shift that makes an official feature family look worse than it is. **Why this item:** Priority block **0 unticked / 37** and `SEED_QUEUE.md` **0 unticked** (both re-measured this run), so the standing free-paper rule applies.
