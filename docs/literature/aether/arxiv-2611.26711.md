## What they wanted, and why

Causal abstraction defines equivalence between a network and an algorithm by asking whether replacing a high-level variable in the network's forward pass reproduces the algorithm's counterfactual output. The authors accept that matching under intervention is the field's working test of faithfulness, and ask whether it is also sufficient. Their answer is no: they construct a class of spurious features that pass the test cleanly while linking two directions the model never uses together. The larger goal is to stop the survey-level practice of treating a high intervention accuracy as a certificate, in a field where those accuracies are the main evidence that a named feature exists. For us the paper is the sharpest available statement of the question our own model has to answer explicitly: what, besides agreement under intervention, licenses a claim about what a network computes.

## What they studied

Language models only: pythia-1b for the constructed syntactic task, Llama-3.1-8b for two-digit addition, and GPT2-Medium and Qwen2-1.5b in an appendix replication. The unit of analysis is a direction in the residual stream at one layer and one token position, together with the alignment map that exposes it. Two directions are distinguished: a discriminative direction that separates hidden states by some input property, and a steering direction that moves the output class when activated. A feature is then a candidate node, defined on both its input side (which values it takes) and its output side (what it does to the answer), and faithfulness is a property of the whole triple - algorithm, network and map - rather than of the map alone.

## How they did it

The argument has a theoretical half and an empirical half. The theory states three assumptions at a fixed layer: a steering direction exists with a threshold, a discriminative direction exists with a threshold, and the separation between classes is large relative to the within-class spread along the discriminative direction. Under those, the authors construct, by hand, an invertible linear map whose first row reads the discriminative direction off the hidden state and whose inverse's first column writes a mixture of the discriminative and steering directions back in - so the source-to-base intervention transports a change in one concept into a change in the other, and the network reproduces the algorithm's output on every pair they test. A second theorem gives stricter conditions under which even a pure rotation suffices. Empirically they train distributed alignment search on two tasks, using a map class they call rotation+linear: an orthogonal map on the whole state followed by an invertible linear map on the first two coordinates only, intervening on the first. The first task is deliberately nonsensical - subject gender is made to license verb number, with masculine subjects given singular verbs and feminine subjects plural ones - and the second localises the carry-one variable in two-digit addition, a feature proposed in a mechanistic interpretability benchmark as a plausible faithful one. Alignment maps are fitted on minimal pairs the model answers correctly, with the network frozen. To diagnose parasitism they sweep the two-dimensional latent space, intervene on a randomly chosen test prompt to each grid point, and plot the output class at every location; a parasitic geometry shows the two input classes cleanly separated along one diagonal and the two output regions along the orthogonal diagonal. As a necessity test on Carry-One they ablate the discovered two-dimensional subspace and compare accuracy with ablations of random two-dimensional subspaces.

## What counts as an explanation here

A counterexample with an existence proof, not a tool. The positive objects are small: an alignment map, a direction pair, and a landscape. What the paper contributes to explanation is a classification - a found feature is faithful, or parasitic, or unfound - and a geometric signature (orthogonal discriminative and steering directions, with the data sitting outside the region where the model's own answers live) that tells the first two apart. The explanation is explicitly incomplete in the direction the authors care about: they note that finding a parasite does not show the hypothesised feature is absent, only that this map did not find it, and that a nonlinearly encoded copy could still exist.

## How they judged success

Intervention efficacy: odds for the syntactic task (how much more probable the intervened label becomes, in the style of the causal-benchmark literature) and exact-match intervention accuracy for addition, both held out. Geometry: the recovered class labels over a swept grid of the two-dimensional latent. Necessity: accuracy of the full addition task with the discovered subspace ablated against random-subspace ablations, with a stated number of random trials and a spread. Replication in other models by the same odds metric per layer. No faithfulness or completeness score in the circuit sense, no human rating, no comparison with a shuffled or random alignment map as a null on the efficacy metric itself.

## What they found

The spurious feature aligns and it aligns strongly. On the nonsensical agreement task, the rotation+linear map on pythia-1b gives substantial odds across layers where the pure rotation does not, so the constructed dependency is reproduced by a map with only minimal extra expressivity. On Carry-One, Llama-3.1-8b aligns the carry-one variable at layer 20 with substantially higher accuracy than the rotation map; ablating the discovered two-dimensional subspace leaves the task at 98.8% against 99.1% with no ablation, and random two-dimensional ablations over ten trials give 99.12% plus or minus 0.13, so the target behaviour is sufficient for the intervention but not necessary to the computation. The intervention landscapes are the paper's clearest object: in both parasitic tasks the two input classes sit outside the output regions entirely, separated along a diagonal, with the steering regions along the orthogonal diagonal, while a control trained on genuine subject-verb number agreement (layer 2, odds 4.3) places prompts inside their own output regions and gives a non-orthogonal geometry - so the diagnostic is showing a real difference in layout, not an artefact of plotting. Appendix A adds two more parasites: the same carry-one direction steers by every offset from one to nine (each aligned at 96.6% or better), and in a sentiment task the noun's topic (food or entertainment) is made to steer sentiment, reaching odds 3.6 at layer 22 on held-out across-class pairs, which the authors describe as the target label being 37 times more probable relative to the other. Appendix E replicates the agreement task on GPT2-Medium and Qwen2-1.5b, where rotation+linear beats rotation in Qwen2-1.5b while the two are roughly equal in GPT2-Medium - a null the authors state plainly and decline to explain, offering both "rotation already finds parasites here" and "parasitism emerges with scale" as open possibilities.

## Limits and open problems they name

The independence of the two directions is left informal: they propose data-level independence (conceptually unrelated inputs) and subspace-level independence (the two directions do not interact in the computation) as sufficient conditions, and say explicitly that they have not shown necessity. The necessity test is weak by the authors' own admission, because networks compensate for ablated components, so a small accuracy drop is evidence rather than proof that the subspace is unused; and no comparable ablation is possible for the constructed task, where the model never performs the task in the first place. The oscillatory pattern in appendix replication - equal performance in GPT2-Medium - is unexplained. The authors also concede that their most dramatic example, the nonsensical agreement task, is one no researcher would have hypothesised, so it demonstrates pervasiveness rather than harm; the harm case is Carry-One, where the variable was proposed as real. They extend the worry only speculatively to probes and sparse autoencoders, without testing either.

## What we take from it

Three concrete things. First, a construction rule: for any direction our white-box model nominates, record separately the direction that *separates* the inputs and the direction that *moves* the output. If those two are near-orthogonal and the observed states lie outside the region where the model's own answers live, the nomination is a candidate parasite - a pathway between two concepts that the network may never combine - and it must not enter the model as a mechanism until something rules that out. The paper's swept-landscape plot is a cheap way to produce this check: intervene to a grid over the exposed coordinates and print the class the model emits at each point, then compare the layout against one you get for a feature you believe is real. Second, the ablation-with-random-control arm as a necessity test, reported as an accuracy triple (full, discovered subspace ablated, random subspace ablated with its spread and trial count) - the shape of statement we should use whenever we claim a subspace is carrying a computation. Third, the budget warning: this failure was found with a map only marginally more expressive than rotation, which is the cheapest map the field uses, so "we only used a linear probe" is not a defence. The corresponding discipline for our reports is that an intervention efficacy number never stands alone in a row; it is printed next to the geometry that says whether the read and write directions coincide, and next to the necessity test where one is possible.
