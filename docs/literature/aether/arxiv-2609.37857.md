# Active Budget Can Kill Sensitivity: Diagnosing and Repairing TopK Sparse Autoencoder Reliability

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.37857
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhenting Huang and Bo Jiang (Beihang University), Junnan Liu (Monash University), Zhixing Tan and Qianren Mao (Zhongguancun Laboratory)
- **Date:** 2026-09-29
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37857
- **Kind:** paper (arXiv preprint, v2; cs.AI; arXiv perpetual non-exclusive license)
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) - a reliability failure that appears at the exact place our own instrumentation is thinnest: the boundary of a hard top-k selection, reported with its own geometry-derived diagnostic.

## What they wanted, and why
Sparse autoencoders are used as the unit of analysis for interpretation, so a dictionary entry only earns its role if it stays available when the same meaning arrives in different wording. The authors ask whether that stability holds as dictionaries are widened, because width is the reason people scale these models and the extra features it buys are the rare ones. Their framing is deliberately usable rather than semantic: a feature that looks coherent on its top activating examples and vanishes under a paraphrase cannot support an audit, a circuit analysis or a steering experiment built on it. The larger goal is a reliability metric that sits beside reconstruction, sparsity and alive-feature count when an SAE is evaluated, and a repair that follows from a diagnosis rather than from regularising cosine similarity and hoping.

## What they studied
TopK sparse autoencoders on the residual stream of GPT-2 small, primary setting layer 8 on OpenWebText, 64-token contexts, 10M token activation budget with 200k and 1M robustness checks, two main widths (3072 with an active budget of 32, and 12288 with a budget of 128) over five training seeds each. Scope checks retrain width-12288, budget-128 SAEs on other GPT-2 layers and fit the same recipe on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B. The unit of analysis is the **(active feature instance, source text, paraphrase)** triple inside a frequency bucket, where buckets come from a separate frequency-estimation partition (rare between 0.005 and 0.05 activation frequency, medium between 0.05 and 0.5, common at or above 0.5). The statistical unit for every mean is the independently trained seed.

## How they did it
Paraphrase pairs are generated with GPT-4o at temperature 0.7 and manually reviewed for meaning preservation; the GPT-2 collection starts from 150 source texts with two paraphrases each and keeps 295 pairs, split by original source text into a validation collection used for comparison and hyperparameter choice and a held-out collection that played no part in either, so the confirmatory number is forward-only. Activations and pairs are partitioned by source text with no source appearing twice. Feature sensitivity is the probability that a feature active on a source text is still active on its paraphrase, and two dropout sources are counted separately: a feature can fall out of the top-k set (a ranking event) or stay in it with a non-positive score and be zeroed by the ReLU (a magnitude event). The authors then run a 3x3 factorial over width (3072, 6144, 12288) and active budget (32, 64, 128) with three seeds per cell, towing everything else fixed, so that the practical scaling path - which raises both at once - is decomposed into a width marginal and a budget marginal. The diagnosis is geometric: the top-k margin is the gap between the k-th and (k+1)-th encoder scores, a feature's active margin is its own score above that cutoff, and margins are used (i) as an AUROC predictor of paraphrase dropout, (ii) as bins over which flip rate is tabulated, and (iii) as the negative-sampling target for a training loss. The repair adds a pairwise ranking term to the reconstruction objective: for each source-active feature, the paraphrase-side competitors ranked just below the cutoff (a band of size R, aggregated by a centred log-sum-exp at temperature 1) must fall below that feature by a target margin, with weight lambda against reconstruction. Eight control objectives are run at the same scale, from generic paraphrase consistency and contrastive alignment to several ways of simply pushing the global top-k gap open. Selection uses the validation collection (lambda 0.004, R 8 chosen from a 4x4 sweep), and the frozen configuration is then scored once on the held-out collection.

## What counts as an explanation here
Nothing here is a semantic claim about what a feature means. The object explained is **why a latent disappears**, and the explanation is a geometric one about the selector rather than about the representation: the feature dropped because a paraphrase perturbation reordered neighbours near a hard cutoff, not because the meaning left the activations. The paper is explicit that sensitivity is a reliability probe and not a semantic oracle, that a low-frequency feature may legitimately encode lexical or syntactic detail that a paraphrase is designed to change, and that activation reliability is a prerequisite for interpretation rather than a substitute for it. The repair is correspondingly a claim about the training objective's blind spot, not a claim that the recovered feature is more meaningful.

## How they judged success
Feature sensitivity by frequency bucket, with standard error over seeds, two-sided Welch tests and Cohen's d where both arms have at least two seeds; near-cutoff share (fraction of examples whose top-k margin falls below 0.01), reported as a crowding indicator; reconstruction MSE; alive-feature coverage (percentage of dictionary entries active on at least one vector); AUROC of the active margin against the paraphrase-dropout label, per layer; flip rate by margin bin with instance counts; and, for the repair, both an in-family comparison on the validation collection and a forward-only evaluation on the held-out collection. Two SAE-internal usability proxies are added - true-retention of source-active cases on the real paraphrase and effect retention (the paraphrase/source ratio of feature contribution mass) - plus a specificity audit checking that the repair does not simply make rare features broadly active. A qualitative audit set of 49 rare drop cases and 20 retained comparisons is read by hand.

## What they found
Rare-feature sensitivity falls along the practical scaling path - 0.650 at width 3072 against 0.585 at width 12288 on GPT-2 layer 8 - while common features stay near ceiling (0.998 to 0.956), and the drop reproduces at every token budget trained (0.802 to 0.793 at 200k tokens, 0.779 to 0.748 at 1M). The factorial assigns the cause to the active budget and not to width: rare sensitivity falls monotonically 0.645, 0.591, 0.497 across budgets 32, 64, 128 (a 14.8 percentage point span), while the width marginals span about 1.9 points, and reconstruction moves the opposite way over the same budget range (MSE 1.35 to 0.89). Crowding tracks the budget: near-cutoff share rises 10% to 27% to 54% with k and stays flat with width, and reaches 54.0% at the widest practical setting against 10.3% at the narrowest. Margin alone predicts which features go: AUROC 0.728 for paraphrase dropout on layer 8, holding between 0.747 and 0.850 across six other GPT-2 depths and declining smoothly with depth, with flip rate falling from 94.1% in the bin nearest the cutoff to 0.6% in the farthest, on bins of roughly 4,700 instances each (the printed counts are 4719, 4719, 4721, 4719, 4719, 4720, 4719, 4720). On the repair, generic agreement training does not solve it: paraphrase consistency reaches 0.655 and contrastive alignment actually falls to 0.505, and direct global margin pressure lowers the near-cutoff share to 26.8% at the cost of MSE rising from 0.880 to 3.584 and alive coverage collapsing from 96.70% to 73.21%. Pairwise rank stabilization gains 12.3 points on validation (0.585 to 0.707) with MSE 0.888 and alive coverage 96.66%, and on the held-out collection 1712 pairs it gains 8.83 points (0.467 to 0.555), with every one of the sixteen lambda-by-R cells improving over baseline. The gain is not achieved by clearing the boundary: near-cutoff share is essentially unchanged at 54.4% against 54.0%, and what changes is which features are kept. Transfer is partial and the authors report it as such - Qwen with a crowded 74.4% boundary gains 1.7 points, Gemma with a sparse 18.6% boundary is neutral at minus 0.8 points, layer 10 gains 5.2, and the near-cutoff share is presented as the room a boundary repair has to work in.

## Limits and open problems they name
The evidence concerns activation reliability only: whether a dropped feature represents a lost human-interpretable concept, and whether the gains improve any downstream interpretation, are both left open. The buckets are a diagnostic frequency partition and not a semantic taxonomy. The near-cutoff threshold of 0.01 is an operational cut on each model's own score scale, so the cross-model readings are within-model shares rather than absolute comparisons. The qualitative audit deliberately scores activation by the post-ReLU sign rather than top-k membership, which means a row in it records a selection-boundary event rather than a confirmed loss of an active feature. Global margin pressure is included only as a mechanistic reference point that prices the boundary move, not as a proposed method. Threshold-selector repair is left as a separate design question even though the margin diagnostic is shown to hold on a JumpReLU dictionary. The corpus is 150 source texts' worth of paraphrase pairs and five seeds per cell, and the main claims live at one layer of one small model.

## What we take from it
Four things. (1) **Selection boundaries are a reliability surface, and they produce failures that look like content failures**: a hard top-k cutoff with a crowded margin turns small representation shifts into discrete identity changes, so any probe or feature panel we build with a fixed-width selector inherits a failure mode that has nothing to do with meaning. Their tell is cheap and we should adopt it: report the share of items sitting within a small fraction of the cutoff, because that share is the amount of our instrumentation resting on a coin flip, and it rises mechanically as we scale the thing we are scaling. (2) **A geometry-derived signal beats a tuned threshold for predicting failure**: their margin ranks feature instances and predicts dropout by AUROC without any per-model scale, and the statistic survives depth changes, model changes and even a different sparsity mechanism. Our version: whenever we suspect a probe is measuring a boundary artefact, build a scalar distance-to-boundary from the mechanism itself, report its AUROC against the failure label, and check the AUROC across layers before believing the failure is about the representation. (3) **The fix belongs at the crossing that fails, not at the margin in general**: paraphrase consistency raised active-set agreement and left rare sensitivity largely where it was (0.655 against 0.585 baseline, with agreement as the thing it optimised); global margin pressure moved the statistic it targeted and destroyed reconstruction and coverage; the objective that worked penalised the specific wrong ordering and left the boundary just as crowded. Reporting discipline follows: state which quantity the intervention was optimised against and whether the gain came from moving the aggregate or from changing which cases survive it. (4) **Two scoring conventions for one event is a reporting mistake they avoid and we should copy**: they score sensitivity by post-ReLU sign, and they say outright that top-k membership is a weaker criterion near the boundary, because a feature can enter the selected set with a non-positive score and was never active. A dropout attributed to ranking when it was actually a magnitude event would point the repair at the wrong place - and the paper's own qualitative audit shows how easy that slip is, since a substantial share of its top-k drop candidates had non-positive source scores and were never active.
