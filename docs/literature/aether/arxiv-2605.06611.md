# The Structural Origin of Attention Sink: Variance Discrepancy, Super Neurons, and Dimension Disparity

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.06611
- **Authors / organisation:** Siquan Li, Kaiqi Jiang, Jiacheng Sun, Tianyang Hu; CUHK-Shenzhen and Huawei Foundation Model Department
- **Date:** 2026-05-07
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06611
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, ~59k chars clean; abstract, §§1-6, Appendices A-D, Tables 1-4 as cells; figures 1-23 read from captions and the prose describing them, not inspected as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Attention sinks - a tiny set of tokens, almost always position 0, taking a wildly disproportionate share of attention scores in decoder transformers - are observed everywhere but the literature explained their *function* (a place to park surplus Softmax mass, a cheap way for a head to do nothing) rather than their *cause*. The authors want to know why position 0 is the one that gets picked, given that LayerNorm, residuals and the FFN all act identically at every position. Their answer is that causal masking makes position 0 structurally unlike every other token, and that this asymmetry is then amplified by weights into a geometry that forces the attention to lock there. The larger goal is controllability: if the sink is an architectural consequence of value aggregation rather than an inevitable product of scale, then a small architectural change should be able to prevent it - and the paper closes by proposing and testing one.

## What they studied

Llama-2-7B as the main dissection subject, Llama-3-8B (grouped-query attention) for replication, both on WikiText-2 and on sequences of fully random tokens. Two more open models are checked in an appendix. The unit of analysis runs across the whole stack at once: per-position variance vectors over hidden dimensions, individual weight-matrix columns and rows (a specific FFN neuron index in Llama-2 layer 1 is followed by hand), singular directions of the query projection per head, and layer-wise statistics of the whole hidden state (effective rank, dominance ratio). Pre-training experiments use three 152M-parameter models (hidden 768, 12 layers, 12 heads, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, RoPE) trained for 40k iterations on OpenWebText.

## How they did it

The argument is a four-link chain, each link measured separately. First, they note that under a causal mask position 0 attends only to itself, so its value vector is never averaged with anything else, while later positions average over a growing set - and they measure dimension-wise variance of hidden states right after layer-1 value aggregation and find position 0 far above the rest, with a sharp decay down the sequence. Doing this on random tokens avoids the trivial zero that a fixed BOS token would produce. Second, they impose that state on arbitrary positions two ways: a mask edit that stops token *k* from attending to any earlier position, and a mean-centred scaling of one token's aggregated output by a factor lambda; a control scales the vector by the same factor without centring, which does *not* produce a sink. Third, they trace the outlier through the weights: rank correlation between each output-projection column and the first token's per-dimension variance; then the FFN gate and up columns whose L2 norms are extreme ("super neurons"), one of which they follow at layer 1 by cosine alignment and raw activation per position; then the heavy-tailed row of the down-projection that receives that activation and funnels it into a few dimensions; then the dominance ratio of the resulting first-token vector, and what RMSNorm does to a vector with one dominant coordinate. Fourth, they read the sink end: for each head in the next layer, the angle between the sink key and the principal singular direction of the query matrix, plotted against the fraction of positions whose query actually scores positive against that key. Finally they re-train from scratch with the offending variance removed two ways - unnormalised sigmoid attention, and their own head-wise RMSNorm placed just after value aggregation and before the output projection - and compare loss, effective rank and dominance ratio.

## What counts as an explanation here

A causal-mechanistic chain expressed as a propagation story across named weight objects: an architectural asymmetry (causal mask) produces a statistical asymmetry (variance), which the output projection preserves, which a small set of large-norm FFN neurons detect and amplify, which a sparse down-projection channels into one or two dimensions, which collapses the post-RMSNorm direction of the first token to a basis vector, which a set of heads' queries align with. It is a full-path explanation rather than a circuit, and it is explicitly partial: they say prior work covers *what the sink is for* and they are supplying *why it lands at zero*. It is predictive in one direction - the chain says which weights to look at (large-norm gate columns, heavy-tailed down rows, aligned query directions) and they find them there - and the interventions it names both create sinks at chosen positions and suppress them during training.

## How they judged success

Sink presence is measured as the average attention mass the first token receives per layer. Variance discrepancy is dimension-wise standard deviation after layer-1 value aggregation, averaged per position over a batch. The causal interventions are graded visually by whether the attacked position becomes the top-attended token, with the norm-scaling control as the discriminator. Propagation is graded by Kendall's rank correlation between absolute output-projection weights and input variance across output neurons, by cosine similarity and raw up-projection activation for a single named neuron, by the skew of that neuron's down-projection row, and by the ratio of the largest absolute coordinate to the mean absolute coordinate of the first-token vector. Sink locking is graded by an SVD alignment between the sink key and the query matrix's leading singular vector, plotted against the fraction of positive query-key scores per head. The architectural fix is judged by validation and training loss and by effective rank and dominance ratio averaged across layers, over four random seeds.

## What they found

The sink and the first token's representation norm spike at the same layer (about layer 2 of Llama-2), and both replicate on Llama-3-8B. Blocking aggregation at index 10 turns that token into the new sink; mean-centred scaling of one token's output creates a sink that grows with the scale factor, while plain norm scaling of the same magnitude does not. The mean Kendall correlation between output-projection columns and first-token input variance is 0.32, i.e. the projection is biased to carry the outlier forward. Layer-1 FFN gate and up columns have a small set of hugely larger-norm neurons (they name index 7890); that neuron shows both high cosine alignment and large raw activation for the first token and essentially nothing for the rest. Its down-projection row is heavy-tailed and channels the activation into one coordinate (they name 2533). After layer-2 RMSNorm the first token's outlier coordinate is 1.2568 against a mean absolute coordinate of 0.0048, a dominance ratio of 262.88, so the normalised vector is effectively a basis vector; heads whose sink key aligns with the query matrix's dominant direction show a near-100% positive-score ratio. In the 152M pre-training comparison, head-wise RMSNorm over four seeds gives validation loss 2.7421 against 2.7812, effective rank 445.96 against 343.71, and dominance ratio 33.74 against 82.67; unnormalised sigmoid attention suppresses the sink but converges worse than the Softmax baseline. Effective rank in Llama-2-7B and Llama-3-8B drops steeply in the first few blocks and stays low, which they call shallow-layer manifold collapse.

## Limits and open problems they name

Pre-training evidence comes from 152M models only, and confirming head-wise RMSNorm at billion-parameter scale is stated as necessary future work; mixture-of-experts is named as untested. The mechanistic dissection is on two Llama-family open-weight models plus a short appendix check, and much of the layer-1 evidence is a handful of hand-followed neuron and dimension indices. The sink is not eliminated, only the variance discrepancy that drives it; the authors also concede that other normalisation methods may already be helping for partly unrelated reasons. Sink measurements are attention mass on WikiText-2 and random tokens, not behaviour under long contexts or after instruction tuning.

## What we take from it

A warning plus a reusable lever. The warning: this paper traces *why* a dominant direction exists all the way back to an architectural asymmetry, and it shows that the geometry every probe in this folder reads (a first token that normalises to almost a basis vector after one layer) is created by three specific weight objects acting in sequence - a projection column correlated with input variance, a handful of large-norm gate columns, and a heavy-tailed down-projection row. A white-box model that treats the residual stream as a passive carrier will mispredict interventions on any of these, because removing the sink's individual contributor does not remove the outlier if the chain survives upstream. The lever: their two interventions are the cheapest known way to *manufacture* a controlled outlier at a chosen position (mask-edit and mean-centred rescaling), which is exactly the manipulation a predictive model should be tested against - a claim about what a representation position means should predict what happens when you are handed an artificial sink there. Their control is the transferable methodological point: the same-magnitude norm scaling fails to produce the sink, so a positive result about a variance statistic is only meaningful against a norm-matched null. Cross-reference run 705 (the spectral account of the same phenomenon, where the outlier is a low-rank spike above a random-matrix edge and the gate is a contraction on that mode), run 704 (the same sink read as an over-mixing brake with a path-product bound), and run 696 (a per-unit score is not a per-unit worth).
