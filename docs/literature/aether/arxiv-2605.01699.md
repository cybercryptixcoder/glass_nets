# Probe-Geometry Alignment: Auditing and Suppressing Representational Memorization in Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.01699
- **Authors / organisation:** Anamika Paul Rupa (Morgan State University, Department of Information Science and Systems); Anietie Andy (Howard University, Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science)
- **Date:** 2026-05-03 (v4 2026-09-21)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01699
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Unlearning is graded on what a model says: prompt it with the first words of a passage and if it stops completing the passage, the passage is reported as deleted. The authors' starting observation is that this test can pass while the activations still carry the content, and that the failures other people have reported (content returning after benign fine-tuning, after quantisation, or on paraphrases) establish that something survived without saying what survived or where. They want an audit a third party can run from weights and activations alone, and then they want to use that same measurement as the objective of a suppression method. The larger goal is a deletion claim backed by a number the claimant did not get to choose.

## What they studied
Four models: Pythia-70M (7 and 8 depth), a randomly initialised Pythia-70M as an architecture control, GPT-2 Medium, and Mistral-7B (7.24B). Size scaling is checked inside the Pythia family at 70M, 160M, 410M and 1B. Two controlled testbeds are also used: a four-layer byte-level transformer (810,496 parameters, vocabulary 43, sequence length 64) trained to recite one injected sentence, and a second model of the same shape trained on nine memorised and nine control prefixes so leave-one-out has held-out keys. The unit of analysis is a **direction in the residual stream at a given depth**, fitted by linear probe, plus the scalar projection of the memorised-minus-control activation difference onto it.

## How they did it
Each source text (software licences, web boilerplate, code) gets a memorised pool that passes a per-model log-probability screen, a **register-matched paraphrase** control built by swapping distinctive terms while keeping style, and a second further paraphrase used as a pure-distinguishability control. Logistic regression is fitted at every depth on all keys but one and tested on the held-out key, over 24 neutral context prefixes so there is within-class variance. Two nulls calibrate the accuracy: a shuffled-label permutation, and the harder control of telling the two paraphrase pools apart (two things that are both not memorised). The **memorisation-specific gap** is the difference of those two accuracies, averaged over transformer depths and excluding the embedding. A corpus-lookup pool of Pile documents replaces likelihood screening where the training corpus is public. To test whether the read direction is the recall machinery, they project the fitted probe direction out of the residual stream at one depth and re-measure both the gap and log-probability on the memorised text. To test what existing erasure methods reach, they run five published objectives, a weight edit (MEMIT), and distillation from a teacher that never memorised, on the testbed and then on Pythia-70M through one shared scoring pipeline. Their own method (PGA) adds a penalty on the scalar projection of the memorised-versus-control difference onto the current probe direction, one scalar per aligned depth, refitting the direction every 25 steps on Pythia, with a clean-text cross-entropy anchor; an isotropic arm with an identical recipe, layers, seeds and budget but a full-dimensional squared-distance penalty is the ablation; and an eight-family attacker suite with free choice of regularisation, seed, sign and pooling is the adaptive test.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **direction** plus a stated protocol: the claim is not what the model is doing but what a linearly-read feature generalises across sequences. The object shared by the audit and the method is the per-depth readout direction, so "explanation" here means a live read-out that both measures retention and is the thing the fix edits. It is emphatically partial — the authors say the instrument reports only what an auditor can compute, i.e. linear decodability, and they pair it with an explicit warning that a readout can be *reversed* rather than emptied. There is no circuit, no dictionary and no prediction of behaviour; the causal claim is confined to a projection experiment at a single depth.

## How they judged success
Leave-one-out accuracy at each depth against two nulls (shuffled labels; pure distinguishability), orientation-corrected accuracy (max of acc and one minus acc, as used for a probe whose sign is not fixed) with a 200-permutation null and its 95th percentile reported rather than 0.5, jackknife and bootstrap intervals over sequences and neutral contexts, suppression measured in nats of mean log-probability per token with the corresponding **held-out cost in nats** and their ratio, held-out perplexity and capability retention against the base model, five zero-shot benchmarks, and an eight-family adaptive-probe suite. Permutation null, random initialisation, register control, vocabulary-matched control and content-type control are the named controls.

## What they found
The gap is positive on every architecture: transformer-layer means of +0.347 on Pythia-70M, +0.191 on GPT-2 Medium and +0.297 on Mistral-7B (peaks +0.537, +0.449, +0.471). Within the Pythia family it is flat, +0.323 / +0.278 / +0.334 / +0.274 at 70M to 1B, sitting 8.0, 6.5, 8.2 and 5.1 standard deviations above each model's own shuffled null, and the peak migrates in depth so it is not architectural. On the corpus-verified Pile pool the corrected gap peaks at +0.413 with true accuracy 0.965, 10.5 standard deviations above the permutation null, and all thirty leave-one-out values are positive. Projecting the probe direction out at depth four collapses the local gap from +0.643 to +0.161 while recall falls by at most 0.116 nats and held-out log-probability moves 0.025 nats — but the signature partly rebuilds at later depths, which is the stated reason the method constrains several depths at once. No behavioural erasure reaches the encoding: on the testbed, a head-localised edit that drives recall to 1.3e-4 while touching 0.76% of parameters leaves the probe at 1.000; all five published methods leave it at 1.000; MEMIT leaves it at 1.000 at 8.4e-5 recall; and distillation from a never-memorised teacher drives recall to 2.6e-5 at held-out perplexity 1.405 against 1.403 yet reads 0.922 where the teacher reads 0.661. On Pythia-70M, PGA suppresses 3.25 nats at 0.055 nats held-out cost (59.4 nats suppressed per nat lost) against IDK's 1.05 nats at 0.794 retention and RMU's 0.02 nats at 0.670, while GA's 62.22 nats comes at 0.019 retention. The isotropic ablation reached 8.96 at best, giving PGA a 6.6x advantage over the best setting and 19.5x over the arm trained at the same weight. Last-token PGA failed to generalise across the pooling axis (a mean-pooled attacker read 0.979); multi-position PGA drove that attacker to 0.548-0.568 at mid depths at 4.84 nats of suppression, but the strongest non-linear family still read 0.789 and the deepest depth returned to 0.929. At Mistral scale, 2.63 nats at 0.72 nats cost (3.6 per nat).

## Limits and open problems they name
The instrument measures linear decodability only, on licence texts and web boilerplate, and stops at 7B; the authors say extending it needs new pools and larger models. Pairing the audit with extraction measurements to get a risk estimate is future work. The multi-position result is scoped to the linear attacker at mid depths. They also flag that the isotropic cost is non-monotone in penalty weight, so the margin cannot be extrapolated past the weights tested. A control that they report rather than bury: a randomly initialised model already produces a gap of +0.188 against the pretrained +0.323, so roughly three fifths of the effect is architecture and only the depth profile separates them.

## What we take from it
Three reusable things. **(1) A discount null.** The gap is not a probe accuracy; it is that accuracy minus the accuracy of the same probe on a pair that is *not* the target contrast (two paraphrases, both not memorised). Any probe in our white-box model that claims "the model represents X" should report its contrast against the nearest matched non-X contrast of the same surface form, because on GPT-2 the nuisance separability alone reaches roughly 0.7 and on Mistral roughly 0.8. **(2) The sign trap.** A constrained readout can invert instead of empty: trained PGA put every held-out sequence on the wrong side, so raw accuracy read 0.222 while the corrected reading was 0.778, and of ten audited configurations only the three trained settings changed verdict. Any erasure or suppression claim of ours audited with a probe must publish corrected accuracy and the direction of the change, or it certifies deletion that did not happen. **(3) The axis of failure names the repair.** The attacker's pooling scheme was the axis the alignment never constrained, and extending the constraint along that axis was what worked — a template for diagnosing why a steering method does not transfer: find the axis of the input the fitted object held fixed, then attack along it.
