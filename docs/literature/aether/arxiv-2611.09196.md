- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Interpretability has drifted from reading a model's own neurons to reading a second model trained on the first — a probe, an SAE, a crosscoder. The author concedes the drift answered real faults (mixed meanings, thresholds with no unit, readings that do not travel between studies) but argues it gave up three things only a direct reading provides: nothing trained in between, the model's own units named so they can then be removed, and repeatability across stimuli, checkpoints and architectures. The goal is not to win that argument but to make the direct reading survive it: to turn "which neurons respond to concept X" into a measurement with a declared population, a stated unit, a pre-use licence and a measured resolution. The larger aim is a reading that can be *compared* — between two constructs, two checkpoints, two models of one architecture — which none of the existing neuron lists (knowledge neurons, language neurons, universal neurons) can support because each is a single-checkpoint number with an arbitrary threshold.

## What they studied

OpenCoder-1.5B (24 layers, MLP hidden width 6,144, SwiGLU), one MLP block's neurons at one read position. The unit of analysis is a **neuron** — the product of the gate branch after the nonlinearity and the up branch, taken before the down-projection, following the key-value-memory reading of feed-forward layers. The whole space of 24 × 6,144 = 147,456 neurons, which the paper calls the product space, is the frame in which all comparisons are made. Constructs are the entries of Python 3.12's grammar that appear as text plus every public builtin — 206 in the inventory, 188 actually built, 169 passing the read-site gate. Stimuli are machine-generated, not hand-written and not sampled from real text. 61 model references were recorded: 35 pretraining checkpoints, 24 annealing checkpoints, 2 untrained, spanning 4.19 billion to 2.03 trillion training tokens.

## How they did it

Six steps, each closing a question the last one leaves open. (1) **Declare the population.** Write down every factor around the target construct — identifiers and literals, scope and nesting, companion constructs, length and surrounding code — and draw each at its rate in a reference corpus of 253,398 parsed Python files, logging every draw. Nothing in a prompt is chosen for the reading. Three sets are drawn: A holds the target in its grammatical role; B holds the target's token *out* of role (in a docstring, a comment, a string, an identifier, or in a sibling construct's role) with its companion mix matched to A's; C is ordinary code carrying no target, drawn at corpus rates. (2) **Read raw.** One position per prompt, the construct node's first token, and four sources are mixed there — the role, the particular prompt, the token, and the neuron's ordinary level and scale at that kind of position. The mean over A removes the prompt, the mean over B removes the token, and their difference is the raw profile. (3) **Standardise.** For each neuron and each *cell* (token class × which fifth of the prompt the position sits in; 4 × 5 = 20 cells, so 2.95M mean/spread pairs per reference), take the mean and spread over set C as the yardstick and state the difference in that neuron's own spreads. Both forms are kept because the yardstick's spread itself drifts over training. (4) **Noise unit.** Draw the battery twice from the same cores with different surroundings — twins — and take the distance between the two profiles, d = √(2(1 − cos)), as the unit every later difference is expressed in; intervals come from resampling cores, not from a distributional assumption. (5) **Licence.** Nine checks run on the (battery, model) pair before anything is read. Two nulls are gates — labels shuffled between A and B, and half-sets compared — and seven others are reported as *restrictions* on what may then be claimed. (6) **Compare across training.** Report norm, sparsity, twin agreement, carrier set (top 5% of standardised entries; 7,373 neurons) and its twin ceiling, against a same-architecture random-weight reference read with the same battery, and report the resolution: the smallest inter-checkpoint step in twin units.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **measurement**, not a decomposition and not a narrative: one number per neuron per construct, in units of that neuron's own ordinary spread at that kind of position, every derived quantity carrying a noise unit and a bootstrap interval, plus a named **carrier set** — the 5% of neurons with the largest standardised response. It is deliberately partial in one direction and complete in another. Partial: it reads only constructs someone named in advance, so it cannot discover anything, and a neuron in no construct's carrier set may still carry something the battery does not contain. Complete in the sense that matters for prediction: the reading is causal in one implemented way (the carrier set is ablated and the loss read at the construct's positions) and it is comparable at every one of 61 checkpoints, so a construct can be followed through two trillion tokens rather than described at one moment. The paper also notes that a whitened profile *is* a mass-mean probe direction, so the probe is one view of this object, not a rival.

## How they judged success

- Nine named checks, two of them gates: scrambled-label null and split-half null, each requiring the real profile's norm to exceed the null's 95th percentile; the other seven (twin agreement, convergence, length, re-weighting, calibration, companion matching, yardstick floor) reported as restrictions.
- A **random-weight reference** on the same architecture and battery, with a pre-fixed concentration criterion: sparsity more than three noise units above the untrained value and twin agreement at least 0.20 above it.
- **Core-bootstrap intervals** for every derived number, with a calibration check on whether they cover the twins' difference at the nominal rate.
- **Ablation against matched random sets**: zero the carrier set, zero a random set of the same size, read loss at the construct's fixed positions, compare against the random mean with a sign test over constructs and a construct-bootstrap interval.
- **The twin ceiling** as the denominator for every carrier-set overlap, so a 0.25 overlap is read against what two drawings of the same battery can reach (0.872 here) rather than against 1.
- A **resolution** in twin units, and a **probe** on the same prompts and the same read site as a comparison instrument.

## What they found

*The licence (final checkpoint):* both nulls pass on all 31 constructs they can run on, by a median factor of 10.66 (minimum 7.22) for the scrambled null and 12.76 (minimum 5.96) for the split-half. Twin agreement is 0.9861 median, rising to 0.9889 over the 169 read constructs; convergence to a cosine of 0.99 takes 56 prompts of 720 at the median. Three restrictions are material. Re-weighting the logged factors to a different mixture moves the profile a median 2.34 twin units, against roughly one unit for a redraw — so the profile *moves with the mixture*, and comparisons are licensed only over the same distribution. Eight statements are length-sensitive, with more than 5% of neurons flagged: `With` 55%, `For` 49%, `If` 47%, `While` 36%, `ImportFrom` 31%, `Assert` 21%, `Delete` 10%, `Return` 8%; holding the band fixed cuts the flagged share by 41% at the median and leaves six above 5%. Companion matching fails for the eight compound statements, by 0.19–0.68 (median 0.37) against a design cap of 0.15, and where B can be re-weighted to A's companion shares the profile moves 2.95 twin units (`For`) and 5.50 (`With`). 33 constructs' intervals are optimistic by a stated factor, up to 7.17 (`NotImplemented`).

*Resolution and drift:* the instrument resolves below 1,000 training steps. Median step between neighbouring references is 8.56 twin units over the first two checkpoints and 3.56 at the end of the dense block, 4.06 across the late tail and 2.91 on the annealing branch; the minimum is above one in every pair except `None` (0.862 and 0.918 in two pairs). Early training moves a profile 2.4× further than late training at the same step spacing.

*The unit is load-bearing:* coarser yardsticks — merging cells, or dropping the position band — name different neurons, with carrier overlap of 0.73–0.87 against the 20-cell sets, below the twin ceiling for 78 to 167 of 169 constructs; the token class matters more than the position band. And the unit separates model from battery: twin agreement, concept norm and carrier overlap with the previous reference are 0.981–0.989, 48.7–68.8 and 0.822–0.851 at every trained reference, against 0.710 / 6.34 / 0.273 and 0.717 / 6.13 / 0.286 at the two untrained ones, recorded days apart under different seeds; the twin ceiling itself rises from 0.28 untrained to 0.84 at the first checkpoint.

*The named neurons carry function:* on the 16 constructs with grammar-fixed next tokens, zeroing the carrier set costs a median 0.06 nats more than zeroing a size-matched random set at 4.19B tokens [0.02, 0.14], 0.25 at 83.9B [0.03, 0.66] and 0.40 at 2.03T [0.10, 0.70], above the random mean for 12, 14 and 14 of 16 (sign test p = 0.038, 0.002, 0.002). Largest: `Try` at 5.95 nats over random on a 0.088-nat baseline, `Lambda` 4.99 on 1.40. At the first checkpoint the median effect is a fifth of its final value — most of the function is acquired in the first 20 billion tokens.

*What the instrument then sees:* `for` as a loop statement and `for` in a comprehension share 0.250–0.266 of their carriers (0.198 for `AsyncFor`) against a chance rate of 0.050 and a ceiling of 0.872 — five times chance, under a third of the ceiling — so the model's `for` is neither a token nor a role. One role across two tokens does better: indented `␣for` and module-level `for` overlap 0.494 against scope-restricted ceilings of 0.837 and 0.850, and across the twelve scope-paired constructs the share runs 0.44 (`Match`) to 0.71 (`Try`), converging over training without merging. Carriers sit in all 24 layers and concentrate on neurons that serve many constructs, reaching up to five times the 5% rate. Clustering the 14,196 construct pairs by carrier overlap recovers the *inventory's* categories — grammar element against builtin name, exception classes (purity 1.000), warning classes (1.000), builtin functions (0.86), builtin types (0.73), comprehensions (1.000, p = 0.004) — and does not recover grammatical categories such as statement/expression or compound/binding statements; the highest-sharing cross-category pairs are a statement and the builtin that does the same job (`Delete`–`delattr` 0.200, `Global`–`globals` 0.094).

*The probe saturates:* a linear probe at the same read site separates A from B at 0.653 median (residual stream) / 0.802 (hidden units) on the untrained reference, then 0.998 at the first trained checkpoint and 0.999 at the end, with 145–148 of 164 constructs at or above 0.99 and a minimum of 0.902 — a range too compressed to order 169 constructs, 24 layers or 61 checkpoints. Its margin does move (13.8 / 16.5 / 14.8 median at 4.19B / 83.9B / 2.03T) but it is one number per construct and names no neurons.

*Three artefacts, each with a rule:* the twin unit is too small for intervals because the twins share cores (agreement corrected by it exceeds 1 for about 150 of 188 constructs) — so intervals come from the core bootstrap. Standardised size rises while real size falls, because the yardstick's spread shrinks over training (standardised norm up for 144 of 169 constructs, raw norm down for 148; for `for`, raw norm 15.7 → 184.5 → 143.0 → 96.0 while standardised goes 30.2 → 239.6 → 270.9 → 251.5) — so size is read in raw units. And the layer of largest response is unstable (it moves for 41% of constructs if the last layer is excluded) — so comparisons are made in the product space, not layer by layer.

## Limits and open problems they name

Six choices the method rests on, two of which are not measured at all: the reference corpus (public Python standing in for a model trained on 607 programming languages) and the read point (MLP neurons, one position, short prompts of median 55 tokens — no attention, no residual stream, no longer contexts). It reads *named* constructs only and therefore cannot discover concepts. One model, one language, and no second-model replication — the licence sentence names that directly. The read-site gate is the realisation's dependence on its tokenizer, and 19 of 188 constructs fail it for tokenizer reasons (symbols merged with neighbours, `␣def`/`def` split across B's subtypes). The record is not released in full — 27.6 GB per reference, 2,022 GB in total — only a worked example, which limits independent re-analysis even though the scripts are released. The untrained reference's random weights do not regenerate across library versions and its seed is owed. And a licence re-run on 19 October, after a fault in how per-prompt vectors were joined to their prompts, changed four checks' values, which is itself a finding about instruments: the published numbers were wrong until the join was fixed.

## What we take from it

- **A method: the licence-before-reading pattern.** Nine checks run on the instrument before any effect is read against it, with two as hard gates and seven as printed restrictions that each name the claims they forbid. This is the cheapest thing in the paper to copy and the folder has no entry that does it: our own write-ups report a measurement and then, if at all, its caveats — this inverts the order and makes each caveat a named restriction on a named class of claim.
- **A measure: the raw-versus-standardised pair, always printed together.** The paper's own sharpest artefact is that its headline size statistic moves the wrong way once standardisation is applied (up for 144 of 169 constructs while the real response falls for 148), because the yardstick itself drifts with training. Any standardised score we compute across checkpoints has the same exposure, and the fix is one column.
- **A dataset: a declared, machine-generated battery with logged draws.** Two-trillion-token checkpoint follow-through with a *stated* resolution (below 1,000 steps) is the honest form of "we tracked a feature through training"; the folder's cross-checkpoint claims have so far been made at whatever spacing the checkpoints happened to have.
- **A warning: a probe's accuracy is not a resolution.** 0.998–0.999 median with a minimum of 0.902 across 164 constructs and 61 checkpoints means the probe can no longer rank anything, while the same representations support the profile's measurable changes. Quoting probe accuracy as evidence of "the concept is represented" is quoting a saturated ceiling.
- **A framing: the choice of unit is part of the measurement.** Coarser yardsticks name different neurons (overlap 0.73–0.87, below the twin ceiling for 78–167 of 169 constructs), so "these are the neurons for X" is a claim relative to a stated yardstick, not to the model.
- **A shortcut: the mismatched-companion control behaves like a probe's control task.** Set B — the token out of role, with companions matched in distribution — is what removes the token's own contribution, and it is the same construction as a content-matched ablation control in the folder's component work, applied to a *corpus* rather than to an activation.
