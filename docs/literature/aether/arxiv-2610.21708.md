# Human Interventions as Labels: Localizing, Steering, and (Sometimes) Editing the Computations Behind LLM Agent Failures

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.21708
- **Authors / organisation:** Cesare Zavattari, Alessandro Tommasi, Giuseppe Prencipe — Dipartimento di Informatica, Università di Pisa
- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21708
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Oversight is the bottleneck for deployed agents, and the signal humans already produce while supervising is thrown away: an intervention is either discarded or replayed as yet another demonstration. The authors propose to read it as a measurement instead — a label that points at the internal computation which just failed — and then to find that computation, change only it, and score the change by whether the supervisor has to step in less often. The programme is run on one stubborn and easily quantified failure: an agent asking a clarifying question when the request was already unambiguous, one line of task against a three-item workspace. The larger goal is a discipline for interpretability work — predictions frozen before outcomes, interventions with matched controls, and an evaluation criterion taken from operations rather than from a probe set. Two deliberate choices shape everything: a rule-based overseer stands in for the supervising human (validated separately by blind annotators), and the headline metric is the reduction in a *budgeted* overseer's burden rather than probe accuracy.

## What they studied

Five open-weight instruct families that both over-ask and sometimes act directly on matched unambiguous requests — Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (36 layers), gemma-2-2b-it (26), OLMo-3-7B-Instruct (32), Mistral-Nemo-12B (40) and Phi-4 14B (40). Over-ask rates at collection were 84%, 90%, 88–90%, 88–90% and 36% respectively; Phi-4 is the only non-saturated "calibrated over-asker". Eligibility is itself a finding: of thirteen candidate families, six sat at a degenerate corner (always-ask or never-ask), several could not hold the reply format, and only five qualified — Llama-3.2-3B and Apertus-8B asked on all hundred unambiguous requests, Phi-3-mini never asked. Units of analysis: a single residual-stream direction per layer (from a logistic probe), one scalar coordinate at one token, whole-layer LoRA parameter sets, and SAE features. Data are scenario *twin pairs*: identical instruction text, workspace content differing so the request is either uniquely resolvable or genuinely ambiguous, and the harness refuses to run if anything but the item lines differs between twins.

## How they did it

Stage 1, collect: roll out the agent on 300 fresh pairs per seed (three seeds per model except gemma at one), store the verbatim message list, raw replies, parsed decisions and overseer verdicts; blocking checks are a replay invariant, a pairwise prompt diff, and an overseer false-positive canary. Stage 2, probe and distrust the probe: at the decision token — the last prompt token, where the model is about to commit to `ACTION:` or `ASK:` — train two logistic probes per layer, one for the upcoming behaviour and one for the ambiguity belief, split by pair so twins never straddle train and test, with permuted-label canaries and a *rich content baseline* (bag-of-words, target position, attribute values, distractor counts) that can predict the decision from prompt text alone. Stage 3, intervene: take the behaviour probe's weight vector as a unit direction, set the activation's projection on it to a target value at one site, and read the flip from the `ASK`/`ACTION` opening-token logit difference, then confirm flips with full generation. Two doses fixed in advance on training episodes only — class-mean, and extrapolated one inter-class gap past the acting class — with site and dose chosen on training over-asks and a single evaluation on test. Three controls under the identical recipe: random unit directions, a *style* direction (difference of mean activations under system prompts forcing always-ask vs never-ask on the same unambiguous scenarios), and patching the belief axis in both directions. Stage 4, edit: two LoRAs on identical data and hyperparameters — `local`, trainable only in the causal band (Qwen: layers 24–31, 3.3M parameters), and `bc`, the interactive-imitation default spread over all layers (15M) — evaluated on fresh scenarios through a separate budgeted overseer with attention limits and per-family trust dynamics, a 50-item capability battery, and six criteria fixed before any run.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **causal handle on a decision**: one direction, at one layer, at one token, whose scalar coordinate can be set to flip the decision, plus a geometry that says which other computations that direction is entangled with (cosine to the style axis), plus a *dissociation* between the belief and the decision — decodable-but-causally-inert-and-near-orthogonal. It is explicitly partial and it is meant to predict under intervention, not to describe a circuit: the paper's own test of sufficiency is behavioural (`did the overseer intervene less?`) and its stated deliverable for the weight side is a map of which family lands in which regime, not a rule. Two failure signatures sit beside the handle: *H-caution* (the ambiguity belief is correct and unused) and *H-misrep* (the belief is genuinely wrong), distinguished by a probe read-out.

## How they judged success

Decodability is reported but disclaimed: behaviour probes reach AUC 0.997–1.0 mid-to-late while the content baseline reaches 0.93–0.99 on Qwen under greedy decoding, so the claim rests on the causal test. Flip rates carry Wilson 95% intervals under the frozen-selection protocol; the random direction is the matched-recipe placebo (largest flip anywhere, 1/74). Runtime steering is scored by *IRR* — the relative reduction of budgeted burden `B = (W + 2I)/N`, where W is episodes watched and I interventions among them, failures on unwatched episodes costing nothing — reported with pair-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals (because point estimates were shown to swing 0.45–0.95 with the simulated overseer's own seed), beside full-attention failure counts, a legitimate-question retention count and a capability battery. Weight edits are scored on four pass/fail criteria per arm — interventions at least halved, IRR ≥ 50%, legitimate clarifying questions down by at most 10 of ~100, capability battery down at most 5 points — plus a localisation-must-pay comparison (at most a third of bc's parameters). Human validation: blind annotation of overseer verdicts and of the steering reduction, κ reported. Integrity devices: predictions written down before outcomes, conventions declared before they mattered, a stopping rule, base-row audits, and an external frontier-model adversarial audit of the code and artifacts.

## What they found

The ask-vs-act decision is causally localizable in all five families: extrapolated-dose flips 97% [91–99] (Qwen, n=74), 83% [73–90] (gemma, n=76), 72% [61–81] (OLMo-3, n=79), 95% [88–98] (Nemo, n=81) and 100% [86–100] (Phi-4, n=24), while random directions flip 0 at the same site on every family. Class-mean doses are far milder and uneven — 20%, 12%, 20%, 16% — except Phi-4 at 75%. Sites are sharp and at different depths: 78%, 81%, 66% (plateau L17–L21), 55%, and 55% (plateau to L34) of the stack; on Qwen layer 24 flips 23% where layers 12 and 20 flip 0%. Flips survive full generation: on Qwen 99% emit well-formed actions and 95% act on the correct object; on gemma all 65 flipped episodes acted on the correct object. In four families the model knows: over-asked episodes sit on the unambiguous side of the belief probe nearly as far from the boundary as acted ones (Qwen mean U-score −2.22 vs −2.65), the belief axis is causally inert in both directions, and belief and decision directions are near-orthogonal — H-caution. Phi-4 is the exception (−12 vs −41, closer to the boundary) — H-misrep. Gated steering on Qwen with three refit seeds drops watched interventions from 67/65/72 to 9/0/2 and total failures from 91/86/95 to 24/17/16 (−74/−80/−83%), with IRR point estimates 73/91/87% and bootstrap intervals [0.59,0.94], [0.68,0.94], [0.71,0.94]; legitimate questions fall only 2–4 of ~93 but 5–9 of ~100 genuinely ambiguous requests are newly acted on; the capability battery is unchanged and the gate fires off-target twice in 36 items. A system-prompt instruction makes burden worse on every seed (IRR −20%, −1%, −11%). On gemma the style direction beats the behaviour direction (IRR 89% vs 63%) because the two axes share half their alignment there (cosine 0.48–0.53 against 0.05–0.12 on Qwen), so the surviving claim is that specificity lives in the *gate* and the best lever is model-dependent. A Gemma Scope SAE at the causal layer reproduces the entanglement at feature resolution: ten features match the dense probe's AUC, the top-50 supports of behaviour and style share 19 features where 15% would be expected by chance, style-orthogonal behaviour features flip 0/78 and style-specific ones flip 0/78, only the AUC-matching readers move the decision (7/78, p=0.014) and they displace style most. On weights, `bc` is the default to beat and usually isn't — nine of its ten seed-arms outside Qwen pass everything — but it fails retention on all three Qwen seeds and, when it breaks something, it breaks something unrelated silently (re-asking an answered question, 15/9/11 episodes of 100). The one replicated localised win is Qwen: all criteria met on 3/3 seeds at 3.3M parameters, burden reductions 84/85/86%, capability 0.84 base against 0.84/0.86/0.84 edited. gemma and Nemo collapse to "never ask" (100/100 on both unambiguous and ambiguous), OLMo-3 erodes retention (94→79), Phi-4 crosses the criterion boundary *across full-pipeline seeds* (IRR 28% fail, 49.5% fail, 72% pass). Two geometric rules were written down in advance and both died out-of-family — the cosine rule (OLMo-3 measured Qwen-like disentanglement but behaved like gemma) and a peak-depth rule (killed by Phi-4, where the two rules made opposite predictions and both were wrong) — and per the stopping rule no third rule was fitted. Human agreement: independent annotator κ=0.900 on verdicts and κ=0.921 on the blind steering study, with −81% judged against the overseer's −87%.

## Limits and open problems they name

A deliberately small causal testbed — one-line tasks, three-item workspaces — with nothing shown about naturalistic traces; the eligibility window is five of thirteen families and every "five for five" lives inside it. Greedy decoding makes behaviour a deterministic function of the prompt, which is why nothing rests on decodability; sampled-decoding replications are pending. The editing regime boundary is mapped, not explained (n=5, and it passes *through one model's seeds*); H-misrep rests on one family; the SAE analysis is one dictionary, one layer, one dose. The style control is model-dependent and was reframed rather than removed. Human validation is two annotators with disclosed corrections, and the burden dynamics themselves are not human-validated — they proved unstable on 30-episode subsamples. The burden weights an over-ask and an act-on-ambiguous request equally although the second is worse, so gated steering's small new error class is under-priced; an input built to fool the belief probe would make the gate force exactly that error. Retention credits any question on an ambiguous request as correct, so a drop in question *quality* (the spurious-option defect that produced every residual annotator disagreement) passes unseen. Simultaneous edits are untested, and none of this should be presumed adversarially robust. Pre-registration is self-administered notes, not a registry — the reader has only the authors' word on ordering.

## What we take from it

A **criterion**, and the most directly usable one in the folder: score an internal read-out by whether the surrounding system has to correct it less, with the metric, its denominators and its seed-instability all printed. A **baseline obligation**: under greedy decoding a prompt-only content model reaches AUC 0.93–0.99 against a probe's 0.997–1.0, so a probe number without a text-only surrogate is uninterpretable — the same rule the folder's KL work arrived at from the other side. A **discipline** we should copy wholesale: predictions frozen per model family before evaluation, conventions (which peak counts) declared before the model that makes them matter, and a stopping rule honoured when it costs the paper its hypothesis. A **warning about attribution by edit**: the same surface failure had a context-level cause (scaffold ablation → zero) and a weight-level cause (bc re-created it), so a cheap scaffold ablation should gate any mechanistic pipeline — and the localised edit's *unconditional collapse* on two families shows that a narrow edit can generalise to "never do the behaviour" just as a distributed one generalises to "do it less". A **transfer warning**: the phenomenon travels, the address does not — five late sites at five depths, and a direction reused across models would have failed three times in five. And an instrument gap worth remembering: the belief that is decodable, orthogonal and causally inert is the exact object a predictive white-box model must *not* report as a cause, and it is only detectable with the both-directions patch.
