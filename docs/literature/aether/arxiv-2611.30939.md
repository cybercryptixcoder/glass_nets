# Beyond Patching: Consistent Circuit Discovery via Gradient Spectral Analysis

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Circuit discovery — naming the small subgraph that implements a behaviour — is dominated by patching, which scores a unit by swapping its clean activation for a corrupted one. The corruption has to be built, one prompt pair at a time, and the paper's motivating claim is that this prerequisite quietly determines the answer: different corruption designs for the same task give systematically different circuits, and circuits found on one prompt template do not always survive paraphrase. They want a discovery procedure that needs no contrast construction at all, is stable across input formulations of a task, and — as a second axis — exposes structure shared between tasks natively instead of comparing separately discovered circuits after the fact. The larger goal is a description of computation that is a property of the task and the network rather than of the experimenter's prompt design.

## What they studied
GPT-2 Small (124M) and Pythia-160M (162M), both 12 layers, d_model 768, 12 heads, so 168 units per model (each attention head's value and output projections as one read-write operator, each MLP's up and down matrices as two separate units; embeddings, layer norms and unembedding excluded). Twelve tasks: IOI, Greater-Than, Greater-Than-Price, Country-Capital, Person-Country, Person-Profession, Colored Objects, Gender Bias, Gendered Pronoun, Subject-Verb Agreement, SVA-Binary, SVA-BLiMP. Unit of analysis: the parameter block owned by a head or an MLP projection, scored through a gradient-derived subspace.

## How they did it
For each task they assemble 100 semantic instances with 5 paraphrases each, and average the per-paraphrase gradient of the task loss over those 5, giving one gradient vector per instance. Instance gradients are stacked into a matrix and, separately for every (layer, type) block — 24 blocks for a 12-layer model — an SVD of that block's slice is taken (via the Gram trick on the 100×100 inner matrix, since the number of instances is far smaller than the block's parameter count). Singular directions are kept until 95% of squared spectral energy is covered. Each unit then receives a product of two factors: a block-level coherence term, the norm of the mean of the block's row-normalised per-instance gradients (a mean resultant length on the unit sphere), and a unit-level share, the Frobenius energy of that unit's rows of the retained singular vectors divided by its parameter count. Sorting by the product gives the ranking. They compare against a random permutation (5 seeds), two first-moment reductions of the very same gradients (magnitude, and directional coherence), and EAP, EAP-IG and EAP-IG-KL, all under one cumulative-patching recovery-score protocol where the top-K attention heads are patched from the corrupted run to clean activations and the residual fraction of the clean-versus-floor metric is plotted against K. Cross-task analysis re-orthonormalises each unit's slice of its block subspace, measures pairwise subspace overlap normalised by the smaller rank, calibrates that overlap against random rank-matched orthonormal pairs at the 95th percentile, weights by the geometric mean of the two tasks' type-normalised importance, and sorts units into Universal, family-shared, task-specific or Mixed by three priority-ordered threshold gates. A causal check restricts the residual stream to a candidate set's weighted unit-SVD subspace and reads the change in target logit difference, with a size-matched Monte-Carlo null giving z-scores.

## What counts as an explanation here
A ranked list of units plus a low-rank subspace per architectural block — a partial, selective account rather than a full mechanism. There is no textual circuit story and no edge-level graph: the object is "these heads and these MLP projections carry this task's gradient-spectral mass", with the subspace available as a geometric signature that can be compared across tasks. The prediction it enables is what happens to the task metric when a chosen set of heads is patched, and — in the cross-task part — which units will lift which tasks in a subspace-restricted forward pass.

## How they judged success
Recovery score r(K) = (metric with top-K heads patched − metric with all heads patched) / (corrupted baseline − all-heads baseline), lower better, read as how fast the curve reaches the floor; Recall@K and average precision against the 26-head canonical IOI circuit; for the cross-task tables, mean lift in target-token logit difference under subspace restriction, with significance from a size-matched Monte-Carlo null at |z| ≥ 2 / 3 / 5 and a bold threshold at lift ≥ 0.25; robustness of the five classification thresholds and the family layout deferred to appendix.

## What they found
SCA matches or beats the EAP family over K in 1..144 on the six tasks shown in the main figures, with the advantage concentrated at small budgets — below all three EAP variants within the top 10 on Colored Objects, Country-Capital, Gender Bias and SVA-BLiMP. On IOI it recovers 10 of the 26 canonical heads within its top 10, matching EAP-IG, with precision near 1.0 at the leading positions. Both first-moment baselines fail: the direction baseline sits at or above the random band on four tasks, which the authors read as directional coherence being uninformative or anti-correlated with causal relevance, and the magnitude baseline tracks random and never beats SCA. Cross-task, the 12 tasks cluster into four families (numeric comparison, subject-verb agreement, factual recall, gendered coreference) plus two singletons, with the two Greater-Than phrasings collapsing onto one near-diagonal block. A 9-unit Universal set lifts all 12 tasks (+0.86 to +4.51 in logit difference, all |z| ≥ 2.8); layers 0–2 are almost entirely universal; mid-layer attention is empty of family or task labels; specialisation concentrates in layers 7–11. Two heads (principally L9H1 and L10H7) carry numeric comparison with z = +79.5 and +46.2 while every other task is below |z| 1; the two-head factual-recall set gives z = +119 on Person-Country and +67.6 on Country-Capital; six of seven IOI-specific units fall inside the canonical 26-head circuit and all three canonical layers (S-inhibition, name mover, backup name mover) are recovered, and the seventh is a layer-mate of an S-inhibition head, without IOI ever being used as a target.

## Limits and open problems they name
They call the subspaces interpretive lenses rather than guaranteed mediators, validated only by a projection-based ablation; the paraphrase set's composition, the 95% energy threshold and the layer-type aggregation are unexamined design choices, and they note the sign-consistency distribution underlying paraphrase generation is itself model-dependent. Experiments are confined to two small models; scaling needs Gram-trick variants. They position SCA as complementary to matched counterfactuals rather than a replacement, and suggest seeding counterfactual patching with discovered subspaces. Not named by the authors but visible in the text: the recovery-score protocol still needs clean/corrupted pairs at evaluation time, so only the ranking is counterfactual-free; Pythia-160M results live entirely in an appendix not present in the v1 HTML; and the threshold/gate parameters of the cross-task classifier are default values with robustness deferred.

## What we take from it
A method (gradient-spectral ranking as a zero-contrast unit scorer, usable where we cannot build counterfactuals), a measure (recovery score under cumulative patching, with the subspace-restriction lift and its Monte-Carlo z as the causal check on a *set* rather than a unit), a framing (a task is a low-rank subspace of the parameter-gradient space, and paraphrase averaging is what separates task signal from prompt noise), and a warning (the first-moment reductions of the same gradients are not merely weaker but sometimes anti-correlated, so averaging direction and reading the spectrum are different measurements, not two ways of smoothing one). For us the transferable idea is that the useful object is a subspace with a per-unit energy share, which is directly a prediction target: a white-box model that claims to know which units carry a task can be scored exactly the way Table 1 scores it — restrict to the claimed subspace, measure lift, and report the size-matched null.
