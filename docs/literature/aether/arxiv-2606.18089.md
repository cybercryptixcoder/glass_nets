# From Reasoning Traces to Reusable Modules: Understanding Compositional Generalization in Language Model Reasoning

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.18089
- **Authors / organisation:** Lingjing Kong; Xin Liu; Guangyi Chen; Martin Q. Ma; Xiangchen Song; Yuekai Sun; Mikhail Yurochkin; Taylor W. Killian; Ruslan Salakhutdinov; Kun Zhang; Eric P. Xing; Zhengzhong Liu — Institute of Foundation Models; Carnegie Mellon University; MBZUAI
- **Date:** 2026-07-05 (v2; v1 2026-06-16)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18089
- **Kind:** paper (theory + controlled experiments + a real-model check)
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v2, all 134.4k chars: §1–§5, references, Appendices A–M including the full Condition C.1, Theorem D.1 with proof, the data-generating process in Appendix I, and the SAE appendix L; Figures 1–18 are images and were not inspected, so every number here is a prose sentence or a table cell — missing curve values are named as missing rather than read off a plot)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful — methodological, not mechanistic about a network we care about)

## What they wanted, and why
Post-training now means SFT followed by RL, and the pair is credited with the leap in reasoning ability, but nobody can say which stage contributes what. The authors' claim is that the two stages do different jobs in a structured sense: SFT supplies the *material* (traces that contain the reusable pieces in working combination), and RL supplies the *decomposition* (re-sampling under a verifier exposes the local contexts that separate one piece from another). They want that split to be a theorem rather than a slogan, and they want a data-design rule out of it: what should each stage be trained on. The larger goal is a statement of when latent reasoning structure is *identifiable* from traces at all, which is the same question this folder keeps asking of activations, moved onto the text channel.

## What they studied
Two levels. (1) A **synthetic string-transformation environment**: 24 atomic transformation functions reused from prior work stand in for skills, plus 10 input-construction mechanisms that decide which earlier intermediate value feeds the next step and how (pass one value, concatenate two, and so on) stand in for routing. A composition has a depth L (how many skills are applied) and a signature that names the skill and the router at every position; the generator knows the signature, the model never sees it, and prompts give only the nested expression and the input string with the function definitions hidden. The latent variables are the per-position skill and router indices; the observed variable is the whole textual trace plus the final answer. (2) As a complement, two open-weight models — Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 — on 643 math problems from MATH-500, AIME 2024/2025, AMC and GSM8K. Base model for the controlled arm: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The unit of analysis is therefore a **module** (a skill or a router) inside a trace, not an activation.

## How they did it
The theory comes first and drives everything. They posit a hierarchy: the problem descriptor induces discrete latent selection variables which index atomic modules, and the modules generate the visible token sequence; higher variables are read as coarse deterministic summaries of the children below them. A set of eight conditions (Condition C.1: faithfulness, rank faithfulness, restricted choice sets, coarsest determinism, a *pure local cue* per module plus a large enough comparison table, observable module signatures, no twins, maximality) is shown to make the latent variables and their adjacency recoverable up to relabelling. Two consequences are then proved. Proposition 3.2 (Theorem D.1 in the appendix): if SFT reveals only a subset of valid traces per prompt, an alternative trace law that treats the visible subset as the whole conditional produces *identical* supervised observations, and the two laws differ in L1 distance by twice the expected hidden trace mass — so identification-critical local events outside the SFT support are not uncertain, they are unidentifiable, even with infinite data. Proposition 3.3: if a hidden event is reachable by the current policy and the verifier rewards traces containing it more than traces omitting it, conditioning on reward strictly enriches that event, with the enrichment proportional to the usual p(1−p)Δ term. Theorem 3.4: composition of learned local constraints transfers to a novel descriptor exactly when every local parent–child configuration it forces has a training witness. Experiments then intervene on nothing but *which signatures are in which split*: remove skills or routers from the RL corpus, re-inject them either as isolated L=1 uses or inside compositions, and cross that with four set relations between the SFT and RL composition supports (RL inside SFT, RL beyond SFT, partially overlapping, disjoint). SFT is 2 epochs at 2e-5 on correct traces only (ten sampled responses per problem, keep the correct ones); RL is final-answer exact match, learning rate 1e-6, 16 rollouts, KL and entropy coefficients 0, prompts whose rollouts are all-correct or all-wrong filtered out; OOD means the *signature* is absent from both supports even though its skills and routers each appeared elsewhere.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **causal abstraction with known ground truth**: a discrete latent hierarchy (skills × routing) that generates the trace, with proof-level identifiability conditions and a predicted division of labour between two training algorithms. It is meant to be complete and it is predictive in a specific sense — from the latent structure plus the training support you are supposed to be able to say whether a novel composition will be solvable, and the experiments are that prediction's test. It is the folder's first explanation whose unit of decomposition is a *step in a written argument* rather than a direction, feature or circuit, and it is unusual in treating the supervising objective as part of the explanatory structure rather than as background.

## How they judged success
OOD accuracy on held-out composition signatures at depths L=1 through L=8 (and depth extrapolation above the training depth), with seen-composition accuracy as the invariant to hold fixed; SFT-only versus SFT+RL at matched initialisation and matched RL budget; skill-removal and router-removal interventions crossed with two re-injection forms; the four SFT/RL support relations; longer 1000-step runs for the two headline curves; and hyperparameter sweeps (samples per prompt, temperature, reward shaping, KL and entropy coefficients) reported as stable. Sensitivity is two seeds, shown as a shaded band. In the real-model arm the metric is the count of unique short skill n-grams and their normalised frequency difference between the SFT and RL models, over traces tokenised into a skill library that a strong LLM summarised from the traces themselves. There is no human evaluation and no comparison against an identifiability baseline that does not exist by construction.

## What they found
**Decomposition happens and it is worth a lot.** Trained only on L=3 compound traces, RL lifts single-skill accuracy by 43.7% over SFT-only, and average unseen-composition accuracy by 40.3% across depths, while the SFT–RL gap on *seen* compositions is small — the gain is concentrated exactly where the theory says it should be. Table 1 is the sharpest single comparison: from the same SFT checkpoint and 300 RL steps, RL on atomic modules reaches 14.8% OOD versus a 4.8% SFT baseline, while RL on compound traces reaches 42.6% — a 27.8-point compound advantage, read as the difference between reinforcing operations and exposing their interfaces. **Re-injection is compositional or it does not work.** With 8 of 24 skills removed from RL, re-injecting them as isolated L=1 examples recovers only partially and does not generalise to deeper traces; re-injecting them inside compositions recovers substantially. The same holds for routers, and there lower-depth re-injection beats deeper re-injection. Appendix H adds a stage asymmetry I did not expect: the *identical* isolated re-injection done in the SFT stage instead of the RL stage produces negligible improvement, so the same data is nearly worthless in one stage and useful in the other. **The support relation is a curriculum hyperparameter.** All four SFT/RL relations are indistinguishable on seen compositions and separate clearly on unseen ones, with the disjoint setting best and the "SFT already covers the RL set" setting weakest — the result that motivates the paper's prescription: SFT should cover the full atomic inventory *through composite traces*, RL should range over novel compositions outside the SFT support. Table 2 confirms the other half: dropping 4 atoms from SFT data costs in-distribution almost nothing and costs OOD accuracy noticeably after RL (0.67→0.52 unseen-2, 0.55→0.36 unseen-3). Real-model check: the RL-tuned Qwen reproduces the signature at the short-combination scale only — more unique n-grams at n=2 and n=3 and relatively more short combinations, similar diversity at longer n — which the authors explicitly defend as consistent rather than as confirmation, since long templates are not what the mechanism depends on.

## Limits and open problems they name
The empirical arm is deliberately a toy and they say so: 24 skills, 10 routers, string functions, one base model, and extending the support-controlled method to real domains (math, code, tool use) is called future work. The identification conditions are strong and, as they note, only *sufficient* — nothing is shown about how partial violations degrade the conclusion. They also name the obvious next objective: adaptively tracking which local interfaces the current policy actually realizes, and steering RL exploration toward the ones with the most identification gain. I add four of my own. (i) The real-model evidence is not evidence of module identification — it counts short n-gram diversity, a statistic that rises if the model produces more varied *texture*; there is no test that its skill tokens denote distinct operations, and the skill library is itself an LLM's summary of the traces. (ii) The SAE appendix is the only mechanistic claim in the paper: pretrained SAEs on Gemma-2-2b-it, tokens partitioned by sequence position, causal discovery over SAE features, and a qualitative reading of the resulting graph as skill-like and routing-like nodes — no metric, no control, no ground truth, so it is an illustration of the theory's vocabulary, not a test of it. (iii) The latent model assumes a discrete module library exists and is finite, which is what the synthetic generator guarantees and what real reasoning is precisely what is in doubt; that is a modelling assumption doing much of the work, and the paper is honest that the theory is conditional on it but does not stress-test it. (iv) Two seeds and one ranker-free metric set: the ordering claims come from mean curves whose shaded bands are shown, but nothing here is a significance test, and the disjoint-beats-superset ordering is the paper's headline prescription resting on that figure alone. (v) The trace channel is treated as the observable whose structure is to be recovered, whereas for our purposes the trace is a *label-provider* whose faithfulness is exactly what other entries in this folder dispute.

## What we take from it
- **Framing (import):** a hierarchy of discrete selections over reusable operations is the most legible version of "what kind of explanation do we want" in the folder. Our predictor emits descriptions of components; this paper's object is a description of a *computation* as a composition, and the two can be made to meet: a composition-level description is checkable against held-out compositions in a way a single component's label never is.
- **Method (import):** OOD defined on the *combination signature* rather than on the input surface. That is a transferrable evaluation shape for us — hold out a combination of two factors the model has seen separately, and score the predictor on whether it called that combination correctly, since that is the only test that distinguishes composition from template storage.
- **Method (import):** the SFT-hidden-support result is the trace-channel twin of the folder's probe controls. If the instrument is fitted only on one canonical output per input, there are entire regions of the output distribution that cannot be *observed* to be missing, and the confusion is identifiability, not sample size. The analogous worry for our read-outs is a decoder fitted on one generation per prompt. Theorem D.1's arithmetic — L1 distortion exactly twice the hidden mass — gives a usable floor: any probability estimated from censored outputs is off by a knowable amount whenever we can bound the mass we did not see.
- **Warning (import):** "RL improves OOD" is not evidence of decomposition unless the alternative — more diverse templates — is tested. Their own real-model check is the weak version of that; a synthetic arm with known signatures is what made the claim sharp, and we should demand the same for any claim that a model has learned a reusable structure, ours included.
- **Idea worth stealing:** the "local witness" criterion is an operational test for whether a newly described component interface is supported by training data — for a component we claim to understand, we can ask whether every input configuration in which it participates has a seen analogue, and mark the ones that do not as unsupported rather than as understood.
- **Shortcut:** the whole experiment is about *data splittings*, which we can control exactly; if any of our predictor work involves training a surrogate on model outputs, the SFT/RL support lesson transfers directly and costs only bookkeeping.
