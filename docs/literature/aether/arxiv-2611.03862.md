# Dr. No: Feature Steering for Abstention in Vision-Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.03862
- **Authors / organisation:** Farid Hasanov (Eindhoven University of Technology), Riccardo Renzulli (University of Turin), Bahram Zonooz (TU/e), Mykola Pechenizkiy (TU/e), Decebal Constantin Mocanu (University of Luxembourg)
- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03862
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Models that always answer are hard to trust, because a confident unsupported answer can be worse than silence. The usual fix is post-hoc: score uncertainty from the output and reject below a threshold. That works but is a blunt instrument — a single scalar is thresholded, so the refusal rate and the preservation of correct answers are hard to control separately. The authors ask whether abstention and commitment are *partly represented as sparse internal features*, and whether nudging a handful of those features before generation can shift the answer/abstain balance more selectively than a threshold on output confidence. The larger goal is a control handle for an epistemic decision made inside a frozen model, without retraining. They also want to know whether one such handle is enough, or whether abstention is source-dependent.

## What they studied
Two frozen models with released sparse autoencoder suites: LLaVA-1.6 Mistral 7B (one 8x-expansion SAE per layer, 32,768 latents over a 4,096-wide residual stream) and Qwen3.5-2B (SAE with 32,768 latents). The unit of analysis is a single SAE latent's decoder direction, read at one layer at one token position — the final prompt token, i.e. the point after image, question, answer options and the explicit abstain option have all been processed. Data: a self-built multimodal mining corpus from the GQA balanced split (5,000 answerable items plus 20,000 synthesised unanswerable ones in four families — semantic drift, object absence, false premise, answer dropping — for 25,000 total), plus the synthetic math corpus SUM for the text setting (each problem unrolled into one answerable and one unanswerable item, 20,000 each). Evaluation: UNK-VQA, the three MM-UPD sub-benchmarks, and four AbstentionBench subsets.

## How they did it
They first run the unsteered model over the mining corpus and *behaviourally filter*: abstention features are mined only from unanswerable items on which the model already chose the abstain option, commitment features only from answerable items it already answered correctly — so the features are tied to realised behaviour, not to the synthetic label. Every SAE latent is then scored by mean activation difference between the abstain and commit subsets and by its single-feature AUROC as a detector of the abstain label; the two rankings are intersected and the top k kept. At inference they sum the unit-normalised decoder directions of the abstention features into one vector and of the commitment features into another, and add a scaled difference of the two to every prompt-token position during the prefill pass only (generated-token activations untouched, image tokens treated exactly like text tokens). Three modes are tried: abstention-up, commitment-down, and bidirectional. The main runs are *gated*: a sigmoid over the mean activation of the abstention features is centred at the midpoint between the class means and scaled by a quarter of their separation, so steering fades on inputs that do not already look unanswerable. Layer, k, strength, mode and gating are chosen on a validation split and then frozen. Controls: an abstention system prompt, six post-hoc uncertainty baselines with thresholds tuned on the same splits, a logit bias on the abstain option, "dense" steering in the raw residual stream with the identical recipe, and randomly selected SAE features.

## What counts as an explanation here
Nothing in the paper is a textual account of a mechanism; the understanding is a **steering vector plus a readout scalar**. The explanation is operational: a small named set of sparse features (the paper prints the top-5 feature IDs per family and layer), an aggregated direction built from their decoder columns, and a gate that reads the same features as a confidence-in-abstention signal. It is explicitly partial — the authors say abstention is not governed by a single universal direction, and the vector is expected to work only on unanswerability of a kind present in the mining distribution. It does predict, in a limited sense: the same feature signal used as a held-out answerability detector tracks the benchmarks unevenly, which is the paper's central diagnostic.

## How they judged success
Primary metrics are the harmonic mean of answerable and unanswerable accuracy (UNK-VQA, AbstentionBench) and dual accuracy on the paired solvable/unsolvable MM-UPD instances, which counts a pair correct only if the model answers the solvable one right *and* abstains on its twin. The prompting baseline is included precisely to show the metric punishes blanket refusal. Robustness is handled in two separate arms: 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples over shared test examples for evaluation noise, and three independent mining seeds for pipeline noise. Ablations cover mode, random features, dense steering in place of SAE directions, a strength sweep on each benchmark, logit bias, and paraphrase robustness on the abstain option's wording. Overhead is measured in wall-clock, latency and GPU memory.

## What they found
On UNK-VQA the unsteered model abstains rarely (37.5 answerable / 33.2 unanswerable, HM 35.2); Dr. No trades about six points of answering for seventeen of abstention (31.5 / 50.3, HM 38.7), the best harmonic mean in the table, with the bootstrap CI [38.00, 39.46] clearing every other method's upper bound in that table (the top uncertainty CI is [36.85, 38.77]). The abstention prompt is the opposite extreme: 6.1 / 93.8 and an HM of 11.5. MM-UPD splits by source — dual accuracy improves most where the answer set or image is *incompatible* with the question (IASD 45.5 against 37.2 baseline, IVQD 57.6 against 53.4) and loses to uncertainty methods on absent-answer detection (AAD dual 30.2 against 36.8 for plain dense steering and 41.3 for log-probability; in the paired bootstrap table's mean-MM-UPD column the prompt-augmented variant, at 49.2, edges the uncertainty baselines, which sit at 43.9 to 48.1). On Qwen, math subsets average HM 77.6 against 71.1 unsteered and 75.9 for the best uncertainty method, while MuSiQue goes the other way: 59.9 against 61.1 unsteered. The language pattern repeats on Qwen's visual benchmarks. Feature separability on the mining corpus is the strongest number in the paper and is explicitly labelled a mining diagnostic: AUROC 0.872 at layer 16, 0.928 at 20, 0.998 at 24, where the mean activations are 19.87 abstain versus 4.53 commit. Ablations: random SAE features give HM 35.6 and MM-UPD 38.1 against the final 38.7 / 44.4; abstention-up alone retains most of it (38.2 / 42.2), commitment-down alone is clearly weaker (36.3 / 39.7), and dense residual steering peaks at 38.2 / 40.3. Mining-seed noise is tiny (UNK-VQA HM 38.70 ± 0.13, MM-UPD 49.08 ± 0.02, and four of five-feature top-5 sets identical across seeds with mean Spearman 0.933 or 1.000). Overhead is +8 ms per round (+1.0%) and 0.5 GiB peak memory, with 2.1 ms of that the SAE encode and 512 MiB the resident SAE weights. Paraphrasing the abstain option moves nothing structurally: HM gains of 3.3 to 3.6 points on all three wordings.

## Limits and open problems they name
The paper's own headline is that abstention control is source-dependent: AAD, where the option is simply missing, is not fixed by steering, and MuSiQue, where unanswerability only emerges after multi-hop retrieval, is made worse. The authors attribute this to the decision-token location — features read from a static prompt cannot see a property that arises during composition — and propose mining across reasoning steps. They flag the dual-use risk (the same knob could suppress caution or censor legitimate answers), say their metrics do not establish how abstention distributes across subpopulations, languages or visual domains, and state the method should not be used as a universal safety or uncertainty detector. Configuration is benchmark-specific (layer 24 with α = 5.0 for UNK-VQA, layer 16 with α = 2.0 for MM-UPD), and the only uncertainty-free statement of a usable *range* is the appendix sweep, so no single setting is claimed to work everywhere.

## What we take from it
**A warning, and a measure.** The warning is the clearest statement the folder has that *a near-perfect detector is a poor predictor of where a fix works*: the strongest mined feature reaches AUROC 0.998 on the mining corpus, holds top-5 identity across three seeds, and still loses to a trivial logit bias on one unanswerability source. **A rule from the numbers:** the authors' own ablation table says the sparse dictionary earns its keep by 0.5 HM points over dense steering (38.7 versus 38.2) and 4.1 dual points (44.4 versus 40.3), so any claim we make for a component's necessity has to be that size, in a paired row, not a comparison against no intervention at all. **An instrument worth copying:** the reading of the model's *own* behaviour before feature selection — filtering to items the model already got right — is a cheap way to keep a probe from describing a synthetic label the model never implemented, and it is the same filter the abstention/commitment contrast needs to be causal rather than textual. **A framing:** the gate (a sigmoid over the same features, calibrated from the mining class means and their spread) is a copy-ready shape for "intervene only where the state variable is already partway there", which is the precondition our own interventions keep lacking. **A gap:** the perturbation sweep in the dense-steering appendix shows the readout is an on/off control with a narrow usable band — at layer 16 with α = 5 answering accuracy reaches 0.1% — and the paper never plots the gate's own operating range on the test sets.
