# Arbitration Failure, Not Perceptual Blindness: How Vision-Language Models Resolve Visual-Linguistic Conflicts

- **Key:** arxiv-2604.09364
- **Authors / organisation:** Farhad Nooralahzadeh, Omid Rohanian, Yi Zhang, Jonathan Fürst, Kurt Stockinger; Institute of Computer Science, Zurich University of Applied Sciences (ZHAW), Switzerland, with Rohanian also at University of Oxford
- **Date:** 2026-04-13 (v2; cs.CV)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09364
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
When a vision-language model is shown a blue banana and answers "yellow", the common account is that the vision encoder failed and the language side never received the detail. The authors want to know whether that is true, because the two stories have opposite repairs: a blind encoder needs a better encoder, while an override needs the downstream competition between image evidence and linguistic prior to be located and changed. They also want to know whether the layer at which the image evidence first wins is the layer at which it should be intervened on — the assumption almost every layer-selection heuristic makes. Behind both questions sits a larger methodological worry: the readout and intervention habits of text-only interpretability were tuned on a residual stream in which the relevant state sits in the last token, and a multimodal model may simply not satisfy that.

## What they studied
Ten vision-language models spanning 7B to 72B across four language backbones (Llama-2, Mistral, Qwen2, InternLM, Vicuna) and three vision encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, InternViT): LLaVA-v1.5, LLaVA-Next, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL, InternVL2, BLIP-2 and DeepSeek-Janus at 7–8B, plus LLaVA-v1.5-13B, InternVL2-26B and Qwen2-VL-72B. Data are the Visual-Counterfact splits: 493 Color examples (an object recoloured away from its stereotype) and 727 Size examples. The unit of analysis is a layer-by-layer logit comparison between two candidate answer tokens, then a hidden state at one layer of the residual stream, then a token position (image versus text), then a steering direction or a set of SAE features.

## How they did it
They run a Logit Lens at the last token, projecting each layer's residual state through the final norm and the language-model head, and track the best-matching surface form of the image-consistent answer against the best-matching form of the prior-consistent answer; they use six spellings per answer (lowercase, capitalised, uppercase, two space-prefixed forms, and hex subwords) because a single-variant readout badly understates models that place the visual token at a high rank. The Multimodal Arbitration Crossover (MAC) is the first layer where the visual logit exceeds the prior logit and stays ahead at the next layer, so transient crossings are not counted. Encoding is then measured as the L2 distance between the hidden states produced by the counterfactual image and by the standard image, at 25, 50 and 75 percent of each sample's MAC depth, on 20 depth points in the appendix; success and failure groups are compared with a Mann-Whitney test, and a logistic-regression probe trained on the attribute label checks that the difference is about the attribute rather than overall divergence. For causal work they run the model on both images, take the standard run's states at the MAC layer and inject them into the counterfactual run, first only at the last token and then at every position, and finally by token type (all images, all text). Finally they steer during inference, either by adding a contrastive mean difference to every token position or by training a 4x-expansion SAE at a layer, scoring features by decoder-weighted differential activation, taking the top 50 each way, decoding original and modified feature vectors and adding only the delta to the untouched state; the SAE is steered bidirectionally (amplify visual, suppress prior) with strengths swept from 0 to 5, and linear strength from 0 to 3. Intervention layers are swept across five depths from 10 to 100 percent of the MAC layer, on 293 held-out Color examples with 200 used to fit.

## What counts as an explanation here
A partial, two-half account rather than a circuit. The first half is a *location*: a crossover layer per model and per attribute, plus the width of the logit gap at the final layer, framed as where the image wins and by how much. The second half is a *dissociation*: encoding strength on one side, the arbitration outcome on the other, with the claim that the first does not predict the second. The causal half is an intervention on a layer (patch the states, or steer a direction), so the explanation predicts what happens to the output when a specific block is replaced — one-directionally, as it turns out. It is not predictive of the answer from the states in any quantitative sense; the closest thing to a predictive claim is the model-level correlation between final-layer logit gap and success rate.

## How they judged success
Final-layer visual-win rate (the fraction of samples where the visual logit still exceeds the prior logit at the last layer), MAC depth as a percentage of layers, L2 distance between counterfactual and standard states, probe AUC at ten depths with five-fold cross-validation, and probe confidence differences between success and failure groups. Statistics are Mann-Whitney tests at the sample level, Spearman correlations across the seven primary models in both directions (encoding to success, encoding to logit gap, logit gap to success), and a sample-level ROC-AUC using encoding strength to predict the outcome. Patching is scored as percent outputs changed, percent visual-to-prior flips, and flips conditioned on a baseline that preferred the visual answer; the token-type decomposition is scored as retention, image-flip count over full-sequence flip count. Steering is scored as accuracy on 293 held-out samples, net change, and the counts improved and degraded — the last being the only collateral measurement in the paper. Paraphrase robustness uses eight question phrasings and reports trajectory correlation and winner agreement.

## What they found
Encoding is present in every model before the crossover and grows monotonically to the final layer rather than being suppressed, with the success-versus-failure L2 ratio inside a narrow band around parity for all ten models (0.81 to 1.20 across the ten), and six of ten models show no significant difference between groups; the four that do are mostly in the direction where failures encode the attribute *more* strongly. Probes exceed 0.86 AUC at about ten percent depth for every model and pass 0.99 mid-depth, with probe confidence on success and failure samples separated by less than 0.03 and with inconsistent sign. Encoding strength predicts the sample outcome at 0.528 AUC, and across models its correlation with success rate is 0.198, while the final-layer logit gap correlates at 0.847 and the visual token's final rank at -0.893; encoding does not predict the logit gap either (0.464). The pattern is a double dissociation, illustrated by InternVL2 encoding more strongly than BLIP-2 yet succeeding on 58 percent versus 92 percent. MAC depth ranges from 36 to 71 percent of layers on Color and is attribute-specific: the per-model ordering partly reverses between Color and Size, which the authors read as separate arbitration pathways per attribute. Full-sequence patching flips 60 to 84 percent of outputs from the visual to the prior answer with no reverse flips anywhere, whereas last-token patching flips 0 to 1 percent; text-only patching flips 0 to 2 percent, and image-only patching retains 97 to 101 percent for the LLaVA family and DeepSeek-Janus but 70 percent for Qwen2-VL, rising to 100 percent for all three scaled-up variants. Steering helps most in early layers, not at the MAC layer: InternVL2 at its crossover layer gains 0.3 percent or less, while at layer 3 linear steering reaches +3.4 and SAE +3.8, and the linear ladder falls off as depth approaches the crossover (+3.4 at layer 3, +2.4 at layers 6 and 13, 0.0 at layer 19, +0.3 at the crossover layer, for InternVL2 on 293 evaluated samples). SAE steering is more precise where it wins (on LLaVA-v1.5 at layer 4 it improves 6 samples with no degradation, against linear's 6 improved and 2 degraded; on InternVL2 at layer 3, 14:3 against 16:6), but on Qwen2-VL, which has the widest logit gap, linear steering beats it at every layer. All reported configurations are net-positive, between +1.4 and +3.8 points, with the largest gains on the weakest baselines; scaling raises final-win rates (InternVL2 from 58 to 87 percent, Qwen2-VL from 75 to 96) and moves crossovers earlier while leaving the dissociation intact.

## Limits and open problems they name
The images are synthetic counterfactuals, so natural conflicts are untested. The Size split turns out to be partly keyword matching — reversing the question's polarity drops agreement to 48 to 54 percent — and the authors say to treat size numbers with caution; InternVL2-26B is also less stable under paraphrase than the rest. Patching covers nine models at 100 samples each and BLIP-2 is not patched at all. Steering runs only on the 7–8B models. The L2 metric is an overall divergence, and only the probe partly narrows it to the attribute. They also name the ambiguity they cannot resolve with these stimuli: a failure could be arbitration, or the image evidence could be encoded in a form the readout finds but the model cannot use.

## What we take from it
**A warning and a method.** The warning: the last-token convention of text-only patching returned 0 to 1 percent flips on seven of these models, which would have been reported as "the layer does not matter" — the *position of the intervention*, not the layer, was doing the work, and the folder's own convention of reading the last token would have produced a false negative on a real causal effect. Our interventions need their token scope declared and the alternative scope run, especially when the evidence is spread across many input tokens. **The separation to steal** is the one between "the quantity is present" and "the quantity decides": an L2/probe pair on one side and a final-layer logit gap on the other, with the correlation between them printed, and a sample-level AUC on the presence measure alone — here 0.528, which is the number that shows a probe can be near ceiling on a state that carries no information about the outcome. **The design lesson for editing** is that the layer where a competition is *observable* is not the layer where it can be *changed*: the crossover was the peak of readability and the worst place to steer, while a much earlier layer moved accuracy more, so any diagnostic layer our model names must be tested as an intervention site rather than assumed to be one. Two smaller things worth copying: the reading of an intervention that can only push in one direction (zero reverse flips) as evidence of a *mediating* rather than merely *correlated* site, and the retained-fraction on the untouched arm as the collateral count we keep insisting on — the SAE residual-application trick exists only because direct replacement cost 2 to 5 points of baseline accuracy in pilot runs, which is the same failure mode as a zero-sum lever, caught before it was published.
