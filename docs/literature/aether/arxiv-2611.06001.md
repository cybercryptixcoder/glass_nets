# How Far Can You Steer? Behavior–Capability Frontiers in Contrastive Activation Addition

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.06001
- **Authors / organisation:** Deepanshu Raj (single author) — The University of Chicago
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06001
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, main text plus Appendices A–H, every table read as cells; figures 2–15 via captions only)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Steering papers usually report whether a direction moves the behaviour it was built for, and stop there. The author's argument is that this answers a narrower question than practitioners need: an intervention is only usable if it moves the target behaviour *and* leaves the rest of the model's competence roughly intact, and both properties vary with the layer the vector is added at and the strength it is added with. So steering is reframed as a constrained selection problem — pick the layer and coefficient that maximise behavioural movement subject to a declared ceiling on capability loss — and the paper asks how much of a validation-selected operating point actually survives when it is frozen and then tested on data that had no say in it. The larger goal is an honest statement of the *range* in which steering works, rather than a single number.

## What they studied
Four instruction-tuned models of similar scale from three families: Llama 2 7B Chat, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, Mistral 7B Instruct v0.3 (all 32 decoder blocks, width 4096) and Qwen2.5 7B Instruct (28 blocks, width 3584). Seven behaviours taken from the CAA setting: sycophancy, refusal, hallucination, corrigibility, survival instinct, myopic reward, AI coordination. Unit of analysis is a mean-difference direction per behaviour per layer, added to the residual stream at the answer-bearing and generation positions; the frontier itself is a (behaviour, layer, coefficient) operating point.

## How they did it
Directions are the CAA mean difference of residual-stream activations at the answer-bearing position over contrastive positive/negative answer pairs, built independently at every layer from a vector-construction split that excludes validation, capability and test data. Because raw vector norms differ across behaviours, each direction is rescaled to the layer's mean behaviour-vector norm; because residual-stream scale differs across depth and architecture, the signed coefficient α is additionally reported as a dimensionless relative dose η = α·m_ℓ / r_ℓ, where r_ℓ is the expected residual norm on calibration data. Layer selection uses behavioural data only: a coarse scan of every block at α = ±1 picks an anchor by the separation between the two signs, and a nine-layer window around it is searched on a nine-point coefficient grid, taking the layer that maximises the *sum* of the best positive and best negative directional gain. With the layer then fixed, coefficients are chosen under a capability constraint measured on MMLU, ARC-Challenge and HellaSwag: for each benchmark the paired-bootstrap lower 95% confidence bound on the accuracy change is taken, the minimum over the three is the margin, and a coefficient is feasible at budget ε when that margin is no worse than −ε. The feasible sign-compatible coefficient with the largest directional gain is frozen, and α = 0 remains available so the frontier may keep the unsteered model. Freezing is the point of the design: held-out behaviour, capability holdout and all later audits cannot trigger reselection. Post-selection audits are specificity against three null families (norm-matched isotropic random directions, label-shuffled constructions that keep the pipeline but randomise the pair's sign, and random directions calibrated to the CAA intervention's off-target next-token KL on prompts from the other six behaviours, within a 10% tolerance), cross-behaviour causal transfer over all 42 ordered behaviour pairs at seven fractional depths and a fixed relative dose of 0.20, secondary capabilities (64-question GSM8K subset, WikiText-2 perplexity over 32,768 scored targets), out-of-distribution transfer (within-domain-built sycophancy directions evaluated on the opposite domain; frozen hallucination directions moved to 150 binary TruthfulQA items) and open-ended generation judged blind on a 0–4 per-behaviour rubric.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanistic account — nothing in the paper says which components implement a behaviour, and the author says so explicitly, framing cosine geometry as a coarse descriptor and the transfer test as causal influence along the extracted directions rather than a decomposition. The product is an *operating frontier*: for each behaviour, model, steering sign and capability budget, a frozen (layer, coefficient) pair with its expected behavioural gain and its measured capability cost, plus a survival verdict on independent data. It is a partial, evaluation-relative claim by construction: the paper insists the frontier should be read as "preserved on this suite at this budget", not as preservation of capability in general.

## How they judged success
Directional behavioural gain from a two-alternative score (logits for the behaviour-matching and behaviour-opposed answers, renormalised over the pair), so the score tracks the semantic alternative rather than a fixed answer letter; paired percentile bootstrap with 10,000 resamples everywhere. A nonzero operating point "survives" only if both the behavioural-gain lower confidence bound is above zero on the test split and the capability margin on each protected benchmark is at or above −ε on an independent holdout split. Budgets are 1%, 3% and 5% absolute percentage points. Specificity is scored as the mean paired CAA advantage over each null family and the fraction of operating points above that family's 97.5th percentile. Geometry versus causal transfer uses signed Spearman correlation over 1176 model-depth-behaviour measurements with an exact permutation over the 7 behaviour labels applied synchronously across blocks; the dose-versus-secondary-capability association uses Pearson and Spearman between |η| and GSM8K loss or ΔNLL. Open-ended judging is a blinded LLM judge with cluster-bootstrap intervals and matched triplets of negative/baseline/positive conditions.

## What they found
Layer localisation holds within a model and moves across models: Llama 2 selects layers 11–13, Llama 3.1 12–16 and Mistral 13–15 (fractional depth roughly 0.35–0.52), while Qwen2.5 selects 17–19 of 28 (0.63–0.70); within Llama 2 the behaviour still matters, with survival instinct at 11, sycophancy at 12 and the remaining five at 13. Validation-selected behavioural gains transfer strongly — across the 109 unique nonzero interventions, validation versus test Pearson r = 0.969 and Spearman ρ = 0.945, mean gain 0.125 validation against 0.124 test, mean absolute difference 0.019, per-model Spearman 0.926–0.959. Capability is the fragile half, and the budget dominates: at ε = 0.01 the frontier keeps α = 0 for 32 of 56 model-behaviour-direction combinations, and of the 24 nonzero points 23 retain behavioural support but only 9 pass capability holdout, so joint survival is 9/24 = 37.5%; at ε = 0.03 it is 40 of 55 (72.7%) and at ε = 0.05 43 of 56 (76.8%), so the 1%→3% step buys most of the range and 5% adds little. Per-model, at 1% Mistral preserves 4 of 5 nonzero points while none of Llama 2's three pass; at 3% joint survival runs from 8 of 14 (Mistral) to 12 of 14 (Qwen2.5). The limiting protected benchmark differs by model — MMLU for Llama 3.1, HellaSwag for the others — which is the stated reason for constraining the minimum over the suite. Specificity holds: mean held-out CAA gain 0.124 (95% CI 0.105–0.144) against null means of 0.001 (norm-matched), 0.001 (label-shuffled) and 0.004 (off-target KL), with mean paired advantages of 0.123, 0.123 and 0.120 and exceedance fractions 96.3%, 72.5% and 84.4%; KL matching succeeded for 94.7% of attempted controls, but the per-model table shows label shuffling is the weak null (53.1% on Llama 3.1, 70.0% on Mistral) while norm-matched and off-target-KL controls stay at 80–100%. Geometry is informative only weakly: signed cosine similarity against signed causal transfer gives ρ = 0.290 (permutation p = 1.98×10⁻⁴, per-model 0.214–0.372), the unsigned association is null (ρ = 0.042, p = 0.598), and transfer magnitude peaks near fractional depth 0.42 at about 0.036 against 0.006–0.016 at the other anchors. Passing the frontier does not mean the model is otherwise unharmed: at the frozen ε = 0.03 points WikiText-2 perplexity rises 6.63% on average with ΔNLL strongly tied to dose (ρ = 0.819, per-model Spearman 0.562–0.908), and GSM8K accuracy falls 10.24 points on average although only 14 of 56 conditions have intervals supporting a decrease. OOD transfer is behaviour dependent: 14 of 16 cross-domain sycophancy conditions keep the intended sign and 9 are supported, with median OOD-to-in-domain retention of 46.5% among the nine conditions with supported in-domain gains; hallucination moved to TruthfulQA gives 7 of 8 conditions in the intended direction, all supported, the exception being Qwen2.5's negative direction. Open-ended generation is the sharpest boundary: sycophancy is monotone across negative/base/positive (1.35, 2.29, 2.77), refusal is asymmetric (2.62 → 2.25 negative, 2.51 positive), and hallucination loses ordering entirely (baseline 1.86, both signs about 2.08); 12 of 288 matched triplets contained a nonterminating condition after a 4096-token confirmation rerun, all on Llama 3.1 (seven under negative refusal, five under positive hallucination steering).

## Limits and open problems they name
Four 7–8B instruction-tuned models, all CAA, no larger or mixture-of-experts models and no other intervention family, so the frontier shape is not shown to generalise. The preservation claim is scoped to the three protected benchmarks, and the paper demonstrates the gap itself with WikiText-2 and GSM8K. Core behavioural evaluation is binary choice; the OOD and open-ended audits cover fewer behaviours and prompts and rest on behaviour-specific rubrics whose judge scores were never validated against human annotations. Cosine similarity is called a coarse description of geometry, and the transfer experiment does not decompose mechanism. The author also warns that the strict 1% budget is sensitive to finite-sample uncertainty, so low survival there may reflect conservative confidence bounds rather than real degradation.

## What we take from it
A protocol and a vocabulary. (1) *A constraint, not a second metric*: report an intervention by the behavioural gain it buys and resolve the capability side with a one-sided confidence bound on the protected measure (here LCB95 ≥ −ε per benchmark, minimum over the suite) rather than a point estimate — a point estimate at a 1% budget is within sampling noise of the acceptance line, and the paper's own 37.5% is partly that. (2) *Freeze before you test*: the layer is chosen from behavioural validation, the coefficient from capability data, and then nothing is reselected; every later audit is a test of a fixed claim. Our battery should adopt the same firebreak, because a report where reselection is still permitted measures the search, not the claim. (3) *Normalise the dose, not the coefficient*: α is incomparable across layers because the residual norm grows with depth, so the relative dose η = α·m_ℓ/r_ℓ is the right cross-layer and cross-model axis for any strength comparison we publish; the paper still reports both, which is the honest form. (4) *A behaviour-specific, model-specific layer*: useful depths are a narrow band per model that shifts by nearly a third of the network between families (0.35–0.52 against 0.63–0.70), so a single published "best layer" for a steering family is a claim about one model, and a layer sweep is not optional. (5) *The three nulls and their ranking*: norm matching tests the dose, label shuffling tests the contrastive pairing, and off-target-KL matching tests functional disruption elsewhere — the third is the strongest and the second is demonstrably weak, so a specificity claim that leans on shuffled labels alone is weaker than it looks. (6) *Geometry predicts sign, not size*: a signed-cosine/causal-transfer rank correlation of 0.29 with a null unsigned association is the concrete form of "alignment is not efficacy", and it is a warning for any post-hoc selection rule that ranks candidate directions by cosine to a known one. (7) *A capability claim carries its evaluation set with it*, and the sub-additivity is real here: three benchmarks pass while perplexity and arithmetic degrade, so our reports should name the suite in the same sentence as the claim.
