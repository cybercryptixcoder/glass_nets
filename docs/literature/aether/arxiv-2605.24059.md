# Spectral Probe-Circuits: A Three-Step Recipe for Identifying Attention-Head Circuits in Pretrained Transformers

- **Date:** 2026-05-22 (v1); this entry reads v2, 2026-06-03

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Circuit hunting is normally post hoc: pick a fully trained model, pick a capability, ablate heads one at a time and keep whatever hurts. That costs a forward pass per condition, and the capability has to be named before the search starts, so it cannot tell you where interesting computation is happening before you know what to look for. The author wants a pre-filter that reads a *training trajectory* rather than a single checkpoint and needs no labels and no attribution gradients — something that ranks heads by "is sustained content-dependent computation happening here at all", after which a cheap pattern screen specialises the ranking to a named task and a standard ablation makes the causal claim falsifiable. The stated larger goal is a methodology that ports across architecture families and training pipelines, so that a circuit claim from one model carries evidential weight for another. The paper is explicitly the anchor of a three-paper programme, with companions on developmental trajectories and on composed tasks.

## What they studied
Seven model configurations spanning roughly an 8x parameter range: six TinyStories 51M seeds (8L/512d/16 heads, key-retrieval probe injected at rate 0.10), a Karpathy nanoGPT GPT-2 124M on FineWeb-10B with 89 logged checkpoints and a mid-training swap to OpenWebText, Pythia 160M, 410M and 1B (Pile, GPT-NeoX dense), OLMo 1B (Llama-style dense, DCLM) and OLMoE 1B-active/7B-total (Llama-style top-8/64 MoE, DCLM). The unit of analysis is the **attention head**, treated as a matrix: its attention output at a task-relevant query position across a fixed evaluation batch. Capabilities screened are induction, previous-token, duplicate-token, first-token/BOS, self and local — six fixed classes — plus single-pattern circuits (induction, prev-token) studied in depth.

## How they did it
Step 1: at every checkpoint, take each head's attention output at the query position over a fixed batch (2000 examples), forming an N-by-d_head matrix, compute its singular-value spectrum, and take the participation ratio — the exponentiated entropy of the normalised squared singular values, i.e. an effective rank. Rank heads by the *time integral* of the positive part of PR minus one, weighted by the change in log token count, so that sustained high dimensionality is rewarded and a noisy random-initialisation spike is clipped out. Step 2: for every head compute selectivity for each of the six attention classes — attention mass on the canonical target positions divided by the uniform-other baseline — and take the heads above a threshold (30x for class assignment, 50x for induction circuit membership, 100x for previous-token because its absolute values reach eight figures). Step 3: zero the per-head slice of the attention-output projection for the screened set and compare against two controls — a matched-random set of the same size in the same layers, and all heads in the picked layers as an upper bound. Thresholds were calibrated by an ablation-floor sweep on Pythia 410M and then transplanted to the rest of the panel, with a per-model re-validation sweep at 2/10/30/50/100x. The 51M panel is trained six times from different seeds on the same task, which lets the author ablate each seed's circuit on every seed's model — a full cross-seed matrix. Every number is measured on the author's own runs; nothing is inherited from cited work.

## What counts as an explanation here
A *recipe*, deliberately not a model of the mechanism: a ranked list of heads (a probe), a tiny circuit (3-6 heads) for a named capability, and a causal claim of the form "these heads, much more than an equal number of their layer neighbours, are what the capability runs through". The author is unusually explicit that each step is unsound alone — Step 1 is a general specialisation indicator that cannot distinguish induction from prev-token from duplicate-token; Step 2 is what makes the ranking task-specific and would face a multiple-testing problem without Step 1; Step 3 is what makes the claim falsifiable. There is no prediction of what any individual head computes, and no attempt to model the computation; the mechanistic story for the 51M circuit (heads attend back to the key position) is added post hoc by an attention-pattern measurement, not predicted.

## How they judged success
Top-1 and top-5 accuracy on a 2000-sequence synthetic induction batch (seed 42), plus per-example mean logit of the target token; for the 51M model, in-distribution and out-of-distribution probe accuracy and plain validation loss. The load-bearing metric is the **differential against matched-random in the same layers** — the ablation drop of the screened set minus the drop of an equal-size random set — with the all-heads-in-layer ablation as a saturation ceiling. Classification quality is scored as precision-at-k (how many of the top-k spectral picks land in a known class). Robustness checks: classification repeated at five query positions; eight extra matched-random seeds per 1B model; null-selectivity calibrated against a random non-special target position drawn 500 times per model; nine alternative trajectory features compared head-to-head against the PR-integral.

## What they found
- **The same task is solved by different heads on different seeds, and the spectral signal finds the seed-specific implementation without labels.** On s42 the four picks are all in layer 0 (L0H3, L0H6, L0H14, L0H15) with PR rising from about 2 at step 400 to about 22 at step 800; s271's picks are L6H{1,10} plus L7H{9,15} with no L0 head above a spread of 11 — the opposite end of the stack. Ablating s42's circuit on s271 still hurts (0.526 to 0.251), but ablating s271's circuit on s42 does nothing at any checkpoint, and removing all 32 heads in L6+L7 from s42 leaves probe accuracy at 0.94. Both seeds share the four L0 heads; only s271 recruits late-layer heads as well, and that broader circuit generalises better out of distribution (0.66 against 0.33).
- **On s42 the four-head ablation is specific and redundant:** probe_in drops 0.84 to 0.15 at step 4000 while the matched control set leaves it at 0.847; no single head is sufficient (largest single effect, L0H15, 0.84 to 0.60); ablating all 16 L0 heads tanks validation loss 1.71 to 3.96 but buys only a marginally larger probe drop (0.13 against 0.15), so the other twelve heads carry general LM, not the task. The four circuit heads attend to the KEY position at 42-95x the uniform baseline against 2-18x for all other L0 heads.
- **The fraction of heads doing identifiable specialised work is conserved at 17-19% across an 8x scale range, while circuits stay small.** Karpathy 124M 19.4%, Pythia 160M 18.8%, Pythia 410M 16.9%, and an independent null-p99 calibration lands at 18.1% and 18.5% on the two Pythia models without targeting that band. But it does *not* generalise across families (Pythia 1B 25.8%, OLMo 1B 4.7%, OLMoE 27.3%), and the author says so. Induction circuits are 3-11 heads throughout — sublinear in head count.
- **Distribution, not dilution.** On Pythia 410M, top-6 spectral picks drop induction by 43%, a mech-interp-derived set by 77%, and the all-head screen at 50x by 100% (11 heads, of which only 3 were in the spectral top-30). Larger models spread induction over more heads at lower per-head selectivity.
- **The three 1B-class models implement induction at three different granularities.** OLMo 1B is carried by two very sharp heads (L2H11, L4H12, both at 100x or above, and adding anything more changes nothing); OLMoE by four heads on a wide plateau (L5H10, L7H0, L9H8, L12H14, identical at 30x and 50x); Pythia 1B distributed across six-plus multi-role heads in the 10-50x band. All three circuits are 3-4 heads at the 50x threshold, and matched-random in the same layers has zero or positive effect.
- **Eliminating the prev-token circuit tanks induction** across the panel (Pythia 1B 4.05% to 0.00%, OLMo 1B 1.00% to 0.00%, OLMoE 4.80% to 0.30%), which recovers the standard compositional dependency from selectivity alone.
- **Every model in the panel has exactly one super-prev-token head** (Karpathy L6H9 at 27,776x, Pythia 160M L3H2 at 81,792x, Pythia 410M L5H2 at 23,634x), found by the integral ranking and missed by spread ranking.
- **The PR-integral wins over eight alternatives** — precision-at-30 0.97 against 0.93 for spread, max PR and mean-post-grok, and mean selectivity in the top five of 5,791x against 155-258x.
- **The one honest counter-example is in the author's own appendix.** The matched-random control on Pythia 1B was reported at a single seed giving a 36% release; across nine seeds the mean is 8% with a standard deviation of 8.2 points and a range of 0.65% to 28.3%, so the originally reported value sits at the top of the distribution, and three isolation controls failed to reproduce it. The author could not identify a mechanism and reports it as seed variance.

## Limits and open problems they name
Everything is computed on one fixed evaluation batch (seed 42), with cross-position consistency spot-checked on 124M only (78% prev-token, 79% self) and not characterised at 1B scale. Only six attention-pattern classes are screened, so negative name-movers, copy suppression and backup heads are out of scope. Causal scoring is restricted to top-1 and target logit, and the companion paper shows these can disagree. All natural-text models are single checkpoints; only the 51M panel tests seed variation, so whether the specific heads repeat across pre-trains at 1B is open. In the sink-dominated regime (54-78% of heads classifying as first-token) the spectral top-k is dominated by generic early-layer heads, so the recipe needs the all-head screen instead. The two thresholds (k and the selectivity cut) were calibrated on one model and transplanted, and the per-model sweep shows Pythia 1B's real circuit is six heads at 30x rather than three at 50x. MoE per-revision analysis is slow. The paper's own framing caveat is the sharpest thing in it: the recipe is a recipe, and no single step is a claim.

## What we take from it
- **A method: rank by the integral, not the peak or the spread.** The reasoning is that a head which genuinely does content-dependent work holds high effective rank for a sustained stretch, whereas a head that starts random and collapses has a *high* spread and a *low* integral — which is precisely the artefact an init-time statistic produces. For us this is the shape any trajectory-derived feature should have: monotone in sustained behaviour, clipped against the initial-condition floor, and normalised by fraction of training rather than raw step count.
- **A measure we should adopt by default: the differential against a matched-random set in the same layers, with an all-heads-in-layer ceiling.** Both controls answer a specific alternative — the first rules out "any equal number of heads in those layers would do", the second rules out "the layers matter and the picked heads are passengers" — and the paper's own appendix is the case study in why one seed of that differential is not enough when the effect is large: the control itself has a distribution, and reporting one draw from it is the same error as reporting one checkpoint.
- **A framing: pattern-selectivity and task-causality are separable statuses.** A screen tells you a head looks like an induction head; an ablation tells you the capability runs through it. The paper separates a *specificity* claim (this set beats matched-random) from an *identifiability* claim (this set is findable without labels) from a *predictive* claim (an earlier checkpoint's spectral signal recovers most of the final circuit) and uses the words consistently. Our entries have been blurring these; we should not.
- **A built-in redundancy check: the cross-seed ablation matrix.** Training the same architecture six times and ablating every seed's circuit on every seed's model is the cheapest possible demonstration that a method finds *the model's* implementation rather than *a* pattern — and it is the cleanest negative in the folder, because it shows that a circuit can be causally necessary in one seed and causally irrelevant in another trained on identical data with identical hyperparameters. Anything we say about "the" circuit for a task inherits that caveat.
- **A warning about super-specialists as a diagnostic of the model, not the task.** One near-perfect prev-token head per model, in a different layer each time, is the kind of invariant worth knowing: it says a single degree of freedom tends to get compressed into one head, and it gives us a calibration target for any read-out we build — a method that cannot find the super-prev-token head is not sensitive enough.
- **A caution on thresholds.** The 50x default catches 94% of Pythia 1B's effect, 95% of OLMo's and 100% of OLMoE's; the per-model floor differs and one model's full circuit is twice the size the transplanted default reports. Any circuit-size claim we publish should come with its ablation-floor curve, not a single point at one threshold.
