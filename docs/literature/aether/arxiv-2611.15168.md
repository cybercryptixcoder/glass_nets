# MAST: Medoid-Anchored Subspace Steering for Visual Grounding in Multimodal LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.15168
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhitao Chen, Zhenxin Cai, Jin Sha — School of Electronic Science and Engineering, Nanjing University; Jingyuan Li — College of Electronic Science and Technology, National University of Defense Technology
- **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15168
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Activation steering for multimodal models is usually one vector: the mean of positive-minus-negative activation differences over all support examples, injected at one layer. In practice this works on some visual subtasks and barely moves others, and the paper's opening observation is that the unevenness is not a tuning problem. Their claim is geometric: different visual operations occupy separate regions of activation space, the pooled mean sits in the empty space between them and is faithful to none, and the local directions of the separate regions are close to mutually orthogonal, so averaging them shrinks the vector while the residual disagreements cancel. If that is true, the unit of a steering vector has to change — from one direction for the whole model to a small set of per-region directions with a routing rule that decides which ones a given input gets. The larger goal is a training-free and inspectable control mechanism for a frozen model, which is the same goal our probe work has, reached from the intervention side.

## What they studied
LLaVA-v1.5-7B as the primary model and Qwen2-VL-7B for cross-model check, one intervention layer each (layer 16 of LLaVA-v1.5's 32 LM layers, layer 4 for Qwen2-VL), chosen from a layer scan on held-out data. Evaluation on CV-Bench (four visual-grounding subtasks: Relation, Count, Distance, Depth) and POPE (three splits: Random, Popular, Adversarial). The unit of analysis is the **sample-level contrastive direction** — one difference vector per support example, from the mean residual over a teacher-forced correct completion minus the mean over an incorrect one — grouped into task-pure clusters, with the cluster medoid as the representative object.

## How they did it
Offline: split a support set by task (128 CV-Bench samples per task), and for each task run k-medoids with five clusters over the *answer-free* last-prompt context states; store each cluster's medoid (its context state and its steering vector), the cluster mean direction, and the leading right-singular vectors of the centred intra-cluster directions as a local basis of rank 8. Online: for a query, take the last-prompt state of the answer-free prompt, score cosine similarity against every medoid context, keep the top three, softmax them into aggregation weights at temperature 0.5, and for each selected cluster blend the raw medoid direction with its truncated local reconstruction using a coefficient that leans on the denoised version when the cluster looks unreliable — reliability being a fixed convex mix of relative cluster size, compactness and routing confidence, with weights 0.4/0.3/0.3. The sum of the weighted blends is added to the residual stream at every prompt position of the first forward pass and to each generated token. The extension reweights the local basis per query: a four-way logistic probe on the same answer-free state predicts which of the four tasks the query belongs to, and that posterior softly mixes per-task direction-family weights that were selected on validation only (one direction family per task, weighted by singular-value scaling, all others zeroed; Depth keeps all-ones because no single family beat equal weighting). Three controls carry the argument: a norm-matched random direction; every steering baseline given its own injection strength tuned on the same validation split, so no baseline runs at a strength the authors chose for it; and a task-oracle arm in which ground-truth task labels choose the subspace direction and weight, giving an upper bound in the four-task average. Ablations: leave-one-out over the four components, and a controlled comparison holding the backbone, routing, rank, layer and support fixed while swapping local subspaces for global PCA, raw local directions, and random local bases.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **steering vector, plus the geometry that justifies its shape**: an offline dictionary of per-cluster directions with a local low-rank basis for each, and a per-query rule that assembles one direction from the top three of them. It is a control mechanism, not a description of the model — nothing about the network is named or described — but it is an unusually explicit statement of what has to be true for a single direction to be the right object. Partial by construction: one layer, a fixed cluster count, a hand-written reliability formula, and a probe that is only used to reweight.

## How they judged success
Accuracy on the four CV-Bench tasks and four-task average, accuracy and F1 on three POPE splits, all on a task-stratified test split disjoint from the 20% validation split used for every selection decision; per-task McNemar paired tests against each of seven steering baselines with discordant-pair counts reported; the task-oracle as an upper bound; leave-one-out ablation averaged over tasks and broken out per task; a one-at-a-time hyperparameter sweep; clustering-quality statistics (separability, Davies-Bouldin, top-1 routing of a held-out query to its own task's medoids); and cross-seed variance over three k-medoids initialisations.

## What they found
On LLaVA-v1.5 the four-task average goes 50.94% → 68.00%, which is +17.06, with per-task gains of 19.42 (Relation), 6.35 (Count), 16.25 (Distance) and 26.25 (Depth); the tuned and untuned static baselines stay within a point or two of no-steering, and two of them land below it (CAA 49.72%, Act-Add 50.56%, Linear-AcT 44.97%), which is the paper's own evidence that the cross-cluster conflict is a property of the method class rather than of its hyperparameters. On Qwen2-VL, whose no-steering average is already 77.40%, MAST reaches 80.84% and the best score on all four tasks. The probe-reweighted variant adds +8.09 on LLaVA-v1.5 (76.09% average, best gain on Count, 53.81% → 74.60%) and +1.45 on Qwen2-VL. The oracle sits 16.63 points above MAST on LLaVA-v1.5 and only 2.09 above on Qwen2-VL, so the amount of headroom for direction specialisation is a property of the model. McNemar against the seven baselines is significant on all four LLaVA-v1.5 tasks (worst case p = 2.0e-3) and on three of four Qwen2-VL tasks, the exception being a near-saturated Count at p = 0.68. Geometry: per-task clustering separability 0.144–0.192 with Davies-Bouldin 1.769–2.142 and 100% top-1 same-task routing, falling to 76.69% when all twenty clusters are pooled against a 20% chance baseline; 17 leading singular directions cover at least 90% of the intra-cluster variance in 4,096 dimensions, with the most concentrated cluster needing five; the cosine between each cluster direction and the global mean is 0.364–0.654 on average per task, with one Relation cluster at −0.106 and one Count outlier at 0.063, and the pairwise similarity between medoid directions within a task runs 0.030 for Distance and 0.055 for Depth. Layer scan: positive gain spans layers 11–20 with a 12–16 plateau varying by under 1.6 points. Efficiency: 3.93 s offline and a 0.938 MiB bank against a 16 KiB global vector, with 0.65 µs per query for the routing kernel.

## Limits and open problems they name
The direction from a teacher-forced correct-minus-incorrect completion is transferred to answer-free generation, and the paper reports its own control on that assumption: replacing the contrast with an answer-free "use the visual evidence" versus "ignore the image" instruction pair, read at the same prompt position as routing, collapses the four-task average to 22.01% against 47.30% for the no-steering arm that was given the positive instruction — so under the frozen configuration the aligned contrast does not carry the effect, and the completion-conditioned transfer is retained by necessity rather than because it was validated. The probe is the weakest link and they say so: it keeps only 36.50% of the posterior mass on Depth and puts 49.62% on Distance, and MAST-PR's own Depth score falls from 99.58% to 90.42% as a result; forcing the Distance weights onto Depth queries instead drops it to 12.08%, and a fixed confidence gate at 0.60 recovers Depth while keeping a 5.83-point average gain. Second limit: the two refinement components are not independent — removing multi-center routing gives 39.54%, *below* the 47.35% of stripping everything down to the global mean, so the local basis and shrinkage only help inside a multi-center regime. Third: gains are thin on POPE (+1.07/+0.73/+0.66 over no-steering across the three splits), the geometric tasks dominate the story, and there is a sharp Distance-only spike at layer 8 (+40 points) that degrades the other tasks on that layer. One methodological note the authors disclose rather than hide: greedy decoding and a fixed support set make every number deterministic, with 0.00 cross-seed standard deviation, so the three-seed stability table is a statement about k-medoids initialisation and not about evaluation noise.

## What we take from it
A **method** and a **warning**, and they are two halves of one point.

The method is the split between a *routing* read-out and a *steering* read-out, computed from different token positions: the routing state is the last prompt token with no answer in context, while the direction is a completion-span mean difference. That separation is free and it is what makes the routing claim checkable on its own terms — their held-out top-1 same-task routing is 100% per task and 76.69% with all twenty clusters pooled, which is a claim about a probe with no answer leakage, before any steering is evaluated. Our battery routinely fuses the two, using one probe to both decide something and define the direction that acts, and this paper shows the cheap version of the fix.

The warning is the more quotable one: **a mean over a mixture is a vector that belongs to no component**, and the diagnostic is a cosine table rather than any downstream score. Their per-task average similarity between the pooled mean and the clusters is 0.36–0.65; two clusters are at or near zero, one is negative. The same arithmetic applies to every pooled direction we compute — a "the model declines" vector pooled across three causes, a refusal direction averaged over a dataset with several registers, an aggregated attribution over items that fall in different circuits — and it means the pooled vector's effect size is a function of how many components it averaged and how mutually orthogonal they were. Two measurements to adopt: the cosine between the pooled direction and each subgroup direction, published as a table, and the pairwise cosines *within* the pooled set. Where they are near zero, the pooled direction is a shrinkage artefact and the honest object is a set of directions plus a routing rule.

Three smaller things worth copying. The **task-oracle** arm is a cheap upper bound and it is reported in a way that changes the reading of the result: the 16.63-point gap on LLaVA-v1.5 versus 2.09 on Qwen2-VL tells you which model has structure left to exploit, so a win over baselines is never presented as if it were close to the ceiling. The **degenerate-variant check** — that removing the routing component is worse than removing everything — is a useful pattern for us because it catches component refits that look independent on paper but are not. And the **efficiency accounting in the right unit** (a bank of directions against one vector, 0.938 MiB against 16 KiB, plus microseconds per query) is the form a white-box model's overhead has to be stated in if it is ever to be placed inside a running loop.
