## What they wanted, and why
The Linear Representation Hypothesis says concepts are linear directions in activation space, and a stronger version says the right geometry for those directions is an inner product whitened by the unembedding second moment, under which causally separable concepts come out orthogonal. If that were true, aligning two models after whitening should beat plain orthogonal alignment at moving concepts across languages. The authors set out to test exactly that. The test failed, and the failure is what they report as the paper: once they had every model's unembedding eigenbasis in hand, they asked where concept directions actually sit in it, and found a systematic, replicated pattern with consequences for every read-out our survey cares about.

## What they studied
Seventeen decoder-only base models spanning roughly 0.5B to 14B parameters and hidden widths 896 to 5120 — Llama 3.2 (1B, 3B), Qwen 2.5 (0.5B–14B), Qwen 3 (0.6B–8B), Gemma 2 (2B, 9B), Mistral-7B-v0.3, SmolLM2-1.7B, and two mixture-of-experts models (OLMoE-1B-7B, JetMoE-8B) — plus Llama-3.1-8B for the SAE and functional tests. The unit of analysis for the headline statistics is the **model**, not the concept, because concepts inside one model share an eigenbasis and are therefore not independent replicates. The objects are 27 word-level counterfactual concept directions in four categories from a published word-pair set (verb morphology, semantic, grammatical, language pairs), later expanded by 15 further BATS semantic categories to 22 semantic concepts in total. Directions are estimated three independent ways: difference of means over final-layer pooled activations, top-k differentially activating SAE features from public Gemma Scope and Llama Scope releases, and L2-regularised logistic-regression weight vectors.

## How they did it
They build a regularised uncentred second moment of the unembedding rows over a token set, take its eigendecomposition, and project each concept vector onto that basis. Two summary statistics are computed: the fraction of the vector's energy falling on each eigenvector, accumulated as a curve against accumulated variance, and the signed area between that curve and the uniform diagonal, which they call Gini deviation and which is negative when energy sits in low-variance eigenvectors. A second statistic, spectral centre of mass, is the cumulative variance at which the concept-energy curve reaches one half, so a value near one means the concept lives in the quiet tail. For the causal-geometry test they compare whitened alignment against a fake whitening built from the true eigenvalues with Haar-random eigenvectors, so any gain attributable to the actual eigendirections versus merely to the spectral shape is separated. The sharper control for anti-concentration is a **norm-matched random-direction null in the same eigenbasis**, because random directions in an anisotropic spectrum are themselves pushed toward the tail. Two functional tests follow: a split-injection steering experiment that projects a concept onto its top-k eigenvectors and separately onto its bottom-k eigenvectors, injects each at four mid-depth layers and six strengths across five models, and measures perplexity on held-out passages; and a part-of-speech probing experiment that trains 13-class logistic classifiers on activations restricted to the top-k or bottom-k subspace across eight models, with a full-dimensional probe as the ceiling.

## What counts as an explanation here
A geometric, model-level claim about where semantic information sits in the spectrum of a single weight matrix, plus a proposed functional division of labour: the high-variance head carries syntax, the low-variance tail carries word-level semantics, and the forward pass rotates concept information out of the vocabulary geometry and into the quiet subspace. It is explicitly partial and predictive rather than a circuit. Nothing here localises a concept to heads or components; the claim is about an eigenspace, the causal test is the split injection, and the authors label the mechanism a candidate rather than a rule.

## How they judged success
Model-level one-sample tests on Gini deviation across 17 models; Wilcoxon signed-rank and paired tests for category comparisons; a norm-matched random-direction null with per-model true-minus-null gaps; SAE and probe replication as extraction-independent evidence; a random token-pair null (1,000 pairs per model) for the unembedding contrast; a matched-spectrum randomisation with 5 seeds over 17 models × 4 language pairs for the alignment claim; a stated **validity window** for steering (a configuration counts only if neither arm raises perplexity past a threshold, so that a comparison between two collapsed arms is not mistaken for interference) with paired Cohen's d over per-concept differences; and POS probing with 5-fold cross-validation plus a token-level bootstrap, a Chinese-treebank control, and a sweep over three spectral cutoffs. A claims table maps each conclusion to its evidence and to a stated strength of robust, partial or suggestive — an explicit self-grading device the folder should copy.

## What they found
The alignment result is a clean null: whitened alignment wins 50.3 per cent of comparisons against naive orthogonal alignment, the fake-eigenvector version with a matched spectrum wins 51.0 per cent, and the paired test is indistinguishable from zero (p = 0.95); so whatever benefit exists is spectral regularisation, not meaningful causal eigendirections. Anti-concentration is large and uniform — mean Gini deviation −0.282 with all 17 models negative (model-level t = −11.5, p = 3.8×10⁻⁹), the weakest category still negative in all 17, and the expanded semantic set reproducing it at −0.278. But the raw statistic fails the null: random unit vectors in the same spectra are themselves negative (−0.16 to −0.28), and against norm-matched random directions only 13 of 17 models remain more tail-aligned, with a mean true-minus-null gap of −0.082 — Llama 3.2 (both sizes), Mistral-7B and OLMoE do not clear it. The two summary statistics also disagree in a way the authors print rather than suppress: the centre-of-mass gap is positive on average (+0.169) but small or slightly negative for Llama 3.2 and OLMoE, i.e. a curve can lie below the diagonal while its centre of mass sits near the random baseline. Extraction-independent replication is strong: SAE features reach mean Gini −0.230 to −0.276 across three checkpoints in two families, and probes reach −0.167 and −0.185 with accuracy above 0.99, while probes fail outright on Gemma-2-9b (21 of 26 concepts at chance), which they exclude from probe claims rather than explain away. The dual geometry is the sharpest result: concept-specific unembedding-row differences **concentrate** in high-variance directions (mean Gini +0.090 across 11 models; +0.046 to +0.103 against random-pair means of −0.071 to −0.124, all p < 10⁻⁴), the opposite sign from activation-space directions on the same concepts in the same basis. Depth carries the rotation: near-neutral at the embedding layer (−0.009), tail-aligned from the first block (−0.171) and −0.277 by the final layer, troughing near mid-depth. Functionally, inside the validity window 13 of 30 configurations qualify and 7 of those reach significance, all favouring the high-variance arm (paired effects up to +1.19 on Gemma-2-2b, +1.09 on Llama-3.1-8B), with no significant reversal inside the window and one significant reversal outside it where the quiet arm had already driven perplexity up by a factor of about two thousand. POS probing agrees in 6 of 8 models (gaps +0.016 to +0.052) and reverses on both Qwen 2.5 models (−0.014, −0.019), a reversal that survives three cutoffs and reappears on a Chinese treebank for both Qwen families, and that the authors leave as an open anomaly instead of attributing to language mismatch.

## Limits and open problems they name
The concepts are word-level counterfactual pairs and nothing is claimed about abstract, relational or discourse-level concepts. The unembedding second moment is one matrix; an activation-covariance spectrum might carry additional structure. The layer analysis is descriptive and a causal per-layer study of the rotation is future work. The steering validity window is post-hoc, applied to a completed sweep, and the effect size is strength-dependent, so it is not a constant per model; Mistral-7B has a single in-window configuration and is undetermined rather than null. Perplexity rests on ten passages, so per-configuration estimates are noisy, and per-layer interference was not logged. POS significance uses token-level bootstrap rather than sentence-clustered resampling, the syntactic reading rests on English data, and only two instruction-tuned checkpoints were tested as a control. Both subspaces are far below full-dimensional probe accuracy, so the syntactic bias is a bias and not a partition.

## What we take from it
Three things, in order of value.

First, the strongest reusable instrument: **a raw spectral (or variance, or second-moment) statistic of a direction is meaningless until it is compared with norm-matched random directions in the same basis.** This paper's headline number is significant at p = 3.8×10⁻⁹ against a uniform baseline and then loses a quarter of its supporting models against the matched null, with the mean gap shrinking from −0.282 to −0.082 and the count from 17 of 17 to 13 of 17. Any claim in our account of the form "this direction, feature or dictionary column lives in a particular part of the spectrum" goes through the matched null before it is stated.

Second, a **two-statistic rule**: an area-style summary and a centre-of-mass summary can disagree, and when they do the claim is about the shape of the curve rather than about a location. We report both, and any sentence naming *where* information sits uses the centre-of-mass or an explicit cutoff rather than the area.

Third, a **functional-anatomy warning**: concept directions in static vocabulary space and the same concepts in contextualised activation space point into opposite parts of the spectrum, so a read-out calibrated on unembedding rows is not transferable to activation space — the geometry is not a property of the concept, it is a property of the view. This is the spectral explanation for the read-out fragility the last several entries kept finding empirically.
