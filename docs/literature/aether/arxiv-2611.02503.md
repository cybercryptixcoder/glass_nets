# Towards Multi-Value Alignment via Value Localization in LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.02503
- **Authors / organisation:** Xueqi Ma, Yanbei Jiang, Sarah M. Erfani (University of Melbourne); Xingjun Ma (Fudan University); James Bailey (Monash University)
- **Date:** 2026-11-02 (arXiv v1; NeurIPS paper checklist included)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02503
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
The paper asks whether human "values" in a language model — not just safety, but response qualities like coherence and verbosity — are carried by findable, individually addressable components, and whether finding them lets you dial several of them at once without retraining. The motivation is that standard alignment collapses plural preferences into one scalar reward, offers no account of *why* helpfulness and harmlessness trade off, and gives no knob at inference time. The authors frame this as a mechanistic alternative to multi-objective fine-tuning: localise, then steer. A secondary ambition is that if values live in small dedicated head sets, then value trade-offs are structural facts about the model rather than artefacts of a preference dataset.

## What they studied
Six decoder-only instruct models across three families and two scales each: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-4B, Yi-1.5-9B, Yi-1.5-6B. The unit of analysis is the **attention head**, treated as a component whose output can be masked or shifted. The things localised are six named "value dimensions": harmlessness, honesty and helpfulness (the paper's "3H", the ones it treats as genuinely value-like) plus complexity, verbosity and coherence (response-quality dimensions). Probing activations are taken at the last token of a question-plus-answer string, per head, per layer, using a linear probe per (layer, head) ranked by held-out accuracy.

## How they did it
For each dimension, a dataset of positive and negative examples is assembled: TruthfulQA for honesty (817 questions split 4:1), the single-turn subset of HH-RLHF for harmlessness (49,436 train / 2,612 test, chosen responses positive, rejected negative), and HelpSteer2 for the other four, binarised at item-specific cut points — rating 4 positive against 0–1 negative for helpfulness and coherence, ratings 3–4 positive against 0 negative for complexity and verbosity. A separate linear probe is trained on every head's last-token activation, and heads are ranked by test accuracy; "value heads" are the top 15% for a dimension. Functional claims come from masking: the head output is scaled by a small epsilon, and the authors sweep K to compare masking K value heads against masking K random heads in equal number. Behaviour is scored by an LLM judge (Qwen3-30B) emitting a yes/no per response, with validation against GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro and a human annotator on 50 responses per value. Steering uses the difference of class means per head as a direction, scaled by that head's activation standard deviation along the direction, with strength alpha in {0.3, 0.5, 1.0}; for several values at once the per-value directions on shared heads are combined linearly with weights summing to at most one in absolute value, and the combination is justified by a first-order Taylor argument under stated local-linearity, sparsity and weak-coupling assumptions.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **probe, plus a direction, plus a component ranking** — the classic "this head is where the value lives" package, not a circuit and not a dictionary. Explanation = a sparse set of attention heads whose probe accuracy is high for a named dimension, an activation direction built from positive-minus-negative class means, and a causal claim carried by masking effects. It is explicitly partial: the authors say causality cannot be fully established, that the dimension list is heuristic rather than derived from an ethical theory, and they do not model what happens inside a head. It does predict something concrete — the sign of the change in a judged value score when you push a head set along its direction — and the paper's theorem is exactly a prediction of that sign under the three assumptions.

## How they judged success
Probe test accuracy per head (ranked, not thresholded absolutely), with the heatmap capped at 0.60. Masking is scored as judged positive-value accuracy on the dimension's own test set, plus JailbreakBench and Malicious Instruct for harmlessness, against an **equal-count random-head masking arm**, with five random seeds, mean ± SD, and a one-sided single-sample t-test. Neutral-corpus check: perplexity on WikiText-2 and Penn Treebank under value-head vs random-head masking. Label-choice ablation: Pearson/Spearman rank correlation between head-importance rankings under the balanced and strict binarisations, plus masking effect under each. Directional-structure check: cosine angles between value directions on heads shared by two dimensions. Steering is scored as judged positive-value rate against the unmodified model, with alpha sweeps and five stochastic-generation runs, and against MODPO and MOPFT trained with a matched one-step adaptation budget.

## What they found
Value-like dimensions are **sparse and localised, and the stylistic ones are not**. In Qwen3-8B under 3% of heads exceed 0.8 probe accuracy for harmlessness, helpfulness, coherence and honesty, while more heads contribute to verbosity and complexity; 3H heads concentrate in middle layers. Masking the top 15% collapses judged scores well beyond the equal-count random arm on the 3H dimensions across all six models, e.g. Llama-3.1-8B honesty 0.140 against 0.403 ± 0.084 random (p = 0.001), helpfulness 0.340 against 0.773 ± 0.114 (p = 0.001), coherence 0.220 against 0.687 ± 0.113 (p < 0.001); Yi-1.5-6B harmlessness 0.090 against 0.173 ± 0.043 (p = 0.006). Verbosity and complexity mostly *resist* this: masking their heads moves scores little or in the wrong direction (Llama-3.1-8B complexity 0.710 against 0.671 ± 0.070, p = 0.860; verbosity 0.960 against 0.921, p = 0.996), and several comparisons favour the random arm. Neutral-corpus perplexity is reported as essentially unchanged, but the appendix's table gives literally the same numbers for base, value-head and random-head masking in every cell and every model (e.g. Llama-3.1-8B WikiText-2 10.14 for all three), so the control as printed carries no variance information. Cross-dimension interaction is real: masking one value's heads costs performance on other correlated values, shared heads are numerous (86–116 per value pair), and the angle between directions on shared heads is smallest for harmlessness-helpfulness (62.8° median for Llama-3.1-8B, 70.0° for Qwen3-8B) and largest for harmlessness-honesty (93.5° and 85.9°). Inference-time steering gives small single-value gains (Yi-1.5-9B honesty 0.62 → 0.69) and modest multi-value gains, and it beats one-step MODPO/MOPFT on the 3H objectives for Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen3-4B (0.54/0.63/0.65 and 0.69/0.70/0.73), with the consolation that it needs no training.

## Limits and open problems they name
The dimension set is heuristic, not drawn from any ethical framework, so "value" here means six datasets' labels. Causality is not fully established — masking is a component-scale intervention and the authors say so. Only attention heads are examined, not MLPs or residual directions. They explicitly flag misuse: the same machinery suppresses safety-relevant behaviour.

## Limits we would add
The judge is a Qwen model grading Qwen and Llama outputs on yes/no prompts, validated on 50 human-labelled responses per value where human–LLM kappa is 0.875 for harmlessness but 0.619 for honesty, i.e. exactly on the dimension whose masking effect is the paper's headline. Neural-network probes on a small held-out split are used to rank thousands of heads with no stated held-out-probe correction, so the "top 15%" cut is a selection on a noisy statistic. Steering gains are one to seven points on a judge, several cells move against the target (Llama-3.1-8B HH-RLHF helpfulness 0.62 → 0.58; Qwen3-8B BeaverTails harmlessness 0.66 → 0.60), and the theorem's assumptions are precisely what the paper's own masking-conflict results call into question.

## What we take from it
Three things, and two warnings. The **transfer** is the reporting convention for a component ranking: probe accuracy to *nominate*, then an equal-count random-component arm to *verify*, then a neutral-corpus arm to show the component is not just doing general work, then a dimension-transfer arm to show what the nomination costs elsewhere. That four-column shape is what any "we found the heads/directions/features that carry X" claim in our white-box model should be required to fill. Second, the **cross-dimension table** is the cheapest statement of a thing our predictive model must represent: components shared between two properties, reported as a count of shared components plus the angle between the two transports, with the interesting cells being where the angle is near or past perpendicular. Third, the **conflict is the finding, not the win** — the fact that harmlessness and helpfulness partly share directions while harmlessness and honesty are near-orthogonal is a structural prediction one could test by intervention, and it is the kind of prediction a predictive model should be judged on. The **warnings**: identical perplexity numbers across treatment arms (base = value-masked = random-masked) mean the neutral-corpus control was not actually run per arm, or the table was copied — either way it must not be cited as evidence; and a judge-validated behaviour score whose agreement is much weaker on the dimension carrying the headline effect (kappa 0.619 on honesty against 0.875 on harmlessness) is exactly the stratified-reliability defect this folder has recorded repeatedly, so the masking effect should be read as "the judge can see the difference" rather than "the value moved".
