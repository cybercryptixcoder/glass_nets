# Beyond Components: Singular Vector-Based Interpretability of Transformer Circuits

- **Key:** arxiv-2511.20273
- **Authors / organisation:** Areeb Ahmad, Abhinav Joshi (equal contribution), Ashutosh Modi; Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur
- **Date:** 2025-11-25
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20273
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1; abstract, S1-S8, references, Appendices A-B; all numbers from printed tables and prose, none from figures)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit discovery scores whole attention heads and whole MLP blocks, which quietly assumes that whatever a component does is the component. The authors think that assumption is wrong in a specific and testable way: one head may run several unrelated jobs at once, and the standard picture has no way to see them because the unit of analysis never goes below the head. Their proposal is to keep the model exactly as it is and change only the coordinates — write each component's weights as an affine map, take its singular value decomposition, and treat each singular direction as a candidate unit of computation. The larger goal is a finer-grained mechanistic picture in which a head is a bundle of directions that can be named, masked and later stitched together, rather than an atom.

## What they studied

One model: GPT-2 small (124M parameters), weights frozen, read through TransformerLens. Three tasks with known reference circuits — Indirect Object Identification (IOI), Greater-Than (GT) and Gender Pronoun resolution (GP). The unit of analysis is a **singular direction** of an augmented weight matrix: four matrices per transformer block are decomposed (the QK product, the OV product, and the MLP's input and output projections), each with its bias folded in by appending a constant coordinate to the input. Effective ranks are reported as 64 for the augmented QK matrix and 65 for the augmented OV matrix; MLP matrices keep their full non-zero spectrum. Dataset splits are small: 1k train / 200 validation / 1k test for IOI, 2k / 500 / 2k for GT, and 1k / 155 / 307 for GP.

## How they did it

Step one is the rewrite: every component is expressed as an augmented matrix acting on `[1, x]` (constant coordinate appended to the input, bias folded into the matrix), so attention and MLP blocks live in one linear form and one SVD machinery applies to all of them. Step two attaches a learnable diagonal mask to each decomposition, so a direction can be scaled anywhere between off and on. The masks are the only trained parameters; the model's weights stay frozen. Step three optimises the masks on clean/corrupted pairs, with the forward pass fed both activations concatenated so that the mask can select directions that carry task-relevant rather than merely frequent signal, under a loss that is the KL divergence between the original and masked-model output distributions plus an L1 penalty on the mask entries. For the OV and MLP matrices the complement of the mask is retained in a parallel slot, which the authors argue keeps the downstream covariance roughly intact; for QK they deliberately drop the complement, because two partially masked similarity kernels inside one softmax would interfere. Step four reads the selected directions: their top tokens through the unembedding, their activation statistics conditioned on label, and their role in specific heads by looking at attention scores along single directions. Step five intervenes: in the GP task they swap the scalar activation of selected OV directions with the opposite-gender mean activation, optionally amplify the singular value by an integer factor, and measure pronoun logit difference and flip rates, plus a per-direction version that swaps only the masculine- or only the feminine-associated directions.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **direction-level attribution with a mask weight**: for each head or MLP, a short list of singular directions, each with a scalar importance, a token-side projection, an activation statistic and, in the GP case, a behavioural flip rate under a one-direction intervention. Understanding takes the form of a decomposition plus labels for individual axes — "start-of-sequence detector", "entity–action separator", "entity salience" — and the paper's stronger claim is that these same axes recur across heads and tasks as reusable primitives. The explanation is explicitly partial: the authors say the decomposition is a first step and that the causal role of each direction is not established end to end.

## How they judged success

Four questions structure the evaluation. Reconstruction: what fraction of directions can be pruned while keeping KLD low, measured with two sparsity definitions (relative to the directions actually trained, and to the full matrix), task accuracy before and after pruning, and exact match; the prose claims learned directions beat top-magnitude and random SVD baselines but the numbers for those baselines sit in an appendix section that is not in the rendered HTML, so the entry does not quote them. Alignment with prior circuits: whether heads identified in the IOI circuit literature (name mover, backup name mover, S-inhibition) carry higher mask values than other heads, shown as visualisations and as an averaged comparison by head type. Sub-function separation: reading the selected directions inside individual heads and checking that their attention patterns separate token categories, with mean ± SD attention reported for entity versus action tokens and for first versus second mentions. Causality: GP flip rates and logit differences from the scalar swap, at amplification scales 1, 2, 5, 10, 15 and 20, against the unmodified baseline on the same prompts.

## What they found

Reconstruction is the strongest printed result. With 91.32 percent of trainable directions and 98.66 percent of all directions suppressed on IOI, KLD is 0.21 ± 0.02, accuracy falls from 0.79 ± 0.05 to 0.70 ± 0.07 and exact match is 0.77 ± 0.06; GT prunes 95.21 / 99.26 percent at KLD 0.23 ± 0.03 with exact match 0.33 ± 0.06 and no accuracy column; GP prunes 96.81 / 99.51 percent at KLD 0.13 ± 0.01 with accuracy 0.77 ± 0.04 to 0.75 ± 0.04 and exact match 0.86 ± 0.07. Within heads, rank-1 slices behave like separable detectors: direction 7 of head 9.6 gives entity tokens +3.52 ± 1.42 and action tokens −4.44 ± 0.68, direction 28 is high on names and low on function words with first mentions above second mentions (5.22 against 2.07 for one name), and direction 1 is the same first-token detector in every head looked at, which is their evidence for reusable primitives. The head-9.6 directions carry different mask weights (0.53, 0.64, 0.97 for the three named roles), so the head is reported as a superposition of named sub-functions rather than as one job. The causal section is the one that needs care. Swapping all gender directions and amplifying by 20 moves the mean pronoun logit difference from +2.53 ± 1.48 to −42.31 ± 8.57 on masculine prompts and +2.84 ± 2.12 to −40.82 ± 15.44 on feminine prompts, with 100 percent flips in four of the six rows at high scale, but the logarithmic-looking growth of the logit swing with the amplification factor (from −1.10 at factor 1 to −42.31 at factor 20) is what an amplified additive term does by construction; at factor 1, before amplification, the same swap already flips 83.5 percent of "he" prompts to "she" and no "she" prompts to "he", and the "she" side flips only 4.7 percent, so the two directions are not symmetric in strength even though the paper's summary reads them as symmetric.

## Limits and open problems they name

Four limits are stated: each augmented matrix is decomposed in isolation, so interactions between components are outside the frame; the assumption that singular directions correspond to interpretable, causally relevant subroutines is supported by activation patterns but has no formal justification; masks are diagonal on fixed singular vectors, so only axis-aligned subspaces can be selected; and everything is GPT-2 small on three benchmark tasks. The authors add that the masking objective is a faithfulness objective, not a causal-mediation test, and that separating true mediation from representational correlation is left open — future work should combine this with causal tracing or activation patching. Two additional gaps are visible in the text rather than named: the auxiliary baselines the prose refers to are not in the rendered appendix, and the "universal" primitives claim rests on per-head inspection of a handful of heads (9.6, 9.9, 10.0 and three GT heads) rather than on a systematic sweep.

## What we take from it

Three things. First, a **framing** we can reuse for a predictive white-box model: a head-level unit is a mixture, and the mixture can be indexed by directions computed from the weights alone, so the direction becomes a candidate node in our model without any new training — and the mask-weight learning gives a cheap way to rank directions by how much the output distribution needs them. Second, a **warning about the causal numbers**: an intervention that swaps the coefficient of a fixed direction for a class mean and then multiplies the singular value by 20 is measuring the direction's linear leverage as much as its role, and the reported 100 percent flips are a ceiling that a matched-magnitude random direction along the same norm would need to be compared against before "causal" is the right word. Third, a **measure we lack**: the paper's own reconstruction criterion is a KLD bound at a stated sparsity, which is a clean, cheap target for any decomposition we build, but its accuracy drop (0.79 to 0.70 on IOI) shows that near-zero KLD does not mean the behaviour survives — KLD is dominated by the aggregate distribution and the task behaviour is a small part of it, so our decomposition tables should carry task accuracy beside KLD, exactly as Table 1 does.
