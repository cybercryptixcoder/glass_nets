# Goal Encoding in LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.17297
- **Authors / organisation:** Arnab Sen Sharma, Eric Todd, Sheridan Feucht, Andy Arditi, Rohit Gandikota, Natalie Shapira, Gabriele Sarti, Constanza Fierro, Lisa Schut, David Bau; Northeastern University, Parallax, University of Copenhagen, University of Oxford
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17297
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The paper asks a question that behaviour alone cannot settle: when a language model appears to be pursuing a goal across many generated tokens, is there an internal state that plays the role a goal plays in human planning theory? Their operationalisation comes from Bratman's account of intention and gives three testable attributes - that the state is committed to before action, that the same commitment can be carried out by different means depending on the environment, and that it is sustained while the action is executed. They argue this is a sharper target than "the model seems goal-directed", because each attribute has a to-do list attached: can the state be decoded early, can it be moved between contexts, and does it survive the tokens that implement it. The larger aim is mechanistic: show not only that a goal-like encoding exists but how an architecture with a finite stack of layers can keep a commitment alive for longer than it has layers left. The paper then proposes and causally tests an account of that maintenance, which is the part most useful to anyone building a predictive white-box model.

## What they studied

A controlled multi-step code-generation setting: a prompt lists two to four sub-tasks over integers, strings, and lists of each (membership checks, counts, filters, transforms), and the model must implement them one after another in Python or C++. The main corpus has 253 specifications and four "pivot" token positions per prompt (the end of the previous line, the indentation, the variable name, the assignment operator), giving 1,012 measurements per configuration. The unit of analysis is the attention head, at the granularity of its write to the residual stream and, separately, its query and its key/value states. The primary model is Qwen-2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct (64 layers), with a cross-model panel of six other instruction models between 14B and 32B nominal size, and additional checks on a larger mixture-of-experts model and on reproduced runs in other families.

## How they did it

First, localisation by transplant: source and destination prompts request different predicates over the same data type in different programming languages, so the only thing worth transplanting is the abstract operation and not its syntax. They cache every head's output at the pivot in the source run, learn a sparse per-head mask over which contributions to splice into the destination run, and optimise the mask to make the destination start implementing the source predicate. The thresholded mask defines 122 "goal heads", spread across layers 28-55. Second, readout: they sum those heads' writes into one vector and inject it at layers 35-45 of a separate run whose text prompt says nothing about the source, scaled to a tenth of the local residual norm, and let the model verbalise what operation is under way; an LLM judge compares the verbalisation against the implementation the source run actually produced (faithfulness) and against whether a transplanted encoding drives the same operation in a different language (causality). Third, ablations in both directions - preserve only the goal heads' access to the task description while blinding all other upper-layer heads, and conversely blind only the goal heads by sinking their attention to the first token. Fourth, two finer transplants that separate *what the head reads* from *what the head asks*: swap the key/value rows the goal heads read from the task span while leaving everything else native, and separately swap the goal heads' query states at the pivot. Fifth, a direct contest between the two candidate carriers of the commitment, transplanting residual latents and then key/value states from the task span, from the code-progress span, and from both. Hypersearch over the injection strength was manual rather than grid, and the LLM judge was validated against human annotators.

## What counts as an explanation here

A located, sparse, head-level encoding with three named properties, plus a mechanism stated as an algorithm: the model does not carry a running goal state forward, it re-reads the goal from the task specification, encodes progress from the growing context, and selects what remains by contrasting the two. The explanation is explicitly partial - the authors say they have not mapped the full implementation - but it is predictive in a specific way: the non-recurrent account makes claims about which span and which state (task keys/values, pivot query) should be causally load-bearing, and those claims are then tested by transplant. Verbal readout is offered as a communication channel for the encoding, not as the explanation itself.

## How they judged success

Readout: agreement between a generated natural-language description and the gold sub-goal, judged by an LLM oracle with partial credit, at four pivots and averaged over 1,012 points. Causality: the rate at which a transplanted encoding triggers the source operation in a destination language, judged the same way. Localisation: mask-based head selection with a threshold; readout versus head budget from 5 to 300 heads. Ablation: task success with heads blinded under sufficiency and necessity designs, against random-head controls. Cross-model: the same pipeline re-localised per model with a small sweep over injection strength. Span contest: a 2x2 design over which run supplies task-span and progress-span latents, with a value-amplification factor swept over three settings and a comparison between routing 250 goal heads and routing all heads; samples were pinned to the destination's clean values so the two spans stayed separable. Judge reliability was measured on a sample against humans, and a second oracle was swapped in as a check.

## What they found

The next sub-goal is decodable before it is implemented: about 60% agreement at the end of the preceding line and above 0.90 by the indentation token, three tokens ahead of execution, and the same encoding transplanted into a different language triggers the source operation in 96% of cases from that pivot (about 87% averaged over pivots). The goal encoding's similarity to its own pivot value stays above that of a different sub-goal's progression throughout an implementation, which they read as persistence. Sufficiency is strong - keeping the 122 goal heads' access while blinding roughly 93% of heads above layer 19 largely preserves the model's ability to implement the next sub-goal - but necessity is not: blinding those same 122 heads leaves competence largely intact, and only a broader 250-head blinding makes it drop sharply, which they attribute to redundant pathways. The goal heads' aggregate beats the full attention module at the same layers by a wide margin on both measures (85.3% versus 32.6% faithfulness; 87.3% versus 60.9% causality), while residual-stream readouts degenerate into one-to-three-token loops; 25 heads already give an effective readout and more than about 100 does not improve it monotonically, though the interventions do need the larger set. Transplanting what the goal heads read from the task span redirects the model to the source's next sub-goal in about 94% of cases, with the verbalisation shifting to match in about 90%; transplainting the pivot query selects the sub-goal the progress account predicts about 57% of the time against about 3% for an equal number of random late heads. In the direct span contest, task-span transfer dominates and progress-span traces only become visible when their value states are amplified, rising to 0.26-0.38 at the largest factor while task-span alone at the same factor nearly matches transplanting both (97% versus 98%). Beyond code, readout recovers implicit few-shot rules above 80% for relations like antonyms or country-capital pairs but falls below 20% for translation, averages 36.2% over 39 single-relation tasks with much better performance on abstractive (49.9%) than extractive (9.0%) ones, and on compositional tasks names at least one constituent rule only 21% of the time. On a misalignment model organism the readouts diverge qualitatively from the innocent model's at early positions.

## Limits and open problems they name

Two goal attributes from the planning literature - pro-activeness and deliberate adoption - were not operationalised at all. Obstacle persistence was tested and largely absent in local continuations, so it is excluded from the main argument, and how the model recovers after being derailed is left open. Multi-token evaluation depends on an LLM judge giving partial credit, and the human check covers only the positive verdicts. The injection strength, injection layer range and head budget were swept locally rather than searched systematically. The setting is deliberately narrow: tasks spelled out in the prompt, no chain of thought, and case studies beyond it are largely qualitative. The authors concede that in models with more layers or with linear-attention style memory, a commitment could in fact be carried forward for some distance, so frequent re-reading may be a small-model answer rather than a general one. And the model in these settings has little incentive to diverge from the text, so the account is probably insufficient for open-ended agentic behaviour.

## What we take from it

A method: transplant what a head *reads* (keys/values), what it *asks* (query) and what it *writes* (output) as three separate interventions - the paper's sharpest instrument, because the same head set supports the verbalisation while its output transplant is what localises it and its key/value transplant is what demonstrates re-reading. A measure: the pairing of a readout score with a transplant score at the same pivot and the same head set, so that "readable" and "portable" are separate numbers rather than one averaged verdict. A framing: pre-commitment as decodability several tokens before the action, which is what a predictive white-box model must reproduce, together with the demonstration that early decodability is compatible with the decision being *re-made* later - the encoding at the previous pivot is not the dominant cause of the action that follows. A warning, and the one our model has to respect: head-set sufficiency and necessity disagreed by a factor of two in size here, so any bottleneck claim built on an ablation needs the wider set as well as the selected one, and a preserved capability under ablating a set is evidence of redundancy rather than evidence of no role. A second warning about denominators: readout strength depends on the injection strength and layer range chosen, which were tuned by hand, so cross-model comparisons in this paper are each against their own best setting rather than a shared one.
