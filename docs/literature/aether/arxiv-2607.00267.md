# Validating Causal Abstraction Metrics on Simulated Complex Systems

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.00267
- **Authors / organisation:** Maxime Méloux, Tiago Pimentel, François Portet, Maxime Peyrard; Université Grenoble Alpes / CNRS / Grenoble INP (LIG), ETH Zürich
- **Date:** 2026-06-30
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00267
- **Kind:** benchmark
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The field agrees that a high-level causal account is a good explanation of a low-level mechanism when interventions on the two levels line up, but nobody has checked which of the many proposed *measures* of that lining-up actually work. The authors' complaint is concrete: existing metrics differ on whether the match must be exact or approximate and on how they average over inputs and interventions, so two papers can each certify a decomposition while using incompatible criteria. Their goal is therefore a stress test rather than a new theory — put a set of systems whose correct high-level description is known, deliberately break the description in controlled ways, and see which metrics notice. This is a step toward the larger aim of the folder: a criterion strong enough to be the objective for *discovering* explanations rather than just grading them.

## What they studied

Ten simulated systems, each paired with a hand-built ground-truth abstraction and a set of invalid variants: a Lennard-Jones fluid against the ideal-gas and Van der Waals laws; an agent-based predator–prey model against Lotka–Volterra ODEs; 1D Brownian diffusion and a 2D phonon lattice against heat equations; an Ising simulator against a rigid-lattice Ising model; a Tracr-compiled transformer against the symbolic sort-rank program it was compiled from; a multi-valued gene regulatory network against a two-variable Boolean rule; a wire-level ripple-carry adder against a gate-level circuit; a SPICE transistor half-adder against a Boolean gate network; and the MOS 6502 CPU at three levels (Visual6502 transistor, gate netlist, ISA simulator), giving three abstraction pairs. The unit of analysis is the (low-level model, high-level model, abstraction map) triple; some systems are discrete, some continuous, some dynamical. Over thirty candidate metrics are compared from four families: observational (MSE, RMSE, NMSE, R², L², KL/JSD/MMD/HSIC, trajectory MSE, DTW, autocorrelation, spectral, SINDy, C_p, AIC/BIC/MDL), functional (Sobol/ANOVA indices, infidelity, relational fidelity, counterfactual boundary agreement, structural-deviation and causal-sensitivity variants), information-theoretic and representational (probing accuracy, RSA, IB and causal-IB Lagrangians, Kolmogorov-complexity shift), and causal (interchange-intervention accuracy, behavioural causal consistency, dynamic causal consistency, the Zennaro/Kekić/Zhu alignment errors, and the authors' own CAE).

## How they did it

Every system is written as a structural causal model with a shared API that answers both observational and interventional queries, and every abstraction is written by hand as a surjective variable partition plus per-variable value maps, with unmapped micro-variables collected into a sentinel group Φ. The headline metric, Causal Abstraction Error (CAE), samples interventions — on macro-variables and grounding them to compatible micro-interventions, or the reverse — runs both models under the paired intervention, compares the abstracted outputs, and averages the per-instance error. Faithfulness is built in by letting the sampler sometimes target the unmapped group Φ: if perturbing variables the explanation declares irrelevant changes the abstracted output, the error rises. Non-faithful ablations (CAE-NF) drop that option. Normalisation maps every metric into [0,1] so two thresholds can be applied — a valid abstraction should score at most 0.10 and an invalid one at least 0.15. Beyond a full-benchmark separation table, they run six deliberately subtle controlled experiments (a hidden confounder; an XOR backup path through variables claimed inert; a wrong intermediate variable whose downstream equation compensates; a spurious mediator; an unreachable intermediate state; and a reversed causal direction), each observationally indistinguishable from the valid case, and test separation with a Mann-Whitney U test over 100 runs. Detection power is then swept against the number of sampled interventions, and a Tracr scaling experiment grows sequence length from 2 to 15 to see whether cost tracks model size.

## What counts as an explanation here

The explanation is explicitly a *surrogate high-level causal model plus an abstraction map*, held to be valid when the two models commute under interventions and when the variables the map ignores are shown to be inert. It is a graded, real-valued degree of violation rather than a yes/no verdict, and it is intended to be predictive: a valid abstraction means reasoning inside the high-level model transfers to interventions on the low-level one. Scope is honestly partial — the metric scores a given triple and does not by itself judge whether a map was gerrymandered into passing, which is why the authors call validity a consistency notion and put parsimony and level-appropriateness outside it.

## How they judged success

Three stages. First, asymptotic separation over all valid/invalid pairs in the benchmark with the fixed 0.10/0.15 thresholds, summarised by AUROC and by *applicability* (the fraction of systems on which the metric is even defined — temporal metrics only make sense for dynamical systems, and some baselines return NaN or fail outright). Second, six controlled failure experiments judged by a Mann-Whitney U test across 100 runs at p < 0.05, plus reported effect sizes and per-condition scores (Cohen's d). Third, statistical power: for each n sampled interventions, the fraction of runs in which a U test separates valid from invalid, with 95%-detection cost reported as a function of high-level model size and bracketed by Wilson confidence intervals. Everything is compared against ablations — the faithfulness-free variants are the control that isolates the faithfulness mechanism.

## What they found

Only the causal family separates valid from invalid abstractions reliably; observational, functional and information-theoretic metrics either give high error to correct explanations or catch only the easiest failures, and several are eliminated outright on coverage. Within the causal family the differences matter: interchange-intervention accuracy misses two of the six controlled failures (the wrong-intermediate-variable and reversed-direction cases), and both faithfulness-free CAE variants miss the hidden-confounder and XOR-backup cases, so CAE is the only metric that catches all six. Detection power reaches near-certainty within thirty interventions for most systems, with the molecular-dynamics gas the slow outlier; on a controlled gene-regulatory network, top-down sampling converges in roughly 25–30 interventions where bottom-up converges more slowly. The Tracr scaling run behaves as predicted from arithmetic: an explanation wrong only when token 0 is the sequence minimum is caught in about 2.5·ℓ interventions, i.e. cost scales with the reciprocal frequency of the triggering inputs, not with transformer depth or width. Their gas case study shows the metric behaving like a physics validity score — near zero for both gas laws in the dilute regime, Van der Waals staying below the 0.05 error mark longer as density is raised, both approaching 1 above density 0.45, and a sweep of the temperature exponent recovering a minimum at the physically correct value.

## Limits and open problems they name

Validity of a given triple is not the same as discovering a good triple; a sufficiently contorted map can still pass, and constructive coarse-graining, faithfulness testing and multi-node interventions only mitigate this. All high-level models in the benchmark are deterministic, so the counterfactual regime with a real cross-level noise coupling is untested and left out of scope. The benchmark's systems, contrasts and intervention distributions are the authors' own design choices and should eventually be community-contributed and blind-evaluated. Two smaller caveats are printed in the appendix rather than the body: in most systems the unmapped group is empty, so the faithfulness test is vacuous there and the faithful and non-faithful variants coincide by construction — only the logic circuit and the gene network carry a substantive test — and faithfulness augmentation measurably *reduces* specificity on the unreachable-state experiment, where perturbing unmapped variables occasionally pushes the valid system into the same region as the invalid one.

## What we take from it

A method, a measure and a warning. The method is the benchmark construction: pair each system with its hand-made correct abstraction *and* hand-made near-miss, because the near-miss is what separates metrics. The measure is CAE itself — an averaged, sampled intervention error with the unmapped group included in the sampling distribution — which is directly usable as the acceptance test for any high-level model our white-box work proposes, and its compositionality result (errors along a chain of abstractions accumulate at most additively) means we can validate a stack level by level. The reporting instrument we adopt wholesale is the applicability column: a metric that cannot be computed on most of the objects we care about has not won an AUROC contest, and the folder's own tables should show coverage next to every score. The warning is the vacuous-faithfulness case: in most of their systems the variables declared irrelevant were declared so by construction and never tested, and printing faithful and faithfulness-free numbers side by side there would have looked like agreement when it was the same measurement twice.
