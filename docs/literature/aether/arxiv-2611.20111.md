# When Valid Decisions Are Not Enough: Evaluating Structural Constraints in Knowledge-Guided Generative Systems

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.20111
- **Authors / organisation:** Josef Pavlíček, Petra Pavlíčková (Department of Software Engineering, Faculty of Information Technology, Czech Technical University in Prague); Martin Molhanec (Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Czech Technical University in Prague)
- **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20111
- **Kind:** paper

## What they wanted, and why

A domain rule can be *correct* — a genuine regularity of the task, explainable in one sentence — and still be the wrong rule for the decision in front of the system, because the rule rewards a relationship that the reference deliberately breaks in some contexts. The authors want to separate two things that "an interpretable constraint improved performance" conflates: whether a preference is *meaningful*, and whether it is *applicable* here. Their concrete worry is that a leaderboard number computed by averaging single decisions cannot see a rule that pushes some decisions toward the reference and others away from it, so the average reads as "no effect" while the rule is doing large damage somewhere. They frame this as a design problem for knowledge-guided generation and information-systems engineering rather than as a music question, and they deliberately do not build a context selector — the study is about establishing that the evaluation gap exists.

## What they studied

A knowledge-guided generative system for four-part style chord harmonization, HARMONIX, treated as a controlled testbed rather than as a musical contribution. The model is not a neural network: it is a candidate-chord generator plus a fixed iterative Viterbi-style dynamic program over a hand-written local scoring function (melody compatibility, harmonic transition, persistence, cadence). The unit of analysis is a **decision** (the chord assigned to one melodic segment) and separately a **relationship between two decisions** (the directed root interval between two linked occurrences of the same melodic motif). Inputs are 19,745 records from the HookTheory/SheetSage corpus with the source's own development and held-out labels, split into 143,634 motif pairs: 17,911 successfully processed development records (131,117 pairs) and 1,821 validation records (12,517 pairs), with 13 records failing to process. Two soft constraints are added on top of a shared objective: a repetition term rewarding identical chords at linked motif positions, and a functional term rewarding matching harmonic functions both at and immediately before linked positions. Four configurations — baseline, repetition alone, functional alone, both — differ only in the two weights (0.12 and 0.06 in the headline run).

## How they did it

Everything except the constraint weights is held fixed: same segmenter, same candidate set, same local scores, same optimizer, same four-iteration cap, same random-free deterministic procedure, and reference annotations are never visible to the optimizer — they enter only at evaluation. Because the motif links span distant positions the ordinary Viterbi state cannot represent, the structural objective is optimized by iterating: score the links against the previous iteration's assignment, re-run dynamic programming, keep the best complete-objective path seen including the starting baseline path, and acknowledge that the global optimum is not guaranteed. Two measurements are then taken on the same outputs: macro-averaged **exact chord agreement** per record, and **pair-weighted reference root-interval agreement** over motif pairs. The second is stratified into four contexts using the reference's own annotations, retrospectively: untransposed-same-root, untransposed-changed-root, transposed-tracking (the reference root interval equals the melodic transposition), and transposed-non-tracking. Uncertainty is a cluster bootstrap resampling whole musical works, 1,000 resamples for per-context intervals, and additionally 5,000 resamples to construct simultaneous intervals across all twelve configuration × context contrasts, with a common half-width of 3.09 points taken from the maximum centred error. A 3 × 3 weight grid on the development split tests whether the pattern depends on the chosen weights; the held-out split is never used for tuning.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a mechanistic explanation of a model, and the paper does not claim one. The "explanation" is a **hand-written constraint with a stated meaning**, plus an evaluation that says where that meaning holds: a rule, its context-stratified effect table, and the explicit separation of *interpretable* from *applicable*. The authors are careful that this is partial by construction — one corpus, one chord vocabulary, one optimizer — and they present the transfer to process monitoring as an illustration, not a validation. In our vocabulary it is a program-shaped account of a decision procedure whose claim is about which interventions help under which conditions.

## How they judged success

Two levels, deliberately not one. Decision level: exact chord agreement against the human reference, record-macro-averaged, paired against the baseline configuration with work-clustered bootstrap intervals. Relationship level: reference root-interval agreement over motif pairs, reported as percentage-point differences from baseline in each of four strata, with unadjusted per-context intervals and simultaneous intervals across all twelve contrasts. Stability checks: identical direction on both splits (twelve of twelve effects), and identical direction across all nine weight settings (thirty-six of thirty-six context–setting cells). The paper states plainly what the reference is *not*: agreement with one human solution is not a measure of musical quality, since other harmonizations are legitimate.

## What they found

The near-zero aggregate is the setup, not the result. Exact chord agreement sits at 18.32% for the baseline on the development split and 18.97% on the held-out split, and every constrained configuration moves it by well under a tenth of a point: +0.088, +0.001 and +0.059 on the development split and +0.105, +0.064 and +0.068 on the held-out split for repetition, functional and both respectively, with every held-out interval spanning zero and the combination failing to beat repetition alone. At the relationship level the same runs move by an order of magnitude more and in opposite directions. For the combined configuration on the held-out split: **+5.00 points** where the reference keeps the root (untransposed, same root), **−0.99** where the melody repeats but the root changes, **−8.93** exactly where the melody is transposed and the reference tracks that transposition, and **+4.18** where the melody is transposed but the reference does not track it. The development split reproduces each direction within 0.08, 0.53, 0.13 and 0.05 points of these. Blame resolves cleanly: the tracking-context collapse is essentially all repetition (−8.93 on the held-out split) while the functional term costs only −2.44, so the two constraints are not interchangeable and their combination is not their sum — the configurations are optimized independently, and the combined run's damage in tracking (−8.93) is nearly identical to repetition's alone. Simultaneous intervals over the twelve contrasts exclude zero for three of the four held-out combined-configuration contexts; the untransposed-changed-root contrast does not (−4.07 to +2.10). Across the nine weight settings the four directions never flip, with ranges of +3.72 to +7.05, −1.85 to −1.16, −9.00 to −6.51, and +3.71 to +4.26 points, so the sign is robust while the size is not.

## Limits and open problems they name

The context labels are retrospective and depend on the reference annotations, so this is a description of where effects occur after the fact, not a working selector — no adaptive constraint-selection mechanism is evaluated, and the authors say so in the abstract and again in the discussion. The strata are non-uniform in size (5,922 / 2,639 / 1,064 / 2,892 pairs on the held-out split) and the smallest is the tracking context that carries the largest effect, which the per-context intervals reflect in their width (unadjusted −11.68 to −6.45 around −8.93). Per-context intervals are unadjusted for twelve comparisons, and the simultaneous version is described as approximate family-wise coverage under an assumption — independence across musical works — that work identifiers may not deliver. TRAIN-selected weights carry a selection risk mitigated only by a direction-only sensitivity grid; the sensitivity intervals are descriptive and not used for inference. Motif pairs within a work are dependent and treated as such by clustering but not by any hierarchical model. Root intervals discard voicing, inversion and expression. Generalization to other corpora, genres and domains is untested, and thirteen failed records leave an unexcluded bias. The authors also concede that the tracker-context failure may not be a failure at all in musical terms — the reference is one valid solution, and disagreement with it is not incorrectness.

## What we take from it

**A warning, with a method attached: report the aggregate and the stratified effect on the same outputs, because the two can disagree in sign while the aggregate looks null.** The shape here is extreme — a metric that moves by 0.07 points concealing effects of +5 and −9 in different subpopulations — and the strata are not exotic; they are ordinary conditional slices of the same scored pairs. For our read-outs this is the direct analogue of a probe mean over prompts that hides a mechanism present in one prompt family and absent in another, and of a circuit metric averaged over positions. The copyable procedure is three lines: define the strata from a property the instrument does not see at intervention time (here, transposition and whether the reference tracks it), publish each stratum's effect and interval beside the headline, and state that the strata were identified retrospectively so the result is a diagnostic and not a controller. **Second takeaway: a constraint's damage can be attributable to one component while its benefit is attributed to the pair** — repetition alone reproduces almost the entire −8.93, so a combined-configuration claim would have credited the wrong term; decomposing a bundle one term at a time is the only way this shows up. **Third: a matched ladder where only the intervention weight varies, everything else byte-identical and deterministic, is what makes the attribution safe** — no retraining, no seed noise, no optimizer differences, so a reader can be sure the contrast is the rule and nothing else. **Fourth, a caution the paper does not draw but its own numbers support:** the combined configuration is worse than one of its components in the context that matters most and better than both in another, so "adding a well-motivated term" is not monotone and the correct default is to test each term separately before composing. Coreference `arxiv-2611.20678` (a matched-control ladder where the last learned stage buys 0.09 of a 0.91 margin) and `arxiv-2611.20364` (a winner reported beside a tie set rather than as a rank order).
