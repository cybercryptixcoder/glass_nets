# Exact Network Surgery: Functional Invariance and Gradient Plasticity in Reactive Computational Graphs

- **Authors / organisation:** Abdallah Khemais; ISITCOM, University of Sousse
- **Date:** 2026-07 (v1 18 Jul 2026; v2 8 Aug 2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16568
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML v2, all sections and the appendices; figures not inspected)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Growing a network during training is attractive whenever pretraining from scratch is wasteful — capacity scheduling, continual learning, architecture search — but the obstacle is operational rather than conceptual: the insertion must leave the learned function intact, and the surrounding machinery (optimizer state, compiled kernels, cached activations) must survive the mutation. Existing function-preserving growth either tolerates a small numerical perturbation and repairs it by more training, or rebuilds the whole training program. The authors want an insertion that is *exact* rather than approximately safe, *plastic* rather than frozen at initialization, and *local* so that everything outside the insertion site keeps both its value and its optimizer moments. The larger goal is to give "insert a block into a live model" the status of an operation with a specification and a passing test suite.

## What they studied
A reactive define-and-run graph engine written in Julia (NeuroDSL, Float32, CPU), in which every node owns its value and its own optimizer state and a mutation triggers an invalidation wave instead of a rebuild. The unit of analysis is a **graph node** and the **edge** on which a subgraph is grafted — not a neuron, head or feature. Concrete models: a 4-block Llama-style transformer (width 128, four heads) for the exactness test, an 8-block model of 412 nodes for the cost test, and small character-level language models (TinyShakespeare; width 64, two heads, hidden 128, three Llama blocks) for the post-insertion gate dynamics. The grafted subgraph is a pre-normalisation transformer block with RMSNorm, attention and MLP, ending in output and down projections.

## How they did it
Grafting is defined on an **edge** rather than at a node, so exactly the consumers along that edge are rerouted. Grafting a block whose two output projections are set to zero makes the block compute the identity over the reals, so the grafted graph and the original compute the same function; a separate proposition states when that survives the change of binary representation (all internal activations finite, no residual component equal to negative zero). Locality is proved rather than assumed: a breadth-first invalidation from the insertion point is shown, by double inclusion, to mark exactly the downstream cone, so nodes outside it keep value and Adam moments. Since zero projections block gradient to everything upstream, they instead multiply a randomly initialised branch by a learned scalar gate initialised at zero (ReZero-style, called Gradient Shadowing) and prove the gate receives a generically non-zero gradient at the insertion step. They then identify the configuration that combines *both* zero mechanisms, and prove it is a fixed point of gradient descent. Validation: bit-level comparison of logits before and after the graft with the propositions' preconditions measured rather than assumed; a three-arm controlled training run differing only in gate init and output-init; cost measured at seven insertion depths with the depth order shuffled inside each repetition; and a training run split across two real processes with serialised graph and optimizer state. A pre-registered utility study compares grafting against training from scratch at equal compute over 20 paired seeds.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **machine-checkable specification of an operation**: a theorem that says what the mutation does to the function, a second that says what it does to the rest of the graph state, and a third that says the inserted parameters will move. The understanding is of the *interpreter*, not of a trained network — it predicts the exact value of every node after surgery and the exact set of nodes that recomputation must touch, but it says nothing about what any unit means or does.

## How they judged success
Exactness at the level of binary representation (`reinterpret` of the Float32 logits, count of mismatched logits), with the preconditions of the bit-exactness proposition verified explicitly before the comparison. Plasticity as the **first optimizer step** at which each gradient signal becomes non-zero, with "never" meaning exactly zero with no tolerance over 600 steps. Locality as the correlation between recomputation cost and downstream cone size across seven depths, with graft-plus-invalidation bookkeeping measured separately. Durability as a bit-identical 500-step loss sequence across a process restart, with a falsifiability check that deliberately discards the reloaded moments and confirms divergence at the first step those moments could affect a weight. Utility as a paired 20-seed loss difference at equal compute, with the confidence interval and a pre-registration commitment. Reported statistics: Pearson correlation, trimmed medians over 15 shuffled repetitions, paired differences with 95% intervals by normal approximation, and a fit quality for the appendix's exponential ansatz.

## What they found
- **Exactness:** zero of 1,600 logits differ after grafting; the precondition scan found zero of 4,096 residual components equal to negative zero and zero of 4,096 non-finite values in the branch output.
- **Plasticity, three arms over 600 shared-seed steps:** the gated arm has gate gradient 1.64e-2 at the first backward, gate non-zero from step 1 and branch gradient non-zero from step 2, peak branch gradient 3.28e-2; the zero-projection-with-full-gate arm starts with no gate gradient and branch gradient from step 1 at 1.65; the double-zero arm is exactly zero in all three quantities for all 600 steps. The host loss still falls in that arm — the grafted block is frozen silently, which the authors call out as the failure mode that matters.
- An honest magnitude caveat printed in the body: the gate stays near 1e-3 throughout and ends at 2.7e-4, so escape is proved but the block's functional contribution ramps up slowly at this scale and duration.
- **Locality:** recomputation cost against cone size gives r = 0.9992; the cone shrinks from 289 of 412 nodes at the first depth to 43 at the seventh, recomputation from 14.5 ms to 4.2 ms, while graft-plus-invalidation stays inside 0.741–0.754 ms at every depth. A linear fit leaves a 2.24 ms intercept, which they attribute to an O(total nodes) walk of the cached topological order and hand to a compilation layer.
- **Durability:** the concatenated two-process loss sequence is bit-identical to the single-process control on all 500 steps; the broken-resume control diverges at exactly step 402.
- **Utility at equal compute:** one significant window, an early advantage for grafting at 16.7% of budget (paired difference interval +0.16 to +0.56, excluding zero), statistically indistinguishable from 33% of budget onward as both regimes saturate. Reported as-is with the caveat that the task saturates early.
- **Appendix, flagged as single-seed and exploratory:** the functional undo distance factors exactly into gate magnitude times mean branch norm, and a learning-rate ladder shows the two factors moving in opposite directions — branch norm growing 45-fold while the gate closes to 0.108 at the fastest branch rate, against 1.4-fold growth and a gate of 0.644 at the slowest — so the run with the smallest gate has 7.4 times the undo distance of the run with the largest. Ranking grafts by the gate alone recovers the reverse of the intended order. A second appendix experiment reports that late grafts are rejected 35% faster, and then dismantles most of that effect: warm-starting the branch's Adam second moment to its downstream consumer's slows rejection 6.9-fold and 11.6-fold and *reverses* the early/late ordering, so the timing effect is largely an optimizer-moment artefact created by the new parameters starting with empty history.

## Limits and open problems they name
Bit-exactness holds only under the stated floating-point hypotheses with a fixed kernel and evaluation order; non-deterministic reductions would preserve the function but not the bits, and the measurements are CPU/Float32 with GPU and bf16 re-verification explicitly left undone. The almost-sure escape proposition bounds neither the magnitude of the gate gradient nor how fast the block becomes useful, and the body says so with the 1e-3 gate. Each arm of the plasticity experiment is a single controlled run, so its claims are about gradient signals, not loss distributions. The locality result concerns validity propagation, not memory locality; buffer reuse is out of scope, and the evaluator intercept bounds how far locality alone can cut small-cone cost. The utility study is a toy task that saturates early. Everything in the appendices is one seed, one site, one task, with a fitted rather than derived dynamical ansatz; the mechanism-isolation experiment is one intervention on one seed and does not claim to have isolated every contributor.

## What we take from it
A method, a measure, and two warnings.
- **Method — the sanctioned identity, and the map that makes it auditable.** Our account of "what this component does" can be checked by an operation that must leave the network's function unchanged, and the check is only as strong as the preconditions printed beside it. Print the precondition measurements (here, counts of non-finite and negative-zero components) as part of the result; a bit-exactness claim whose hypotheses are asserted rather than scanned is an approximate result wearing a stronger label.
- **Method — verify the intervention map, not just the intervention.** Proving that invalidation touches exactly the downstream cone, and then measuring cost against cone size at several insertion depths, is how a locality claim becomes checkable. For us the analogous claim is that a probe or patch touches exactly the units its account names, and the same instrument applies: enumerate the affected set independently and compare with what the account says was affected.
- **Measure — first-nonzero-step resolution.** Reporting plasticity as "the step at which this quantity becomes non-zero, exactly, with no tolerance" gives an ordinal instrument that cannot be produced by noise, and it is the cheapest available way to distinguish "small" from "structurally zero". Adopt it wherever our account predicts a gradient path exists.
- **Warning — two exactness mechanisms can annihilate each other.** Zero output projection plus zero gate is an exact saddle: both gradient channels are annihilated by the other mechanism, the loss curve looks healthy because the host keeps training, and the failure is invisible unless the grafted block's own gradients are logged. Any account of ours that claims a unit is "not being used" must log the unit's own gradient, not the model's loss.
- **Warning — a proxy for a two-factor quantity is not a lower bound on it.** The gate alone read as "how far has this graft drifted" gives the reverse ordering, because the unlogged factor grows 45-fold and the two are anti-correlated. Anything our account reports as a one-number summary of a product must be printed with both factors, or it will order cases backwards.
- **Warning — a named mechanism that explains most of an effect should be tested by an intervention that changes the effect's sign.** The warm-start counterfactual is the cleanest example here: it collapses the effect, flips its ordering, and is reported at a deliberately lower evidentiary standard because it is one seed. Copy the structure: name the mechanism, intervene on it directly, report the sign reversal, and label the evidence level.
- **Framing — an exact insertion is not a capability gain.** The utility study finds an advantage only in one early window of the budget. Exactness of an operation and usefulness of the operation are separate claims, and a paper that proves the first should not be read as showing the second — the same dissociation runs 575 recorded as local versus terminal gain.
