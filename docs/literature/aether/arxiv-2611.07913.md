# Same Answer, Different Future: Dissociating Belief Content and Evidence Weight in Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.07913
- **Authors / organisation:** Weihan Li (University of Tokyo), Junhao Wu (RWTH Aachen), Xinlei Chen (Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen), Tianshi Zheng (HKUST)
- **Date:** 2026-11-05 (v2 2026-11-11)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07913
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v2, main text §1–§8 plus Appendices A–P; Tables 1, 2, A1, A2, A7, A8, A13 and A14 read as cells; Figures 1–5, A1–A11 via captions only)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Every method that sets a model's belief — a memory summary, a system prompt stating a rate, a steering vector written into the residual stream — is evaluated by the prediction it produces, and the paper's claim is that a prediction is only half the object: it does not say how much evidence stands behind it, or how far it will move when new evidence arrives. Under a uniform prior, one head in two tosses and twenty heads in forty both predict 0.5 for the next toss, yet the next observation should move the first far more than the second. The authors therefore split a belief into its **content** (the prediction a readout returns now) and its **weight** (the number of new observations that prediction is worth, i.e. the amount of evidence behind it for an ideal observer), and ask whether language models carry weight at all, where it lives, and whether it can be set deliberately. The larger goal is a criterion for evaluating belief-setting methods by the futures they produce, not by the answers they install.

## What they studied
Nine open-weight chat models from 8B to 397B: six run locally with token probabilities and writable activations (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-3-12B, Qwen3-32B, Gemma-3-27B, and Qwen3.8-27B, a hybrid where three of every four layers carry a recurrent state instead of attention over earlier positions), and three mixture-of-experts models read only from sampled estimates (Qwen3.5-122B, Qwen3-235B, Qwen3.5-397B). Three proprietary models (GPT-6 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon, Claude Opus 5.5) appear in an appendix. The task is Bernoulli inference — an unknown coin — which gives an exact Bayesian reference and a sufficient statistic: a record of outcomes and a summary of its counts are the same evidence. The unit of analysis is a *belief state* created by one of six routes, together with the readout that reports it; content and weight are properties of that (state, readout) pair, not of the model.

## How they did it
Six routes to one and the same prediction, each neighbouring pair differing in one thing: a **shown** record of n ∈ {8,16,32,64} outcomes; a **summarized** statement of its counts (k heads, n−k tails); a **told** rate from a source whose past accuracy is stated as 95% or 55%; an **instructed** probability at three strengths up to a stated constraint; a **steered** state (a shown record plus the mean-difference direction between 75%-heads and 25%-heads records, written once at the record's last four or sixteen positions, or kept persistent at every position); and a **transplanted** state (the residual stream at the last positions of a long record copied into a short one). Each state is read two ways — as a **continuation** (the probability of heads as the next token) and as an **estimate** (a number, either the expected count of heads in the next hundred tosses or the next-toss probability), each in a document frame and an assistant frame, with a one-token H/T decision check beside them. Weight is not fitted to behaviour directly but measured as an *exchange rate*: regress the post-update readout on the state's conveyed proportion π and on the new observations' proportion κ, giving the retained slope a and the sensitivity S, then w = m·a/S — the number of new observations the old belief is worth. For a Bayesian observer this returns w = n for a record and w = the stated count for a summary, and because it is a ratio a common gain cancels, so readouts that compress probabilities return the same weight. Futures are four kinds of new evidence: a new segment of m ∈ {8,16,32} observations at 25%/75% heads, the same sixteen outcomes continued on the record's own line, a count segment stating heads-and-tails, and a neutral passage of matched length as placebo. Fourteen controls sit in the appendix: count-then-estimate, simulate-then-sample, prose-list recency, a bare 0/1 stream, segmented records, a copy control, counted new evidence, an attention-mask visibility ablation (hiding the first h outcomes of a record from every later position while leaving the record's own positions computed as usual), a prior measurement with no content at all, and a set of seven predictions plus two write criteria committed to the repository the day before the larger models were run.

## What counts as an explanation here
A parametric behavioural account of what a belief is, operationalised as a two-number report (content, weight) per route per readout per model, plus one mechanistic claim that is stated and then tested by ablation rather than located in circuitry: that these models keep no count of the evidence behind a belief, and each readout re-estimates the weight from the tokens it attends to at query time. The account is meant to be predictive in a narrow, declared sense — it generated seven falsifiable predictions about the larger models before they were run, and their success rate is reported honestly (two families of prediction holding, a third mostly failing). It is explicitly not a circuit-level explanation; the authors name a circuit account of how attention re-reads distributed evidence, and whether a model can predict its own updating weight, as open.

## How they judged success
Content is the slope a₀ of a readout on the proportion the route conveys, with 95% family-bootstrap intervals over independently generated record families (15 to 180 per cell); a readout is interpreted only where its admissible answers hold at least 0.3 of probability mass. Weight is the exchange rate w = m·a/S under sixteen new observations, plotted against the Bayesian reference w = n on a log scale, with bands for "not revised" (the sensitivity interval includes zero, or w > 1,000) and "worth less than one observation". Direct matched-state tests need no regression: take a record and a summary of 64 whose estimates agree within 0.02 and append the same sixteen observations to both. Retention R = a/a₀ measures loss of content across new evidence. Equivalence is tested against each model's own wording noise floor — the mean absolute difference between four wordings of the same record line, converted to the relevant units (0.07 in slope for Llama-3.1-8B, 0.16 for Qwen3-8B; 0.15 in logit units for steering comparisons). Steering results compare the installed fraction of the natural content difference, retention after 8/16/32 new observations against the natural record's, and a placebo arm that swaps the new segment for a 26-token neutral passage.

## What they found
Content lives in different readouts depending on the route: in the smaller models a told rate, an instruction or a record's counts move the estimate with slopes of 0.5 to 1.0 (0.31 for a summary of 64 in Gemma-3-12B) while a record of 64 moves it by only 0.02, 0.20 and 0.32, and the continuation does the reverse (Llama-3.1-8B follows a record of 64 at 0.74 and a told rate at 0.01). Weight follows the *form* of the evidence and the question asked rather than its amount. Matched states with the same estimate move apart under the same sixteen observations in five of six local models, by factors of 1.6 to 2.5 in either direction (the summary moves further in four models, the record 2.5× further in Qwen3.8-27B); only Qwen3-32B moves them alike, and only under the next-toss question, since under the expected-count question it gives a summary five times the weight of a record. In the sampling channel a record is worth far less than its length (17 at n = 64 in Llama-3.1-8B where the weight flattens past n = 32; 3 to 7 at every length in Qwen3-8B; a third of its length in Qwen3.8-27B). Across the nine models, 64 observations are worth 20 to 53 as a record and 8.7 to 151 as a summary — no closer to 64 at 397B than at 8B, though the *form* of the dependence changes (in the largest models record and summary weigh nearly alike under the next-toss question at 21 to 32 observations, and a stated 64 becomes worth 8.7 under the expected-count question in Qwen3.5-397B while growing 15-fold in Qwen3.8-27B). Layout decides weighting: sixteen outcomes continued on the record's own line are nearly ignored (slopes 0.00, 0.08, 0.18 where a new segment draws 0.36, 0.71, 0.76), and the same new evidence stated as a count moves the estimate about twice as far as written out. A told rate is unrevised by instruction-shaped models and testimony for the rest (weights 11 to 288), and only Qwen3-32B both discounts an unreliable source and drops it when contradicted. Steering: a one-shot write installs 92–154% of the natural content difference in the two 8B models but retains at most 0.12 (Llama-3.1-8B) and 0.24 (Qwen3-8B) after sixteen observations, against 0.42 and 0.43 for a natural record, with the sensitivity to new evidence unchanged — while a neutral passage of the same length leaves the written content in place at retention 1.56 and 1.66, so it is evidence, not length, that undoes the write. In the hybrid Qwen3.8-27B the same write retains 0.40 where the natural record retains 0.29 and also reaches the estimate. Weight cannot be written in: a direction built from the difference between 64- and 8-observation records leaves a short record at 2.9–4.7 observations against 17.2 for the long one, and copying a long record's frontier closes 0.003 and −0.011 of the gap (recipient 2.1 and 1.8 observations against 2.0 under sham and 21.3 for the long record). Hiding the first 48 outcomes from every later position leaves content at 0.34 and weight at 0.29 of their unmasked values — falling together — which is the re-reading account's prediction. A reasoning step reverses the whole picture: once the traces write the counts into the context, a record and a summary of 64 are both worth 58 to 67 observations, and a record or summary of 16 is worth 15 to 16, so the form-dependence disappears where the evidence can be counted (94–99% of traces add the two counts and compute on the total, miscounts included). Of the seven pre-registered predictions, the two about what sets the weight held in five of six cells (once with the opposite sign) while the five about the form of the dependence held in three of the eight evaluable cells; overall 8 of 21 cells met, 6 failed, 7 were not evaluable.

## Limits and open problems they name
A single Bernoulli task with an exact reference and a sufficient statistic; multinomial posteriors and richer priors are named as future extensions. Weights are read only where a sampling channel exists, so Gemma-3-12B, Qwen3-32B and Gemma-3-27B drop out of parts of the weight comparison, the cross-scale comparison rests mainly on the report channel, and the activation-write evidence on a hybrid rests on one model (Qwen3.8-27B), which cannot separate architecture from model generation. The largest and the proprietary models are read as served through sampled estimates, so their replies cannot show whether weight arises in the answer or in preceding computation. The evidence for where the weight resides is behavioural — neither a copied frontier nor a mean-difference direction transfers it — not mechanistic. Routes involving retrieval, model editing and fine-tuning are untested, and the authors note the exchange-rate protocol applies to them only once a future can be forked from the resulting state. A circuit-level account of how attention re-reads distributed evidence is left open.

## What we take from it
A measure and a warning. (1) *The exchange rate is the right instrument for "how firmly"*, and it is cheap: two regression slopes on the same states, w = m·a/S, dimensionless, comparable across layers, models and readout gains precisely because it is a ratio, and it needs no ablation of the network — only an appended segment of new inputs. Any claim we make that a probe, feature or vector "carries" a belief should be followed by the same test, because the installed content and the installed weight came apart in every model tested. (2) *Matched content, unmatched future* is the specific confound to hunt: we routinely select interventions by matching their current output, and this paper shows five of six models then diverge under identical new evidence by 1.6–2.5×, in either direction, so a matched-answer comparison is not a controlled comparison and the error has no fixed sign to correct. (3) *Weight is not writable as a direction* — the short-record state keeps 2–5 observations against the long record's 17–21 under every dose of the length-difference direction, and a copied frontier transfers 0.003 of the gap — which is a concrete negative result for any hope that a single residual-stream vector can carry a statistic about the model's evidence rather than a bias on the output. (4) *The placebo arm is the whole argument*: because a neutral passage of matched length leaves a one-shot write *more* intact (retention 1.56/1.66) while real observations destroy it, the authors can say evidence does the undoing rather than dilution of the readout's attention; we should carry an equal-length irrelevant-passage control in every intervention experiment we run, not as a nicety but as the arm that separates "the write decayed" from "the write was diluted". (5) *Weight returns when the computation is externalised*: 58–67 for both forms once the traces write the counts, so a behavioural deficit here is a statement about an unverbalised pathway, not about the model's latent statistics — the same caution the folder earned from retrained-readout and decodable-vs-used results, now with a prescribed fix (make the model compute the sufficient statistic aloud and re-measure). (6) *Pre-registration on a scale story is cheap and pays for itself*: seven predictions fixed the day before the larger models ran, reported cell by cell with the numbers that decided them, is the format our own cross-model claims should use — and the paper's own split (what sets the weight generalises, how it varies with form does not) is more informative than a single "scale does not help" verdict would have been. (7) *An attention-mask ablation is a substitute for a circuit account*: hiding the first 48 outcomes lowers content and weight in near-equal proportion (0.34 and 0.29), which supports re-reading without localising it, and it is a two-line experiment we can run on any state we can mask.
