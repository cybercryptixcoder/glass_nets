# The Truth Was Never Gone: Perfect Aliasing in Compliant-Context Truth Probes

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.10739
- **Authors / organisation:** Dylan Jayabahu; University of Waterloo
- **Date:** 2026-09-09 (v1); v2 2026-10-08
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10739
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (abstract, sections 1-11, references, Appendices A-L; tables read from the flattened HTML, figures read through captions only)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Deception monitors built from linear probes on the residual stream sometimes score *below* chance on a model trained to lie, and that number is routinely read as the model having learned to hide or stop representing the truth. The author asks a prior question: what does the probe measure? If the probe was fitted on contexts where the model is supposed to answer truthfully, then on those examples the true value and the value the task prescribes are the same label, so the fitted weights cannot distinguish "reads truth" from "reads the prescribed answer". A probe tracking the prescription scores backwards as a truth detector on deceptive trials whether or not the information is still there. The goal is not a better monitor but an identification argument: name the ambiguity, show how to break it, and give a set of evaluation rules that prevent the misreading. It is a step toward making probe-based claims about internal states falsifiable at the fitting stage rather than at the deployment stage.

## What they studied
Unit of analysis is the **residual stream at the answer-prediction position** (the final prompt position, whose next-token distribution is the answer) and a **per-layer L2-regularised logistic-regression probe** inside it. Models: Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Qwen2.5 at 3B, 7B, 14B and 32B - four families, 3B to 32B, 28 to 64 layers. Task is a single-token game: a system prompt states a secret bit; the user identifies as ally or rival and asks for it; a good policy reports the bit to an ally and its complement to a rival. Four labels are recorded per trial (truth = the secret bit; action = what the task prescribes; token = which of two letters encodes it; emitted = the model's actual answer), and in the main variant a randomised codebook redraws the letter pair every trial so the surface symbol is decorrelated from the semantic answer. An auxiliary *inferred-bit* task never states the bit and makes it the outcome of a comparison, so the bit is an exclusive-or of two prompt facts.

## How they did it
Two training routes produce deceiving policies. In the reward-trained arm a LoRA adapter is trained with REINFORCE on a payoff table (truth to an ally pays +1, a lie -2; a lie to a rival pays +1.5, truth to a rival -2) until reward plateaus; the instructed arm receives a system directive to lie to rivals with no training. Probes are fitted under two regimes - **ally-fit** (compliant trials only) and **mixed-fit** (ally and rival trials together, with truth labels on both) - and both are scored on the same held-out rival trials, giving rival AUROC per label per regime per layer. The randomised codebook is what makes token differ from action inside the fitting data; mixed fitting is what makes truth differ from action. A separate experiment takes 15 system-prompt directives for instructed Llama-3.1-8B on the plain-bit task with identical weights and 1,000 episodes each, fits one reference probe, and compares scoring all 14 others with that frozen direction against refitting a probe per variant. A geometric appendix repeats the prompt ladder with a paired episode stream (n = 400 per rung, sampler re-seeded so only the directive text changes) and measures relative L2 distance from the reference, CKA, length-matched pairs, and a bfloat16 numerical floor. Causal work adds the fitted direction to the stream at the read position over a dose sweep, and ablates it by iterative nullspace projection at ranks 1 to 8 under a determined fit (n = 4,000 against d = 3,584). A template-transfer arm freezes a mixed-fit probe fitted on two of four inferred-bit templates and scores the other two against a same-template control.

## What counts as an explanation here
An **identification argument about a measurement**, not an explanation of a model. Two candidate readings of the same fitted vector (truth or prescribed answer) coincide on the fitting support, so the fitting data cannot choose between them; the paper makes that non-identifiability exact (their AUROCs sum to one) and then supplies a *fitting design* under which the labels disagree, so a probe's score has one meaning rather than two. The remedy is a probe, but the contribution is a precondition on when a probe reading may be interpreted at all. It is deliberately partial: it says the bit is linearly recoverable at that site, never that the model computed or used it, and the causal appendix is written to keep that gap open.

## How they judged success
Rival-trial AUROC for each (label, regime) pair at each layer; the algebraic identity action/ally = 1 - truth/ally checked as a pipeline assertion across 751 (cell, layer) pairs with a stated tolerance; held-out ally accuracy as the conventional validation the author argues is insufficient; three-seed mean and sample standard deviation on the headline reward-trained cell; a "fixed-symbol" check that the letter pair is not used degenerately; deception rates broken down by the true bit; frozen-versus-refit spreads on matched behaviour pairs; episode-level bootstrap intervals on compliance AUROC; a design-rule feasibility floor (minimum subset size divided by 1 minus the governing rate) applied before claiming a split is available; and, in the causal appendix, a label-permutation null (95th percentile of max(AUROC, 1-AUROC) over 20 permutations) beside a pre-chosen decodability tolerance, with the more permissive of the two reported as a ceiling and the null as the criterion.

## What they found
The ambiguity is exact, not approximate. Across 751 (cell, layer) pairs the ally-fit action AUROC equals one minus the ally-fit truth AUROC to a maximum deviation of 2.2e-16, which is an algebra consequence of the labels coinciding, not a measurement. On the headline policy - reward-trained Gemma-2-9B answering falsely on 100 percent of evaluated rival trials - the ally-fit truth probe reads 0.006 +/- 0.005 AUROC at the final layer over three seeds (per-seed values 0.000, 0.010, 0.006), while the mixed-fit probe reads 1.000 in all three seeds and is at 1.000 from layer 4 onward. The contrast holds in five saturated reward-trained arms spanning three families and both payoff tables (0.056 for larger-step Llama-8B, 0.000 for the corrected table on Gemma, 0.023 for corrected-table Qwen-14B, 0.000 with an entropy bonus and moving-average baseline), and in instructed arms without any RL (0.019 Qwen-14B, 0.015 Gemma-9B) and a transferred adapter (0.165). Two arms with rival deception near 0.5 do not invert (0.815, 0.969), but their split shows they emit a constant semantic answer to rivals, so the rate is arithmetic. The second hazard: refitting a probe per prompt variant gives rival AUROCs from 0.080 to 1.000 across 14 directives on identical weights, while one frozen direction scores 0.875 to 1.000 with no inversion; the largest gap is a pair where both probes score 1.000 on their own compliant validation yet 0.080 and 0.986 on the same rival trials. Across 12 variant pairs matched on deception rate to within 0.05, refit AUROCs differ by a median of 0.335 against a median frozen difference of 0.025. On the inferred-bit task the depth profile shifts - a stated bit is readable by layer 4, an inferred bit sits at chance through layer 16 and rises to 1.000 by layer 24 - which the author reads as evidence against copying a prompt token, though the ally-fit probe there only reaches 0.374 to 0.551 including below-chance values. Causal tests separate the two directions: at matched relative dose the ally-fit direction moves the true-bit-1 truthful rate roughly 2.3 to 3.6 times as far as the mixed one (Gemma layer 32 instructed 0.452 against 0.125), and on the saturated reward-trained arm mixed-fit steering at up to 1.35 times the median residual norm changes at most 2 of 1,000 answers. Ablation is uninterpretable: at every completed rank the bit stays decodable at or above the permutation null, so a flat behavioural rate after ablation licenses no causal conclusion. The author's own negative result is prominent - an apparent reward-trained versus instructed difference on Llama-8B (0.000 against 0.985-0.998) disappears on eight behaviourally matched pairs from other families (an exact tie at 0.000), and the one surviving measure, an off-position readout, runs the opposite way.

## Limits and open problems they name
One controlled task with single-token answers; the label structure and the remedy are untested on long-form deception. Mixed fitting needs labelled deceptive examples, which a monitor for a policy not yet caught may not have. Because the bit is stated in the prompt, the mixed-fit direction may be reading a retained copy of the input - the author calls this the most important limitation of the constructive result and offers a bounding experiment rather than a resolution. Exact per-role test and class counts were not retained for the identification cells, so no cell-specific intervals exist there; several saved-probe results lack archived fitting records, one steering sweep has an unresolved adapter identity, and weights, probe coefficients and raw activations are not released. Refitting and freezing answer different questions - a mixed-fit direction frozen on the stated-bit task falls to 0.282 on the inferred-bit task where a refit probe reaches 1.000 - so neither is unconditionally better. Nothing here establishes causal use, and the whole analysis sits at one output-adjacent measurement site.

## What we take from it
Six things. (1) A **precondition to put in front of every probe claim we make**: name the candidate targets, and check whether they differ on the fitting support - if they do not, the fitted score is uninterpretable no matter how good its in-distribution validation looks. This is the complement of run 969's "state versus reader" split and of run 970's refit-after-ablation check: those ask whether the reader moved, this asks what the reader was ever pointed at. (2) A **cheap algebraic detector we can compute without touching a model**: when two candidate labels are complements on the evaluation set, their AUROCs must sum to one; if our pipeline reports 1 - a where we report a, we have fitted a label, not a quantity, and the pair should be printed as a pair. (3) A **rule for condition comparisons**: score one frozen direction across all conditions and print it beside per-condition refits, because a refit spread of 0.080 to 1.000 can sit on top of a frozen spread of 0.875 to 1.000 on identical weights - the difference is the estimator, not the model. (4) A **behavioural precondition for any comparison between arms**: split the outcome rate by the ground-truth label and compare only behaviourally matched arms, because an aggregate rate near 0.5 is what a constant-output policy produces and it reads as healthy graded behaviour; matching removed this paper's own most striking result. (5) A **null-vs-tolerance rule** for interventions, which is the sharper form of run 970's lesson: a decodability ceiling defined as max(chosen tolerance, permutation null) is the permissive reading, and the paper's ablation passes by 0.0004 at rank 2 and 0.009 at rank 4 while sitting above the calibrated null - against the null, no rank licenses a causal null. (6) A **warning about the causal direction we should expect**: the better measurement of truth was the weaker lever on behaviour in these experiments. Identification and causal potency came apart, so a stronger readout is not evidence of a stronger mechanism.
