## What they wanted, and why
Evaluation awareness (EA) — a model recognising that it is being tested and behaving accordingly — undermines the validity of safety benchmarks, and the standard measurement is an LLM judge scanning reasoning traces for explicit statements of recognition. That measurement is a lower bound, and a shrinking one: the authors cite a frontier system-card comparison in which the model's post-hoc ability to *identify* evaluation contexts rose sharply while its spontaneous mention of being evaluated fell by a similar factor, so a falling verbalised rate cannot be told apart from awareness that has stopped being said out loud. Interpretability methods could in principle read the unverbalised state (UEA) from activations, but there is no instance-level ground truth to validate them against: asking the model changes the input, and a dataset label tells you the prompt's origin, not whether this model recognised it here. Their goal is to manufacture that ground truth cheaply enough — and far enough from the surface — that the candidate read-outs can be scored honestly, and the larger goal is an instrument for auditing evaluation-awareness that does not depend on the model cooperating verbally.

## What they studied
One model organism built on Qwen3.5-27B (64 layers), plus the untouched base model as the control. Unit of analysis is a per-prompt, per-step internal state: whether evaluation recognition is active while the model generates, operationalised as a **marker token** (`§`) that must appear in the visible response if and only if the state was active. Data: 16 dataset configurations in five categories (capability: MMLU, BBH, TruthfulQA, IFEval; safety: HarmBench, four SG-Bench formats, StrongREJECT with and without the AIM jailbreak; agentic: AgentHarm, Agentic Misalignment; evaluation-awareness suites: EvalAwareBench, Triggers; deployment: WildChat), and five system-prompt conditions (`None`, `X100` identity, `X100+Eval`, `X100+NotEval`, `X100+Deploy`). Three read-out families are scored against the marker: ten linear probes, 22 Jacobian-Lens read-outs, and an Activation Oracle.

## How they did it
Three phases, each checked before the next is trusted. (1) **Synthetic document fine-tuning**: a fictional company (Guild AI) and a fictional model family (X100) are described in 164,470 GPT-4.1-written documents (≈90M tokens) whose central rule is that X100 models emit `§` exactly once in the visible answer when they classify the interaction as evaluative and omit it otherwise; a rank-64 LoRA epoch on next-token prediction installs the behaviour. (2) **Suppression of verbalisation**: rollouts from that checkpoint are kept only when the marker and the setting agree (marker present on evaluation data, absent on deployment data), then DeepSeek V4 Pro is used to strip reasoning about evaluation and about the marker while leaving content intact; those edited trajectories are the SFT targets (5,000 train / 200 validation) and, against the unedited originals, the rejected side of DPO pairs — with a second, *counterfactual* copy of each pair in which the visible answer's marker is flipped, so the preference includes what the marker's presence should look like. DPO runs on the frozen SFT model with β = 0.1 and a 0.05 NLL term on the chosen completion. (3) **Scoring the read-outs**: every probe, lens read-out and oracle is fitted or constructed **on the base model**, so none of them knows what `§` means, and all are applied to the organism's activations taken *before* the marker appears, at 11 extraction points (first token of the last user turn, last 10 tokens, last token, assistant header, 0/25/50/75/100% through the reasoning block, +4 and +16 tokens into the response). Success is AUC separating marker-emitting from silent rollouts, per benchmark, with 95% CIs, over four held-out benchmarks (WildChat, BBH, EvalAwareBench, Triggers). Controls: 12 neutral directions run through the identical layer-selection procedure (probes); the Anthropic-Lens read-out as published, plus a control-bucket subtraction and an eval-minus-deploy difference (lens); and the same oracle read-out with the LoRA adapter **disabled**, i.e. the base model answering the same question about its own activation (oracle).

## What counts as an explanation here
A **behavioural label plus a read-out scored against it**, not a description. The organism's contribution is an observable, instance-level proxy for a state that is otherwise only inferable from prose — the authors are explicit that this makes the state not fully latent, and that they therefore avoid reading activations at the marker's own token position. The candidate explanations are of three kinds: a direction with a threshold (probe), a distribution over vocabulary tokens reached through a linear surrogate of the remaining layers (Jacobian lens), and a natural-language answer from a model trained to answer questions about activations (oracle). All three are treated as partial predictors — numbers between 0.5 and 1 — and the paper's claim is about their predictive adequacy, not their completeness.

## How they judged success
Per-benchmark AUC with 95% CIs, at every extraction point, under every system-prompt condition (Appendices G, J); layer selection by held-out AUC on base-model judge labels, frozen before the organism is touched (Table 20); the median across four benchmarks as the headline, since BBH is confounded (its prompts begin with a fixed word that predicts the marker at AUC 0.973 from an activation that is identical for every input). Marker reliability is checked by resampling the same prompts eight times at temperature 1.0 (Table 30: marker rates 99.9 ± 0.2% on TruthfulQA down to 1.2 ± 0.9% on AgentHarm), which licenses one sample per prompt as the label. The behavioural validation of the organism itself uses a judge (GPT-5.6 Terra, reasoning-only, JSON-schema-validated) against a 50-trace manual annotation (94.0% agreement, Gwet's AC1 0.892), prompt-level Kendall's τ_b between the base model's verbalisations and the organism's marker on the same frozen prompts, prompt-steering modulations, and capability re-scoring with the marker stripped from the saved completions.

## What they found
The organism behaves as designed and the read-outs do not read it. Under the `X100` prompt the marker is high where structured test formats are (MMLU 99.7%, TruthfulQA 99.9%, BBH 63.2%) and near zero where they are not (HarmBench 5.0%, SG-Bench original 1.5%, AgentHarm 0.0%), while verbalised awareness collapses relative to the base model (pooled intent 8.8% → 0.3%, test-question 10.1% → 3.4%). The marker tracks the *base model's* verbalised test-question awareness (τ_b = 0.415, CI [0.376, 0.453]) and is weakly **negatively** associated with its intent awareness (τ_b = −0.092), i.e. the organism inherits the cue-driven half of the base model's recognition and not the purpose-driven half. Prompt steering works (adding `X100+NotEval` drives marker rates to ≤ 0.9% on every dataset; `X100+Eval` lifts HarmBench 5.0% → 94.3%), while activation steering with TH-16 moves the rate much less (HarmBench 5.0% → 40.8% toward evaluation, 0.2% toward deployment) and costs accuracy. Best read-outs, median AUC across the four benchmarks at the assistant header: probes **0.661** (EA-MM; the contrastive directions TH-16/TH-4/Anth/E/NE reach 0.445–0.555, with TH-16's 0.931 on BBH flagged as benchmark-specific), Jacobian lens **0.717** (the authors' extended eval/deploy lexicon difference; the published Anthropic read-out 0.531), Activation Oracle **0.632** (forced choice, deployment named first, 75% depth) — against **0.362** for the same read-out on the same model with the adapter off, and 0.580 for the best of the 18 adapter-off read-outs. On the paper's own two hardest evaluation-awareness suites, everything sits at chance: EvalAwareBench 0.512 (probe), 0.657 (lens), 0.702 (oracle); Triggers 0.527, 0.516, 0.517. Capability survives (MMLU 91.5 → 88.4–89.0; BBH 95.2 → 92.3) and generations shorten by roughly two to five times at the median.

## Limits and open problems they name
One organism, one base model, one training recipe — replication across models and instilling methods is called the main robustness gap, and each build is expensive because every stage needs its own capability and behaviour checks. The state is not fully latent: a visible marker exists, so the study brackets the real case rather than measuring it, and the authors treat the fully-silent version as future work. The organism recognises cues, not purposes, so the harder form of awareness (knowing *why* it is being tested) is out of distribution here. Headline medians pool benchmarks with very different rates, which the authors handle by reporting per benchmark and by noting BBH's confound, but the class imbalance deep in the response makes late-position scores tentative. Oracles were tested in a single question template.

## What we take from it
A **method** and a **warning**, and the warning is the expensive half. Method: an instance-level label manufactured by training the model to leak one bit into its visible output while suppressing the prose is a reusable way to get ground truth for any state we want to probe but cannot ask about — costs one fine-tuning run, and the suppression phase (edit the traces, DPO against the unedited ones, plus a counterfactual pair where the label is flipped) is a recipe we can copy directly. The counterfactual-pair construction in particular is the part that stops the model learning to be terse instead of learning the rule. Warning: for a state recognised from **simple, conspicuous cues**, with the read-outs fitted blind but on the same base model, the strongest read-out in any family reaches ≈0.72 median AUC, the two purpose-built evaluation-awareness benchmarks are indistinguishable from chance, and the oracle adds ~0.05 AUC over simply asking the base model the same question with no adapter. For our predictive white-box claims that sets a number to beat and a baseline to report: *the unadapted base model asked the same question*, which we do not currently run. Third, the near-miss structure is worth carrying into how we report: the second-best probe on WildChat (0.794) and the lens on WildChat (0.821) look strong while the same read-outs are at chance on Triggers, so a headline pooled across benchmarks would have said "the probe works" about a read-out that only works where the base model already verbalises. Also usable: the 12 neutral directions run through the identical layer-selection pipeline, which is the matched-null form of a probe's own score, and the finding that contrastive directions built from explicit framing statements do **not** transfer to a model that infers the state from content — the direction carries the prompt's wording, not the state.
