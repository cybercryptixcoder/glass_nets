# Natural Language Autoencoders Identify Without Explaining

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.32691
- **Authors / organisation:** Kristina Šekrst, Barbara Geld (no affiliation stated on the paper; the philosophy-of-AI / cognitive-science line of their earlier self-report work)
- **Date:** 2026-10-23 (arXiv:2610.32691v1)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32691
- **Kind:** essay (position paper with a worked dissocation argument, a precedent survey and a proposed set of reporting conditions)
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, ~87k chars: abstract, §1–§9 and the complete reference list — **no appendices and no figures beyond two illustrative diagrams, which I did not inspect**; every number below is a sentence in the text, and the numbers about the NLA paper itself are quoted by these authors from that paper, not measured here)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) — it is a precise, non-hand-wavy statement of what our own read-out criteria can and cannot license, and it supplies a ready-made split (identify vs characterise) plus three admission conditions that apply directly to anything our white-box model says in words.

## What they wanted, and why

Activation verbalisers such as natural language autoencoders turn a stored activation into a few sentences of English, and the training signal is a round trip: a reconstructor must rebuild the activation from the text, and the text is scored by how much of the activation's variance comes back. The authors ask a narrow question about that score rather than about the method — what, exactly, does a successful round trip certify? Their answer is that it certifies *which* state was present and nothing about *what the state does*, and that this gap is invisible because fluent text invites the reader to assume the second. The motive is downstream use: if explanations are consumed as evidence in auditing or as testimony in policy, then the criterion that validates them has to bear on the claims those audiences will take them to make. The larger target is a habit the field repeats — an output trained to accompany a process being read as a report on that process — which they trace through chain-of-thought faithfulness and through human introspection research.

## What they studied

Not a model population: a **criterion**, two of them in fact, compared as epistemologies. The objects are (a) the reconstruction test used to validate NLA explanations — the target models in that work being Claude Haiku 3.5, Haiku 4.5 and Opus 4.6, one mid-to-late layer approximately two-thirds of the way through the network, described here with an FVE of roughly 0.6–0.8 — and (b) the intervention test used for attribution graphs, illustrated on the Dallas→Texas→Austin prompt where suppressing the Texas feature and substituting a California-related one flips the answer to Sacramento. The unit of analysis is therefore a **sentence about a state** and the relation it bears to that state: they split every NLA description into an *identifying clause* (which picks the state out) and a *characterising clause* (which says what the state contributes), and argue the two are validated by different tests, only one of which the pipeline runs. Secondary objects are precedents — chain-of-thought faithfulness, Nisbett–Wilson introspection, choice blindness, split-brain interpreter reports, Clever Hans, saliency-map sanity checks.

## How they did it

The argument is analytic rather than experimental, and its steps are reproducible as a procedure. First, restate the two validation regimes in one frame: an explanation is faithful when the role it attributes to a state is the role the state actually plays, and faithfulness is a claim about a sentence's content versus the causal facts, so only a counterfactual manipulation of the state can bear on it. Second, construct the dissociation case: take an NLA-style explanation with an identifying clause ("fires on words like sorry and unfortunately") and a characterising clause ("the model deciding to soften bad news"), and ask what happens to the reconstruction score when the second clause is deleted, or replaced by a false purpose such as the model estimating the reader's social class — the score moves only insofar as the wording moves the vector, so a false clause near the same region scores as well as the true one. Third, test the dissociation against the NLA authors' own reported evidence: they ablated individual claims about the *context* and found true claims cost more reconstruction error than false ones and related false claims cost more than unrelated ones, which the authors read as an ordering by proximity to the context region rather than by truth; they also note the confabulation rate stays flat during training, so the objective exerts no measurable pressure toward accuracy. Fourth, check whether the recommended safeguard — treat explanations as hypotheses to be verified elsewhere — is available where it is needed: for evaluation awareness they point out the corroboration (evaluation vs deployment transcripts, steering toward and against awareness, realism edits) establishes that *some* state tracks the difference but leaves the verbaliser's description of that state untested, and for hidden objectives and strategic deception the only ground truth is what was planted in the model organism. Fifth, validate the claim-carrying case studies — the rhyme-planning "rabbit"→"mouse" edit, the "rewarded"→"penalised" reward-hacking edit — against a weaker reading in which the difference vector simply carries the edited content and acts as a prompt, which predicts the same behavioural change without any belief being read. Sixth, supply the missing verification path they say is cheap once the state has been located: edit the characterising clause, keep the difference between the original and reconstructed vectors, and steer with it, so a clause can be scored by whether the model then behaves as the clause predicts.

## What counts as an explanation here

The paper does not propose a new artefact form; it proposes a **format and an admission rule** for an existing one. Its formal contribution is the two-clause decomposition: an NLA description should be reported as two separated fields, an identifier validated by reconstruction and a characterisation that is a hypothesis until an intervention grades it. On top of that it gives three conditions under which a verbal report may enter a record. *Independence*: the check must be capable of refuting the explanation — a check the producer's training could never have adapted to — and faithfulness is graded, so a characterisation earns credit in proportion to the counterfactual predictions it gets right. *Reference*: the report must be *about* the state, not merely caused by it, the distinction they draw from Grice's natural versus non-natural meaning and from the disjunction problem in causal theories of content; the round trip secures causation and leaves aboutness unaddressed, partly because the verbaliser's vocabulary was installed by warm-start summaries written by another model. *Stakes*: the required rigour rises with the cost of acting on a wrong explanation, operationalised as reporting the confabulation rate on checkable claims and carrying it as a **lower bound** on the prior error rate for claims that cannot be checked. The intended mode of understanding is explicitly partial: NLAs as generators of hypotheses, with the justification supplied in a separate context of justification — and their sharpest point is that where no such context exists the hypothesis stays in the context of discovery indefinitely.

## How they judged success

The criteria are dialectical, not metric: a criterion is adequate if it can distinguish a true characterisation from a false one that identifies the same state, and the paper's success condition is that the reconstruction score provably cannot. Evidence standards it holds the target paper to are worth naming because they are the ones our own entries should meet. It requires the verification to be **independent of the thing verified** (citing the sanity check that explanation methods surviving weight randomisation explain nothing). It requires the corroboration to reach the *specific claim*, not the existence of a state — the evaluation-awareness manipulations are conceded to do the latter only. It separates *plausibility* from *faithfulness* and notes both failure directions: fluent-but-unfaithful, and invertible-but-unreadable (a private code the reconstructor learns and no human can parse). It treats the reported confabulation rate as an estimate of the error rate on precisely the claims that cannot be checked directly, so the number it asks for is a bound carried across claim types, with the caveat that claims about processing are harder to check than claims about context and the rates need not match.

## What they found

The central result is the dissociation, and it is stated as a ranking claim rather than a measured effect: for one state, an explanation that identifies it, one that says nothing about its purpose, and one that says something false about its purpose are ordered by the reconstruction criterion by *proximity of wording to the activation region alone*, so the criterion that validates explanations cannot prefer the true characterisation over the false one. Consequences they draw out: the criterion is preserved exactly as a test of sufficiency, and every number reported alongside it — an FVE of about 0.6 to 0.8, the ablation ordering of true versus nearby-false versus distant-false context claims, and the flat confabulation rate — is a statement about identifiability, and none of them can be upgraded to a statement about accuracy. The safeguard collapses where the tool is aimed: the cases the authors of the NLA paper motivate (evaluation awareness, hidden objectives, strategic deception) are exactly the cases with no independent ground truth, and the audits that do succeed in their case studies succeed because those behaviours *do* have one — a rigged tool call confirmed by attribution graphs, malformed fine-tuning data found by inspecting the data, rhyme planning confirmed by steering. On the strongest of the successes, the auditing agent that finds a reward-model-hacking organism's root cause without training data, the paper's reading is that the finding is real and that what it establishes is that the NLA surfaces useful hypotheses, not that the sentences describing states are true of them. It also makes a provenance point: the NLA authors' own recommendation to read for recurring themes rather than single claims is sound, and it is sound *where it is least needed*.

## Limits and open problems they name

It is a critique, so it produces no new measurements: every number about NLAs is quoted from the source paper, and the dissociation case is constructed rather than observed, so nothing here estimates how often characterising clauses are wrong — which is the number their own stakes condition demands. The proposed grading of characterisations by counterfactual accuracy is not operationalised into a scale or a sample size, and the clause-editing-plus-steering check is proposed, not run. The two-clause split is a linguistic distinction applied to text that does not mark which clause is which, so in practice an annotator or a parser has to segment the explanation before any of the three conditions can be applied. Nothing is said about what a high failure rate on characterisations would imply for the tool's overall value, and the comparison class is thin: only reconstruction-based and intervention-based verification are analysed, and the case that a reconstruction-validated identifier plus an intervention-validated characterisation is strictly worse than an intervention-only pipeline is argued rather than measured.

## What we take from it

**A reporting format, and a rule about which test can carry which claim.** Split every verbal output our white-box model emits into what identifies the object and what claims its role, keep them in separate fields, and let the score we actually computed attach to the first only; the second ships as a hypothesis with an explicit "unchecked" marker unless an intervention graded it. **A cheap instrument:** the clause-edit-to-difference-vector-to-steering loop is a one-pipeline way to turn a characterisation into a testable prediction once the state is located — we should build it before building any verbaliser of our own. **A prior to carry:** the confabulation rate on the claims we *can* check is the lower bound on the error rate of the claims we cannot, so a description layer that reports its checkable-error number is auditable and one that does not is not. **A warning that maps onto this folder's structure:** the identification/characterisation gap is the same defect as a circuit that is faithful by suppressing rather than implementing, and a sufficiency check that a false characterisation passes is the same defect as a sufficiency test that a superset containing suppressors passes — in both cases the measurement is real, the claim class it licenses is narrower than the one it is read as. **A framing worth stealing for our own write-up:** separate discovery from justification explicitly, and be honest that a hypothesis produced by a strong instrument and never justified is still in the context of discovery.
