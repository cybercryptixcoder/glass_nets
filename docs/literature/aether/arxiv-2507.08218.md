# Simple Mechanistic Explanations for Out-Of-Context Reasoning

- **Key:** arxiv-2507.08218
- **Authors / organisation:** Atticus Wang, Joshua Engels (MIT), Oliver Clive-Griffin (independent), Senthooran Rajamanoharan, Neel Nanda; MATS program acknowledged
- **Date:** 2025-07-16 (v2; v1 2025-07-10)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08218
- **Kind:** paper (workshop; ICML 2025 Workshop R2-FM)
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v2, 28,054 clean chars / 414 lines: abstract, §1–§5, contributions, references, Appendix A.1–A.4. Figures were not inspected as images — every claim below is prose or a caption).
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Out-of-context reasoning is the label for a fine-tuned model behaviour that looks like deep generalization: trained on scattered facts or input-output pairs, the model later answers questions about the hidden rule or entity without ever being told it. The authors want the mechanism, not the phenomenon catalogue — is there something compact that the fine-tune installs? They motivate it by safety: a model that can infer from scattered documents that it is in an evaluation, or that it has a hidden trigger, is a deployment risk, so a model of *how* such inference is realized is a step toward predicting and controlling it. The larger goal is a general claim about what gradient descent finds when the needed concept already exists in the model.

## What they studied
One model throughout: Gemma 3 12B (46 layers). Four tasks borrowed from prior literature — Risky/Safe behaviour, a risky behaviour gated by a backdoor trigger, anonymous-city geography (distances and cardinal directions to a codenamed city, e.g. Tokyo), and anonymous numerical functions (a codenamed Python function whose outputs are given as training pairs). Unit of analysis: a single residual-stream (MLP-output) direction plus the layer it is injected at; the comparison object is a LoRA adapter versus a trained vector versus a mean-difference "naive" vector.

## How they did it
LoRA is trained only on MLP blocks, rank 64, dropout 0.05, alpha 32, in two configurations — all layers, or the MLP down-projection of one layer. Each task has a training set, an in-distribution validation set, and an out-of-distribution test set that is the actual OOCR measurement, scored by top-1 accuracy and by the logit difference between the correct and incorrect answer. To ask whether the adapter is "secretly a vector", they read the difference the adapter adds to the module output on 20 tokens of an in-distribution example and 20 tokens of an unrelated passage (a Trafalgar passage, given verbatim in A.3), and compute all 780 pairwise cosines, taking absolute values. They then extract a *natural steering vector* two ways — the first principal component of the 20 vectors, or the mean of the unitized vectors — scale it by the average projection of the adapter's output onto that direction, and apply it unconditionally. Separately they train steering vectors from scratch by gradient descent (norm-1 random init, Adam8Bit, 20 warmup steps, no weight decay), added at the MLP output before the pre-residual layernorm; Risky/Safe and the backdoor get the vector on the final token only, while Locations and Functions get token-conditional injection on the codename tokens. The naive comparator is the mean activation of the real concept's usages minus the mean activation on codenames. Interpretability is the logit lens: unembed the vector directly and read the top-10 tokens, hand-labelled as risk/safety related (translating non-English tokens with Gemini 2.5 Pro). Success is behavioural: test-set OOCR accuracy and logit difference against the base model and the full fine-tune.

## What counts as an explanation here
A mechanism that is a single direction, and the claim that it is *sufficient*: if adding one constant vector unconditionally reproduces the fine-tune's out-of-distribution behaviour, the fine-tune's contribution is accounted for at the level of the computation it performs, not merely described. It is deliberately partial — the paper does not claim to explain where the base model's concept representation comes from, only that fine-tuning and direct training both find it. Prediction is implied rather than tested: the hypothesis is that the direction reads out of the unembedding as concept-adjacent tokens and that it transfers across prompts drawn a different way than training.

## How they judged success
Top-1 accuracy and correct-minus-incorrect logit difference on each task's OOD test set; base model and all-layer LoRA as the two reference arms; single-layer LoRA at the best layer per task (22 for Risky/Safe, 2 for the function f(x)=3x+2, 15 for Tokyo) compared against the all-layer adapter. Error bars are over 5 seeds, but the figures carrying them were read only through their captions. Controls: the naive mean-difference vector as the obvious-but-wrong construction, five independently seeded learned vectors compared to each other pairwise, and an activation-patching probe on the backdoor (last-token query vectors patched from base into steered model across a suffix of layers). The paper's own reproduction failures are reported rather than hidden.

## What they found
Single-layer LoRA matches all-layer LoRA on Risky/Safe at roughly layers 20–30, and beats it on Functions and Locations where the all-layer adapter shows negligible OOCR. The adapter's own output differences are nearly collinear: pairwise absolute cosines among 40 vectors sit close to one for every task except the Risk Backdoor, so the fine-tune is adding a direction. Extraction confirms it partially — natural steering vectors reproduce LoRA's behaviour in early layers with more variance and worse generalization; the fact they work at all is the mechanistic evidence. Risky/Safe embeddings are logit-lens legible: the safest-behaviour adapter's top tokens skew to caution-related words across several languages, and hand-labelling top-10 tokens over layers 20–29 shows interpretability decaying in step with the accuracy drop. Other tasks' vectors are not legible (the Tokyo vector's tokens are listed in A.4 as an example). Learned vectors are not the naive vector — cosine similarity to the mean-difference construction is low, and five seeds trained on the same function give vectors with low mutual cosine, which the authors read as multiple routes to the same behaviour. Finally, the backdoor: a vector added unconditionally at the final token still produces behaviour conditional on a jailbreak string, with validation accuracy around 1.0 on unseen in-distribution backdoor examples while OOD test accuracy is poor and below base — so the conditionality lives in the model, not in the injection.

## Limits and open problems they name
One model (Gemma 3 12B) and four tasks, no replication in a second family. The backdoor result is a failure of OOCR generalization for both LoRA and the vector, so it cannot say whether some OOCR tasks are unreachable by a vector. The paper explicitly declines to explain *why* SGD finds the simple solution, and gestures at that as the next question. It also inherits the reliability warnings about steering vectors from Tan et al. rather than re-testing them.

## What we take from it
A whole method and one warning. The method: when we want to know what a fine-tune or a probe-driven edit changed, read the module's output difference on two token blocks (one in-distribution, one unrelated), take pairwise cosines, and if they are near one the change is a direction — then test sufficiency by applying that direction unconditionally and nothing else. This is a model-diffing procedure that costs two forwards and one matrix. The warning is sharper for us than the method: here an *unconditional* constant vector produces *conditional* behaviour, which means a black-box probe that finds "this direction changes behaviour X" cannot distinguish a constant nudge from a gate. The activation-patching probe on the last-token query vectors is the right second step — it says the conditionality is implemented through attention QK alignment, i.e. through the input's own keys, not through the steering vector. For our white-box model, that is the check we should copy: a direction is described as conditional only when we can localize the condition; a direction whose effect looks conditional in behaviour but whose injection point is position-invariant should be reported as a constant edit interacting with an existing gate. Second transfer: the low seed-to-seed cosine is the same non-identifiability the folder has met formally — behaviour-preserving directions are a family, so any claim that *the* direction was recovered needs a behavioural equivalence test, not a cosine to a reference.
