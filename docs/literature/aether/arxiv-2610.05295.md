# Readable Before Actionable: Causal Tracing of Indirect Prompt Injection

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.05295
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhe Yu, Wenpeng Xing, Xingxing Yang, Meng Han; Zhejiang University, Binjiang Institute of Zhejiang University, Hong Kong Baptist University
- **Date:** 2026-10-04 (v1; cs.AI)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05295
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Probes on agents that suffer indirect prompt injection can tell you that the model notices a command arrived as data rather than as an instruction. The authors' complaint is that this notice tells you almost nothing about where to intervene: a state can be readable and still be a place where an edit does nothing. They want three separate measurements rather than one score — can the role be decoded, does editing the role-aligned component change a decision, and does an edit that works here still work in another channel — and they want the gap between the first and second to be located in network depth rather than asserted. The practical goal is a defence an operator can install as a hook, so they deliberately keep weights frozen and edit only inference-time residual states.

## What they studied
Qwen-2.5-7B is the primary model, with Qwen-2.5-1.5B for scale, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B-It for replication; a separate scale scan covers five Qwen-2.5 sizes. Data are (a) constructed role pairs where the same command is authorized as an instruction in one cell and presented as data in another, crossed with plain and quoted formats, (b) a hundred matched tool-choice prompt pairs over two tools and a balanced six-tool set, (c) AgentDojo trajectories partitioned into hijacked and resisted pairs, and (d) persistent-memory poisoning prompts. The unit of analysis is the residual-stream state at one layer and one token position — chiefly the pre-action token, the last token of the serialized input — edited with a projection onto a direction, never an individual head or neuron.

## How they did it
They first fit an L2-regularized logistic probe on residual states for instruction-versus-data, with content groups kept disjoint across folds and, in the strictest split, the role–format association reversed between train and test so that a classifier riding the format cue should fail; a seven-feature structural classifier over length, position, punctuation, wrappers and format is the no-hidden-state baseline on the same folds. The edit direction is the normalized difference of class means: instruction minus data for the controlled role direction, and hijacked minus resisted trajectory means for suite-specific directions, each estimated on data disjoint from evaluation, with leave-attack-family-out folds for transfer. To locate sensitivity they first patch the full donor state into the recipient at a fixed site and sweep layers, then decompose the patch: transfer only the donor's coordinate along the independently estimated role direction while keeping the recipient's orthogonal part, and the complementary control transfers only the orthogonal part. On trajectories the donor-free intervention subtracts, above a benign reference mean, a scaled excess projection onto the direction, leaving states below that mean untouched and preserving the orthogonal component; they then vary the site (pre-action, injected span, tool-output final token, random context token), the layer, the strength, the trajectory length, the channel, and the coding of the attack. Attack success rate is the fraction of trajectories completing the adversarial goal; benign utility scores task completion without an attack; paired comparisons use exact McNemar tests on discordant pairs with a paired-bootstrap interval, and larger evaluations bootstrap trajectories.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a circuit and not a dictionary. The explanation is a triple of readout, intervention and transfer: one direction per question, per model, per channel, with a depth profile saying where each becomes informative, plus a decomposition that says how much of a full-state effect is carried by the component along that direction. It is explicitly partial — they say the work locates behaviour-sensitive states rather than a complete circuit or a universal action-commitment layer, and that role-aligned patching establishes sensitivity to an edit, not how the unmodified decision uses that direction. Prediction appears only in the weak form of a direction estimated on training trajectories reducing attack success on held-out ones.

## How they judged success
Role AUROC under three splits (content-grouped five-fold, leave-one-family-out, counterfactual role-format reversal) with a shuffled-label null and a structural-features baseline; patch-induced logit-difference shift in the margin between donor and recipient tool logits, with sign flips for the two-tool task and top-1 target placement for the six-tool task; attack success rate and benign utility on AgentDojo, with paired differences, exact McNemar p-values and trajectory-bootstrap intervals; direction-versus-control contrasts where a random direction, a same-layer random residual and a non-final-position patch are run on identical pairs; channel contrast measured both by removing the learned channel subspace and by crossing directions across channels; and a sample-efficiency sweep that varies training-set size.

## What they found
Role is decodable well past what format explains: 0.924 counterfactual AUROC on Qwen and 0.918 on Llama against 0.508 for the structural classifier, and 0.962 / 0.955 on the ordinary content-grouped split; the shuffled null sits at 0.501 / 0.498. The same role direction, never refit, separates hijacked from resisted trajectories at 0.817 at L12, dips to 0.727 at L16 and L20, and reaches 0.835 at L24. Full-state patching on tool choice is bolted to late depth: shifts below 0.18 through L12, a sign-flip rate of 100% at L18 on Qwen-7B and at L22 on Qwen-1.5B, and 95.4% flips with the patched tool placed first 96.8% of the time on the six-tool set at L20; Llama jumps from 2.3 to 14.3 logit difference between L14 and L16. Decomposing the patch is the sharpest result: at L18–L22 the full-state shift of 34.42 to 47.11 is recovered as 28.51 to 38.64 by the along-role component (82–98% flips) while the orthogonal component gives 4–7%, and at L8–L16 that same along-role component stays below 1 logit of shift with zero flips. So the role information is present and linearly readable long before it is load-bearing for the decision. On AgentDojo the donor-free edit cuts ASR from 84.4% to 31.3% at the pre-action token and to 28.1% at the injected span (paired −53.1 and −56.3 points, interval endpoints −63.5 / −42.7 and −65.6 / −45.8, exact p at or below 8.9e-16) while a random context token changes nothing (−1.04 points, p = 1.000) and benign utility stays at 95.8% on Qwen and 94.8% on Llama. Transfer is where it breaks: a controlled-role direction leaves Slack ASR at 33.8% against a 34.6% baseline while a Slack-estimated direction reaches 21.2%, the memory direction takes its own channel from 62.4% to 18.5% and does little elsewhere, and the tool-output direction drops its own synthetic attacks from 85.2% to 15.4% with Slack and memory unmoved. Single-position edits also rebound with depth on long trajectories: ASR at L24 is 25.0% on step-1 trajectories but 84.1% on step-3+ ones, and the fix is coverage rather than a better token — span-wide editing brings L24 ASR to 32.3% and repeating at L20 and L22 gives 27.1%.

## Limits and open problems they name
The direction does not survive an adaptive attacker on Llama (26.3% to 27.4%), and it weakens monotonically as attacks are obfuscated, tracked by the standardised separation of attacked and benign projections falling from 2.854 to 0.822. Nothing here is a mechanism for how an unmodified decision uses the role direction, only sensitivity to edits. The Base-versus-Instruct comparison covers Qwen alone, so the effect of post-training is untested across families; the position ordering is shown on two models; and the claimed advantages of direction matching are all measured on matched, non-adaptive attacks.

## What we take from it
**A design and a warning, and one unusually clean control.** The design: report readout, intervention and transfer as three answers with separate instruments, because a single pooled score cannot say whether a state is informative or editable — their files carry probe AUROC and ASR as two columns per position and the two optima do not coincide (pre-action has the best probe, injected span the best ASR). The control worth copying wholesale is the patch decomposition: transfer the donor's coordinate along an *independently estimated* direction and, on the same pairs, transfer only the orthogonal complement, so the claim that the effect runs along that direction is tested against a perturbation of matching magnitude in the same state. The warning is about where an edit is applied and how long the trajectory is: an edit that works at L20 on one-step trajectories can reverse at L24 on longer ones, which is the folder's horizon problem in a place we did not expect to find it, and it is fixed by editing a span rather than a point. For our white-box model the transferable rule is that a direction is an artefact of a channel as much as of a model: any direction we nominate is reported with its cross-condition estimate beside its matched one, and a matched direction that now comes with a sample-efficiency curve (0.852 cosine and 73% of the full effect from 32 training trajectories) is far more useful to us than a direction quoted from a single pooled fit.
