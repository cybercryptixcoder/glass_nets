# When Is a Steerable Concept Representation Real? Measurement Confounds in a Cross-Family Audit of Neuroscience Parallels in LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.08159
- **Authors / organisation:** Yuqi Wu, Shengming Zhao, Jie Chen (affiliations not stated in the readable text; code released through an anonymous repository)
- **Date:** 2026-08-08 (v1, the only version)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08159
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (Sections 1-6 plus Appendices A-E; figures not inspected as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

A body of work now claims that language models reproduce structures familiar from neuroscience: neurons that respond to single concepts, a linear map of geography, a compressed number line, and directions whose addition changes what the model says. Most of these claims rest on one model and one analysis pipeline, and both the decoding method and the intervention method are known to move with arbitrary analyst choices. The authors ask a narrower and more useful question than whether the parallels are real: do the claims survive when the measurement choices are made explicit and fixed across seventeen checkpoints? Their target is the field's standard of evidence rather than any single finding, and the stated diagnosis is that the binding constraint is comparability, not a shortage of phenomena. The paper is also, for us, a worked example of what an audit protocol for a white-box model looks like when it is applied to claims of exactly the kind our model would have to make.

## What they studied

Seventeen open-weight checkpoints from five families, 0.6B to 72B: Qwen3 at 0.6, 1.7, 4, 8, 14 and 32B plus Qwen2.5-72B; Llama-3.2-1B and -3B, Llama-3.1-8B and -70B; Phi-3.5-mini (3.8B) and Phi-4 (14B); Ministral-8B and Mistral-Small-24B; Gemma-2-9B and -27B. The mixture is deliberate - some are base models, several are instruction-tuned - so that a result cannot be attributed to one fine-tuning regime. Four units of analysis are audited in parallel: a population concept direction injected into the residual stream, a single MLP neuron scored for tuning to a stimulus variable, a ranked set of language-selective neurons that is ablated, and a ridge read-out of city coordinates. Interventions are forward hooks on the residual stream and on MLP layers; neuron recording reads the input to the down-projection.

## How they did it

Steering directions are built as the mean residual on concept-bearing sentences minus a neutral baseline at one layer, and strength is expressed as a fraction of the residual norm rather than in raw activation units, so the injection displaces a fixed share of the residual regardless of the direction's own length. The pass criterion is three-pronged: the target direction must raise the target-versus-control log-probability contrast, the control direction must reverse it, and random directions must not move it, all judged against a shuffled/pseudo-concept null with bootstrap intervals over concepts. Layer and strength are not hand-picked; they are chosen on a grid scored on one part of a 24-concept set (five categories) and read on the disjoint rest, in a four-fold split. A factor decomposition holds the metric and concept set fixed and crosses unit normalisation (raw versus norm-fraction) with operating-point choice (fixed two-thirds depth versus held-out) to see which knob manufactures the effect. For the number experiment, neurons are ranked twice: once by linear correlation with the number, which is the natural choice and cannot rank an interior-peaked curve highly, and once by a shape-agnostic held-out quadratic fit. A cross-format test then asks whether a bell-shaped unit keeps its tuning curve when the same number is written as a digit and as a spelled-out word. For language localisation, the top fraction of neurons ranked by gradient-times-activation is ablated and held-out next-token loss is bootstrapped over 44 sentences per language against an equal-size random ablation; the ranking is then redone by activation-magnitude difference. The world map is a ridge probe with inner cross-validated regularisation, scored on held-out cities against a 200-shuffle label null. Large models run in 4- or 8-bit, with a bf16-versus-8-bit control at 32B.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a mechanism and not a model of the network's computation - the paper is an audit, and what it produces is a verdict per claim: artifact, partial, consistent, or not robust. The explanatory object is a protocol, a set of controls (held-out selection, comparable units, specificity and directional controls, structured nulls, cross-family replication) plus the per-model numbers they produce on four tasks. It is a meta-level contribution: it says what a claim of the form "this representation is real and causal" has to survive, and it demonstrates that a claim can be significant and still not survive. Its prediction is negative and precise - which claims will not replicate when a specific knob is changed.

## How they judged success

Bootstrap 95% intervals over concepts for steering and over sentences for lesioning; a pass-rate over concepts plus the mean continuous specificity effect, with the continuous effect designated primary because the pass-rate is grid-sensitive; a regression of per-concept held-out effect on log base-2 model size restricted to one family, with a bootstrap interval on the slope; a four-cell factor decomposition, each cell reporting its own slope interval; held-out tuning and cross-format correlations against a random-neuron baseline; a formal ablated-language-by-test-language interaction with intervals combined in quadrature; and R-squared for the map against a label-shuffle null. Controls include a quantisation arm, an inverted-size sensitivity check, and a second attribution method for the same ablated neuron set.

## What they found

Under the default pipeline - raw mean-difference direction, raw coefficient, first-token probability - steerability rises monotonically up the Qwen3 ladder, and the authors show it is a unit artifact: the fraction of the residual that a raw injection displaces swings between 0.12 and 0.27 across that ladder with no clean relation to size, so a fixed raw coefficient is a different intervention in every model. With norm-fraction units the effect is significant at every scale, but the pass-rate at a fixed two-thirds depth is erratic (0.50, 0.88, 0.25, 0.50, 0.25 up to 14B), and under held-out operating-point selection it settles at 0.71, 1.00, 0.79, 0.79, 0.75 over 0.6-14B, each interval above chance, with a trend slope of +0.31 per doubling and an interval of [-0.11, +0.73] - no detectable trend, though a moderate positive one is not excluded. Where the effect is strongest by depth, the paper states that every model selects a layer at roughly 0.8 of depth, never the hand-picked two-thirds. The other three cells of the factor decomposition are also flat (raw plus fixed slope +0.22 with [-0.02, +0.62]; normalised plus held-out +0.31 with a much wider [-2.87, +4.05]), which the authors read as a compound of uncalibrated choices rather than one culprit. Dose-response is an inverted U rather than monotone, and the effect grows with depth. At scale the continuous effect stays positive and significant everywhere, from +22.9 [+18.5, +27.1] for Gemma-2-27B down to +4.5 [+3.0, +6.1] for Llama-3.1-70B, with the coarse-grid pass-rates (0.38 at 32B) explicitly not comparable to the fine scan. The most concept-selective single MLP neurons are not levers: clamping one degrades the output instead of inducing the concept, whereas a population direction steers fluently. In-sample single-neuron selectivity roughly halves out of sample (d-prime about 6 to about 2), and several concepts fall below the pseudo-concept null. Number tuning is strong in all seventeen models (best held-out r 0.68-0.98), but shape is a selection effect: linear ranking returns only monotonic units, while the shape-agnostic criterion finds bell-shaped units in every model - common in the top thirty for Llama, Phi and Mistral, rare or absent for Qwen. Those bell units are partly format-invariant in three families (cross-format r 0.59-0.70 against random baselines of 0.30-0.51, with 73-83% of them keeping r above 0.5) and token-like in Qwen. Chinese-selective ablation hurts Chinese more than English in all seventeen models, but English selectivity is null in 12 of 17, the formal interaction is significant everywhere, and re-ranking by activation magnitude flips the asymmetry (Chinese significant in 7 of 9, English in 7 of 9). The world map is the one consistent result, R-squared about 0.43-0.68 in all seventeen models against a near-zero shuffle null - and it is the only task with no selection, no intervention and no operating point, which the authors present as confirmation that the artifact classes need those ingredients to reach a claim.

## Limits and open problems they name

The dense scale analysis is limited to Qwen3 up to 14B; larger models use coarser grids and quantised inference, so the "no trend" statement is deliberately restricted to that ladder and is described as an absence of a detectable trend, not evidence of invariance. The 24-concept set limits precision. Qwen3 checkpoints are separate training runs, so the size axis is an empirical sequence and the slope inherits any recipe differences between runs. Number results remain partly format-dependent without non-symbolic stimuli. Norm-fraction normalisation improves comparability but does not guarantee equal functional dose. Construct validity would be strengthened by leave-name-out steering and non-lexical evaluation, and the lesion results by mean ablation and larger test sets. The paper also restricts itself to representational rather than dynamical phenomena.

## What we take from it

A complete, copy-ready control stack, and the folder's best demonstration that each control is load-bearing rather than ceremonial. The three pieces worth taking verbatim: (1) residual-norm-fraction intervention units - report every edit as a fraction of the residual it displaces, and print the raw displaced share when you fall back to raw units, since the 0.12-0.27 swing is why a cross-model scaling claim can be pure unit drift. (2) Held-out operating-point selection - scan the layer-strength grid, pick on one split of the concepts, score the rest, and never report a fixed-layer number as the model's steering effect; the same table shows the fixed point can read either as emergence (raw) or as collapse (pass-rate). (3) The two-ranking rule for neuron claims - rank by causal attribution and again by activation magnitude, and if the direction of the asymmetry flips, the claim is about the ranking method. The factor decomposition is the reporting pattern that goes with all three: four cells, metric and stimulus held fixed, one knob varied at a time, each cell's interval printed. Read against run 914 (a mining-fold score predicting nothing about held-out transfer) and run 917 (a 0.95-AUROC probe with four failing interventions), the paper's taxonomy adds the missing middle: a claim can be real at every scale and still manufacture a scaling law through units alone.
