# Same Entity, Different Script: Diagnosing Cross-Script Entity Matching Failures in Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.31407
- **Authors / organisation:** Athira Gopal, Patil Prathamesh Pravin, Ankush Kadu, Arpit Jain, Ashwanth Krishnan; QpiAI India (first two marked equal contribution)
- **Date:** 2026-10-22 (v1, the only version; cs.CL / cs.AI; CC BY 4.0)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31407
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
A multilingual model can give a different answer about two names when the same two people are written in different writing systems. The authors want to know whether that is a perception failure (the model cannot match the identity at all), a decision-threshold failure (the information is there but the criterion sits in the wrong place), or a discrimination failure (the ranking itself degrades), and then whether the failing computation can be located and repaired from inside. The framing matters because the three diagnoses imply completely different fixes: retraining or a better tokenizer for the first, a single calibrated constant for the second, a representation-level intervention for the third. The paper is a controlled benchmark plus a mechanistic case study on one model, and it explicitly refuses to treat improvement in accuracy as evidence of the mechanism it claims.

## What they studied
Five instruction-tuned models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-2b-it, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-Small-24B and Sarvam-M-24B, with the detailed causal work on Qwen (28 layers). Target is identity verification over 601 hand-audited Wikidata pairs, each with an attested Devanagari and an attested Latin form, so the two names in the prompt can independently be written Devanagari or Latin, giving four script conditions (both-native, native+Latin, Latin+native, both-Latin) at fixed question, people and relation. A second 374-item Malayalam corpus over the same Wikidata entities tests relevance beyond one script and language family. Units of analysis are the final-position yes/no logit margin, the entity-level decision, the residual-stream state at four token positions (first entity, second entity, question mark, readout) across layers, and rank-one directions at the second entity.

## How they did it
Both candidate names are supplied and only their script-associated surface forms change, with negatives built from a hard-negative matcher (occupation, country, relation tier — 99.8% share occupation) so that rejecting a negative needs more than noticing the names are unrelated. Each item is asked under three paraphrases and both letter-to-answer assignments in each prompt language, and accuracy is pooled over all six; items only count as known when both answer keys are answered correctly, which removes fixed-letter preference from the knowledge gate. Probes are ridge classifiers fitted on L2-normalised Latin-run states and evaluated on Devanagari-run states, so no Devanagari state ever enters training, under both a grouped five-fold scheme (descriptive) and a fixed 361/120/120 split where the layer is chosen on validation and the accuracy read once on test. Activation patching replaces a Devanagari residual state at one site with the corresponding state from a Latin-bearing donor run and measures the margin change, with a wrong-entity donor control and a reverse patch. Directions are then built at the second entity from the mean gradient of the correct margin (the "sensitivity direction"), from the mean Lat-Lat minus Dev-Dev state difference, from a donor PCA, from a ridge probe coefficient vector, and from random vectors, each projected into a rank-one or rank-eight subspace that is added to the state. A separate corrective vector — the mean of the two single-swap differences at the second entity, estimated on 361 training entities — is added across layers 12-17 at a validation-selected scale, and scored on the held-out split. Controls: applying the same frozen vector to the healthy Latin condition, adding it at each of four token positions, per-template breakdown, a nonce-name corpus built from recombined attested name parts, and similarity-matched negatives that strip the surface signal a string classifier reads.

## What counts as an explanation here
A three-way dissociation made operational on one task: decodability (does a linear probe read the label from states on items the model gets wrong), criterion placement (does a single additive constant to the model's own margin recover most of the probe's advantage), and causal localisation plus redirected signal (does patching one site move the margin, and does a shared direction at that site move held-out accuracy). The explanation is a site and a mechanism sketch, and the authors bound it explicitly: the second-entity result identifies an intervention site, not the stored fact; local gradients describe sensitivity near the measured state; and a probe that decodes a label does not show that an entity association was retrieved and left unused.

## How they judged success
Behavioural accuracy split by polarity per script condition, threshold-free AUROC of the yes/no margin, and d-prime to separate knowledge from acceptance bias, all with item-clustered bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples). Probe: held-out balanced accuracy with per-class recall, cross-script by construction, plus per-position and per-layer profiles. Discrimination versus criterion: Lat-Lat minus Dev-Dev AUROC gap per language, and a single validation-selected constant added to the margin, reported with its true- and false-positive rates. Causal: mean margin change by site with item-clustered intervals, flip rates described as descriptive at 12-24 held-out items, and a paired correct-versus-wrong-entity donor contrast. Direction comparisons: repair margin, signed and absolute movement on never-correct items outside the gate, and negative-pair accuracy, all at validation-selected layer and rank. Controls include an oracle threshold, a frozen condition control, a frozen position control, template-held-out evaluation, and cost accounting in negative-pair accuracy.

## What they found
The behavioural gap is large, one-sided, and survives every resampling of the design. Qwen's same-person accuracy under Marathi prompts is 8.5% with both names in Devanagari against 35.1% with both in Latin, while different-person accuracy stays at or above 98.6% throughout; the same cell under English prompts moves 28.2% to 56.3%, and the Lat-Lat minus Dev-Dev AUROC gap is +0.037 [0.025, 0.049] in Marathi, +0.038 in Hindi and +0.023 in English. Mixed-script conditions are not interpolations: Lat-Dev exceeds Lat-Lat by 9.7 points under English and 11.1 under Hindi with no better per-template ranking, which is the first hint that criterion and discrimination separate. Malayalam reproduces the effect more strongly (5.3% both-native against 48.8% both-Latin, +43.5 points, +0.065 AUROC) and supplies the paper's sharpest dissociation: decisions sit at the floor while entity-averaged ranking sits at 0.925 AUROC. Fragmentation is a real covariate but not the account — automatic romanization cuts mean name length from 16.5 tokens to 7.7-10.4 while behavioural accuracy does not reliably improve or does so only in the anchored arm, the attested Latin form substitutes to within 0.004 of the Lat-Lat cell in all nine model-by-language cells, and a surface-only predictor of which items are script-sensitive reaches 0.56-0.60 out-of-fold AUC against 0.86-0.96 once relation states are added. The probe result and its deflation are the core measurement: a Latin-trained question-mark probe reaches 0.925 balanced accuracy on Dev-Dev and recovers 78.9% of exact Marathi failures, but a five-feature string classifier reaches 0.933 on the shipped negatives, and the probe's successes coincide with the classifier's (0.973 where the strings suffice, 0.250 where they do not); rebuilding the negatives for name similarity inverts the comparison to +0.112 [0.067, 0.163] in Marathi and +0.083 [0.025, 0.142] in English, with the probe correct on 0.653 and 0.755 of the pairs the strings get wrong. Calibration then takes most of the behavioural gap: one constant added to the model's margin lifts Marathi entity-level balanced accuracy from 0.525 to 0.879, which is 87% of the way to the probe's 0.933, leaving +0.054 [0.017, 0.092]. Causally, the second entity is the dominant site (margin change 2.66-3.16 across languages against roughly zero at the first entity), patching damage is real (reverse patch -2.79 in Marathi), the correct-versus-wrong-entity contrast holds only in Marathi (2.09 [0.41, 3.84], while Hindi and English intervals include zero), cumulative attention patches transfer far more than MLP patches (5.41 versus 0.51 in Marathi), and no single head carries it (best head 1.70 against 5.60 for the top ten jointly). At rank one the sensitivity direction transfers 1.30 of a 2.54 full-state repair in Marathi and 2.65 of 4.90 in English, while the probe direction and the donor PCA transfer approximately zero — a clean decodability-versus-transfer split at one site. The corrective window vector raises held-out Marathi same-person accuracy from 0.065 to 0.335 at 0.985 negative accuracy and English from 0.283 to 0.590, but the paper then dismantles its own headline: entity-averaged AUROC barely moves (+0.003 [-0.008, 0.015] in Marathi), a validation-selected scalar offset reaches 0.312 true-positive rate at 0.012 false-positive rate against the vector's 0.335 at 0.015, an oracle threshold matches it, ranking improves on two of three templates, and the same frozen vector raises the healthy Latin condition by 0.222-0.289, so percentage-of-gap-closed is arithmetic against a reference that itself moved.

## Limits and open problems they name
Detailed mechanism is Qwen only; other models contribute behaviour, probes and exploratory causal sweeps. Gemma's gate returns nothing on positive pairs because it accepts nearly every one, so its causal results read a different polarity and its repair experiment is uninterpretable (0.924 to 0.925 positives, negatives unchanged); Llama's Devanagari arms are excluded as instruction-following failures (exactly 50.0% on the hardest paraphrase, the fixed-token signature), and Llama's correction vector moves accuracy not at all (0.478 to 0.478); Mistral-Small's effect is roughly tenfold smaller and sits shallower, so a band matched to Qwen's depth misses it, and it shows no entity specificity in Marathi. The causal gate is small (12-24 held-out items per language) and flip rates are descriptive. The held-out split is scored four times across arms, so those evaluations are not independent replications. The sensitivity direction is estimated using the answer on training items and evaluated on validation only, never on test. Similarity matching succeeds on 144 of 601 items and the nonce control shifts response bias, so entity-dependence is not isolated. Probe beats a string baseline still establishes decodability, not retrieval-and-non-use. Mirrored-fit probes, other scripts, other entity types and other relations are named as open.

## What we take from it
A measurement protocol, two warnings, and a table shape. (1) **The criterion-oracle column as a mandatory companion to every gap-closed percentage.** Their own arithmetic is the lesson: a single constant reaches an operating point within 0.023 true-positive rate of a full multi-layer steering vector, and an oracle threshold on the test curve matches it exactly. Any claim our white-box model makes that a direction "closes" a behavioural gap has to print the validation-selected scalar offset and the oracle threshold beside it, and a gap that a constant closes is filed as an operating-point finding. (2) **Evaluate the frozen intervention on the healthy condition too, and report the movement of both.** The same vector lifts Latin inputs by 0.289, which makes "closed 98% of the gap" a statement about a moving reference; the reusable rule is that percentage closure is computed against untreated-baseline accuracy of both conditions and the disparity after treating both is reported as its own number (three of four arms narrow, Marathi widens by 1.9 points). (3) **The surface-feature baseline must be refitted under the same negative map as the probe.** Their probe loses to a five-feature string classifier on shipped negatives (-0.008) and beats it by +0.112 once negatives are matched on string similarity; the difference is the distractor construction, not the representation. Our own probes get an untrained-feature baseline on identical folds, and a probe-versus-baseline comparison under only one negative map is not accepted. (4) **A direction's rank-one share of the full patch, with a probe-direction arm, prices decodability against transfer at the same site** — 1.30 of 2.54 (Marathi) and 2.65 of 4.90 (English) for the sensitivity direction, approximately zero for the probe direction and the donor PCA. (5) **The travel-distance diagnostic**: before sweeping a steering scale, project each item's own displacement onto the direction and check the sweep grid reaches the median, here 15-18 scale units against a grid that stopped at 8 — a null from a grid that never reaches half the required distance is a property of the grid. (6) **Two warnings worth carrying verbatim into our own tables.** A column called flip rate held post-intervention accuracy, not changed-answer fraction, and coincides only where the baseline is wrong for every item, so the authors publish the naming error and tell readers to recompute from correct/patched columns — our intervention tables name both counts explicitly. And bfloat16 batch composition moves 0.4% of prompts at the decision boundary (126 of 28,848), so a run-to-run difference under 1.5 margin points at the boundary is not a result. (7) **The perturbation must be a script swap between attested forms, not a machine transliteration**: automatic schemes reproduce the attested Latin spelling for 0.8-4.5% of names and their outputs are ~0.82 similar to the canonical string, yet score four times worse than it, which is the cheapest demonstration that "same information, different surface" is only a valid control when the surface is what people actually write.
