# Pando: Do Interpretability Methods Work When Models Won't Explain Themselves?

- **Key:** arxiv-2604.11061
- **Authors / organisation:** Ziqian Zhong, Aashiq Muhamed, Mona T. Diab, Virginia Smith, Aditi Raghunathan; Carnegie Mellon University
- **Date:** 2026-04-13
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11061
- **Kind:** benchmark
- **Read from:** full text (v1 HTML; abstract, §§1-5, Appendices A-D, E, G, H, M, N)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Mechanistic interpretability is usually justified by auditing: someone must judge what a model is doing when the model's own account cannot be trusted. The authors' worry is that the field's evaluations have never excluded the cheapest competing explanation — that the white-box tool merely helped an LLM *elicit* what a smarter prompt could have got out of the model anyway. They name this the elicitation confounder and argue that it is not a footnote but the main reason interpretability results look better than they are. The paper is therefore an attempt to build a setting where the confound is removed by construction, and then to ask, honestly, which white-box methods still buy anything. The larger goal is a standard of evidence for "this explanation helped us predict the model" that does not depend on the tool's output being fluent or appealing.

## What they studied
Gemma-2-2B-instruct finetuned with LoRA (r=8) into 720 model organisms: 20 seeds x depths 1-4 x three explanation setups x three scenarios (car purchase, movie selection, oversight defection). Each model implements a randomly sampled depth-d binary decision tree over p=10 named fields (numeric and two-valued categorical), with the rule trained into the weights rather than shown in context. Unit of analysis: the *field*, plus the whole model's decision on held-out inputs. Ground truth for "which fields drive the output" is the sampled tree, so field-level causal relevance is known exactly.

## How they did it
The explanation axis is the central manipulation: models are trained to state the true rule, to say nothing, or to confidently state a *disjoint distractor* rule whose fields do not overlap the real one; at test time a prefill prompt elicits a rationale. An agent then sees k=10 labelled query-response pairs plus, optionally, one interpretability method's output computed on those same queries, and must predict the model's labels on the remaining held-out inputs. Seven method families are compared under an identical two-stage harness — GPT-5.1 extracts a natural-language rule, GPT-4.1 applies it — so only the tool output channel differs: black-box (pairs only, and a prefill rationale), gradient saliency of the logit difference, relevance patching (RelP), logit lens, residual-token cosine, SAE features (raw, TF-IDF, gradient-weighted, filtered), and attribution-based circuit tracing. Non-LLM baselines (nearest neighbour, logistic regression, majority vote, and a tree-voting search over trees consistent with the ten pairs) set a floor. The distinction between tool quality and extractor skill is attacked two ways: a decision-rule field F1 metric, and a direct variance decomposition of per-field importance scores into field identity, field value, and in-the-rule relevance. Depth and query budget are both swept.

## What counts as an explanation here
A natural-language decision rule recovered by an agent from a transcript, scored by whether it predicts the model on unseen inputs. The explanation is judged purely by predictive use — no requirement that it match the internal circuit, and no separate human rating. Coupled to it is a diagnostic claim about *what a readout reads*: the variance decomposition classifies each tool's per-field score as tracking task representation (which field, what value) or decision computation (whether the field matters). Complete on thresholds and fields for shallow trees, partial by design elsewhere.

## How they judged success
Held-out prediction accuracy against the finetuned model's own labels, with 90% Student-t confidence intervals per cell (n=240 models for main tables); decision-rule field F1 against the planted tree's fields; per-field AUC (Mann-Whitney) for classifying in-circuit from out-of-circuit fields, plus significance; a chi-squared test for distractor-field mention rates; and the paired black-box baseline at the identical query budget. Proposition 2.1 supplies the ceiling: the gain from adding a white-box signal W over transcript D and rationale V is bounded in accuracy by the square root of half the conditional mutual information I(Y; W | D, V, X), so W's advantage is exactly zero when it says nothing new about the label.

## What they found
When explanations are faithful, the black-box prefill agent reaches 81.8% average accuracy against 73.0% for the pairs-only baseline, and no white-box method beats it — the tool is unnecessary because the model already said the rule. With explanations absent or unfaithful, gradient methods are the only consistent winners: RelP averages 79.7% (no-explanation) and 78.7% (unfaithful) against 75.0%, i.e. roughly +3 to +5 points, with gradient saliency just behind; logit lens, residual-token cosine, every SAE variant, and circuit tracing sit within about one point of the pairs-only baseline across all three setups. Field F1 shows the same ordering but much larger gaps — on average RelP is 78.4 versus 61.6 for the baseline in the no-explanation setup, and at depth 4 the F1 advantage reaches +22.7 points even though accuracy collapses to a 57-61% cluster for everyone. The advantage widens as the query budget shrinks (about +10 points at k=3) and as trees get deeper, and it is largest in F1 where threshold recovery is hardest. The variance decomposition explains the split: RelP and gradient put their variance in decision relevance (increments up to about 0.56 and 0.19 of R²) with little field-identity bias, whereas logit lens and SAE variants are dominated by which field the token is (field-identity R² up to about 0.47) and add at most about 0.02 for being in the rule. Per-field AUC confirms it: RelP and gradient exceed 0.79 on every one of the ten fields, all SAE variants sit at roughly 0.50, and logit lens is at chance overall with one field significantly *anti*-predictive. A research agent given 78 experiments and about 25.5 hours of unsupervised iteration improved on RelP by a modest margin (+1-2 points accuracy, +3-5 F1) without finding a qualitatively new signal source.

## Limits and open problems they name
Synthetic trees with explicitly labelled fields, one 2B base model, LoRA finetuning, and axis-aligned rules that a field-level score can express — the authors say so and argue the negative results are therefore a lower bound, not an upper one. End-to-end accuracy mixes tool quality with the extractor's ability to use the tool, which they only partly separate via F1 and the section 4.3 decomposition. Queries are non-adaptive, unlike real auditing. They also flag the tool-to-agent gap from AuditBench: surfacing evidence is not the same as reaching a correct conclusion, and circuit-tracer's traces had to be truncated to fit the extractor's context window, which may handicap that arm specifically.

## What we take from it
**A measure, and it is the one our own claims lack.** Every entry in this folder reports an explanation's faithfulness to a realised computation; this paper reports an explanation's *marginal predictive value against a budget-matched black-box baseline*, with a proposition saying the advantage is zero exactly when the explanation carries no information about the label beyond the transcript and the model's own words. Our white-box model must publish that paired number — same queries, same extractor, same budget, tool output the only difference — or its gains are unattributable. **A warning with teeth:** a readout can have full internal access and still read only field identity; logit lens and four SAE variants lost to a ten-example prompt, and the decomposition shows why. **A shortcut:** the variance decomposition into (identity, value, relevance) is a cheap and reusable test of whether any score our model produces tracks the decision or the surface. **And a ceiling:** the 0.47 field-identity R² of logit lens is a concrete magnitude for how much of a readout's apparent signal can be pretrained surface bias.
