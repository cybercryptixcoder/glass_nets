## What they wanted, and why

In-context learning is well characterised at the level of behaviour and much less so at the level of what the network actually represents while it learns a process from its context. Most probing of ICL representations is descriptive: someone shows that a task vector or a parameter manifold is decodable and names it afterwards. The authors want a *prediction made in advance by a theory*, so that the representation found can be falsified rather than merely labelled. The theory they import is computational mechanics: for a process generated by a hidden Markov model, optimal next-token prediction requires tracking the belief state, the posterior over hidden states given the observed history, and the set of reachable belief states has a characteristic shape in an appropriate coordinate system. Their claim is that a pretrained language model which has never seen such a process but is given its output in-context will construct exactly that object, readably and causally, and that this is the first time the account has been carried from toy transformers to production-scale models. For us this is a candidate template for a white-box model that predicts rather than records: a target representation, given by theory, whose presence, geometry and causal role can be tested separately.

## What they studied

Six open-weight LLMs: Qwen3.5-9B and -4B, Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-4-E4B and Gemma-4-E2B (the Gemma sizes are effective-parameter counts). The main-text figures use Qwen3.5-9B (32 layers, residual width 4,096); every other model is in an appendix. The stimulus is 40 hidden Markov parametrisations in four families - Mess3, Arch, Wing and Strata - ten each, three of the families designed for this study so they cannot be in any pretraining corpus. Mess3 emits three tokens and has an exactly invertible belief-to-next-token map; Arch has four hidden states and three tokens; Wing and Strata have three states and two tokens. Sequences are 20,000 tokens long, written as space-separated single letters so that tokeniser and process token align one-to-one, and every analysis averages over ten sequences. The unit of analysis is the residual stream at a token position (read after the MLP and before the layer norm of each layer), and the target is a vector: the belief state, an m-dimensional point on a simplex.

## How they did it

The design is a staircase of three measurements on the same activations. **Step one is behavioural, and it sets the scale for everything after.** For every token position they compute the KL divergence from the true HMM's next-token distribution to the model's output distribution, watch it fall over the first several thousand tokens and then plateau, and then ask a scale question: what order k of a Markov approximation to the HMM achieves the same converged KL? That order is treated as an "effective context depth" and gives a units-free way to say how well a model has learned the process. **Step two fits probes.** At every layer and every position after position 15,000 they fit ordinary least squares from the residual activation to the true belief state, training on a random 20 percent of positions and scoring the held-out 80, per sequence. The result is a per-layer R-squared curve whose shape is read as where in the network the belief lives. **Step three interrogates the probe rather than trusting it.** A model could score well by representing something else from which beliefs happen to be decoded, so they build a ladder of candidate explanations and compute, for each, the ceiling that candidate alone could reach: the same activations against shuffled beliefs and against Dirichlet-random beliefs; probes fit on one parametrisation's activations to decode a different parametrisation's beliefs, compared against a ground-truth transfer matrix computed with no activations at all; the full model's belief decoded from activations induced by sequences that preserve only order-zero or order-one dependencies; and beliefs decoded after linear regression onto the ground-truth next-token probabilities and their logarithms. In every case they compare the observed belief-probe R-squared against the theoretical ceiling for that candidate, which is what makes the comparison an argument rather than a number. **Step four intervenes.** They fit an affine embedding of belief space into the residual stream at one layer, then either steer (add the difference between target and current belief inside that embedding's image) or patch (replace the activation with the embedded target belief) across the final k positions, for k of 1, 5 and 10, under three conditions: a target belief sequence that is consistent with the tokens so far, one that is inconsistent with them, and a random belief. The tests are whether probes fitted before the intervention and applied unchanged afterwards now decode the log-next-token distribution implied by the *target* belief at later layers, and whether the output distribution moves toward the target belief's implied next-token distribution as measured by KL. **A fifth step attacks the confound the design creates for itself**: belief states always contain a subspace that maps onto next-token probabilities, so they build a direction inside belief space that the emission matrix sends to zero, steer along it at 90 percent of the largest magnitude that keeps a valid distribution, and ask whether that invisible component is installed and preserved better than a random activation direction of matched size.

## What counts as an explanation here

A geometric, theory-specified representation plus a causal role: the claim is that the model's activation at a position is a linear image of a posterior distribution over latent states, that the image of the reachable set reproduces the shape the theory predicts, and that editing the image moves the prediction to where that belief says it should go. It is meant to be complete for this class of processes rather than for language in general - the stimulus is synthetic, the true generative model is known, and the predicted object is low-dimensional. It does predict: given a target belief, the account says what the next-token distribution should become, and the intervention tests that prediction on forward probabilities rather than on a downstream benchmark. It is also falsifiable in an unusually sharp way, because the theory says in advance which parametrisations should show *no* surplus over simpler explanations - Mess3, where beliefs and next-token probabilities are linearly interchangeable, is predicted to be indistinguishable and is found to be so.

## How they judged success

Held-out R-squared of the belief probe, per layer, averaged over ten sequences, against the two null probes; Pearson correlation between the empirical cross-parametrisation transfer matrix and its activation-free counterpart; the difference in R-squared between full-HMM and order-one activations at a 20-token suffix, correlated against the theoretically computed gap; the belief-probe R-squared minus the R-squared of regressing the ground-truth next-token distribution (or its log) onto beliefs, again correlated against its theoretical counterpart; R-squared of the target log-next-token implied by a steered belief at later layers (a steering-layer by probe-layer heatmap); and KL from the intervened output distribution to the target belief's implied next-token distribution, compared against the KL to the original belief's distribution, with random-belief steering as the control. Robustness is checked by repeating the probe under two splits that remove the slow-mixing artefact of a random split - a contiguous train-then-test block and a held-out sequence - with medians over 240 model-process combinations. Confidence intervals are 95 percent over sequences, and a per-layer tuned lens trained to the ground-truth next-token distribution supplies a behavioural cross-check that layers where beliefs are most decodable are also the layers whose early-exit predictions are best.

## What they found

Converged in-context prediction is strong but not unlimited. For Qwen3.5-9B the converged KL matches a Markov approximation of order 4.2 to 11.0 on the four representative parametrisations, with a median of 3.4 to 4.7 (range 2.2 to 11.0) across the ten parametrisations per family, well below order zero and one. Gemma-4-E2B, the smallest model by effective parameters, is no better than a first-order predictor on six of ten Wing and four of ten Strata parametrisations. Belief states are linearly decodable at peak R-squared 0.83 to 0.99 across all model-process combinations, from early to late layers; the shuffled and random controls stay near the floor at every layer, and probes fitted only on the first 5,000 tokens recover visibly coarser geometry when evaluated on the first 250. Cross-parametrisation transfer shows HMM-specific information - the empirical transfer matrix correlates with its ground-truth counterpart across layers for all four families and all six models - while the empirical values sit higher than the ground truth, which the authors read as some HMM-generic information riding along. The order-one test is the more interesting result: after sufficient suffix length, full-HMM activations decode the full belief better than activations induced by order-zero or order-one sequences for most parametrisations, and the size of that difference tracks its theoretical value with Pearson correlation 0.59 to 0.86. Against next-token probabilities the picture is family-dependent by design: for Wing and Strata activations beat both the probabilities and their log; for Arch the log is beaten throughout while the plain probability comparison varies by parametrisation, holding for about half; for Mess3 there is nothing to detect because the map is invertible, exactly as the theory says. Those differences correlate with the theoretical ceilings at Pearson 0.85 to 1.00 for the probability comparison and 0.43 to 0.88 for Arch under the log. Under a contiguous or a held-out-sequence split the medians across all 240 combinations barely move (0.986 random, 0.970 contiguous, 0.976 held-out), except in Arch, where peak R-squared falls monotonically with the family's parameter and the random split had hidden it: for Qwen3.5-9B at parameter 0.99 the three protocols give 0.88, 0.64 and 0.74. Intervention behaves as the causal story requires. Steering beliefs at layers beyond roughly the first third installs the target's implied log-next-token distribution at later layers, and steering earlier has no lasting effect - the target representation is erased and the original reinstalled, a self-repair pattern. Patching and steering the final third of layers moves the output distribution toward the injected belief, at times matching or beating the untouched model's KL, while injecting a random belief against the same target does not move it at all. The next-token-invisible direction also survives: it is installed in later layers when steered in the final half of the network, and its self-repair after early steering is rarer and more persistent than that of a matched random direction.

## Limits and open problems they name

The processes are deliberately easy: low entropy, slow mixing, non-trivial but low-dimensional belief geometry, chosen from a region where LLMs are known to predict well. Whether the picture holds for high-entropy or high-dimensional geometry is untested, and a broader parameter sweep already turns up a subset of parametrisations - listed in the appendix, from Mess3, Wing and Strata - that show the right KL behaviour but **no** gap between order-one and full-HMM belief decodability, which the authors say suggests the results are specific to particular parameterisations. Linear regression can manufacture apparent structure for low-dimensional targets, and although the shuffled and random controls address it, unsupervised evidence for the geometry is left to future work. Other explanations than next-token probabilities remain available: the model may represent a different feature set from which beliefs happen to be linearly decodable. Because probabilities are linear in beliefs, an intervention could be driven by the probability component, and the invisible-direction experiment shows the extra information can be installed and decoded but does not show that it influences predictions beyond the current token. Scale is unexamined - the paper calls for scaling laws for in-context HMM prediction and for how geometry sharpens with size.

## What we take from it

The first entry in this folder where the *target of the probe is derived from theory rather than chosen by the analyst*, and the reusable content is the discipline that follows from having such a target. **(1) The candidate-ceiling comparison, as a reporting rule.** For every readout the paper reports the observed surplus over a candidate explanation *and* the surplus the theory says that candidate leaves available. This converts "beliefs are decodable above controls" into "beliefs are decodable by exactly the amount the process makes non-redundant", and it is the only way we have seen in 919 entries to state that a representation carries information beyond a simpler one without arguing over whether the gap is big. Our white-box model can adopt it directly: for every quantity we claim a component represents, compute the ceiling obtainable from the nearest simpler quantity we can construct, and report the pair. **(2) Units-free behavioural anchoring.** Reporting model performance as "the Markov order that matches it" gives a scale on which 0.83 and 0.99 sit on a continuum with a meaning, instead of a bare KL. Any synthetic or semi-synthetic battery we build should state its result on an equivalent-strength scale rather than in raw divergence. **(3) The self-repair signature as a locality test.** The finding that steering before some layer is undone and after it holds - and that the invisible component repairs less and more persistently than a matched random direction - is a cheap instrument for locating where a representation becomes commitment, and it costs one sweep of steering layers. **(4) The split-protocol warning.** Arch shows why a random split on slow-mixing stimuli is not neutral: consecutive positions carry similar targets, so test positions sit between training positions, and the headroom the random split reported (0.88) collapses to 0.64 under a contiguous block. Any readout we fit on temporally ordered or correlated material has to be reported under a contiguous or held-out-unit split too, and the drop is the number of interest. Read against run 917 (a 0.95-AUROC probe whose four interventions returned nothing after it was probed on mixed-outcome problems) and run 914 (a near-perfect mined feature predicting nothing about which failure source would move), the contrast is instructive: here the probe is not perfect (0.83-0.99 with a theory-derived ceiling), the intervention is run on the *same* distribution the probe was fit on and it moves the output exactly where the target says it should, and the authors still list "another representation from which beliefs are decodable" as an open alternative. That is what a probe that has earned its causal claim looks like, and it is earned by the intervention arm, not by the accuracy.
