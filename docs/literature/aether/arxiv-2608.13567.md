# Modular Cognitive Architecture Emerges in Large Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.13567
- **Authors / organisation:** Pengrui Han, Jacob Andreas, Evelina Fedorenko, Andrea Gregor de Varda; MIT (Equal contribution marked for Fedorenko and de Varda)
- **Date:** 2026-06-27
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13567
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text

## What they wanted, and why
Human cortex shows a stable division of labour: a language network, plus separate networks for formal/executive reasoning, intuitive physics and theory of mind. Because every neurotypical brain has it, the authors ask whether that organisation is a requirement of intelligence or an accident of biological substrate and metabolic budget. Their lever is a second kind of optimiser: LLMs, trained only to predict the next token, with no energy term that rewards sparse activation. If the same four-way split appears in a network that never faced a metabolic pressure, modularity looks less like a constraint of tissue and more like a solution that diverse-task optimisation keeps finding. This is a comparative-cognition argument run inside a mechanism-interpretability pipeline, and it is the reason the paper matters beyond its headline.

## What they studied
Six instruction-tuned models spanning 24B to 123B parameters and four families: Mistral-Small-24B, Qwen2.5-32B, OLMo-2-32B, Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen2.5-72B, Mistral-Large-123B. The unit of analysis is the **feed-forward intermediate neuron** (MLP hidden unit), never heads or edges. The task battery is 46 tasks in four domains — 8 language, 20 formal (arithmetic 9, logic 4, code/algorithm 7), 9 physical, 9 social — each mapped a priori to a human brain network. GPT-2-small (124M) is the low-performing contrast model.

## How they did it
Every task is a set of **minimal pairs**: an original prompt and an alternative differing in one task-relevant feature, so that the correct continuation flips. Items are filtered to pairs the model answers correctly both ways, at a per-task both-correct rate of at least 60% against a 25% chance level. Each neuron gets an attribution score that multiplies the activation difference between the two passes by the gradient of the task logit metric with respect to that neuron, at the final prompt token and at the MLP down-projection input; scores are summed over items within a task. Prompts in a pair are length-matched at the token level and violations are dropped. Per task they keep the top 0.1% of **positively** attributed neurons (1,311-2,523 neurons depending on model), compute pairwise Jaccard overlap between tasks, average across models that contain both tasks, and normalise rows/columns to sum to one only for the chord diagram. To test whether the correlational structure is causal they run all 2,070 ordered task pairs: identify the source task's top 0.1% neurons, then, while the model solves the **target** task, replace those neurons' activations with the values they take on the target's alternative input. The diagonal is excluded as trivial. A free-form generation check with the same intervention at a wider 5% set is shown for one physical-reasoning question.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **partition**: a set of domains and, for each, a set of MLP neurons held to implement it, plus the claim that those sets are causally load-bearing. The object is neither a circuit nor a feature dictionary — no edges, no composition, no algorithm is described — so the explanation is structural and group-level rather than mechanistic. It is explicitly partial: each neuron set is the top-ranked 0.1%, not a claimed complete implementation, and the paper never characterises what a domain's neurons compute.

## How they judged success
Four instruments, all named. (1) A 10,000-permutation test on the within-domain minus cross-domain overlap difference, with task-to-domain labels shuffled. (2) An unsupervised check: k-nearest-neighbour graph (k=5) on the overlap matrix, average-linkage hierarchical clustering cut to four clusters, scored against the predefined domains by Adjusted Rand Index. (3) Kendall's tau between model-specific overlap matrices, and separately between model-specific ablation-effect matrices, as a cross-model consistency statistic. (4) Ablation effect size as the accuracy drop on the target task relative to its unperturbed baseline, with within- versus cross-domain drops compared by the same permutation test. Threshold robustness is checked at 0.05/0.1/1/5%, ablation-method robustness against mean and zero ablation on one model, and a semantic-similarity control (TF-IDF, GloVe, SBERT, and the model's own input-token embeddings) is run as both a clustering ARI comparison and a partial regression over the 1,035 off-diagonal pairs.

## What they found
Within-domain overlap of top neurons is 12.9% against 3.0% cross-domain, a ratio above 4x at p < 0.0001; per model it ranges 11.1-14.2% within against 2.3-3.5% across, ratios 3.5-5.9x, significant in all six. Clustering recovers the four domains at ARI 0.78 (p < 0.0001). Cross-model consistency is a mean pairwise Kendall tau of 0.70 +/- 0.06 on overlap and 0.59 +/- 0.05 on the ablation matrix. The causal asymmetry is larger than the correlational one: within-domain ablation costs 25.9% accuracy against 2.5% cross-domain (10.3x, p < 0.0001), with per-model ratios 6.1-12.3x. Domain profiles differ in specificity — language neurons cost 20.4% within and under 0.5% outside (44.3x), formal 32.0% versus 3.0%, physics 16.0% versus 4.1%, social 7.9% versus 2.0%. Domains occupy the same mid-to-late layers with largely non-overlapping neurons; only language is present in early layers. Social reasoning is the weak spot: its two directional tests miss significance (p = 0.074 and 0.146), leaving ten of twelve domain-direction cells significant. Widening the threshold monotonically flattens the ratio, from 5.4x at top 0.05% to 1.4x at top 5%. The semantic controls are beaten cleanly: ARI 0.78 for neuron overlap versus 0.39 SBERT, 0.36 GloVe, 0.12 TF-IDF, 0.04 (n.s.) for the model's own input embeddings, and the same-domain coefficient stays positive and significant in the partial regression against each baseline. GPT-2 fails the reasoning domains far below chance (0.03 formal, 0.05 physical, 0.09 social, against 0.25 chance and 0.77 on language, because it prefers the same continuation on both members of a pair) and shows only a small within-domain effect (2.67x, Cohen's d = 0.37) that collapses to 1.57x once language tasks are removed.

## Limits and open problems they name
There is no dedicated limitations section — the paper runs its own controls and stops. What it concedes in passing: the domain-to-network mapping is a hypothesis for many tasks, since several were never tested against neural responses, so the "mirrors the brain" claim is partly a claim about the authors' own taxonomy. The headline is an average over six models, and the per-domain split is weakest exactly where the task battery is most interpretive (social). Everything is instruction-tuned, justified by a citation that base and tuned circuit structure broadly agree rather than measured here.

## What we take from it
Mostly a **framing** and a **measure**, with one warning. The framing: modularity as a thing a white-box model should be able to *report and then falsify* — a partition is a claim, and the claim is only worth publishing with a permutation-based domain-label null, an ARI-style unsupervised recovery check, and a cross-domain ablation asymmetry beside it. The measure: the **within/cross ratio of the same statistic** is a far better instrument than either number alone, and here it moves monotonically with the threshold in a way that tells you the effect lives in the head of the ranking (5.4x strict to 1.4x broad). The warning is the one this folder keeps re-learning: a battery whose tasks the model cannot solve still yields an overlap matrix, and GPT-2's matrix shows a detectable "module" that is entirely the one domain it can do. For a predictive white-box model, the rule is that any partition we publish is scored only on tasks the model solves both ways above a stated floor, and the partition is reported with its per-domain weakest cell rather than its average — because 12.9% against 3.0% and 8.6% social with two failed significance tests are the same run, and only the second number tells us where the partition is real. Also useful: the 0.1% ranking is largely a statement about the top of the attribution distribution, so a partition that survives only at strict thresholds is a statement about the ranking's head, not about the network.
