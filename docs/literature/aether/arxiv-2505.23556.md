## What they wanted, and why

Refusal is the behaviour that safety tuning is supposed to install, and the field's best-known internal account of it is a single linear direction obtained by differencing mean activations. These authors treat that account as too coarse: a direction tells you that refusal can be switched on and off, not which parts of the network do the switching or how a jailbreak reaches them. They want the same behaviour decomposed into named, individually testable latent features, because a feature set can be intervened on one element at a time and can therefore express an upstream/downstream ordering that one vector cannot. The larger goal is mechanistic: not "which direction controls refusal" but "which features compute it, which features feed them, and what an adversarial prompt does to that pipeline". A second, applied goal rides alongside — whether a classifier built only on those features survives distribution shift better than a classifier built on the dense residual stream.

## What they studied

Two safety-aligned chat models: Gemma-2-2B (GemmaScope SAEs, expansion factor 32, JumpReLU) and Llama-3.1-8B (LlamaScope SAEs, expansion factor 8, TopK). Unit of analysis is the SAE latent on the **residual stream** only — MLP and attention features are explicitly left for later work. Data: JailbreakBench, HarmBench and AdvBench (100 samples each) for faithfulness; Alpaca and Pile for coherence, GSM8K and ARC-Challenge for reasoning; CATQA (7 of 11 categories, each with base-model refusal above 80%) for behaviour categories; WildJailBreak for adversarial paraphrases; the AdvSuffixes collection for adversarial suffix tokens. The refusal direction itself is the standard difference of harmful-minus-harmless mean activations at every layer.

## How they did it

They build a two-stage search that starts from the direction and ends with a small causally chosen feature set. Stage one: at each layer, keep the K₀ = 10 features whose decoder vectors have the highest cosine with the optimal refusal direction (selected by sweeping layers; layer 15 for Gemma, layer 11 for Llama). Stage two: run attribution patching *inside that restricted pool* — integrated gradients with N = 10 steps, scoring the indirect effect on a metric that is the log-probability gap between the corrupt output token and the clean one — average the effect over the sequence, and take the top K* = 20 (layer, feature) pairs per prompt, or globally by averaging over the dataset first. Two engineering choices are worth naming: because patching normally needs equal-length clean/corrupt pairs, they substitute a *steered* activation state as the counterfactual (approximately the state a successful refusal-ablation would have produced) instead of a second prompt; and they set the clean target token to "I" (the first letter of the refusal phrasing) and the corrupt target to the first token the steered model emits. Faithfulness is then measured by scaling the chosen activations by a negative constant c and asking whether the model complies: c = −3 for Gemma, c = −1 for Llama, greedy decoding, 256 new tokens, HarmBench classifier as the judge. Comparisons are against three feature-search baselines (pure cosine similarity, activation difference, plain attribution patching without the direction restriction) and against activation steering itself, which they treat as the upper bound the feature set is trying to approximate. Separately, they partition the found features by whether they recur across CATQA categories — the intersection across all seven behaviour-specific sets is called common (7 features in Gemma, 10 in Llama), the remainder specific — and clamp each subset while freezing the other, then transfer specific sets between behaviour categories. Finally they train a linear probe on the sparse refusal-feature activations and compare it to a dense probe on the layer-chosen residual stream, both trained on vanilla WildJailBreak splits and tested on a mix of held-out vanilla and adversarial prompts.

## What counts as an explanation here

A short feature list per behaviour, with each member justified causally rather than by its auto-interpretation — the authors say outright that they establish causal relevance by intervention first and only then check that the auto-interp descriptions look plausible. On top of the list sits a small directed story: behaviour-specific harm features (violence, drugs, legal terminology, consequences) sit upstream and a shared refusal set sits downstream and directly gates the reply. The explanation is deliberately partial (K* = 20, residual stream only, two models) and it is *predictive in one specific sense* — it should tell you which interventions jailbreak and which do not, and it does so better than the direction alone on the coherence and reasoning columns. Nothing here predicts a token.

## How they judged success

Three scoreboards. (1) **Faithfulness**: jailbreak score under negative clamping across three harmful benchmarks, averaged over feature sets derived from each benchmark; refusal score on Alpaca when c is positive and the activation is set directly rather than scaled. (2) **Cost**: cross-entropy on 1,000 Alpaca instructions and 1,000 Pile sequences (on-policy rollouts capped at 256 tokens), plus GSM8K and ARC-Challenge accuracy, all against base and against activation steering. (3) **Causal ordering**: the suppression rate ΔA(R) — the fractional drop in refusal-feature activation when the upstream harm set is clamped, at the chat tokens where those activations peak — against a random feature set 100 times larger as the control, with the probability of the "I" token tracked alongside. Generalisation is judged by probe accuracy on adversarial WildJailBreak samples the model actually complies with, reported as a three-column table (average, vanilla, adversarial) with the vanilla-to-adversarial gap as the number that matters, plus a random-feature probe as a floor. Two ablations: base versus chat-fine-tuned SAEs on CE and recovered CE, and the cosine fidelity of the reconstructed refusal direction.

## What they found

The hybrid search beats every baseline and lands close to activation steering. On faithfulness, cosine-similarity selection works on Llama but fails on Gemma (the authors blame a bias toward the refusal-associated layer); plain attribution patching underperforms on both because its single-token metric is too narrow for a multi-token behaviour; activation difference is the unstable one, driving Alpaca CE from 0.291 to 1.012 and GSM8K accuracy from 62.2 to 23.2 on Gemma. Their own method keeps CE at 0.310 on Gemma (against 0.337 for steering) and 0.224 on Llama (against 0.150), and costs 2.1 GSM8K points on Gemma and 0.8 ARC points, with Llama losing 4.1 ARC points — the one column where it is clearly worse than steering. Harm and refusal are separate feature sets: clamping the upstream harm set suppresses downstream refusal activation by 0.48 (Gemma) and 0.51 (Llama) against 0.05 and 0.19 for random features, and drops the "I" probability by 0.71 and 0.37 against 0.06 and 0.04. Clamping the *common* set jailbreaks across every category more effectively than clamping category-specific features even within their own category — on Llama the specific set alone is largely ineffective — which is why the common set gets the name refusal and the specific ones harm. Jailbreaking is suppression with a specific signature: on successful adversarial paraphrases, refusal-feature activation falls 0.73 (Gemma) and 0.40 (Llama) relative to the same instruction rewritten to look plainly harmful, while failed jailbreaks move it only 0.18 and 0.10; the harm features move less and in different directions across models (0.49 Gemma, 0.10 Llama on success). Individual suffix tokens do much of this work — for Gemma, adding a token like "frictional" produces a large jump in suppression and simultaneously removes most of the extra suppression that clamping the harm set would have bought, i.e. the token occupies the same slot in the pipeline as the upstream harm features. On the applied test, the sparse refusal-feature probe holds up where the dense one collapses: adversarial-vanilla gap 0.03 (Gemma) and 0.17 (Llama) against 0.32 and 0.93, and the dense Llama probe actually scores *better* on adversarial prompts (0.97) than on vanilla ones (0.03) — a textbook shortcut. Chat-fine-tuned SAEs reconstruct the refusal direction with cosine 0.98 versus 0.86 for the base SAE while CE is essentially unchanged. Refusal-feature activations peak on the final chat-template tokens, and stripping those tokens collapses the measured activation without proving refusal is gone (the authors attribute the refusal-score drop to out-of-distribution behaviour, not to the missing features).

## Limits and open problems they name

A small K* may omit features that matter; the direction-restricted pool is only as good as the direction, and the authors flag that this may not extend to instructions that resist jailbreak; restricted-pool selection is itself called potentially suboptimal. Base-model SAEs transfer but not every selected feature is interpretable, and they recommend training SAEs on chat activations. They note in an appendix that their category partition leaks — features outside the common set are still shared across categories — and sketch a deeper conditional chain (harm → legality → consequences → refusal) as an open question. The societal-risk statement concedes that refusal can be undone by intervening on a small feature set, which they frame as corroborating prior work rather than as a new vulnerability.

## What we take from it

A **method** and three **measures**. The method is the one we should adopt for any behaviour we decompose: use the differencing direction only as a *candidate filter*, then run an interventional importance score inside that filter to pick a handful of units — which converts attribution from "the top features are whichever correlate most" to "the top features are whichever the network will pay for". Two engineering tricks transfer directly: substituting a steered state for the counterfactual prompt removes the equal-length constraint that makes patching awkward on real instructions, and averaging the attribution over the sequence rather than reading one token target is what makes a multi-token behaviour tractable. The measures are the suppression rate with its 100× random control (a cheap, falsifiable statement of upstream influence), the CE-and-reasoning cost table printed beside every faithfulness number (the folder's remit for the collateral column, here applied to a feature set rather than a direction), and the vanilla-versus-adversarial gap for any probe we claim generalises. The **warning** is the dense probe that scores 0.97 on adversarial prompts and 0.03 on vanilla ones: a probe trained on a label can learn the label's surface form so well that it becomes an anti-detector exactly where detection matters, and only a held-out adversarial split reveals it. The **shortcut** is the restricted pool: we can borrow it immediately for our own feature selection, but must state that its coverage is bounded by the direction we started from.
