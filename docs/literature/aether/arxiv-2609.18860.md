## What they wanted, and why

When a vision-language model gets a harmful-meme label wrong, two very different stories fit the same wrong answer: the model never represented the evidence, or it represented the evidence and failed to get it to the output. The two call for different repairs — better perception or more data in the first case, a better decision pathway in the second — so a practitioner cannot choose between them from behaviour alone. The authors set out to tell them apart with an instrument rather than an argument: hold the frozen model and the examples fixed, and compare what a supervised readout can recover from the internals against what the model itself emits. Meme harmfulness is a deliberately hard test case because the label usually depends on the juxtaposition of image and text, and the standard benchmark (Facebook Hateful Memes) was built with benign confounders precisely so that a model cannot score well on one modality alone. Behind this sits the goal that matters to us: a discipline for four separate questions — is the label decodable, where in the sequence does it appear, do those directions actually move the native answer, and can the signal be converted into better behaviour.

## What they studied

Two vision-language families: Gemma-3-4B-IT (plus Gemma-3-12B for one focused study) and Qwen3.5-9B-Base. Six binary harmful-content benchmarks — CrisisHateMM, FHM, MAMI, HarMeme, MMHS150K, MultiOFF — plus Spanish/English EXIST-2025 holdouts and Hindi–English code-mixed MultiBully for robustness. In Gemma's case the LLM enters directly through a frozen public Gemma Scope layer-22 dictionary (width 65,536) plus a trained residual JumpReLU SAE (20,480 latents); in Qwen's case a single public layer-20 TopK layer-20 dictionary (width 65,536, k=100) is used across all six tasks, with a trained residual SAE (32,768 latents) as an ablation. Units of analysis are SAE features and their decoder directions, token positions grouped by origin (prompt/OCR, image-origin, generated, and unions of those), layers, and — at the output — the yes/no logit channel and full-label string scoring.

## How they did it

The core design is a four-pathway comparison on identical example IDs with everything frozen. **Native**: the model's own constrained label scoring, summing token log-probabilities of each class verbalisation. **Sparse probe**: a per-feature max-pooled SAE code for a given token role, fed to an L2-regularised logistic regression trained on the task's training split only. **Residual reconstruction hook**: the base-SAE reconstruction plus decoded residual is blended into the residual stream at strength 0.35 and the model runs on. **Direct logit routing**: a calibrated probe score is added to the native margin, with the coefficient and threshold fitted on a disjoint 30% calibration partition and frozen on the remaining 70%. The headline number is a *readout gap*, probe macro-F1 minus native macro-F1 on the same reporting examples. Then two mechanistic tests on Qwen's dictionary. They define each feature's direct alignment with the yes/no output channel as the projection of its decoder direction onto the unembedding row difference (final normalisation folded in), which is a static, no-upper-layers approximation; features in the probe's top twenty with alignment below a printed threshold are called *output-silent*, and a *routed* set of twenty is chosen independently on alignment alone, without labels. Silent features are then zeroed at every token position and the probe score and marginal recomputed; routed features are donor-patched by broadcasting another example's max activation across the target's positions. Both are scored as a ratio of mean absolute paired change. Gemma repeats the static separation with zero-overlap set comparison and a Jacobian-based, upper-layer-aware variant. A separate Gemma-3-12B study tests cross-modal structure on FHM by adding an explicit rank-*r* bilinear image-by-prompt term to a gated pairwise baseline, trained with ranking losses on opposite-label same-image and same-text pairs, swept over rank against seeds, and evaluated on a locked test split. Finally, recovery: a threshold-only calibration arm to separate miscalibration from missing ranking signal, and LoRA distillation of the probe with gold-only, compute-matched controls.

## What counts as an explanation here

A *gap decomposition* plus a direction-level account of its cause — not a circuit and not a description of the model's reasoning. Three levels are kept explicitly distinct: decodability (a supervised readout can recover the label from the state), routing (directions selected for output alignment control the native margin), and recoverability (the signal can be turned into better predictions). The explanatory claim is geometric: in Qwen the dictionary directions that discriminate the label are largely disjoint from those that control the yes/no output, and the same separation appears statically in Gemma. It is explicitly local — to one layer, one dictionary, one output anchor (they name the mismatch that arises when a task uses full-label scoring but the anchor is yes/no) — and explicitly not claimed as a universal two-subspace decomposition, because Gemma's native-output mediation is not consistently routed- or silent-dominant. Partial by construction, and predictive in only a weak sense: it predicts that calibration alone will close most but not all of the gap, which they then test.

## How they judged success

Macro-F1 as primary metric (class-balanced, so a majority baseline is printed beside every cell), with accuracy, class-wise precision/recall and predicted class frequencies as secondary diagnostics, all on a locked protocol where layer, token role, representation, checkpoint, threshold and routing coefficient are selected on training or calibration data and frozen before the reporting split is scored. Probe-versus-native differences are paired on identical examples. Significance for the main locked comparison uses a label-stratified paired bootstrap interval and an exact McNemar test on discordant pairs. Intervention effects are reported as mean absolute change in probe score and in the monitored margin, with the ratio printed and labelled as scale-dependent rather than as a mediation fraction, and example-level bootstrap intervals on the signed changes. Controls are unusually complete: constant/majority baselines, shuffled-label probes (0.4745–0.5429), random sparse dictionaries, dense-state probes on the same pooling, a within-role quadratic control, a compute-matched gold-only adapter, cross-model negative controls (the same bilinear readout on Gemma-3-4B and Qwen), and a no-OCR condition beside blank-image and same-split shuffled-image perturbations.

## What they found

Every task shows a native-to-probe gap in both families. Gemma's six-task mean goes 0.532 to 0.714 (+0.182) and Qwen's 0.432 to 0.740 (+0.308); the largest single gem is Qwen's CrisisHateMM, 0.310 to 0.860. Getting the signal back through the native pathway barely helps: the reconstruction hook lifts Qwen's mean to 0.486, which is 0.176 of the gap, and even the static alignment check says these are different feature sets — for Qwen's public dictionary no analysed feature combines high probe importance with high direct output alignment on five of six tasks (MAMI, 1% of records). The interventions separate cleanly in magnitude: zeroing twenty silent features moves the probe score 24.2–62.8 times more than the monitored margin (FHM: −2.512 [−2.769, −2.250] against −0.033 [−0.063, −0.008]), while donor-patching twenty routed features moves the margin 16.2–140.2 times more than the probe on the four literal yes/no tasks. Gemma's routed and probe-derived sets have zero overlap on all four analysed tasks, with silent-set perturbation moving the probe 13–180 times more. Token role is not universal: image-position features win on HarMeme and CrisisHateMM, generated features on MAMI, MMHS150K and MultiOFF, and MAMI's best readout is prompt/OCR states at 0.845. Sparse representation itself only matters much on one task (Gemma CrisisHateMM, +0.283 over dense); elsewhere the dense stream already carries the signal. On recovery, the decomposition is the sharpest result in the paper: calibration alone takes the five matched tasks from 0.4640 to 0.6593, which is 0.698 of the gap, but routing still adds 0.0944 (to 0.7537) and beats the probe alone on FHM and MAMI — so part of the gap is threshold error and the rest is ranking signal the native channel does not have (native AUROC only 0.579 on MMHS150K and 0.661 on MultiOFF). Distillation is the weak link: the dedicated FHM adapter beats gold-only (0.7138 against 0.6462), but on a shared seven-task adapter the compute-matched gold-only control wins the mean, 0.5780 against 0.5686, and the joint objective collapses MMHS150K six-class from 0.2688 to 0.1886 — interference, not transfer. The Gemma-3-12B FHM interaction is real but distributed: rank-32 reaches 0.7560 test versus 0.6853 native (Δ 0.0707, CI [0.0461, 0.0954], McNemar 103 to 37, p = 2.18e-8), 11 of 32 factors have measurable leave-one-out effects but no single factor moves validation macro-F1 by more than 0.007, and the same readout loses to its simpler baseline on Gemma-3-4B (0.654 against 0.688) and Qwen (0.528 against 0.641). Robustness holds: image shuffling costs the probe 0.148–0.206 and the router 0.160–0.292 on every audited dataset, with conditional image credit 55.8–88.8% for the probe against 0.0–38.1% for native, while OCR removal moves the strongest text-bearing readouts by at most 0.0671.

## Limits and open problems they name

The comparison is not like-for-like and they say so repeatedly: probes are supervised while the native output is untuned zero-shot constrained scoring, so the gap measures *supervised accessibility*, not a fair classifier comparison — and it is explicitly a routing failure *under this protocol*, not an architectural impossibility that better prompting could not bypass (they did not test rationale, few-shot or retrieval methods). Generated-token readouts are post-generation states, so a strong generated-role probe locates where the representation becomes separable, not what was available before deciding. Token roles are origins, not modality-pure states. Mechanistic claims are local to the analysed layer, dictionary and output anchor, with small intervention cohorts (five donor–target pairs per task, fifteen knockout examples) that they call exploratory on the patch side; the static alignment ignores upper blocks and is used only to select features. The multilingual and code-mixed results use internal holdouts; on Gemma's EXIST transfer the probe-minus-native gain changes sign across split seeds with SD exceeding the mean, so they claim decodability only. Fine-grained classes stay hard — HarMeme's "very harmful" class gets 2 of 21 right (F1 0.125). The receiver note worth printing: the five-pair patch estimates establish output control, not improved correctness, and donor broadcasting creates activation patterns that do not occur in ordinary inference.

## What we take from it

**A measure, and it is the one we should adopt wholesale**: the four-way split *decodability / routing / recoverability / correctness*, scored in the same table on the same examples, with the readout gap defined as a paired difference and the caveat about supervision printed inside the definition rather than in a limitations paragraph. Run 926's dissociation and run 924's detect/predict pair were both instances of the first two columns diverging; this paper makes the pair a formal object and then adds the two columns that say what to do about it. **A method**: the threshold-calibration arm is the cheapest missing control in the folder — before crediting a probe with information the model lacks, re-threshold the native margin on calibration data and report what fraction of the gap that alone recovers (0.698 here). Our own gap claims should carry that number or they are overclaiming. **A second method**: the two-sided intervention battery on independently selected sets — silent-feature knockout to establish probe dependence, routed-feature donor patching to establish output control — is the cleanest version of the folder's recurring along-versus-orthogonal idea (run 928) done without needing a subspace estimate, at the price of being scale-dependent, which they price honestly. **A warning with teeth**: their own best result does not transfer — the bilinear readout that wins on Gemma-3-12B loses to its simpler baseline on Gemma-3-4B and on Qwen — so any interaction or circuit claim we make must be re-run at a second scale or filed as local to one checkpoint. **A dataset**: FHM's benign-confounder construction, used here as matched pairs sharing an image or a text with opposite labels, is a ready-made minimum-pair test for any multimodal claim, and it is the same instrument run 926 lacked.
