# Where Causal Discovery Breaks on Real Data: Measuring the Assumptions One Property at a Time

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.00484
- **Authors / organisation:** Padmaja Jonnalagedda, Avinash Baidya, Ruocheng Guo, Kamalika Das; no affiliation block printed on either the abstract page or the HTML byline, so no institution is recorded
- **Date:** 2026-10-31
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00484
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Causal discovery is learned almost entirely on data where the answer is known by construction: simulators that can reward their own artefacts, and a few physical or engineered systems whose structure follows from design. The authors' complaint is about what happens off that regime. When discovery does badly on a business, clinical or economic panel, the failure is explained by listing assumptions that might be violated, and the list is never measured, so a practitioner cannot tell which violation is doing the damage or whether a different method would have helped. They want the observable properties of the data that bear on those assumptions turned into numbers computed *before* any method runs, together with an evaluation whose reference points are calibrated to the same properties. This is a step toward knowing when a discovered structure should be trusted at all — for us, the general form of the question of when a white-box account's implied dependencies are recoverable from the evidence.

## What they studied
One real panel: 2,901 US public firms over 31 quarters (2018Q2–2025Q4), 89,931 firm-quarters, kept balanced by selection so firms that listed, delisted or were acquired are absent. Interventions are 71 binary corporate-action types extracted from SEC 8-K filings (subtypes of 10 filing items, assigned partly by a language model); outcomes are 11 financial-statement line items (five stock-like, six flow-like). Ground truth is an expert graph over 651 scored action–state pairs — 222 direct, 429 not-direct, 130 uncertain — labelled by three annotators from a written rubric on whether an accounting mechanism exists, not on effect size, and never estimated from the panel. The unit of analysis is the action–state pair at lags one to four quarters, not a graph as a whole.

## How they did it
Three pieces, and the first two need no labels and no method. First, a *catalogue of properties*, each with a diagnostic: excess kurtosis and the share of squared deviation carried by the top 1% of rows; within-firm AR(1) of the levels with Kendall's small-sample bias correction, plus an intraclass correlation; an auxiliary R² for heteroscedasticity against firm size; R² of each state on the other ten at the same date, and whether accounting identities can be computed at all; and a *planted-effect power grid*, where an additive effect of a chosen size is injected into real firm-quarters and a fixed marginal test is rerun, giving the number of firings needed for a given detection rate. Second, an *evaluation protocol*: one fixed marginal test (an event-study comparison of firing against non-firing firms on one-quarter changes, firm-clustered) is scored under eight different nulls of 400 replicates each — label permutation within action, row permutation, firm-block permutation with and without activity strata, and circular shifts within firm at any distance or only at five quarters or more — with the strictest valid null as the reference, plus a placebo that scores each action against four pre-filing quarters, a label-only baseline, and a within-target re-ranking. Third, *methods from four families* (PCMCI+, SVAR-RFCI; DYNOTEARS, NTS-NOTEARS; longitudinal LiNGAM; VAR Granger, MiTCD) run over eight preprocessing combinations and, where affordable, 40 hyperparameter configurations, all tuned on the full label set, and scored under the same protocol; plus an *expert-parent test* in which the conditioning sets a conditional-independence method would have to estimate are supplied from the expert graph, so the graph's own implications are tested directly, with a nonlinear (gradient-boosted residualisation) variant as a check.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a graph of the world and not a model of a mechanism: the paper's product is a *checklist* — for each data property, a diagnostic, its value on the panel, the assumption it bears on, and the response it should provoke — plus a calibrated way of reading any structure-learning score. It is diagnostic rather than predictive. The one thing it does assert predictively is an upper bound: the power grid says what a marginal comparison can see given support, which is a claim about the data rather than about any method.

## How they judged success
Within-action AUROC (each action's direct targets ranked against its own not-direct ones, which cancels differences in firing frequency), the *excess* over the method's own null refit through its full pipeline, and a within-target AUROC that removes what the target alone predicts; 90% intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples over actions; detection rates rather than AUROC for the 27 actions that have only one class; a label-error bound from a Dawid–Skene model over the annotators' votes; and a confounding bound via robustness values expressed as the share of residual variance a confounder would have to explain, benchmarked against observed covariates (log firm size, the firm's other actions, quarter shocks).

## What they found
The panel breaks the conditions discovery relies on, measurably. Levels are savagely tailed — excess kurtosis in the thousands as reported, 18 to 451 after 1% winsorising, and the top 1% of rows still carry 27% to 76% of each state's squared deviation — so a least-squares fit on 89,931 rows is dominated by fewer than a thousand of them. Persistence splits the states not into stocks and flows but five against six: equity, both asset items, current liabilities and *revenue* have AR(1) 0.83 to 0.93 and, after correction, 42% to 64% of firms above one, while the rest sit between −0.05 and 0.43; the effective independent sample is 3,645 to 58,452 firm-quarters (ICC up to 0.795). Ten of the 71 actions fire fewer than 100 times, and a planted 0.1-SD effect is detected about half the time at 200 firings and needs roughly 752 for 80% power — a support 40 of 71 actions lack — while 0.05 SD is barely visible even at 2,000 firings. Actions are bundled: the median action shares its firm-quarter with another in 89% of firings. Evaluated properly, chance is not 0.5: label and firm-block permutation nulls sit at 0.501, but shift nulls reach 0.556, and a shift of only one to four quarters leaves the real effect inside the window (0.627 to 0.675 at one quarter against 0.658 observed), so only shifts of five quarters or more are admissible. Against the strictest valid null the fixed marginal test scores 0.658 within action, an excess of 0.120, beating every one of the 400-replicate nulls (empirical p at its 0.0025 floor), and a label-only baseline — each pair scored by how often its target is direct for other actions — reaches 0.860 without touching the data. Every tuned discovery method scores 0.528 to 0.700 within action, all below plain lagged cross-correlation at 0.824 (excess 0.237), and none beats cross-correlation within target either (best 0.579 against 0.643); NTS-NOTEARS lands below chance within target (0.380), MiTCD has exactly zero excess over its null, and SVAR-RFCI never marks a single direct pair as directed — it marks almost everything bidirected, covering 77% of direct and 81% of not-direct pairs at one setting. Supplying the expert parents *lowers* discrimination rather than producing the graph's implied separation (0.647 to 0.533 within action on raw levels; 0.505 on the nonlinear variant), 64% to 83% of marginally detected not-direct associations survive conditioning, adding 35 disputed edges as extra parents restores nothing, and a confounder strong enough to erase the median surviving not-direct detection would have to be 11 times as strong as log firm size. Most direct pairs are simply invisible: detection rises from 31% in the lowest support quartile to 80% in the highest, and 132 of 222 direct pairs sit on actions below the support needed for 80% power against 0.1 SD.

## Limits and open problems they name
The panel is balanced by selection; extraction by a language model can turn an extraction error into an apparent action with no effect (an audit of 30 events finds two wrong subtypes and none invented, and about 1.6% of firm-quarters miss an event); the expert graph is partial and may be wrong; quarter-level timing hides the order of events inside a quarter, and binary indicators ignore action size; every test is linear and additive, so nonlinear or firm-varying effects are outside what it can see; and the constraint-based methods run under computational caps, so their numbers say what a feasible instantiation recovers, not that the unrestricted algorithm fails. Because the graph can be incomplete, a low score is not by itself proof a method fails — they bound how much label error could explain instead.

## What we take from it
Four transferable instruments, in order of use to us. **Measure the data's properties before running the account** — tails, persistence, collinearity, support per intervention, how often interventions co-occur — because these are diagnosable without labels and they upper-bound what any account can recover; a support/power grid built by planting known effects is the cheap version, and it converts "our account missed this effect" into "no test of this design could have seen it". **Calibrate the metric against a null that keeps the data's properties**: here the gap between the lenient and strict nulls is a factor of 1.5 in the excess (0.157 down to 0.102), and a shift shorter than the test window is not a null at all. **Test the graph's own implications with graph-supplied conditioning sets**, which separates "the algorithm cannot find it" from "the evidence is not there" — and the answer here is that on this data the evidence is not there. And the sharpest warning in the entry: a *label-only baseline computed from the structure of the annotation* reaches 0.860, above every method, so any score we report must be printed beside the score a label-free, data-free convention achieves on the same pairs.
