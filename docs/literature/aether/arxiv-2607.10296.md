# SPARK: Susceptibility-Guided Profiling and Steering of Latent Reasoning States in Large Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.10296
- **Authors / organisation:** Dongxu Zhang, Yiding Sun, Zihao Guo, Xiangyang Yang, Kai Tang, Lin Chen, Cheng Tan, Jihua Zhu — Xi'an Jiaotong University (1), Peking University (2), Tencent (3); correspondence on an XJTU student address
- **Date:** 2026-07-11
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10296
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
A wrong final answer does not say why the model was wrong, and the authors separate three causes they think benchmarks conflate: the capability is absent, the reasoning trajectory is unstable, or the capability is present in the frozen weights but the forward pass never enters the state that uses it. Their target is the third case, and their diagnosis is that the field's activation-steering methods apply one global direction to every input regardless of whether that input needed intervention. So they want a cheap per-example internal signal, available in a forward pass and requiring no parameter updates, that says whether the model is internally "reasoning-active", plus a steering method that only intervenes on examples the signal flags. The motivation is explicitly about *diagnosis before treatment*: measure the latent state, then decide which examples deserve a direction.

## What they studied
Four Qwen3 models (0.6B, 4B, 8B, plus the family swept for ablations), and two cross-family checks, Llama-3.1-8B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. Evaluation benchmarks are GSM8K and MATH-500; the diagnostic setting is their own constructed suite, FRONTIER-4.5K — 4,500 instances, three domains of 1,500 each (symbolic composition, logical inference, algorithmic reasoning), answers produced by deterministic solvers rather than a model judge, and each instance carrying a normalised difficulty in [0,1] built from domain generation parameters (composition depth, rule-chain length and distractor count, graph size / edge density / query complexity) plus its token length. The unit of analysis is the *example's* pooled hidden state at selected layers, and the intervention is a single mean-shift direction injected into selected layers at inference time.

## How they did it
Everything rests on a "susceptibility" statistic: perturb the input embeddings with small Gaussian noise K times, measure the relative change in a pooled layer representation, and average the squared ratio over perturbations and over a chosen set of layers. This response intensity is then treated, and this is the paper's central move, as *confounded by input length* — harder serialised algorithmic instances are simply longer, and longer inputs show attenuated hidden-state response. They fit, per domain and on a calibration split, a log-log regression of susceptibility on token length and keep the residual as length-controlled susceptibility, which they read as "more response than same-length inputs get" (positive) or "under-activated relative to its length class" (negative). A second signal, cross-layer coordination, is the average pairwise cosine between normalised consecutive-layer update vectors across a set of layers, meant to capture whether layers are moving consistently rather than merely moving a lot. The two are z-scored within the calibration split and combined with a weight into the SPK score, used to select two sets: reasoning-active anchors (solved correctly, at or below a behavioural capability boundary fitted as the difficulty where pass rate crosses 50 per cent, with high corrected response) and under-activated target candidates (hard, at or above the boundary, with low corrected response). The steering direction is the mean over anchors of the difference between a representation with SPK-selected demonstrations prepended and the representation of the bare problem — an average latent shift toward the active state, normalised and injected as h + αv at a layer chosen to coincide with a susceptibility peak. A stricter control replaces the bare problem with a length-matched random demonstration input, to test whether the direction is the reasoning content rather than the added context length. Note the calibration bookkeeping: no parameters and no predictor are trained, but correctness labels on calibration questions are used to pick anchors and fix quantile thresholds, and the reported accuracies come from held-out questions.

## What counts as an explanation here
A two-state profile rather than a mechanism: each example is placed in a latent state (reasoning-active, under-activated, or neither) by a scalar derived from two measured statistics, and the explanation of a failure is "this hard example is under-activated relative to same-length inputs". It is deliberately partial and predictive in a narrow band — it predicts that correcting for length changes which examples get treated, and that intervening on the under-activated subset moves accuracy on hard cases while leaving easy cases alone. It is not a circuit, not a feature dictionary, and not a description of what the direction encodes; the direction is a mean shift between two prompt conditions and its content is never characterised beyond the fact that it improves accuracy.

## How they judged success
Exact-match accuracy on GSM8K, MATH-500 and the FRONTIER held-out split, with average generated tokens reported beside every accuracy to rule out "longer chain-of-thought, therefore better" — a control that this folder rarely gets. Against: the unsteered frozen model as baseline, a random-direction arm, alternative anchor choices (easy-correct anchors, high raw susceptibility, high length-controlled susceptibility, high combined score), and alternative injection layers (early, middle, late, susceptibility peak, SPARK-selected). The paper prints a strength sweep rather than picking a best α, both in the main table at low strength and in a wider sweep per model. Statistics are thin: no seed spread, no confidence intervals, no significance tests, and no test of what fraction of the gain comes from the *selection* (treat only flagged examples) versus from the direction itself — the selective part of the method is described in Section 4 and the ablations test anchor construction and injection layer but never compare "steer only under-activated targets" against "steer everything".

## What they found
The headline MATH-500 numbers, from the wider sweep: Qwen3-4B 82.0 to 84.6 per cent (best at α = 2.5 and 3.0), Qwen3-8B 82.4 to 85.6 (best at α = 2.0 and 3.0), Qwen3-0.6B 52.0 to 53.4 (best at α = 1.0 and 1.5). GSM8K moves little, as expected near saturation: 55.8 to 56.3, 89.7 to 90.5, 92.1 to 93.2. On FRONTIER the algorithmic subset carries most of the gain, from 12.5 to 17.2 on the 0.6B, 19.8 to 25.7 on the 4B, and 20.1 to 26.4 on the 8B, with average token counts within a few tokens of baseline in nearly every row. Cross-family: Llama-3.1-8B goes 45.6 to 48.4 at α = 0.5 (tokens 757 to 847), while DeepSeek-R1-Distill goes 85.4 to 86.4 at α = 0.25 but *collapses* to 32.0 per cent at α = 1.0 with generated length rising from 4,138 to 7,519 tokens — the paper's own reading is over-steering of an already long-trajectory reasoner.

The ablation table on Qwen3-8B is the most useful page, because it is a ladder over how much of the paper's machinery actually contributes: random direction +0.2, easy-correct anchors +0.8, high *raw* susceptibility anchors +1.4, high length-controlled anchors +2.4, high combined score +3.2; and on the layer axis, early +1.8, middle +2.2, late +2.6, susceptibility-peak +3.0, SPARK-selected +3.2. So the length correction is worth about a full point over the uncorrected version on the same anchor recipe, and the rest of the recipe is worth another 0.8 — but a random direction still buys 0.2 and any of four anchor recipes buys most of the total, which the paper reports without remarking on the shrinkage each rung implies. Selected layers track susceptibility peaks, and the peak moves earlier for the 8B than for the 0.6B and 4B.

## Limits and open problems they name
There is no Limitations section, and no ethics, reproducibility or AI-use statement — the paper ends at a Conclusion after the ablation table, then the reference list. What is conceded in the text: susceptibility is a response-intensity measure and explicitly not a difficulty score or a predictor of correctness; the method is train-free only in the parameter sense, since it is data-calibrated on benchmark-matched calibration questions and the thresholds and layers are fixed per benchmark; the DeepSeek-R1 collapse is put down to over-steering without any test that distinguishes over-steering from a badly chosen layer for that model; and "the first susceptibility-guided steering framework" is claimed with a hedge, with nothing benchmarked in it besides four anchor recipes and five layer choices. Absent and not named as absent: any seed variance, any interval, any comparison against a generic contrastive-activation direction built the standard way on the same budget, any test of the noise perturbation scale ε and count K, and any test of whether the length correction generalises — the regression is fitted per domain on calibration data, so a domain whose difficulty is not length-correlated gets its variation residualised anyway.

## What we take from it
A warning, a method, and a control. The warning is the length confound and it is the cleanest statement of it in this folder: a read-out built from hidden-state response scales with prompt length, harder serialised problems are longer, so the naive read-out partly measures input scale — and the paper's own ablation prices the correction at about a point of accuracy on the same anchor recipe. The method worth stealing is the residualisation itself: fit the read-out against the nuisance on a calibration split and keep what the nuisance does not explain, which is the same recipe runs 595–597 met for confound directions applied now to a *scalar read-out over examples* rather than to a vector. The control worth stealing is the one they do not run but name: replacing the base input with a length-matched random demonstration isolates content from added context — we should refuse any "prepending X creates this direction" claim that does not carry it. Two things to refuse to copy: reporting a strength sweep as sensitivity analysis when the best α is chosen after seeing held-out accuracy (the wider table mixes a sweep and a selection without saying which single number is the honest one), and calling a method selective on grounds that its selection rule is *described* while no ablation compares selective against universal steering.
