# RolloutFaith: Auditing Persistent Internal Interventions in Visual World Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.36843
- **Authors / organisation:** Junchi Yao, Ziyi Wang, Youling Huang, Lijie Hu; MBZUAI, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, Dalian University of Technology
- **Date:** 2026-09-29 (v1; cs.LG)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36843
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Editing a model's internal state and checking that the next output changed is the standard way interpretability work demonstrates that a representation matters. The authors point out that this test is too weak for any model whose outputs are fed back as inputs: what is repaired once can be quietly undone by the next few steps, so a correction that looks large at the moment of intervention may be worth nothing a few frames later. They want an evaluation that separates repair-at-the-edit from repair-that-survives-autonomous-use, and they want to know where inside such a model a correction travels once the edit stops. The larger motivation is a mismatch between how editors are trained (minimise error on the edited prediction) and what they are for (change the future of the trajectory).

## What they studied
Three world models with different predictive mechanisms — DIAMOND (a diffusion model that generates frames), DreamerV3 (a recurrent state-space model with a deterministic hidden state and sampled categorical latents), and STORM (a stochastic transformer world model with a sampled latent plus a cached history) — on Crafter, DeepMind Control Suite Cartpole and Procgen CoinRun. The test roster is 38 Crafter episodes / 41 events, 48 Cartpole episodes / 96 events and 47 CoinRun levels / 92 events, run with three fixed prediction seeds. The unit of analysis is a native intervention state: the final up-block activation of DIAMOND's denoiser calls (64x64x64, applied at all three calls of the edited frame), DreamerV3's 32-by-16 array of categorical prior logits, STORM's 32-by-32 prior logits. Downstream units are the generated frame, the recurrent state, and the transformer cache.

## How they did it
Each event fixes a source (episode or level), a model checkpoint, an edit time, a requested semantic target, an interface and a deterministic action sequence. From a real-observation prefix of four, 64 or eight frames, the model emits four predictions, receives one intervention while producing the edited frame, and then runs forward autonomously for eight more steps; edited and untouched branches share actions, prediction noise and scoreability masks, so only the edit differs. Two comparators are scored: *Reference Activation Patching* (RAP), which replaces the recipient state with the event-matched activation taken from the real observation and therefore measures how much correction the interface can carry when the answer is handed over, and ten *fitted editors* that must infer the correction without the test-time reference — mean training delta, difference of means, an inverse probe (map the semantic residual through the pseudoinverse of the readout), a normalised probe, PCA plus ridge, an uncentered low-rank carrier, a two-layer MLP, Immediate LoReFT, Concept DAS, and a nearest-neighbour manifold editor — all with a common norm cap at the 90th percentile of training correction norms and a dose chosen on validation. Two metrics hold the two timescales apart: Immediate Semantic Gain at horizon 0, and Sustained Semantic Gain averaged over steps 1-8 against the untouched branch. For the propagation study they hold the original edit fixed, restore one component — sampled latent or memory — to its untouched value at step 1, and measure how much of the later gain (steps 9-24) disappears. Finally they retrain the LoReFT editor with a loss on four frozen future transitions instead of the edited frame, and compare.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a circuit and not a dictionary. The explanation is a *persistence accounting*: a signed, horizon-resolved gain curve for a named interface, plus a component attribution that says which of two state components carries the effect forward in each architecture, with restoration loss as the attribution measure. It is explicitly partial — the authors give a local Taylor expansion to say what first-order direction and magnitude would predict and then state that the finite experiments establish operational pathway dependence rather than a decomposition into semantic variables.

## How they judged success
Per-task quality scores on a 0-100 scale (class-weighted tile accuracy for Crafter, body-mask IoU for Cartpole, balanced terrain recall for CoinRun through a frozen convolutional readout with mean class recall 0.999846 on eight held-out development levels), averaged over seeds and events within a source and then weighted equally across sources. Uncertainty is a 10,000-draw bootstrap over sources, so intervals are conditional on the selected editor and exclude training-seed variation. Comparisons are paired against the untouched branch and against a *random edit matched to the same realised norm*; leaderboards are additionally adjusted simultaneously across nine cohort contrasts. Controls include RAP rescaled to each fitted editor's norm, the fitted direction rescaled up to RAP's norm, a request with zero change (asking an editor to preserve the predicted image), eight-steps-stale references, horizon sweeps at 1/2/4/8, action-variation strata, and per-seed leaderboards.

## What they found
RAP has positive sustained gain in all nine model-task combinations (e.g. +10.76 pp Crafter, +9.01 Cartpole, +4.14 CoinRun on DIAMOND; +1.24, +0.92, +1.20 on DreamerV3; +3.62, +8.90, +3.35 on STORM) and beats the best fitted editor in eight of nine, with seven of nine surviving simultaneous adjustment. Fitted editors are mostly near zero: of roughly four dozen fitted cells in the main table, only a handful have sustained intervals clear of zero, and 68 of 90 fitted rows lose ground from validation to test with 25 reversing sign. The one robust reversal is DreamerV3 Cartpole, where Immediate and Delayed LoReFT reach 3.22 and 3.46 pp against RAP's 0.92 (paired difference -2.30 pp, CI [-2.68, -1.90]) and hold across all three seeds. Direction and magnitude fail separately: on DIAMOND-Crafter, giving the fitted direction RAP's magnitude recovers most of the gap, while on DreamerV3-Crafter the fitted direction is negative at a norm where RAP is positive. Sustained gain also decays: RAP's endpoint at step 8 falls below its step-1 average in eight of nine cohorts, and on the audited CoinRun event terrain accuracy goes 72.2% to 96.4% at the edit and back to 70.0% eight steps later against 61.3% untouched. Restoration identifies different carriers per architecture: DIAMOND loses its later effect when the newest generated frame is restored (later RAP gain +5.43 falls to +0.01) rather than the three-frame context; DreamerV3 loses it when the deterministic recurrent state is restored (+1.22 to +0.12 on Cartpole) and not the sampled latent; STORM loses it partly under either restoration (+5.54 to +2.44 and +2.74). An outdated reference is worse than no correction at the edited frame: shifting the reference eight steps earlier takes DreamerV3-Crafter RAP's immediate gain from 2.71 to -0.48 and its sustained gain from 1.24 to 0.42, while the mean edit norm is essentially unchanged (73.64 against 73.47). Delayed LoReFT, trained through four future transitions, beats its immediate twin in 3/9 cohorts on immediate gain, 5/9 on sustained gain and 6/9 on steps 17-24, but is badly harmful on DIAMOND CoinRun (-8.67 pp immediate) and on STORM Crafter.

## Limits and open problems they name
Data budgets are tiny — 15 to 36 training events — and the authors say outright that the small editors cannot establish the headroom of a fully data-scaled editor, and that the main obstacle is estimating reliable correction rules from little data rather than missing correction capacity at the interfaces. Intervals are conditional on the selected editor and exclude training-seed variation. Hyperparameter searches across editor families are not compute-matched. Component restoration creates hybrid states, so a large restoration loss is compatible with off-manifold mixing rather than a clean semantic variable, and matched random restorations exist for only three cohorts. The RAP comparator is privileged by construction and is not a deployable method. They also report dropping Atari Breakout after its observable could be scored on 86.4% of DIAMOND frames, 0% of DreamerV3 frames and 5.1% of STORM frames, and note that they reserve for future work correction inference that transfers across unseen sources and edits of the state components that carry corrections.

## What we take from it
**A measure, a control, and a warning.** The measure: any intervention evaluation we publish reports immediate repair and repair retained over the *model's own* subsequent steps as two separate numbers, plus the endpoint, because here the three disagree often enough to reorder the leaderboard — the fitted leader at horizon 8 differs from the horizon-1 leader in four of nine cohorts. The control: every edit should be paired with a random edit of the same realised norm, since that is what separates "the direction encodes something" from "any displacement this size did something", and their norm-matching trick (give the fitted direction the reference's magnitude, and the reference the fitted direction's magnitude) is a cheap way to say whether a gap is direction or dose. The warning: the reader effect our folder has recorded repeatedly as a persistence failure (run 916's repair not surviving decoding) reappears here as the *sustained-versus-immediate* gap on a model that samples its own future, and the load-bearing asymmetry is which component carries the effect — the same architecture is explained by the *frame* in one case and the *recurrent state* in another. For our own white-box model, the transferable piece is the restoration protocol and its matched-random correction: restore one component of the pair (current output versus carried state) at the first step after the edit and re-measure the later window, then subtract the same statistic computed for a random edit; where the semantic-minus-random interval excludes zero the component is implicated, and where it does not, the operational description is all we have.
