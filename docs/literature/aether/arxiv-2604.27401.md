# Perturbation Probing: A Two-Pass-per-Prompt Diagnostic for FFN Behavioral Circuits in Aligned LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2604.27401
- **Authors / organisation:** Hongliang Liu, Tung-Ling Li, Yuhao Wu (equal contribution, author order settled by paper-scissor-rock); Palo Alto Networks
- **Date:** 2026-04-30
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27401
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Alignment behaviours - refusing harmful asks, pushing back on a false premise - are known to be fragile under small internal edits, but the field's methods for finding the units that carry them each give up something. Probe classifiers and sparse dictionaries are correlational, patching and automated graph search are causal but cost one forward pass per component, and gradient attribution is cheap but task-agnostic and sign-blind. The authors want a screen that is simultaneously cheap at the hypothesis stage, task-parameterised, causally validated afterwards, and - the unusual part - *diagnostic*: able to say in advance, from one forward pass, whether the neurons it ranks will actually be causally effective. The larger goal is a fragility audit for alignment: a way to say which behaviours live in a thin editable layer and which are carried by machinery that absorbs edits.

## What they studied

Thirteen models across four families at the main comparison point (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-2B/4B, Qwen2.5-3B/7B/14B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-3-4B, Gemma-4, SmolLM3-3B, Qwen3-0.6B/8B/14B/32B appear across the appendix tables), and up to nineteen models in the direction-injection sweep. The unit is the individual FFN neuron, ranked per behaviour, with the last-layer residual stream as the readout point. Behaviours are eight binary first-token decisions, each defined by a pair of token sets: safety refusal, sycophancy agreement, English-vs-Chinese language routing, factual recall, chain-of-thought activation, math detection, JSON tool use, code generation. Data: 16 identification prompts per behaviour, 200 HarmBench prompts, 200 benign prompts, 520 AdvBench prompts, 200 TruthfulQA wrong-premise prompts, 30 multi-turn challenge questions.

## How they did it

A first-token behaviour is collapsed to one scalar, the logit gap `F`: mean logit of the refusal-token set minus mean logit of the affirmation-token set. Because the logits are a linear map of the final hidden state, `F` is also a fixed direction `d_F` in residual-stream space - the difference of the two token sets' mean unembedding rows - applied to that hidden state. Each FFN neuron `n` then gets a *signed importance* `I_n = c_n * Δa_n`, the product of a structural coupling `c_n` (the behavioural direction projected through that neuron's `W_down` column, computable from weights with no data) and a perturbation response `Δa_n` (how much the neuron's activation moved between the clean prompt and a perturbed one, measured by two forward passes). Neurons are ranked by `|I_n|`; the top 50 are then validated by three interventions - column ablation (necessity), patching harmful activations into a benign prompt (sufficiency), and restoring the top neurons on a scrambled prompt (closure). Perturbations are heuristic counterfactuals designed to flip the target decision while preserving everything else: adjacent-character transposition of safety keywords (a jailbreak trick reused as a probe), wrong-vs-correct premise pairs for sycophancy, English-vs-Chinese prompts for language, digit swaps for arithmetic. A second per-forward-pass statistic, the FFN/Skip ratio, divides the last layer's FFN contribution to `d_F` by the residual stream entering it, and is used as a predictor of whether ablation will work at all. An appendix replaces the coupling with a two-sided finite-difference "dressed propagator" for architectures with post-normalisation, and another runs contrastive activation addition as an independent steering method on the circuits ablation could not move.

## What counts as an explanation here

A ranked list of about fifty neurons per behaviour, each carrying a signed weight and a layer, plus a behavioural direction and a scalar architecture statistic. It is explicitly partial and local: the claim is not that these neurons compute the behaviour, but that they are the units through which a specific binary first-token decision can be moved. The predictive content is the ambition: the ranking is claimed to predict the size of an ablation's effect (a linear prediction with a stated error) and the FFN/Skip ratio is claimed to predict in advance whether the whole approach will find anything causal. The authors also read the sign structure as mechanism - positively coupled "gatekeepers" that promote the behaviour and negatively coupled "amplifiers" that suppress its opposite - and report that the two-arm decomposition is quantitative, with joint ablation landing within 12.6% of the sum of the arms.

## How they judged success

The primary metric is the percentage drop in the logit gap under ablation of the top-N neurons, with dose-response curves from N = 1 to N = 300. Controls: ablation of the same number of random neurons drawn from the same layers, random neurons from early layers, and per-layer-count-matched random sets at every dose. Statistics: bootstrap confidence intervals and p-values on the held-out HarmBench dose-response, and a split-half identification check. Behavioural translation is measured by generating 60 tokens greedily at each dose and classifying 520 AdvBench responses into formulaic refusal, warning-without-compliance, unclear, and actionable compliance. Additivity of the per-neuron decomposition is tested directly by pair-ablation, with the violation `ε_ij = |ΔF_ij - ΔF_i - ΔF_j| / |ΔF_ij|` computed on all pairs of the top 15 neurons on four models. Gradient baselines are vanilla gradient, gradient-times-activation, and integrated gradients, all on the same models at N = 10 and N = 50. Direction injection is scored by a hand-built classifier for fluent, factually correct Chinese with more than ten Chinese characters, on 580 prompts, with a contrastive-activation-addition control and a hand-audited note on where that classifier produces false positives.

## What they found

On Qwen3-4B, 50 neurons (0.014% of 350,208 FFN neurons, concentrated in layers 29-35) drop the refusal gap by 64% on the identification prompts while matched random sets from the same layers move it by +0.2% and early-layer random sets by -0.7%. Patching those neurons from harmful to benign prompts explains 89.5% of the gap and induces refusal on 6 of 6 benign prompts; restoring them on scrambled prompts recovers 107.7% of the gap. The dose-response is a sigmoid in neuron count with an effective width of about 37 neurons, and on 200 held-out HarmBench prompts the 50 neurons still give -56.6%. The linear prediction tracks the measured ablation: Pearson 0.97 at K = 50 with 3.7% relative error, and 12 of 14 refusing prompts flip to compliance under greedy decoding, rising to 14 of 14 at K = 1000. What the ablation actually does is replace the refusal template: 296 of 520 AdvBench responses (57%) shift from a formulaic refusal to a harm warning with no actionable content, 18% become hedged, and only 3 are classed as compliance, all carrying disclaimers. The method finds causal neurons for exactly two of eight behaviours - safety and sycophancy - and for neither of the other six does ablation of 50, or in checked cases 200, neurons move the behaviour at all. The FFN/Skip ratio predicts ablation effectiveness across the panel with R² = 0.81. Direction injection switches output language on 99.1% of 580 prompts on Qwen3-4B and 99.0% on Qwen2.5-3B, but only for 3 of 19 models, because it needs bilingual training and a moderate ratio. On the smallest model, 20 neurons govern sycophancy: ablating them takes multi-turn capitulation from 36.7% to 0% of 30 questions, and doubling 10 of them raises correction on 200 TruthfulQA prompts from 52% to 88%. On the budget question the method beats the best gradient baseline at N = 10 on Qwen3-4B (-56.2% against -40.7%) and at N = 50 on the same model (-61.2%), while losing on both Llama models and on Qwen2.5-7B, where integrated gradients leads at both doses - and the paper's own summary of this comparison ("competitive or better on some models" above N = 50) understates the number of models where it loses.

## Limits and open problems they name

Seven limitations, all substantive: only binary first-token decisions are addressable; post-normalisation architectures need the corrected coupling; causal neurons appear only where the trained behaviour opposes a pre-training tendency and the opposition sits in the FFN; direction injection needs bilingual training and a moderate ratio and failed on all five Llama models and on Gemma; direction injection is not surgical, since at high strength a language injection cuts the safety gap by roughly half; the method addresses only the fragile template layer of safety, and the deeper warning and factual layers survive 500-neuron ablation untouched; and the method's advantage over gradient attribution is a model-family property that the FFN/Skip ratio does not predict. Beyond their list: perturbation choice is called heuristic with no theory of an optimal perturbation; the language injection's targeted classifier needed a hand audit; and the authors admit the additivity test diverges to noise on the 2B and 7B models, so per-neuron attribution there is unsupported by their own test.

## What we take from it

Four transfers. (1) **A prediction-first screen.** The signed importance is a product of a *structural* term computable from weights and an *input-conditional* term measured on a contrastive pair, and the paper scores it as a quantitative predictor of the intervention it feeds (r = 0.97, 3.7% relative error, and prompt-level flip counts) rather than as an explanation - which is exactly the standard our white-box model should be held to. (2) **The architecture statistic as a no-result predictor.** FFN/Skip at R² = 0.81 is the folder's first measured instance of a quantity computed in one forward pass that says whether the whole method will work on a given model; when our model returns nothing on a behaviour, the first duty is to report the analogous diagnostic before re-tuning anything. (3) **Signed ranking, and a warning about what the magnitude means.** Ranking by the unsigned response made the 50-neuron ablation *worse* than the 10-neuron one because the set contained oppositely signed contributions - so a magnitude-ranked set can be self-cancelling, and any screen we ship should be evaluated at several doses rather than at one. (4) **The best negative control ladder in the folder so far**: same-layer random, early-layer random, and per-layer-count-matched random, all three reported, plus a behavioural classifier that distinguishes "the template changed" from "the behaviour changed" - because "ablation broke 80% of responses" turns out to mean the refusal wording was replaced by a warning, which is the difference between an audit and an alarm.
