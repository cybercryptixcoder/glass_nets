# Unlearning or Obfuscating? Jogging the Memory of Unlearned LLMs via Benign Relearning

- **Key:** arxiv-2406.13356
- **Authors / organisation:** Shengyuan Hu, Yiwei Fu, Zhiwei Steven Wu, Virginia Smith; Carnegie Mellon University
- **Date:** 2025-03-17 (v1 2024-06-19, v4 read; ICLR 2025)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13356
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (abstract, sections 1-9, full reference list, Appendices A-J with Tables 1-9; figures read through captions and restating prose only)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Most unlearning methods for language models are approximate: they update the weights so the model behaves as if it had been trained without the data to be forgotten. The authors wanted to know whether such a model has actually lost the information or has merely stopped saying it. Their probe is deliberately weak and cheap — an ordinary fine-tune on small, public, loosely related text — because an attack nobody needs special access to is the honest test of what a released checkpoint still contains. The larger goal is to separate two states that the field's benchmarks conflate: knowledge gone from the weights, versus knowledge present but no longer reachable from the prompt. The paper's answer is that for every gradient-based method they test, the second state is what they observe.

## What they studied

Models: Phi-1.5 (TOFU), Llama-2-7b and Llama-2-7b-chat (following the Who's Harry Potter setup), zephyr-7b-beta and Llama-3-8b (WMDP), plus a synthetic Llama-2-7b memoriser. Data: TOFU's forget05 split (200 QA pairs over 10 fictitious authors, each holding 20 pairs, four fifths of which are used as the unlearn set in one arm), the Who's Harry Potter construction (a model fine-tuned to memorise Harry Potter text, then unlearned by fine-tuning on alternate labels), and WMDP's bio and cyber attack corpora. The unit of analysis is the **whole model's behaviour under a second fine-tune** — no internal unit, direction or circuit is ever inspected, so the paper works entirely on the input-output map.

## How they did it

Three stages per experiment. **Memorise**: fine-tune a base model until the target content is reproducible verbatim (or use a benchmark model that already contains it). **Unlearn**: apply a gradient-based method to the forget set — gradient ascent, gradient difference, KL minimisation against a frozen reference on the retain set, negative preference optimisation, SCRUB, or the RMU representation-corruption recipe that matches layer representations to scaled noise; full-parameter in the main WMDP arms, LoRA in others. **Relearn**: fine-tune the unlearned model on a small auxiliary set with plain negative log-likelihood, then re-ask the same forgotten queries. The key design decision is what goes in the auxiliary set: it is constructed so that it cannot itself answer the evaluation query, by partitioning the unlearn set into two disjoint halves and relearning on one while testing the other (TOFU book titles, the name Hermione Granger in the Harry Potter setting), or by generating general public text about the topic — two public influenza articles, or GPT-4-written character facts — and filtering out any sentence containing the answer. A third, synthetic stage isolates the mechanism: a memoriser trained on concatenated name sequences is made to learn a correlated pair (Anthony followed by Mark), unlearned until the pair is gone, then fine-tuned only on prefixes ending at Anthony, and the loss on the never-optimised Mark token is tracked.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **behavioural, channel-level explanation**: the unlearning edit lowers the conditional probability of the forget-set tokens but leaves the association structure among them intact, so a generic fine-tune re-inflates the whole structure from any part of it. The formal statement is about per-token loss — objectives like gradient ascent act on every token of every example, so no objective in this family is aware of the relations between tokens, and those relations survive. It is a partial account by construction: nothing is said about which weights or units hold the association. What it does deliver is a prediction with a shape — recovery should scale with how strongly the relearn text is correlated with the target — which the synthetic and WMDP text-relevance tables then test.

## How they judged success

Four metrics matched to the three tasks: GPT-4 LLM-as-judge scores on 1-10 for answer quality on hazardous questions (70 sampled WMDP MCQ items with the choices stripped; RMU additionally scored on MCQ log-likelihood accuracy), ROUGE-L against the true continuation for verbatim copyrighted text (15 eighty-word chunks each split in half), and binary keyword-in-generation accuracy for name recovery. The controls carry the weight: the unlearned model must score low on the same queries while the pre-unlearning model scores high; the relearn set is verified to be uninformative by fine-tuning the *base* model on it alone and checking that helps none of the three tasks; a no-relearning row is printed beside every relearn row; relearn-text variants (English, German, female names, gibberish, none) are swapped while everything else is held fixed; and the synthetic experiment prints a success rate per number of pair repetitions.

## What they found

Every gradient-based method tested is reversed. On WMDP with zephyr-7b-beta, the forget-set judge score is 5.92 for the original model, 1.27 after gradient-ascent unlearning, and 6.2 after relearning on public text; the LoRA arms move the same way (1.67 unlearned, 5.2 relearned for gradient ascent; 1.0 to 5.08 for negative preference optimisation). Keyword recovery from a disjoint half of the unlearn set exceeds 70% attack success when unlearning is shallow and falls as unlearning deepens, and more relearning steps are not monotonically better — past 40 steps on TOFU every checkpoint degrades, and a 1600-step run on the Harry Potter example drifts from the correct name to a different character. Relearn-set relevance governs the size of the effect: on the WMDP multiple-choice task, English knowledge gives 47.79% accuracy against 24.67% for no relearning, German 43.76%, and gibberish 25.05%. Relearning on the base model alone moves nothing (Holmes-recovery accuracy 0% and 0%, verbatim ROUGE 0.1446 to 0.1407), which is the paper's evidence that the recovery comes from the unlearned model rather than from the auxiliary data. The synthetic mechanism is clean: with seven repeated pairs the pair is recovered 100% from a deep unlearning checkpoint, with three only 23%, with one 0%, and the loss on the never-optimised second token tracks the loss on the first. RMU is the exception worth reading: relearning restores multiple-choice accuracy from 0.3086 to 0.4812 against the original's 0.5177, but the free-text judge score barely moves (1.89 to 2.24) because the relearned model still emits repeated tokens, and only re-initialising the corrupted layer to zero before relearning lifts it to 4.84.

## Limits and open problems they name

The same prompt is used before and after the attack, so prompt filtering and guardrails are named as a plausible defence and the interaction with jailbreaks is untested; in-context relearning is suggested but not run. Coverage is four base models and three benchmarks, with the authors noting that model choice interacts with data format (Llama-3-8b-Instruct was harder to unlearn on WMDP, which they attribute to the academic-article form of the unlearn set against a QA-tuned model). The RMU result shows that success depends on the evaluation format, which is the paper's own instance of the fragility it is warning about. No confidence intervals or seed repetitions are printed anywhere in the text read, and no internal representation is measured, so every claim is about generation.

## What we take from it

A **measure and a control**, both directly reusable. The measure: recovery is scored from data disjoint from the query, so an attack that never sees the answer cannot be accused of teaching it — our white-box model's own audits should hold the probe set and the elicitation set disjoint by construction, not by inspection. The control: fine-tune the *unedited* model on the same auxiliary data and print that number. Their version reads 0% and 0.1446-to-0.1407, and it is the cell that converts "relearning works" into "the information was in the weights". A **framing**: per-token objectives cannot remove relations between tokens, which is the sharpest statement in this folder of why output suppression and information removal are different operations, and it predicts that any localisation claim our model makes about a "deleted" fact must name the relational structure it would have to have broken. And a **warning about elicitation strength as a free variable**: RMU looks robust under free-text generation and porous under multiple choice on the same checkpoint, so an audit that reports one readout is reporting the readout, not the model. The synthetic pair-correlation ladder (0% to 23% to 97% to 100% as repetitions go from one to seven) is also the cheapest available calibration curve for "how much correlational support does a state need before it is elicitable", which is exactly the axis our model has to be predictive along.
