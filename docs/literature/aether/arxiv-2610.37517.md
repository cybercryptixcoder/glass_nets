## What they wanted, and why
Activation steering is cheap and controllable, but the paper opens on two practical complaints that make it hard to reason about. First, the same nominal strength `alpha` produces wildly different displacement for different traits, because a contrastive vector's norm is a property of the contrast data and not of the trait; existing tools patch this with a shared scaling across behaviours, which makes one trait's vector depend on which other traits happened to be in the set. Second, when a model is steered on several traits at once, correlated vectors drag each other and the generation leaves the distribution the user meant to edit. Their goal is a steering operator whose strength has an interpretable meaning, and a composition rule that keeps two traits independent of each other. The motivating application is concrete and clinical (a therapist model that should be warm without being sycophantic), and the underlying belief is the linear representation hypothesis plus the geometric assumption that correlated traits live at small angle to each other.

## What they studied
Llama-2-7B-Chat as the main subject, at the layer that prior work already found best for these evaluations, plus a replication on Qwen3-8B at a layer chosen by their own layer sweep. The unit of analysis is a one-dimensional direction in the residual stream, built by the two-means (contrastive activation addition) construction: normalised mean of a positive set minus mean of a negative set, times the difference's norm. Traits come from Anthropic's model-written evaluation sets (five safety-relevant traits: coordination, corrigibility, myopia, power, wealth) plus a purpose-built 1,000-family synthetic warmth corpus, and three standard bias/truthfulness datasets (TruthfulQA, ToxiGen, BBQ) are used for an external comparison. Evaluation is binary multiple-choice with the two answer positions randomised, scored by the share of items where the target answer gets the higher logit and by the average logit difference between target and non-target answers.

## How they did it
Three components, layered. (1) **Steerable-range clipping**: sweep `alpha` on training data, then search all intervals containing zero for the segment that spans near the global minimum and maximum of the score curve while staying steep and monotone, using a loss that trades endpoint coverage against slope with one trade-off weight, then affine-map that segment onto [-1, +1] so `alpha = +1` means "the steepest useful push" for this vector. (2) **Parameterized steering**: instead of adding `alpha` times the vector's norm, move each activation along the direction by exactly the amount needed to place it at the same projection along the direction as a target point interpolated between the negative and positive means; the step size becomes a function of the activation, so `alpha = +1` means "sit at the positive mean's projection", and the operation is idempotent. (3) **Orthogonalization**: form the matrix whose columns are the k steering vectors, take an orthonormal basis of its span, express the vectors in that basis, and map any activation to k concept coordinates plus a preserved residual outside the span; the map sends vector j to the j-th unit coordinate, is losslessly invertible, and leaves the component orthogonal to the span untouched. A final per-coordinate affine reparameterisation places the positive and negative means of each concept at +1 and -1. They prove the properties (coordinate separation, exact reconstruction, that steering one coordinate changes only that coordinate, that steering operators on distinct concepts commute, and that linear statistics like the mean commute with the maps), and give an implementation that avoids any d-by-d inverse by working only in the k-dimensional span, with cost claims for preprocessing, per-activation work and storage. Multi-trait success is scored two ways: sensitivity (the mean across `alpha` of the standard deviation of the first trait's score as the second trait's `beta` is swept, lower better) and range (max minus min of the first trait's score across `alpha`, at `beta` fixed to each extreme, higher better). An LLM judge (GPT-5-mini, 0-10 per response, threshold 5) re-checks the range comparison on generated text.

## What counts as an explanation here
A geometry plus an operator, not a description of the trait. The claim is that a trait is a single direction whose usable extent can be measured (the clipped interval), whose strength can be represented as a coordinate (the parameterized space), and whose entanglement with other traits is exactly the linear overlap between their directions, removable by a change of basis. It is meant to be complete within that linear model of the trait and explicitly partial about everything else: the authors say entanglement that is not a single shared linear direction survives orthogonalisation.

## How they judged success
Primary metrics: `percent_steered` (share of items where the target answer wins the logit comparison) and `average_score` (mean logit gap between target and non-target answers), on binary multiple-choice items with randomised answer order. Controls and baselines: no-steering baseline; unclipped `alpha`-iterative steering (the classic method at the same layer); clipped `alpha`-iterative steering; parameterized steering without orthogonalisation, evaluated both with the measured trait steered first and steered second (the order-dependence control); orthogonal parameterized steering; and MAT-Steer (a token-level gated multi-attribute method) replicated as far as the authors could. Robustness: a second model (Qwen3-8B), a second metric (`percent_steered`) for every multi-steering table, an LLM-judge evaluation of generations, and a documented dataset-construction audit for the new warmth corpus (label-blind semantic review, classification probes, ordinal-ordering test, and a surface-form/length shortcut probe).

## What they found
On the headline two-trait example (sycophancy and warmth), steering sycophancy down alone drops the warmth score from 61.5 to 26.5, and naively steering both leaves warmth at 30.0; the two orthogonalised arms recover it to 42.0 and 43.0 while sycophancy sits at 51.5 in all three multi-trait arms. On the highly correlated pairs (cosine 0.62-0.87), `alpha`-iterative sensitivity averages 1.508 where parameterized measures are 0.975 and below, with orthogonal parameterized at 0.827; range at the two `beta` extremes is about 1.550 and 1.794 for `alpha`-iterative against 3.221 and 3.511 for orthogonal parameterized, i.e. roughly double, with the orthogonal arm's gain over the non-orthogonal parameterized arm at `beta = +1` being 0.472. Order dependence is real without orthogonalisation (moving the measured trait to first position inflates its sensitivity by 0.62 on average, to more than the orthogonal arm) and vanishes with it, matching the commutation proof. Crucially, the orthogonalisation does not buy peak effect: on power/coordination the `alpha`-iterative curve reaches a higher maximum than the orthogonal parameterized one. The external comparison against MAT-Steer is weaker than the headline: the authors could not reproduce MAT-Steer's split, and their own no-steering baseline already beats MAT-Steer's steered numbers on two of three datasets (59.5 on BBQ, 89.0 on ToxiGen and 63.0 on TruthfulQA against 60.3 / 57.6 / 61.9), so the MAT-Steer deltas are not comparable; within their own frame, unclipped `alpha`-iterative steering scores *below* the no-steering baseline on all three (48.0 / 47.0 / 43.5), which is the paper's cleanest evidence that clipping the range is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. The Qwen3-8B replication is mixed: at `beta = -1` orthogonal parameterized beats both baselines on average while at `beta = +1` it beats parameterized by only about 0.082; and the warmth/sycophancy geometry stays entangled after orthogonalisation, with the authors attributing this to representations that overlap beyond the single shared linear direction.

## Limits and open problems they name
Not all behaviour is steerable and interference is reduced, not eliminated; they point at non-linear steering and multi-layer steering as future directions. The warmth case is the explicit failure: the warm-and-not-sycophantic region of the projected activation space is still nearly empty of training examples, and orthogonalisation only removes the projection of one vector on the other. The authors also report that the synthetic warmth corpus retains a length shortcut (length alone classifies warmth labels well above chance, and the warm end is longer than the cold end in 79.5% of families), that most traits are evaluated on one model at one layer, and that the MAT-Steer comparison could not be run on the same data split.

## What we take from it
A **measure** and a **warning**, in that order. The measure: "usable range" is an empirically found, per-direction quantity, and the paper's clipping procedure is a cheap instrument for it - sweep the intervention strength, find the interval containing zero that spans the curve's extremes with the steepest monotone segment, and report all strengths as fractions of that interval. That is exactly the calibration our white-box model needs before any cross-direction or cross-model comparison of intervention strength, because it replaces "alpha times the vector's norm" (a unit that means different things for different contrasts) with "fraction of this direction's own usable extent". The warning is sharper: on the paper's own evidence, an intervention can gain roughly double the range and lose its peak, so our reports need both the excursion and the maximum; and the authors' own finding that orthogonalisation cannot fix entanglement that is not a single shared linear direction is the confirmation, from the geometric side, of what this folder has been finding repeatedly - that the linear object we fit is a projection of the mechanism, not the mechanism.
