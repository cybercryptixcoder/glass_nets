## What they wanted, and why

Audio language models are trained and benchmarked mostly on transcription-like work, which is thought to teach them to prefer the words they hear over how those words were said. That preference matters wherever the voice carries information the transcript cannot: a crisis-line caller's flat tone, a claimed identity contradicted by the voice, sarcasm that exists only in the delivery. The behavioural version of this failure is well documented, and one mechanistic study had already located the circuit behind conflicts in *lexical* content and proposed a training-free repair for it. This paper asks the next question: when the conflicting evidence is prosodic rather than lexical, is it the same circuit, and does the same repair transfer? The authors are explicit that this is a diagnostic question before it is a repair question, and they structure the work as encode-then-use-then-intervene: first establish whether the acoustic signal survived into the model at all, then ask what the model does with it, then intervene on whatever that analysis implicates. The larger goal is the one that matters to us — separating "the model could not see it" from "the model saw it and did not use it" without assuming which is true.

## What they studied

Six audio language models across two encoder families. Four use Whisper-style encoders (Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, SALMONN-13B, DeSTA2.5-Audio, and a 30-billion-parameter model called Audex brought in to ask whether the bias survives a large scale-up); two do not (Phi-4-multimodal, convolutional-conformer encoder, and Baichuan-Omni-1.5, residual-vector-quantised audio tokenizer, deliberately included because it shares Qwen2.5-7B as language backbone with Qwen2-Audio and so isolates the encoder). Data is a 3,594-item conflict set built from existing annotated speech corpora — emotion labels from RAVDESS file names, sarcasm labels from MUStARD++ — organised into three conflict categories: emotional prosody (cheerful transcript, angry voice), speaker identity (male transcript, female voice), and sarcasm/pacing (sincere transcript, sarcastic delivery). Units of analysis span the whole stack: audio-encoder layers, the projector where audio enters the language model, individual decoder layers 0-32, token positions and spans, and attention edges between positions. All inference was run in 16-bit, deliberately, so that accuracy and dominance measurements are not confounded by precision.

## How they did it

Four stages, in a fixed order, and the order is load-bearing. **One, capability screening.** Before any attribution, every model in every category is tested on the audio-only condition and kept only if it beats chance — because a high dominance rate on a model that cannot do the task from audio is not suppression, it is absence. Baichuan falls below chance on prosody (0.099 against 0.125) and is dropped from that category; SALMONN falls below chance on identity (0.453 against 0.500) under log-probability scoring and is dropped from that one. Five of six pass in both analysed categories. **Two, linear probing** through encoder and projector, layer by layer, to see whether the paralinguistic attribute is still decodable where the language model receives it — with speaker-independent splits and a shuffled-label control. **Three, attribution patching** (gradient-based approximation rather than one forward pass per component) to score text-circuit against audio-circuit contributions by layer and prompt position, followed by the faithfulness check the approximation requires: rerun the model on the top-scoring component subset alone and see whether it reproduces the full model's output. **Four, two opposite interventions.** The audio-side one is back-patching, taken from the lexical-conflict literature: copy late-layer hidden states of the audio span into an earlier layer and let the model run on, swept over source layer, destination window width and audio-span amplitude. The text-side one is attention knockout: block the answer position, or the intervening positions, or all positions, from attending to the conflicting claim tokens, at late, mid or all layers, with free-form generation re-applying the block at every decoding step. Every intervention is scored in both closed-set multiple-choice and open-ended generation, and beside each one sit isolated controls — matched items where the claim is true, text-only items where no audio exists and the claim is the only evidence, and a claim-free fluency readout. They also rebuild the lexical task that the borrowed repair was designed for (from a published cross-lingual benchmark, audio resynthesised with a different TTS system) and run each of its four flip types at its own published layer configuration, to check that the repair still works where it was invented — a positive control for their own pipeline. Two further robustness checks guard the replication: a layer-index-base check (the whole sweep rerun under the other reading of the published indices) and a claim-order control (claim-first versus claim-last) against recency confounds.

## What counts as an explanation here

A routing account, and it is deliberately weaker than a circuit. The claim is not "this edge carries the effect" but "no single edge does": the conflicting claim is re-encoded into intervening tokens and reconstructed at each generation step, so severing the direct claim-to-answer connection removes nothing while attenuating the whole relay removes most of it. Supported by three separate measurements — probe (information is present), attribution (text outweighs audio about eighteen to one, and audio's share peaks early and collapses to half a percent by late layers), and knockout bisection (answer-position-only blocking inert, all-intervening-positions blocking effective). Partial by construction, and no predictive model is offered: nothing here forecasts the dominance rate of an unseen item or an unseen model. The one forward-looking claim they make is about measurement — that a closed-set benchmark format cannot see an intervention of this shape — and they support that with the format comparison rather than with a prediction.

## How they judged success

Text-dominance rate on mismatched text+audio items as the behavioural target, with modality-split scoring (text-only items excluded, since pooling them inflates the rate), complete-case denominators with dropped-trial counts published per cell, 95% Wilson intervals, Cochran's Q as the omnibus test for repeated binary outcomes on the same items, and pairwise McNemar with Holm–Bonferroni across all fifteen model pairs. Probe accuracy on speaker-independent splits with a shuffled-label control. Attribution faithfulness as the fraction of the full model's behaviour reproduced by the top twenty percent of components. For back-patching: change in the log-probability margin between audio label and claimed label in nats against a printed baseline margin, plus a paired confidence interval and a McNemar exact test on discordant pairs in the lexical replication, with a stated count of items fixed and items broken. For knockout: audio-consistent accuracy, dominance rate before and after, and — the part that makes it a repair rather than damage — matched-item accuracy, text-only accuracy, and residual fluency under the unmodified model. Ablations over layers (late / mid / all), positions (answer only / intervening / all), source layer, window width and amplitude.

## What they found

Text dominance is widespread: averaged over the six models on mismatched items the rate is 0.78, or 0.86 with sarcasm excluded, and it is not an artefact of the pool — on identity the five non-SALMONN models are statistically indistinguishable from one another across an eight-point spread of raw rate, which the authors print to bound their own behavioural claim. Per-category rates range from about twenty to about sixty-five percent on sarcasm to above ninety percent on prosody and identity in most models. **Not a perception failure:** in Qwen2-Audio, the model with the strongest measurable bias, speaker gender is linearly recoverable at accuracy 1.000 from encoder layer 13 onward and remains decodable at the projector, so the information reaches the language model intact. **Attribution is lopsided:** text contributes 3.29 against audio's 0.18, roughly eighteen to one, and the top fifth of components reproduces the full model's behaviour exactly; audio influence peaks at decoder layer 9 and is down to 0.5% by the late layers. **Audio amplification does not work at any setting tried.** Back-patching's best source layer is 16, gaining 0.511 nats on a baseline margin of −7.32 nats, which closes about seven percent of the gap; later source layers push the margin as far as 0.381 nats below baseline; widening the destination window to 1, 3, 5 and 7 does nothing; and scaling the audio span 1.5x, 2.0x, 3.0x, 5.0x does nothing until 5.0x, where it destroys capability from 1.000 to 0.100 and leaves the margin undefined. Crucially, the same procedure on the lexical task it was designed for also does nothing — text dominance of 0.793 reproduces against an audio ceiling of 0.908, but the repair moves 26 of 300 items, helping 14 and hurting 12, for a pooled gain of 0.007 with an interval straddling zero and an exact-test p of 0.845, against 0.160 previously reported — so they decline to read its null on the new task as any sort of evidence. **The text-side intervention works, and only in one format.** In multiple-choice, blocking the answer position from the claim does nothing (prosody accuracy stays 0.000 to 0.000, identity moves 0.067 to 0.083); blocking every position after the claim recovers most of it (prosody 0.000 to 0.533, identity 0.067 to 0.717), at a capability cost of 1.000 to 0.367 and 1.000 to 0.800. In free-form generation, mid-layer knockout drops dominance from 1.000 to 0.375 on prosody and to 0.433 on identity while matched-item accuracy rises from 0.767 to 0.900 and from 0.933 to 1.000 — the readout that says the intervention helped rather than hurt. Blocking at all layers drops it further (0.182 and 0.300) but costs capability on matched prosody items down to 0.667, and late-layer blocking changes nothing, which is what rules out "the model just defaulted to audio because the text was gone".

## Limits and open problems they name

Acted corpora (RAVDESS, MUStARD++) with categorical, non-naturalistic conflicts; English only. The mechanistic work is one model, two categories — sarcasm/pacing is measured behaviourally but excluded from the mechanistic analysis because its audio-only labels were too underdetermined to support a suppression claim. The second model establishes only that the information is recoverable, not that the mechanism replicates. The repair is scope-limited to free-form generation, is unconditional (it attenuates the claim whether the claim is correct), and they explicitly did not test cases where an ambiguous audio should properly be overridden by a reliable transcript. The free-form arms use few items, so per-configuration differences should not be over-read. The lexical replication's mismatch of task instantiation and TTS is stated plainly as a likely reason for its null, and they treat it as configuration sensitivity rather than as a contradiction of the original. They also flag the privacy consequence: the same sensitivity that enables audio-grounded reasoning enables inference of emotion and identity.

## What we take from it

Four things, and the first is the most valuable to us.

**A borrowed repair's null is uninformative unless the repair has been shown to work in its own setting.** This paper is the folder's cleanest instance of a paper running that check on itself and then refusing to draw the conclusion its own null would have supported. Back-patching failed on paralinguistic conflict, which would have been a tidy story ("different circuit, the repair does not transfer"); instead they rebuilt the original lexical task, ran it at the published configurations, found the procedure inert there too, and wrote that "its null on paralinguistic conflict carries no information". The lesson for our white-box model is mechanical: whenever we import an intervention from another setting and it produces nothing, the run is not evidence about our setting until the same intervention has been shown to move something in the setting it came from, on our own instantiation. Without a working positive control a null result is a null instrument. Their layer-index-base check (rerunning the null under the other reading of the published indices, gain 0.007 or 0.003, null unchanged) is the cheap companion to this: a procedure pinned to published indices is only as portable as the indexing convention.

**An intervention's effectiveness is a function of the readout format, not only of the mechanism.** The same knockout, on the same model and the same items, is completely inert in closed-set multiple-choice and effective in free-form generation — and the reason is structurally sensible: option tokens multiply the positions that carry claim-informed content, so removing any one direct edge leaves the others to relay it. Read against run 926 (a token-scope ladder where last-token patching gave a false negative) and run 925 (reliance without following, where where-the-answer-landed mattered more than whether it moved), the recurrence is that **the folder keeps treating an intervention's null as a fact about the component when it is a fact about the evaluation surface — the position axis, the token scope, and now the output format.** Mechanical rule for us: **every intervention our white-box model reports is scored in at least two output formats — a constrained readout and an open generation — and where the two disagree the component is recorded as relay-dependent rather than absent, with the format named as part of the claim rather than buried in a methods paragraph; a benchmark whose readout is a fixed option set is not permitted to adjudicate a knockout-style intervention at all.**

**A direct-edge knockout on an autoregressive readout is nearly always the wrong instrument.** The bisection here is worth copying as a table, because it is four rows and it rules out the whole family: no block (prosody 0.000, identity 0.067), answer position only (0.000, 0.083), all intervening positions (0.533, 0.717), every position (identical to all-intervening). Two rows of that table are identical, which is exactly what a relay account predicts and what a single-edge circuit account forbids. For us the operational form is: when a component's effect does not survive a direct-path ablation, do not conclude the component is innocent — ablate the intermediate positions that could be relaying it, and if the effect returns, the component is upstream of a relay rather than absent.

**Perception-versus-use needs its screen run before its mechanism, and the screen can exclude a model for the wrong reason.** Their capability screening is the right shape — beat chance from audio alone before any attribution — and their honesty about its limits is the part to copy: SALMONN's behavioural identity accuracy (0.600) is higher than its screened accuracy (0.453) because the behavioural scorer silently drops unparseable responses (about a fifth of items) while the log-probability screen cannot fail to parse, so they state that the exclusion is a statement about what they can measure, not about what the model can hear. That is run 925's label-artefact lesson arriving from the screening side, and the rule it gives us is that **any capability gate we use to admit or exclude a model prints both scorers — the forgiving one and the strict one — and states which one the gate used.**
