## What they wanted, and why
Sparse autoencoders have handed the field dictionaries of vectors that are supposed to be the model's concepts, but almost all evaluation of those dictionaries asks whether a *single* feature is interpretable, feature by feature, one at a time. The authors want to know whether the dictionary as a whole has *shape* — whether the concept point cloud has structure at scales larger than one feature, the way a physical system has atoms, mesoscale domains and a global shape. Their motivation is partly safety-adjacent (a hidden objective is easier to spot if you know what a normal concept neighbourhood looks like), but the honest framing is that of a physicist applying statistical-mechanics and astronomy machinery to a data set nobody had described geometrically. They explicitly do not try to explain *why* the structure forms, and say so in the conclusion.

## What they studied
Gemma-2-2b — its residual-stream activations and, mostly, the publicly released Gemma Scope SAE feature vectors at various layers and widths (the meso-scale analysis uses a layer-12, 16k-width SAE with mean L0 of 41; one appendix figure uses a 16k-width, L0 = 176 dictionary). The unit of analysis is the **feature vector (decoder direction)**, treated as a point in activation space, and the objects studied are tetrahedra of four such points, partitions of all features into groups, and the covariance of the whole point cloud. Text sample: The Pile, 50k documents for co-occurrence histograms and 10k for lobe labelling.

## How they did it
Three analyses, one per scale, each with its own null model. **Atom scale**: they take word pairs and the word-to-word "function vector" transformations already in the literature, compute all pairwise difference vectors between pair members, cluster the differences with k-means, and ask whether members of a cluster line up so that quadruplets form parallelograms (`b - a ≈ d - c`, a cluster of unnormalised differences) or trapezoids (`b - a ∝ d - c`, cosine clustering). A first pass on SAE feature vectors returned mostly noise; they then looked at early-layer residual activations directly, found the classic (man, woman, king, queen) quadruplet is visibly *not* a parallelogram, diagnosed the distortion as "distractor" directions unrelated to meaning — the first principal component of the layer-0 difference space turns out to track the last-token length difference between the two words — and re-projected the data with linear discriminant analysis, whose signal-to-noise eigenmodes are defined by inter- versus intra-cluster covariance, before re-clustering. **Meso scale**: they define functional groups without any reference to position — fire a layer-12 SAE on Pile documents, count a feature as active when its coefficient exceeds 1, count two features as co-occurring when both are active inside the same 256-token block (so that co-occurrence means "in the same passage", not "at the same token"), build a co-occurrence affinity matrix, and spectral-cluster it into k lobes. They tried five different co-occurrence affinities (Jaccard, Dice, overlap, simple matching, phi). Whether the functional partition is *also* a spatial one is then measured two ways: adjusted mutual information between the co-occurrence-based labels and labels from spectral clustering on feature cosine similarity, and the balanced test accuracy of a logistic regression predicting the functional lobe from the feature's coordinates (80/20 split). **Galaxy scale**: they treat the whole feature cloud as a point sample, compare its covariance eigenvalue spectrum against the isotropic-Gaussian / Marchenko-Pastur expectation, fit the slope of the eigenvalue power law as a function of layer, and estimate the differential entropy of the cloud with a k-nearest-neighbour estimator, subtracting the Gaussian entropy with the same covariance to get a "clustering entropy" (what physics would call negentropy).

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation of any computation — a **geometric description** of the dictionary, and explicitly a descriptive one. The deliverable is three named structural claims (crystal faces, functional lobes, a power-law-shaped cloud) each backed by a statistic tested against a randomised null. Nothing here predicts model behaviour, and the paper is candid that no mechanism produces the structure it reports; it treats "we found shape, we did not explain it" as the correct stopping point and flags explaining the origin as the obvious next paper.

## How they judged success
Each scale has a null hypothesis and a test that can fail. Atom scale: cluster quality and visual parallelogram/trapezoid quality before and after LDA projection, on the same quadruplets — a qualitative criterion, not a number, and the weakest evaluation in the paper. Meso scale: adjusted mutual information (which corrects for chance agreement) and balanced logistic-regression accuracy, both compared against a **permuted-labels shuffle** and against **randomly re-drawn unit-norm feature directions**, reported at 954 and 74 standard deviations from the null respectively — the strongest evaluation in the paper, because the null is the right one (geometry random, function fixed). All five co-occurrence measures are compared rather than one, and phi wins on both statistics while every measure recovers the code/math lobe. Galaxy scale: the eigenvalue spectrum against the Wishart/Marchenko-Pastur expectation, with the smallest eigenvalues dropped as a finite-sample artefact and the analysis restricted to the 100 largest principal components; the clustering-entropy estimate is reported with 95% confidence intervals across layers. No held-out evaluation, no downstream task, no human rating.

## What they found
**Atom scale**: after projecting out distractors, word-transformation differences form recognisable clusters, and the parallelogram/trapezoid reading of the classic analogy quadruplets becomes credible where it was not before; the example they show as a trapezoid is Austria, Vienna, Switzerland, Bern, with the shared difference read as country-to-capital. The concrete finding is therefore negative-then-corrective: the well-worn analogy pairs are poor parallelograms in raw activations, the distortion is largely a semantic-irrelevant direction such as word length, and a supervised projection recovers the structure. They describe the classifier's own discriminative structure as easier to learn in middle layers (an appendix silhouette-score panel). **Meso scale**: a two-lobe partition splits the cloud roughly in half, one part dominated by math and code documents and the other by English text; a three-lobe partition subdivides the English side into short-form dialogue (chat, parliamentary record) and long-form scientific papers. The functional partition is recoverable from geometry far above chance under both measures and under both nulls; a per-document-type histogram confirms lobe 2 is disproportionately active on code and math. **Galaxy scale**: the spectrum is not Wishart — the eigenvalue decay is a power law, steeper for SAE features than for raw activations (which decay much more slowly), with the steepest slope in middle layers (layer 12 at about -0.47 against about -0.24 at layer 0 and about -0.25 at layer 24), and clustering entropy lowest in the middle layers. They name the shape informally and describe it as width falling off like a power law across successive principal dimensions.

## Limits and open problems they name
The authors themselves list the big one: **no explanation of why the structure exists**, with the hope that answering it would refine superposition theory or improve SAE training. They also flag the scale question — it is not obvious whether individual features, co-occurring groups, or larger units are the right unit of analysis, which feature splitting makes sharp, and they leave it open. The crystal analysis is the weakest link methodologically and they partly concede it by moving from SAE features to raw early-layer activations when the SAE version returned noise. Everything is one model family (Gemma-2-2b) and mostly a handful of SAE checkpoints selected by width and L0. The distractor removal is supervised (LDA needs the cluster labels) and some distractor directions could not be interpreted at all. The lobe labelling depends on a token-block length (256) chosen by hand, and on an activation threshold of 1.

## What we take from it
(1) **A null model per claim, and the null must randomise the thing you did not use.** The meso-scale test is the template: build the partition from *function* only (co-occurrence) and test whether *position* predicts it, with the geometric side randomised to unit-norm Gaussian vectors as the comparator. Our batteries name read-outs that are "structured" or "aligned" all the time; the honest form is always "aligned with respect to a null in which the geometry is random", and the two-null version (permute labels, then regenerate geometry) is cheap. (2) **A distractor direction can hide a real structure, and the first principal component is where to look first for it.** The word-length component is measured, not asserted, by regressing the first difference-space PC on last-token length difference; any time our read-out's analogy or separation claim is mediocre, the first diagnostic is to regress the leading residual direction on a surface feature (length, frequency, position) and re-score after projecting it out. The warning that comes with it: the projection here was fit with labels in hand, so the gain is not a held-out gain — a fair version of this idea for us requires fitting the distractor projection on one split and scoring on another. (3) **Co-occurrence at the passage scale, not the token scale, is what recovers functional grouping.** Their 256-token block is a deliberate choice of *time resolution*, and it is the reason the lobes align with document genre; for us this is the argument that a feature-grouping statistic should be computed over windows chosen to match the unit at which the behaviour is defined (a paragraph, a reasoning step, a whole answer), and that the window length is a hyperparameter to be reported. (4) **A layer profile is a result, and the middle-layer pattern repeats.** Steepest power law and lowest clustering entropy both peak mid-stack, joining the folder's growing set of mid-layer phenomena — and it is the same column discipline runs 495-505 demanded: report the per-layer curve, not the average. A limitation worth carrying: this paper's whole geometric programme is *static*, computed on the dictionary's decoder vectors without any intervention, which is exactly the gap `arxiv-2609.13529` names — structure you can describe but have not shown anything computes with.
