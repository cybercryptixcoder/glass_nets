- **Key:** arxiv-2410.23903
- **Authors / organisation:** Augustin Lemesle, Julien Lehmann, Tristan Le Gall; CEA (PyRAT has been developed at CEA since 2019; the paper names recent co-contributors Serge Durand and Samuel Akinwande as past)
- **Date:** 2025-09-05 (v2; v1 2024-10-31)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23903
- **Kind:** paper (tool description)
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
The authors present a mature verification tool and want to make its design legible: which abstractions it offers, how they are implemented, and where each one wins or loses. The larger goal is regulatory and industrial — guaranteeing that a network used in a high-risk system (health, transport, energy, aeronautics) cannot behave badly, at a point where proof is required rather than testing. The paper is therefore less an argument than a catalogue with experiments attached, and its value to us is exactly that: it is the field's own inventory of which abstractions a practitioner reaches for, and the cost/precision trade between them is printed rather than asserted.

## What they studied
Feedforward, convolutional and residual networks in ONNX (also Keras/TensorFlow and PyTorch, with narrower support), verified against VNN-LIB or the tool's own property format. Sizes run from a 7-input mooring-line classifier (three hidden layers of 25 units) through ACAS-Xu and LinearizeNN (4-5 inputs, 100-256 units per layer) to the CIFAR-100 medium network with about 2.5 million parameters and 32x32 inputs. The unit of analysis is the **reachable output set of a box-shaped input region**, represented in one of several abstract domains: intervals (boxes), zonotopes, constrained zonotopes, hybrid zonotopes, plus polytopes, polynomial zonotopes and small sets. Nothing about individual features, neurons' meaning or weights is studied — the whole apparatus is set-valued.

## How they did it
The pipeline is: parse network and property into an internal layer graph; apply conservative simplifications (dropping redundant transposes, fusing matmul with add, rewriting max-pool as convolution plus ReLU, and appending an extra linear layer that rewrites the output property into differences of outputs so that relational domains can see it); build the abstract element for the input box; push it through the layers, with each nonlinearity replaced by a sound over-approximation (unstable ReLU becomes a linear function of the pre-activation plus one fresh noise symbol; sigmoid gets a slope plus computed deviation; cast/floor/ceil get a half-width symbol); then evaluate the property on the concretised output box. If the result is inconclusive, two branch-and-bound schemes partition the problem — splitting an input coordinate (choosing the coordinate by its estimated influence on the outputs) or splitting an unstable ReLU into its negative and positive branches (choosing the node by an influence estimate). Domains can be run simultaneously, with the cheap interval domain intersected into the others because it is exact at ReLU. Bounds for the constrained-zonotope concretisation are obtained by taking the Lagrangian dual and maximising it with gradient ascent, which keeps every intermediate result a valid bound, in preference to a constraint-programming solver that is faster but not sound in real arithmetic. Operations run on NumPy or PyTorch, with GPU optional and soundness in real arithmetic disabled in that mode.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation of the network at all — an **enclosure**. The account is a set that provably contains every reachable output for a stated input region, and the verdict is one of four values: property verified, property falsified with a witness point, unknown, or timed out. There is no component-level attribution, no directions, no dictionary; a network with permuted weights would be analysed identically. Completeness is explicitly not claimed either: the enclosure is one-sided, so anything it cannot decide is left undecided.

## How they judged success
Three benchmark families from VNN-Comp, each reported as counts of falsified / proven / unknown properties plus wall-clock time, with per-domain and per-branching-strategy rows. Two synthetic-scale checks are printed alongside: the number of reachability analyses each strategy needs on the mooring-line example, and average single-analysis time (about 0.006 s for zonotopes, 0.03 s for constrained zonotopes). External anchors are VNN-Comp 2024 placement, a soundness benchmark on which PyRAT is one of two tools assessed correct, and two industrial deployments (Airbus int8 quantised networks, and 224x224 classifiers across large projects). All timings are single-threaded on one CPU/GPU pair; the authors note VNN-Comp properties were re-perturbed with an unknown seed, so their numbers are not the competition's.

## What they found
Branch-and-bound turns inconclusive analyses into verdicts but costs three orders of magnitude in time: on LinearizeNN, plain zonotopes prove 3 of 60 properties in 11.67 s while zonotopes or constrained zonotopes with input splitting prove 58 of 60 in about 123 s and 192 s. Which branching wins depends on the ratio of input dimension to unstable ReLU count — inputs are few on LinearizeNN and ACAS-Xu (up to 500 unstable ReLUs), so input splitting dominates there (19 proven for ReLU splitting against 58), whereas ReLU splitting is the only thing that works on the high-dimensional CIFAR-100 case (8 proven without branching, 32 with). Constrained zonotopes consistently beat plain zonotopes in precision when branching on input, needing far fewer splits (445/2967/607/557 against 879/10113/1369/1763 on the mooring example) yet sometimes running slower overall because each analysis costs five times more. Hybrid zonotopes, which represent a piecewise-linear network exactly, are the fastest on the small example (6.28 s for all four properties, over ten times faster than branching) and then fail to scale — they prove 11 of 60 on LinearizeNN, worse than constrained zonotopes on ACAS-Xu, and time out on every CIFAR-100 property. On CIFAR-100 fewer than half the properties are settled at all. Against the competition winner, PyRAT verifies small instances quickly but accumulates roughly 500 more timeouts, 400 of them on genuinely safe properties.

## Limits and open problems they name
Exact representation transfers all the hardness to a MILP solver, so it only scales on small networks. The branch-and-bound heuristics are acknowledged as crude: the node-influence estimate ignores the effect of a split on later ReLUs and on other constraints, and improving it is named as future work. ReLU-based branching is the bottleneck on large inputs. Architectures beyond feedforward/convolutional/residual (RNNs, transformers) are future work, as are embedded and mixed integer/float networks. Soundness in real arithmetic is lost on GPU and in fast modes. The tool is closed-source, released under academic licence only.

## What we take from it
- **Method (inventory):** this is the empirical answer to the question the last four runs circled — practitioners do not select a domain per network; they *stack* them. Boxes are always on, not because they are tight but because they are exact at ReLU, and relational domains are intersected with them layer by layer. That is a design principle for us: keep one cheap exact instrument and one expensive relational instrument and intersect at every layer, rather than picking the best single representation.
- **Measure (the decision column):** every table prints four outcomes and prints them separately — proven, falsified, unknown, timed out. The lesson is that "falsified" is not a domain-precision statistic (a sharper domain finds more counter-examples, so falsification count moves with the domain in the *opposite* direction from what a naive reading expects), which is a warning against ever collapsing this to a single accuracy.
- **Warning (soundness is a mode):** the tool can be run fast-but-unsound, or exact-but-slow, and the paper says so plainly. Any guarantee we report needs its arithmetic semantics named alongside it.
- **Framing:** the branch-and-bound choice is a *ratio* rule — cheap per-node splitting of inputs when the input is low-dimensional, splitting the nonlinearity when inputs are high-dimensional — which is a reusable heuristic for any account that refines a region of the input space rather than a set of components.
