# When Every Choice Is Correct: A Causal Study of Numerical Preference in Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.06025
- **Authors / organisation:** Hongnian Huang (Department of Mathematics and Statistics, University of New Mexico); Hongliang Liu (independent researcher)
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06025
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
A model asked to pick any integer in 1-30 has thirty equally valid answers, yet it reliably favours some. The authors treat this as a clean stand-in for every choice among functionally equivalent options - tool selection, unspecified programming language - where usefulness and familiarity are tangled and the cause of a preference is hard to isolate. Because validity is checkable and the output is one or two tokens long, an intervention can change the chosen answer while leaving the constraint satisfied, and the authors can watch a single edit propagate from one position to the next. Their question is threefold: how an activation edit redistributes probability over the candidates, how its influence survives across a token boundary that the edit never touched, and whether later computation transforms the influence it produced. The larger ambition is to connect a controlled, imposed preference change to something closer to explaining a natural preference, and they are explicit that the second half is not achieved.

## What they studied
Three related Qwen3 checkpoints - Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen3-8B - with a five-checkpoint search that also included Qwen3-14B and GPT-OSS-20B, where a single-unit selection criterion failed. The unit of analysis starts as a single MLP intermediate activation (the scalar just before the down projection) and then widens to KV-cache entries at one position, attention-head output slices, and late MLP output vectors. Prompts are English number requests over small integer intervals (1-10, 1-20, 1-30, 1-35, 1-40, 1-50, 1-100), with generated arithmetic, copy and comparison tasks used only as protected-behaviour controls. The main candidate readout is an event probability: mass on the canonical digits of a number followed by a non-digit, renormalised over the interval, deliberately distinct from parsed generated replies and from first-digit-only scores.

## How they did it
Candidate neurons in the last quarter of decoder layers were nominated by two first-order screens - a perturbation-probing score that couples an activation displacement to the first-digit unembedding contrast, and a gradient score on the complete-numeral log-margin - with signed-mean aggregation across templates; the top twenty from each branch were pooled and reranked, giving twenty candidates. Survivors were screened by zeroing: the edit had to leave the pooled baseline modes intact at three intervals. The winner for 4B was L29:8172, ranked first by gradient and 31st by perturbation probing. A frozen directional panel then edited two sites (the winner and a separately retained L34 candidate) with fixed positive and negative doses, each paired with a residual-write-matched control placed in the same layer by a seeded draw, predicting the sign of the 17-versus-23 log-margin in twelve historical templates plus six extensions, with effects under 0.05 nat counted unresolved. Distributional work applied the same dose across contexts and compared per-number response profiles, a leave-one-out median estimate, and a KL chain-rule split into leading-digit branch mass and within-branch conditional redistribution, with a branch oracle as a ceiling. The continuation study used one prompt selected post hoc because its positive edit flipped 17 to 15 while the greedy first digit stayed 1; the source hook was removed before the model processed that digit, then source-position KV entries for layers 30-35 were swapped window by window, values separated from keys, L33 attention outputs split into one nominated head and its complement, and L34-L35 MLP outputs clamped at recipient values to test whether downstream responses transform the reader contributions. Natural-context transfer replaced the selected activation with values recorded under different instructions or histories and scored donorward progress by Jensen-Shannon divergence, alongside a matched instruction sweep of scalar versus whole-residual replacements. A twelve-wording fresh screen with fixed eligibility rules and a single new-prompt validation panel bound reusability.

## What counts as an explanation here
A conditional, partial causal account of one imposed preference change: which cached state carries a pre-answer edit across an unchanged token, which reader consumes it, and how later modules change the relative weight of two readers. It is a set of selective interventions with named positions, denominators and endpoints, explicitly not a faithful or minimal circuit - the authors say so repeatedly and use the field's faithfulness/completeness/minimality vocabulary only to say they have not demonstrated those properties. No symbolic representation of the intended number is claimed. The distributional half adds a decomposition and a predictive estimator, but the authors label the estimator retrospective: it is fitted on related templates and has no prospective validation. The most predictive object in the paper is not the explanation but a bound - the branch oracle's 40.9% ceiling - which says what no leading-digit controller can ever explain.

## How they judged success
Six separate endpoints, each kept apart: the sign of a frozen directional prediction with a 0.05-nat resolution floor; sampled reply fractions equal to 17 across 32 replies per eligible prompt with template-bootstrap intervals; retrospective KL and total-variation prediction error against an unedited baseline; the size of an intervention's effect on a next-token log-margin; full-cache transport as an implementation positive control with identity tolerances; and donorward progress against a natural-contrast denominator. Controls are extensive and specific: a residual-write-matched neuron in the same layer, self-replacement identity cells, 100 random neurons matched on down-projection column norm, head 0 as a head control, a frozen write cap at 10% of the recipient MLP output norm, natural-donor arms, and a shared hardcoded analysis in FP64 with FP32 confirmation of the principal results. Where inference is invalid they flag it rather than pooling - prompts sharing templates are not independent, the two extension cohorts are dependent conditions, and the eighteen-prompt panel is eighteen prompts and not ninety independent cells.

## What they found
Zeroing the selected neuron raised sampled 17 replies on new wordings in all three checkpoints, from 0.02 to 0.77 (4B), 0.00 to 0.69 (2507) and 0.15 to 0.95 (8B), against 100 norm-matched random neurons whose best increase was 0.003, 0.000 and 0.001. The frozen directional panel met its predicted sign on 70 of 72 target doses including 22 of 24 on the extensions, with mean absolute effects of 0.636 nat at the 4B site against 0.060 for its control, and 0.317 against 0.007 at the L34 site - but the L29 control itself met the directional criterion on 20 of 36 doses, so the sign test alone does not separate the target from its matched control at that site. A shared response profile fits the distribution well and still misses the answer: the leave-one-out median reduces pooled KL prediction error by 89.0% with a median per-prompt reduction of 96.3%, while the selected switch case retains a predicted 17-versus-15 margin of +0.110 nat where the observed margin is negative, and a branch-only comparator reaches 38.5% against an oracle ceiling of 40.9%. In the selected continuation, the source edit moves the post-1 next-token margin from +0.389778 to -0.029976 nat and the reply from 17 to 15; replacing all affected L30-35 cache entries reproduces the continuation exactly, the L32-33 window carries about 68% of the margin difference in each direction, values carry about 54% of the movement against 14% for keys, and L33 head 26 supplies roughly 82% of the all-L33 effect but only 36% of the source movement - while replacing the complement also crosses the boundary and restores 17, so the behavioural pivot is not unique to the nominated head. Similar immediate reader contributions do not stay similar: the L33 output perturbations are 1.739 and 1.733 in norm with immediate readout contributions of +0.02237 and +0.02322 nat, yet final margin movements are +0.15263 and +0.03374 nat, a ratio near 4.52; jointly clamping L34-L35 MLP outputs collapses the head-minus-complement gap from +0.118896 to +0.002480 nat while both interventions still yield 17, and in a reused wording the clamp reverses the sign of the gap from +0.044605 to -0.009998. Single-MLP clamps show the same component changing roles with the background: holding L34 strengthens the complement's effect to +0.062106 while holding L35 weakens it to +0.010002. On a new prompt the edit moves the margin from 10.930855 to 10.570538 - only 3.3% of the starting margin - and every continuation stays 17, and the freshest twelve-wording screen yielded one eligible context in which the same clamp reduction recurred (gap +0.147114 to +0.010330 nat) with all continuations unchanged. Natural transfer is weak on average and heavy-tailed: scalar swaps progressed 0.009, 0.017 and 0.001 against natural total-variation contrasts of 0.66, 0.65 and 0.71, with 7-13% of swaps moving more than 0.1 TV, while whole-residual replacement reached 0.42 and 0.35 against -0.06 and -0.04 for scalar swaps on a matched instruction sweep.

## Limits and open problems they name
The answer switch is a single prompt chosen near a 17/15 tie (baseline margin 0.390 nat against a median of 19.775 across the panel), so its frequency is unmeasured and the account is an existence example. Prompt coverage is one eligible wording in twelve for the downstream test and one new prompt for validation; eleven contexts have no intervention evidence. The head control was not magnitude-matched (about 4.4% of the candidate's displacement), so specificity of the head result is unresolved, and complementary components remain influential. Source, cache and head fractions use different denominators and must not be multiplied into a path probability. Single-MLP readouts are reconstructions rather than new generated answers, and a negative pairwise margin does not establish a completed 15. The origin of the natural 17 preference and the upstream inputs that recruit the source neuron are open; the authors decline to claim a minimal or exclusive circuit. The earlier interval predictor's full records are unavailable, so its summaries stay historical. The 2507 and 8B coordinates retain a magnitude-based selection procedure that was not repeated under the corrected nomination.

## What we take from it
A measure and a warning. The measure: an effect ratio must be reported against two different denominators on the same figure - here the nominated head is 82% of its layer's readers but 36% of the source movement - because a reader who sees only the first number believes the pathway is largely localized when the second says most of the movement is elsewhere. The warning: near a decision boundary, the same measured intervention produces different answers at different doses (+0.014328 nat yields 17 and -0.004700 nat yields 15 in one prompt) while a much larger identical movement on a held-out wording changes nothing because it consumes 3.3% of a margin of 10.930855. For our predict-then-intervene model this is a hard constraint on any claim of the form "removing unit u flips behaviour": the starting margin is a covariate that must be printed with every flip count, and a flip is only informative when the intervention's size is compared against that margin rather than against another intervention. Second, the paper's KL split gives a reusable accounting for any intervention whose effect has a routing part and a within-class part - an oracle bound of 40.9% prices the routing-only family before any predictor is trained, which is a cheap way to retire a whole modelling class. Third, the clamp result is the cleanest demonstration the folder has that a component's signed effect is a function of the computational background: holding L34 changes the complement's effect in one direction or the other depending on whether L35 is also held. Any of our own component-effect tables that report single intervention sizes without stating the background conditions under which they hold will be wrong in sign somewhere.
