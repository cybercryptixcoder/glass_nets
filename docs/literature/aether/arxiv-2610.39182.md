# Reasoning or Pattern Exploitation? Mechanistic Insights from RL-Trained Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.39182
- **Authors / organisation:** Aishik Nagar (Carnegie Mellon University); Cheston Tan (Institute of High Performance Computing, A*STAR; Centre for Frontier AI Research, A*STAR)

- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39182

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards is routinely read as evidence that a model has acquired reasoning, because scores on the training task leap. The authors want to know whether that leap is the acquisition of higher-order structure or a sharper use of what was already sitting in the prompt. To make the question answerable they pick a domain where the two hypotheses predict different things and where ground truth is exhaustively computable, and they watch the representation *across* training rather than only at the end. The larger goal is a template: an interpretability-first design for asking "did this training build a capability or reveal a shortcut" in any closed domain.

## What they studied
Three instruction-tuned small models — Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct — trained with GRPO on Tic-Tac-Toe, in two objectives (predict any legal move; predict a minimax-optimal move) and two data splits (random, and a canonical-symmetry split that holds out whole rotation/reflection classes). The unit of analysis is the **checkpoint**: eight saves every 300 steps to 2,240 steps, eleven of twenty-eight layers read at the last prompt token, an SAE trained per layer on a generic corpus (C4-10k) rather than on in-domain activations. Hypotheses are fixed in advance as seven families: three prompt-level (whose turn it is, game progression, line purity as a board-shape template discovered by cluster mining) and four higher-order (strategic situation, symmetry class, legality, best-move correctness).

## How they did it
1. Build the full state space by depth-first enumeration (5,478 reachable boards out of 3⁹ assignments, 765 symmetry classes), label every state with exhaustive minimax, and also construct 5,000 unreachable boards as an out-of-distribution set.
2. Train with GRPO on equally weighted legality, format-tag and correctness rewards; a supervised baseline was attempted and abandoned because the models repeated the prompt instead of learning continuations.
3. Fit one SAE per (model, layer) on generic text, reconstruct activations through a feature mask, project with PCA and t-SNE, and cluster; compute a one-way ANOVA F on the principal embedding axis for each hypothesis, plus Cramér's V and adjusted mutual information on the cluster-by-hypothesis contingency table.
4. Repeat every hypothesis test with probes on the raw residual stream (logistic regression, LDA, and a one-hidden-layer MLP) with no SAE, no projection and no clustering in the path, scored by five-fold cross-validated macro AUC, and with 999 label permutations per comparison.
5. Report a projection-free twin of the ANOVA: the share of raw variance explained by class means along the first principal component.
6. Run causal interventions: activation patching on 100 minimal board pairs (identical except one relocated piece, non-terminal, unique differing optima) overwriting the donor's residual stream at the answer position, at several layers; and difference-in-means steering along the turn direction at six layers and several magnitudes.
7. Test behavioural symmetry equivariance over all 5,478 states (do symmetry-related boards get corresponding moves?), compare training against held-out state accuracy, and run a whole-game arena against untrained and frontier models.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **claim about what training changed**, supported on two levels at once: a geometric reading of the representation (which axis organises the state manifold, and by how much) and a causal reading (where in depth a board's content actually decides the emitted move). It is explicitly partial: the object explained is the *difference* between checkpoints, not the mechanism of the move itself, and the hypothesis set is the authors' rather than the model's. Nothing here predicts an unseen intervention from weights; the predictive content is the ordering prediction that prompt-visible structure should be amplified and search-like structure should not.

## How they judged success
Behavioural: best-move accuracy per state on both splits and four input conditions (natural language, natural language with player symbols swapped for random characters, ASCII board, ASCII plus swap), chance-corrected skill against a uniform random legal-move policy, and per-position breakdowns by branching factor, move multiplicity and game phase. Representational: ANOVA F, Cramér's V and AMI on the SAE-plus-t-SNE geometry; probe AUC with folds and permutation nulls; class-mean variance share on the first principal component of raw activations. Causal: count of the 100 pairs flipped to the donor optimum, against an unpatched rate, a random-unrelated-donor control, and the same patch at layers that should not matter; steering flips between the X and O move-token ranges, with norm-matched random directions at extreme magnitude as the disruptive control. Behavioural abstraction: symmetry equivariance with Wilson intervals, with the unique-optimum subset carrying an exact 100% ceiling.

## What they found
- **The behavioural gain is real and the mechanical gain is absent.** GRPO takes best-move accuracy from 8.0% off-the-shelf to 71.6% (from base) and 75.6% (warm-started from a legal-move checkpoint) on natural-language prompts for Qwen2.5-1.5B, and legal-move accuracy to 100%; arena play eliminates forfeits entirely (0 against 121 for the untrained model), reaches 77% per-move minimax optimality, and draws all sixteen games against a frontier flash-tier model.
- **Prompt-level structure is amplified; higher-order structure is not.** At layer 12 of Qwen2.5-1.5B the turn-identity F falls 5,285 → 316 (17×) while game progression rises 274 → 2,330 (8.5×); strategic situation moves 82 → 194 and symmetry 26 → 12, and the authors state plainly that every F in the table is nominally significant at p < 10⁻⁶⁸ because the sample is all 5,478 states, so the argument rests on relative magnitudes and on the effect sizes (turn V 0.70 → 0.36, AMI 0.33 → 0.07; progression V 0.48 → 0.62, AMI 0.13 → 0.22; strategic V 0.24 → 0.22). The projection-free statistic reproduces the direction without any embedding: turn's class-mean variance share on PC1 falls 0.151 → 0.062 while progression holds near 0.20.
- **The SAE geometry is redundant with a probe on raw activations, and the probe says the concepts were already there.** Turn, progression and legality are at AUC 1.00 in the *base* model at every checkpoint of all three models; strategic situation is 0.686 base against 0.684 final (Llama-3.2-1B 0.65–0.70 flat, Qwen2.5-0.5B 0.61–0.65 flat), and an MLP probe recovers at most 0.06 more without moving across training. So training neither added nor removed the information — it changed which features dominate.
- **The decision is late, not mid-layer.** Patching at layer 25 of 28 flips 66 of 100 pairs to the donor's optimum, against 10 unpatched, 12 for a random unrelated donor, and no effect at layers 13, 17 or 21 (p < 10⁻¹⁴ for the 66-versus-12 two-proportion test). Steering along the turn direction never flips output between the X and O move-token ranges at any layer or magnitude, while norm-matched random directions at extreme magnitude do disrupt — the reorganised mid-layer axis is not what produces the move.
- **The model does not use symmetry at all.** Across all symmetry-related board pairs the predicted moves correspond only 56.7% of the time on training symmetry groups and 57.7% on held-out ones (random split 56.1%), and 60.0% / 62.7% on the subsets where each board has a unique optimum and a symmetry-respecting policy must score 100%. Held-out groups are no worse than seen ones, which rules out the split design as the explanation.
- **Brute accuracy on "easy" positions is a chance artefact.** Low-branching positions look better (83.1% against high-branching 63.6%) but the chance levels are 76.0% and 39.2%, so chance-corrected skill is 29.7% low-branch versus 40.2% high-branch. What is learned instantly is terminal-state recognition: 97.8% already at the first checkpoint.
- **Representation and policy come apart out of distribution.** Unreachable boards are separated from legal ones by probes at AUC 1.00 at every layer, and the policy still emits ordinary legal-looking continuations on them; in the SAE geometry illegal boards sit next to legal clusters matched by line pattern rather than by legality.
- **Modality is the soft spot.** ASCII boards cost the strongest model 40 relative points on best-move (73% → 44% on the reported comparison), ASCII plus symbol swap is worse still, and the base model's turn macro-pattern is absent under ASCII in the SAE reads.
- **Chain-of-thought is ungrounded even when the answer is right.** The paper's rollouts show a correct minimax-optimal move accompanied by a verbalised board description that misstates piece counts and row contents, and a success case whose stated winning diagonal does not exist — a verbalised plan with a false premise.

## Limits and open problems they name
Scope is deliberately narrow: 0.5–1.5B instruction-tuned models, GRPO only, one solvable game; no claim about frontier RLHF, PPO/DPO, or open-ended reasoning. Representations are read at one token position (last prompt token) and eleven of twenty-eight layers, sampled every 300 steps, so circuits built transiently inside a reasoning trace, at an unsampled layer, or reversing between checkpoints are invisible. The hypothesis set H1–H7 is closed and authored by them, so negative results are statements about those seven hypotheses; line purity was not pre-specified and emerged from cluster mining. The SFT baseline failed outright and was dropped, so the comparison against supervised training is only to an off-the-shelf baseline. They note that with 80% of 5,478 states in training, memorisation and abstraction are hard to separate in principle, and answer it with convergent indirect evidence rather than a direct test.

**Limits I would add, marked as mine:** the four-way split of "what counts as higher-order" is doing heavy work — symmetry is measured behaviourally but symmetry-*class* only as a 0.70 pair-separation score that the paper itself says partly reflects shared piece counts and line patterns, so H4 is close to unfalsifiable as constructed. The symmetry-split arm is reported as "learning curves closely track", which means the design that would isolate symmetry use was run and produced no separation, but the numbers behind "closely track" are figures, not cells. The eleven sampled layers are unevenly spaced (4, 8, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 20, 24) — dense exactly where the mid-layer claim lives and sparse at depth, so "early and late layers stay comparatively stable" is asserted over four late layers of which three are adjacent. The per-position table is printed raw with the chance correction deferred to prose, which is the right call but leaves the branching gradient unquotable as a table. And the difference between "training cannot help because the information is at ceiling" and "training was not given a signal that would require search" is not separated: the GRPO reward here is correctness of a single move, so the two explanations for the missing strategic abstractions are both consistent with every measurement shown.

## What we take from it
- **Method — score a property by its prominence, not only its decodability.** Every predictor we built would be tested by whether a probe can read a variable; this paper shows a case where the probe is saturated at AUC 1.00 in the untrained model and the interesting quantity is which axis of the state manifold the variable organises. Two cheap statistics make it usable: the class-mean variance share on the first principal component, and the same F statistic computed on each layer and each checkpoint so the trajectory is a curve rather than two endpoints. This is the trajectory-level companion to this folder's entry 206 result that a per-unit series is mostly shared trend — here the trend is a training-time reorganisation, and the perturbation control is the untrained checkpoint, which the paper has for free.
- **Method — the pipeline and the probe must be run as two arms of one test.** The SAE + t-SNE + clustering path says progression separation grows 8.5×; the raw-activation probe says progression is at 1.00 before training ever starts. Both statements are true and only the pair is informative: the first is about organisation, the second about recoverability, and a predictive model that reports only the first will mistake a rearrangement for a discovery. Appending a no-pipeline probe arm to any dictionary-based readout costs one logistic regression per layer per checkpoint.
- **Warning — a saturated statistic cannot show change, and the tell is a probe at ceiling.** Turn identity is 1.000 at every checkpoint of every model, so no experiment of this design can measure an improvement in turn processing even if one occurred; the paper is correct to move the claim to prominence, but the same trap is waiting in every probe battery we run on an easy variable. The design rule: choose variables with headroom for the trajectory claim, and report the ceiling value explicitly so a reader knows which column can move.
- **Framing — "the representation is there and the policy is not using it" is a two-instrument claim, and this is its cleanest instance yet.** Illegal boards at AUC 1.00 with ordinary legal continuations, and strategic information present but flat across training, are both dissociations between what is decodable and what the output depends on; the causal arm is what says where the decision actually lives (layer 25 of 28, with the mid-layer axis steering-inert). This extends this folder's value–policy dissociation entry (arxiv-2611.10457) from agents to a fine-tuning trajectory: the same 2×2 logic, applied to a training curve instead of a single model.
- **Dataset — the canonical-symmetry split and the behavioural equivariance test are free abstractions checkers.** Holding out entire symmetry classes and then measuring whether predicted moves correspond under the transformation, with a unique-optimum subset that has an exact 100% ceiling, is a ground-truth test we can copy wherever the task has a known group of invariances. It costs no training and it produced the paper's sharpest negative result (56–58% equivariance).
- **Warning — the trace is not the computation.** Correct minimax moves narrated over false board descriptions is the strongest version in this folder of the reason to score the internal readout rather than the rationalisation, and it was obtained on a model small enough that nothing about scale can excuse it.
