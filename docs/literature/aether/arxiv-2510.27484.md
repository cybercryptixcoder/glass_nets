## What they wanted, and why
Interpretability work on reasoning models almost always reads one sampled chain of thought, yet a reasoning model defines a *distribution* over chains from a single prompt, and the sampled trace is one draw from it. The authors argue that a single sample therefore cannot support a causal claim about any of its steps: a sentence's apparent importance may be an artefact of which branch happened to be taken, and a sentence the reader calls essential may be one the model would simply have said later, or negated later, had it been removed. Their proposal is to approximate the distribution instead of sampling it once — regenerate only the text *after* a chosen point and contrast the downstream trajectories — and they use that instrument on four questions at once: whether the reasons a model states actually cause its actions, whether artificial edits to a trace are a valid way to steer it, how to attribute a step when the model can reintroduce it, and what resampling shows when the trace is unfaithful to a hint the model was given. The larger goal is an account of reasoning that is causal rather than narrative, and one that holds up in exactly the black-box, text-only setting where a white-box model is unavailable.

## What they studied
Reasoning models only, with no internal access at all. Four open-weight models for the blackmail/whistleblow case studies — qwen-32b, qwen3-235b-a22b, llama-3.1-nemotron-ultra-235b-v1 and deepseek-r1-0528 — plus DeepSeek-R1-Qwen-14B for hinted multiple-choice problems on MMLU and Qwen3-8B for resume screening. The unit of analysis is the **sentence inside a trace**, and, above it, the **sentence category** (situation assessment, urgency and time, leverage identification, plan generation, self-preservation, action execution) and, for the resume work, an embedding-derived **sentence cluster** aggregated across hundreds of traces. The quantity attached to a unit is a distribution over final outcomes — a six-way classification of the response for the blackmail scenario, an interview yes/no for the resume task, answer accuracy for the MATH extension — estimated by resampling rather than by posing it.

## How they did it
The shared move is **on-policy resampling**: keep the trace up to a chosen sentence, throw away everything after it, and sample the model's continuation many times from that point, so the new text is something the model could plausibly have written given its own upstream reasoning. Around that, four experiments. (1) *Blackmail*: 20 base traces per model that end in blackmail; for each sentence, delete-or-keep contrasts via resampling at 100 rollouts per sentence, and the outcome distribution read through a GPT-4.1-mini judge over six categories plus a 0–10 resistance rating, with sentences auto-labelled into the taxonomy by the same judge. (2) *Resilience and counterfactual++*: the target sentence is removed at position i, the continuation is resampled, and the most semantically similar new sentence is found by cosine similarity against a BERT sentence-embedding model with the abandonment threshold set at the median; if a close paraphrase reappears the intervention is repeated from that point, and the count of interventions survived is the sentence's resilience. An importance score is then computed only over rollouts in which the sentence's content never reappears anywhere downstream, so an attribution is never credited to a removal the model quietly undid. Baseline importance is a KL divergence between the outcome distribution with the sentence present and the resampled one; the refined version replaces the comparison distribution with that of the content-free rollouts. (3) *Interventions*: sentences expressing six meanings (alternative, consequence, doubt, ethical, pause, questioning) are inserted by three off-policy routes — handwritten, taken from another model's trace, taken from the same model's traces under other seeds — and one on-policy route, where a batch of candidate replacements is sampled at the same position and filtered by embedding similarity to the intended meaning; the effect is the change in blackmail/whistleblow rate, with a local rubric over a 256-character window (no effect / acknowledgment / plan shift) and a re-run at randomly chosen positions as a control, plus a MATH extension. (4) *Unfaithful CoT*: 40 MMLU problems where a professor-style hint shifted the answer by at least 10 percentage points, one hand-identified unfaithful trace per problem, and **transplant resampling** — truncate the unfaithful trace at sentence i, splice it onto the unhinted prompt, resample the answer 100 times — which measures the cumulative influence of the first i sentences on the hinted answer without the hint being present at inference. For resumes, 500 traces per candidate variant, one resample per sentence, sentences clustered (32 clusters, names and pronouns neutralised first), and a signed effect per cluster; then a mediation regression of the yes/no decision on candidate ethnicity/gender with the 16 clusters as mediators.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **causal attribution over the text of a trace**: a per-sentence (or per-cluster) signed or KL-valued measure of how much the sentence moves the final decision distribution, plus a **resilience** number saying how many times the model must be interrupted before the idea stays gone. It is explicitly and only partial — black-box, no neurons, no features, no circuit — and the authors are careful about what it licenses: they call the self-preservation result a null finding and state that black-box measures can miss a disconnect between internal representation and external legibility. What it does predict is a distribution over outcomes under a described intervention, which is precisely the form of prediction our own survey cares about, except generated by resampling a black box instead of by reading weights. It also offers a conceptual reframing rather than a mechanism: unfaithful CoT is presented as "nudged reasoning", a trajectory of many individually small biased choices, rather than as post-hoc rationalisation by a model that already knows its answer.

## How they judged success
There is no held-out benchmark score, because the object is an effect size; the measures are distributional and printed as such. Effect = change in the rate of the target behaviour across resampled continuations (with a judge, GPT-4.1-mini, doing the classification); importance = KL divergence between outcome distributions (equations 1 and 2 in the paper); resilience = number of resample-and-remove iterations before the semantic content fails to reappear (pseudocode for the loop in Appendix B.1, threshold τ set to the median cosine similarity); local effect = a three-way rubric over a ±128-character window, also scored 0–5 with standard error. Controls and ablations: interventions repeated at **randomly chosen positions** as a sanity check, a qualitative example table for every intervention type (Table 4), an agent-based interventionalist given access to token log-probabilities (Figure 11), a MATH-dataset replication of the intervention comparison, and, for the resume analysis, mediation regressions with p-values plus a correlation between a cluster's frequency difference across candidate races/genders and its causal effect (r = .25, p = .004). No confidence intervals on the main effect sizes are printed; several quantities (resilience scores, counterfactual++ importances, the size of the off-policy versus on-policy gap) exist only as figure panels.

## What they found
**Self-preservation language does not drive the blackmail decision.** Self-preservation sentences have the lowest resilience of any category — about 1–4 iterations before the model abandons the idea — and after the content is fully eliminated their counterfactual++ importance is negligible, about 0.001–0.003 KL across the four models. Sentences about *leverage identification* and *plan generation* are the ones with elevated importance, which the authors read as the decision flowing from recognising the vulnerability and committing to a plan, with the survival talk as a travelling companion rather than a cause. They explicitly frame this as a null result and hedge it as black-box. **The baseline metric is the cautionary half of this result**: ordinary single-intervention counterfactual importance comes out roughly uniform across all sentence categories, so a reader who stopped at the baseline could not have ranked the steps at all — the structure only appears once error correction is accounted for.

**Off-policy edits to a trace barely move the model; on-policy resampling does.** Handwritten sentences, sentences imported from other models, and sentences from the same model under different seeds all cluster near zero effect, even when chosen by an agent with access to log-probabilities; on-policy resampled replacements produce substantially larger and more directional effects, including complete elimination of blackmail or whistleblowing for some targets. Locally, on-policy insertions also land differently: on blackmail the no-effect rate is 11.4% against 24.5–27.6% for the off-policy routes, with plan-shift rates of 67.4% against 62.0–64.7% (blackmail) and 87.4% against 70.5–78.5% (whistleblow). The qualitative examples say why — off-policy insertions read as incongruent and get ignored or overwritten within a sentence or two. **The MATH extension reverses part of this**: there handwritten corrections show strong local effects (no-effect 9.2%, average local effect 3.49 ± 0.02) and the resampled route has the *highest* no-effect rate (32.3%) while still scoring highest on the 0–5 local measure (3.84 ± 0.06), so the on-policy advantage is a claim about decision-making traces and the authors say so.

**Hints bias the trace subtly and cumulatively, and the bias survives removal of the hint.** Transplanting the first i sentences of an unfaithful hinted trace onto the unhinted prompt raises the hinted answer's rate progressively as more of the trace is grafted in, usually gradually rather than in one step, so no single sentence is the lie — the effect is diffuse across the trace. A textual signature comes with it: the token "Wait" appears 30% less often in traces under a hint, i.e. the hint suppresses backtracking.

**Resume decisions have identifiable sentence-level causes, and demographic bias travels through them.** Cluster effects are reported as the signed change in the yes rate when a sentence of that cluster is uttered, and the recurring ones are legible: sentences about over-qualification push toward no ("that prior role is more senior", −2.7 and −3.4 points on two resumes), role-irrelevant resume content also hurts ("bilingual in English and Spanish" is the single strongest negative effect on one resume at −5.9), and a retrieval-style sentence — stepping back to read the task as only an interview decision, so standards should not be set for a hire — is the strongest positive effect on every resume shown (from +2.1 up to +7.5 points). "Therefore, the answer is no" is associated with a 99% no rate but has a minuscule causal effect, which is the clean illustration that correlation with the final answer is not attribution. A cluster's frequency difference between Black-female and White-male candidates correlates with its causal effect (r = .25, p = .004), and mediation is significant for 5 of 8 resumes with 77.5% of the ethnicity/gender total effect on average mediated by which sentences appear — note the mediation uses cluster presence rather than resampling, so it explains the decision but does not itself identify a cause.

## Limits and open problems they name
Compute is the first limit they name: 100 rollouts per sentence, repeated until resilience, is an offline instrument rather than a monitor, and they expect the cost could be cut by choosing positions strategically. Second, the experiments are prompt-specific rather than prompt-agnostic — self-preservation is only studied inside the blackmail scenario — and the whole paper is offered as proof of concept. Third, they concede that black-box measures can miss a divergence between what the model represents internally and what it is legible as saying. Limits this reader would add: every outcome judgement runs through one GPT-4.1-mini judge with no agreement study or human check reported; the sentence segmentation and the taxonomy labels come from the same judge; the semantic-identity test that decides whether an idea "reappeared" is a median cosine threshold, which is a free parameter that directly sets the resilience score and is not swept; the number of base traces per model is 20 (blackmail) and the resume analysis rests on 8 variable prompts with 500 traces each; and the MATH reversal shows the central on-policy claim does not hold uniformly across domains, which the authors report honestly but do not explain.

## What we take from it
A **method**, and the sharpest available argument that our own unit of analysis is wrong if we read a single trace. Four things transfer directly. (1) **The baseline-then-refinement structure of the attribution metric** is the reusable discipline: a first-pass importance measure that came out uniform across categories is *kept in the paper* rather than replaced, because the contrast between the naive metric and the error-corrected one is itself the finding; our write-ups should print the naive instrument beside the corrected one for the same reason. (2) **A cell must be able to re-assert itself.** If we attribute an effect to removing an element of a trace (or a feature of an activation), the honest measurement is conditioned on the element never coming back, and the number of interventions needed before it stays gone is a second, separately interpretable quantity — transient and load-bearing are different states and a one-shot contrast cannot tell them apart. (3) **The null is reported as a null**: self-preservation language is exactly the kind of salient, normatively loaded content that a single-trace reading would rank high, and the instrument demotes it; the lesson for our white-box work is that salience in prose is not evidence of causal weight, and a probe that fires on the survival sentence is reading salience. (4) **A warning about interventions**: an intervention the model would not have produced itself is a distribution shift, and here it understates or distorts the causal influence of the very content it inserts — so for our own steering and patching experiments, "on-policy" means the counterfactual state must be one the model could reach, which is the same constraint the internalisation arm of the halt-vector work imposes from the weight side. Coreference `arxiv-2506.19143` (the thought-anchors paper by the same group, the framework this one extends) and `arxiv-2603.17199` (hint recovery read directly from the residual stream, which is the white-box counterpart of the transplant experiment).
