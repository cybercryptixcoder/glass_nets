## What they wanted, and why

The observation motivating the paper is that the same request wrapped in a different envelope, padded with unrelated material, or lightly punctuated can switch on a different set of sparse features inside a language model, even though the thing being asked is unchanged. The authors call this *representation redistribution*: the sparse code moves, and the move has a stable shape. From that they ask two linked questions — which feature changes recur when a prompt is perturbed in related ways, and do those recurring changes actually do work in the downstream computation, or are they noise that merely repeats? The larger goal is to make a claim of the form "these latents responded repeatedly and their response mattered to the decision" that is earned on variants excluded from selection, rather than asserted from the same prompts that produced it. For us it is a candidate instrument for the folder's recurring problem: how to nominate a unit as relevant to a behaviour and then have any confidence the nomination travels.

## What they studied

The unit of analysis is a single pretrained SAE feature at a chosen residual-stream hook, studied as a change between a clean prompt's activation and a transformed variant's activation at a character-aligned target span (final non-special token overlapping the preserved phrase). The representation study covers 27 released SAEs across eight models — GPT-2 Small, Pythia-70M, Gemma 2 2B, Gemma 3 270M / 1B / 4B, Mistral 7B, Llama 3.1 8B — at three or four layers each, widths from 16,384 to 65,536, ReLU and JumpReLU architectures. Inputs are 1,000 English prompts generated before any SAE inference across 12 content categories, each presented under six transformation settings, five operators with three realizations each. The functional half swaps the representation corpus for 1,800 balanced WildGuardMix safety prompts and studies two frozen linear safety readouts on Gemma 3 4B layer 17 and Llama 3.1 8B layer 24.

## How they did it

The design has two stages and, crucially, a split between the variants used to select and the variants used to evaluate. In stage one they define the redistribution sensitivity score in three filters over the selection observations: a magnitude filter (mean absolute activation change, with opposing directions not allowed to cancel), a recurrence filter (the feature must respond, above a response threshold, in at least four of five operators), and a directional-consistency filter (the dominant direction must hold in at least 0.8 of the observations, with ties ineligible). The eligible features are ranked by magnitude, capped at a candidate pool of 400 and a support of 20, which yields a per-prompt support and a per-prompt, per-setting summary — MeanRSS — of how strong the supported responses are. Two propositions are proved about the selector: that it is the size-capped maximiser of the magnitude objective under the recurrence and consistency constraints, and that the finite-sample directional margin it enforces (at least four observations in the dominant direction, at most one opposed) cannot be reversed by changing or removing a single observation. Stage two holds the selected support and its directions fixed and evaluates them on disjoint realizations and, in a separate check, on a whole operator withheld from selection. For the functional half they fit an elastic-net logistic probe on standardised clean training activations, calibrate a threshold at 5% benign false-positive rate on clean validation, freeze everything, and then apply the six transformations to test prompts. Each probe coefficient gives an exact per-feature contribution to the margin shift; they decompose the shifted margin into a full erosion term, the part carried by the RSS support, and the omitted remainder, and define a retention fraction for the support, a residual margin that keeps the full clean margin but replaces full erosion with support erosion, and a readout counterfactual that restores the selected features' readout inputs to their clean values while leaving the language model untouched. Controls are: magnitude-only selection with the same support size, selection by the largest readout coefficients, a support selected for another transformation (mismatched), globally unstable features, and random features matched in size and candidate pool. Uncertainty is 5,000 bootstrap resamples clustered by base prompt.

## What counts as an explanation here

A compact support: a named, per-prompt set of roughly twenty latents together with a direction for each, which is claimed to be the piece of the sparse code that carries a given prompt transformation's effect on a fixed downstream computation. It is deliberately partial — the paper never claims the support is the whole change, and prints the retention fraction and the reconstruction of coefficient against the full logit change so the omitted part is visible. Its predictive content is explicit and checkable: the direction rule implies a sign for the readout contribution, and the clean margin plus the support's erosion should predict which prompts flip. The authors are careful that the counterfactual lives inside the readout only, not in the model's activations or generations.

## How they judged success

Seven measures, all with the selection/evaluation split enforced: (1) held-out response rate in the selected direction against magnitude-only selection; (2) mean absolute held-out contribution of support features against unselected features matched on readout coefficient magnitude, with the paired difference and an interval, plus the share of cases where the directional sign rule's prediction matches the observed contribution sign; (3) retention of absolute held-out contribution as the support budget varies, against mismatched supports and readout-aware selection; (4) Spearman correlation of selection-variant erosion with held-out erosion, and AUROC of erosion-based and residual-margin-based rankings for a defined "recurrent readout failure" (at least two of ten held-out margins negative, among cases with a correct clean prediction); (5) coefficient of determination of the support's reconstruction of the held-out logit change, and the share of transformed probe errors reversed by readout restoration; (6) capture of errors and harmful false negatives at a 20% offline review budget; (7) in the supplementary, split-half reliability, profile stability and support-overlap sensitivity to selection size, plus a selector-parameter ablation. Intervals throughout are bootstrap; no significance tests.

## What they found

The transformation profile is systematic: normalised MeanRSS is highest for added context (irrelevant 1.466, distractor 1.341) and wrappers (1.199, 1.159) and lowest for local edits (punctuation 0.525, typed lexical substitution 0.309), the context-and-wrapper mean exceeds the local-edit mean in all 27 SAEs with an average difference of 0.874, and after aggregation it holds for all 1,000 prompts, with 997 of 1,000 having every context or wrapper setting above both local edits. In raw terms Pythia layer 3 responds to irrelevant context at MeanRSS 8.32 versus 0.46 for a metalinguistic wrapper, and blocking attention to the inserted context drops the irrelevant and distractor responses to 5.5% and 9.6% of their original values. Persistence survives the split: the held-out response rate in the selected direction is 0.876 (Gemma) and 0.871 (Llama) against 0.816 and 0.794 for magnitude-only selection, and 0.952 against 0.758 for size-matched random supports in the representation study; absolute held-out response is 107.670 for RSS versus 5.797 for random. Functionally, RSS features carry a mean absolute held-out contribution of 0.2397 against 0.0221 for coefficient-matched unselected features in Gemma (0.3702 vs 0.0220 in Llama), larger in 96.9% and 98.0% of matched pairs, and the sign rule agrees with the observed held-out contribution sign in 97.4% and 97.1% of cases; against selection by the largest coefficients the means are 0.240 versus 0.110 and 0.370 versus 0.188. At a 20-feature budget RSS retains 0.430 and 0.562 of absolute held-out contribution (0.326 and 0.435 for mismatched supports), while readout-aware selection retains more than 0.9. On failure, clean margin alone gives AUROCs of 0.863 and 0.901, subtracting support erosion raises this to 0.935 and 0.958, and the full residual margin reaches 0.965 and 0.964; every case with negative full residual margin on the selection variants had recurrent failure on the held-out ones. Compact supports reconstruct the held-out logit change at a coefficient of determination of about 0.58 and 0.63, reverse 72.2% and 71.8% of transformed probe errors by readout restoration (mismatched 60.3% / 61.1%, globally unstable 6.7% / 2.3%, random 1.2% / 1.5% — and the corresponding reconstructions fall from 0.579 / 0.627 to 0.017 / 0.009 for globally unstable supports and 0.014 / 0.017 for random ones), and at a 20% review budget capture 89.2% / 92.8% of errors and 87.3% / 94.4% of harmful false negatives with median supports of 20 and 19 features against 415 and 862 nonzero probe coefficients.

## Limits and open problems they name

Two of the paper's own numbers cut against its headline. In the full risk table, MeanRSS on its own is barely a risk signal — failure AUROC 0.526 and 0.506 against 0.863 and 0.901 for plain clean margin — so the audit's strength comes from combining a ~20-feature support with the *full* clean margin, and the support-only residual margin's AUROC (0.935, 0.958) is not separated from the full-margin version by an interval in the main text. The probe AUROC stays above 0.90 under every transformation while 4.2–9.5% of decisions still flip, and the translation from a representation profile to a readout effect is not order-preserving: for Gemma the same context-versus-local-edit gap in margin erosion has opposite sign for benign and harmful prompts, and Llama's punctuation edits erode the most (0.459) while carrying the lowest normalised MeanRSS (0.567). The frozen-readout design is the enabling simplification and also the boundary: contributions are exact only for a linear readout, the authors note nonlinear downstream computations need higher-order interaction terms, and the counterfactual changes only the readout. Coverage is two safety probes at one layer each, six controlled transformations on synthetic generated prompts, and one prompt-generation model; the retrained-probe extension with MeanRSS-guided augmentation is reported as inconsistent — Gemma gains, Llama loses on clean, mean and worst-setting AUROC — and the authors say so.

## What we take from it

A method and a discipline. The method: score a unit by whether its *response to a controlled perturbation* recurs in a consistent direction, not by how strongly it fires in general — recurrence and directionality are cheap filters that cost the same forward passes as a magnitude screen and here buy a 0.06–0.08 lift in held-out directional persistence and a ten-fold-plus lift (10.8× and 16.8×) in realised contribution over coefficient matching. The discipline, which is the more transferable part: select on one set of variants and evaluate on disjoint ones, keep a mismatched-control arm (a support selected for a different perturbation) beside the random arm, because the mismatched arm is the one that shows how much of the effect is "any support of this size" — here 0.326 versus 0.430 retention, versus 0.014 for random. Second method: the residual-margin construction — take a baseline score that works (clean margin), and credit a component only by what it adds on top of it. That is exactly the shape we want for our white-box model's claims: a component earns its place by improving a prediction that already existed, not by being the best single ranking in a table. Warning: a representation-level profile is not a readout-effect profile, and the same transformation group can erode the margin differently by class; any of our reports that translates "this unit moved more" into "this unit mattered more" needs the signed decomposition (magnitude × coefficient × alignment) and the clean margin alongside.
