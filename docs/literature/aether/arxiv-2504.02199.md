# ESC: Erasing Space Concept for Knowledge Deletion

- **Key:** arxiv-2504.02199
- **Authors / organisation:** Tae-Young Lee, Sundong Park, Minwoo Jeon, Hyoseok Hwang, Gyeong-Moon Park (Lee and Hwang equal contribution; Park and Gyeong-Moon Park corresponding); Korea University and Kyung Hee University
- **Date:** 2025-04-03
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02199
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Machine unlearning as usually evaluated is measured against a retrained model and judged on logits or membership inference. The authors argue that this misses what a user actually asked for: a deployed model should lose the *ability* to use the forgotten knowledge, not just the label the head happens to emit. They show that the standard methods satisfy the usual metrics while the frozen encoder still carries the forgotten classes, so that retraining only a new linear head on that encoder recovers most of the forget accuracy. Their goal is therefore a stronger setting - they call it Knowledge Deletion - plus a metric that reads knowledge off the *feature extractor* rather than the head, plus a method that deletes it there. The larger aim is a removal guarantee that survives an adversary who has the features rather than the outputs. The paper is positioned against the retrained-model gold standard, which it cites as theoretically ill-posed for this purpose.

## What they studied

Image classifiers split into a feature extractor and a head. Three model/dataset pairs in the main table: All-CNN on CIFAR-10, ResNet-18 pretrained on ImageNet-1K fine-tuned to CIFAR-100, and a ViT pretrained on ImageNet-21K on Tiny-ImageNet. Extensions use ViT on ImageNet-1K and CUB-200-2011, All-CNN on the facial dataset Lacuna-10, and an application to a 3D-aware generative identity model (GUIDE with EG3D). The unit of analysis is the **pooled embedding feature vector** of the encoder, treated as a linear space: the object they examine is a *subspace* of that feature space, spanned by the top principal directions of the forgetting data.

## How they did it

Class-wise deletion: 10% of the classes are forgotten. Pass the forgetting training set through the frozen encoder to get a feature matrix, take its singular value decomposition, and keep the left singular vectors as an orthonormal basis of the feature space ordered by singular value. **ESC** simply drops the first p percent of those directions (p between 1 and 3 in practice, most stable at 1.7) and, at inference, projects every feature onto the span of the surviving directions before the unchanged head. So the unlearned extractor is a rank-reduction of the original one, no training, one forward pass. **ESC-T** keeps all directions but learns a multiplicative mask over the whole principal-direction matrix: initialise the mask to ones, optimise *only the mask* on the forgetting data with a penalised cross-entropy that is the negative of the ordinary CE whenever the model still predicts correctly (and zero otherwise), clamp mask entries to [0, 1] at every step, stop when every forgetting sample is misclassified or a step limit is reached, then binarise the mask at a threshold tau (0.7-0.8 empirically) and re-project. Evaluation is four-way: forget and retain, each on train and test splits; accuracy in the ordinary head-based sense and in their Knowledge Retention score, which freezes the unlearned encoder, attaches a randomly initialised linear head, trains it 10 epochs by SGD at learning rate 0.001, batch 64, on the *union* of forget and retain training data, and reports accuracy on the same four splits. Headline aggregates are the harmonic mean of (100 minus forget accuracy) with retain accuracy, computed separately for train and test. Membership inference and a Jensen-Shannon score against a randomly initialised network are reported as privacy arms. Baselines: negative gradient, random label, boundary shrink, layer attack unlearning (the four that use only forgetting data), plus fine-tune, Fisher forgetting, SCRUB, weight-sparsity unlearning and SalUn, which use the remaining data as well.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **direction**, in the plainest sense: a subspace of the feature space whose removal is supposed to delete the concept's separability. The claim is explicitly partial - they do not claim the concept's weights are gone, only that the model can no longer *combine* the surviving sub-features into an identity, because the line they removed is the line that carried the combination. There is no surrogate model and no circuit; the artifact is a projection matrix, which they present as something you can bolt on after a black-box encoder or fold into a released white-box one. ESC-T refines the same object from "drop whole directions" to "drop the important elements of each direction", so the explanation moves from a subspace to a sparse *subspace mask*.

## How they judged success

Two arms of their own invention plus conventional ones. Their Knowledge Retention score is the important one: it is a *linear probe* trained on all training data after deletion, which means success requires that no linear readout of the frozen features can separate the forgotten classes. They also compare four ways of averaging forget and retain accuracy (arithmetic, an exponential "unlearning score", geometric, harmonic) and argue for the harmonic mean on the grounds that the arithmetic mean sits at 50 for both a completely broken model and a completely undeleted one, while the harmonic mean collapses toward zero in both cases. On top of that: standard accuracy on the four splits, membership inference, the Jensen-Shannon zero-retrain score, run time, and a Grad-CAM and t-SNE read as qualitative evidence, with an ablation ladder over the mask loss, the clipping and the threshold.

## What they found

Their central diagnostic is that every baseline loses in the feature space even when it wins on logits. With a frozen unlearned ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100, retraining a head recovers 83.06% forget-train accuracy for the original model, 40.68% after fine-tuning, 86.68% after SalUn (which reports 0.76% forget accuracy through its own head), 52.39% after weight-sparsity unlearning and 81.81% after layer attack unlearning; ESC-T brings that to 8.59%. Layer attack unlearning, the strongest baseline, scores 96.62 on harmonic mean but its Knowledge Retention harmonic mean is 30.38 against ESC-T's 85.59 - the two arms disagree about the same model, and the paper's claim is that the second is the one the user asked about. On Tiny-ImageNet with ViT, the Retrain arm itself scores a Knowledge Retention harmonic mean of 49.15, below ESC's 97.19, which the authors explain by arguing that pretraining entanglement leaves the concept present in a model that never saw the forget classes. ESC-T does not dominate everywhere: in the random-data-forgetting table it is beaten by ESC on average gap (2.77 against 1.45) and it costs about thirteen times ESC's run time (29.47 seconds against 2.24), while ESC is reported as faster than the previous fastest baseline by a factor of 9.17. A Grad-CAM read on Lacuna-10 shows the baseline and the original attending to faces while both ESC variants attend to background. A p sweep from 1 to 90 percent shows deletion is flat and safe up to about 10 percent, usable to about 50, and destructive past 70.

## Limits and open problems they name

They name the deployment problem themselves: the projection is an extra layer after the encoder, trivially mergeable if the layer is a plain matrix, unresolved if the released artifact is the whole network. They name extension to diffusion and language models as untested, and say the latent space of generative models is more sensitive than a discriminative embedding. They treat incremental deletion as supported because projection matrices compose, which is true for ESC but not obviously for a mask learned per batch. Not named but visible in their tables: the threshold is "empirically found", there is no penalty anywhere for retained *readability* of the forget classes' surviving sub-features (only linear separability is destroyed), the whole measurement rests on a probe they trained 10 epochs without a stated budget for what a probe could otherwise learn, and the retrain-comparison is weakened by the fact that Retrain for ResNet-18 and ViT starts from pretrained weights, which the authors state.

## What we take from it

A **measure** first: the frozen-encoder probe trained on *all* training data is the cheapest screen in this whole folder for "is the internal object still there", because it is a linear readout of exactly the representation our white-box model claims to describe. Any deletion, edit or intervention we report should carry a companion number of the form "retrain a linear readout on the edited model's features - if it recovers the target, our model was still representing it". Their 86.68-versus-0.76 pair for one method on one model is the sharpest warning in the paper: the metric built into the method's own output and the metric built on the internal object disagreed by two orders of magnitude, and the internal one was right.

A **framing** second: rank-reduction of the feature covariance in the direction of the forgetting data, applied as an orthogonal projection, is a legitimate *white-box model of a concept* (a subspace hypothesis), and it composes - which is what makes it useful beyond unlearning. If our white-box model carries a subspace description of a concept, incremental evidence should update it by composition, and a probe should confirm that the composed subspace is not leaking.

A **warning** third: ESC's deletion is exactly their own central claim about entanglement and it is worth holding against the previous entry in this folder, which showed that a selected feature set can restore a confidently "deleted" concept. A projection removes the *linear* route through those directions; it leaves the rest of the space untouched, and the paper itself concedes that the concept's sub-features survive and can be recombined. So "the concept is deleted" here means "no linear readout recovers it", not "no readout recovers it" - and the t-SNE note that the forget class's activations go close to zero is the giveaway that the paper is describing a resource the model *cannot use* rather than a concept that is *gone*.
