# Behavioral Compositionality of Sparse Autoencoder Representations

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.21261
- **Authors / organisation:** Haotian Ma; Ziye Mei; Frederic Sala — Department of Computer Sciences, University of Wisconsin–Madison
- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21261
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Sparse autoencoders cut activations into sparse, linearly decoded latents, and the field routinely combines those latents into multi-feature explanations and multi-direction steering recipes. The premise behind every such combination is additivity: if the effect of each feature is known, the joint effect is the sum. The authors point out that a linear decoder only guarantees additivity for *reconstruction* of the activation, not for what the model then does with it — the remaining layers can make feature effects reinforce, suppress or depend on one another. Their goal is to measure that gap directly, as a function of the intervention order required to close it, and to separate compositionality that belongs to the SAE's coordinate choice from compositionality imposed by the downstream computation. The larger aim is to make "are these features jointly usable?" a measured quantity rather than an assumption.

## What they studied
Seven model/SAE pipelines and six scalar behaviours. The three main pipelines are Gemma-3 4B (Gemma Scope 2), Mistral-7B (Engels et al. residual SAEs) and Llama-3.1 8B (Llama Scope); the wider sweep adds GPT-2 Small with Bloom's SAEs, Pythia-70M with Marks' SAEs, and two smaller Gemma-3 models (270M and 1B). Behaviours are simple copying, subject–verb agreement, gendered pronoun preference, CounterFact, IOI, and greater-than comparison, each written as a logit difference or target-token logit at a fixed layer. The unit of analysis is a *coalition* of the top-K active SAE contributions at one layer and token position (K = 16 in the main analysis), and the outcome is the downstream behaviour after the coalition is retained or removed. A separate experiment applies the same machinery to sixteen published CorrSteer steering directions on Llama-3.1 8B over MMLU-Pro.

## How they did it
The intervention is a subset patching game, not a reconstruction test: for prompt x, the decoded contribution of each of the top-K active latents is written as its activation times its decoder direction, and for a retained set S the activation is rebuilt as the fixed remainder (everything else, including the SAE reconstruction residual) plus the sum of the contributions in S. The full set reproduces the original activation exactly, so the zero point of the game is the real model, and only the selected contributions vary. That patched activation is propagated through the frozen upper layers and a scalar readout gives g(S). They then fit two function classes on sampled coalitions: order 1 (individual feature effects only) and order 2 (individual plus all pairwise terms), by least squares on 256 training coalitions and evaluation on 128 held-out ones, all drawn from the uniform distribution over the 2^K subsets. The central statistic is the SAE Additivity Residual — the behaviour-weighted squared error of the best order-r projection, normalised by the intervention-game variance — so SAR at order 1 is the share of behavioural variation that individual features cannot account for, and the difference between order 1 and order 2 is the share recovered by pairs. The coordinate-choice control is the clever part: they replace the SAE basis by a random orthogonal rotation of the *same* selected subspace that also preserves the all-ones direction (hence the total contribution), so the subspace, the total contribution and the downstream model are all held fixed and only the coordinate system changes. Additional analyses sweep layer depth, vary K from 6 to 20, halve the removed contributions, change the inclusion probability, and pool six matched SAEs at one Gemma-3 1B site to compare SAR against reconstruction quality.

## What counts as an explanation here
A low-order set function: an explicit model of the behaviour as a function of which features are present, with the *order* of interaction required being the measured object. The explanation is partial by construction — an order-2 approximation of an interaction game that can be arbitrarily high-order — and it is explicitly behavioural rather than representational, since what is being approximated is the model's output after the rest of the network runs. The paper also makes the interaction structure exactly identifiable: because memberships are independent Bernoulli draws, the residual at order r is the normalised energy of all higher-order interaction terms, so an additive explanation is not just a good-enough fit but the claim that every interaction of order above r is negligible.

## How they judged success
Three families. (1) Residual magnitude: SAR at order 1 and order 2, with prompt-level medians and bootstrap intervals, validated against synthetic games with known residuals (0.1, 0.3, 0.5 recovered exactly, third-order game giving 0.180 then 0.066) and against *affine controls* — a behaviour defined as a linear readout of the intervened activation, where the theory says SAR must be zero. (2) Predictive fidelity on held-out coalitions: Spearman rank fidelity between predicted and actual behaviour, sign error against the full-coalition behaviour, and selection regret (the normalised gap between the best coalition in the evaluation set and the coalition the model picks). (3) Controls that carry the argument: the rotation baseline for coordinate choice, a parameter-matched control that keeps the same number of second-order terms but replaces them with randomly chosen third-order terms, repeated fitting/evaluation splits, five fresh coalition draws per prompt, and a synthetic check that an additive game gives residuals at 1e-12.

## What they found
First-order additivity is never exact: median residual is positive in all 57 model/layer/behaviour combinations, but small — around 0.02 to 0.08 at the main layers — and affine controls sit at numerical zero (largest 1.55e-13), so the instrument is calibrated before it is used. The magnitude varies by behaviour and by model: simple copying and subject–verb agreement are consistently more additive than CounterFact in the three main pipelines. Learned SAE coordinates beat sum-preserving random rotations of the same subspace in every one of the 18 model/behaviour settings, e.g. 0.0218 versus 0.1196 for Mistral on subject–verb agreement, with the SAE below the rotation median on 92–100% of prompts in most cells. Residual size predicts explanation fidelity: rank fidelity falls and selection regret rises from the lowest to the highest SAR decile in all three models, and the prompt-level correlation between SAR and rank fidelity is negative in 17 of 18 settings. Pairs matter: adding all pairwise terms improves held-out rank fidelity in all 18 settings (mean 0.9782 to 0.9959), reduces ordering error in all 18, and cuts mean selection regret from 0.0272 to 0.0051 — and it beats the parameter-matched random-third-order control in all 18. Depth trends are mixed by cause: residual falls from early to late layers in 8 of 9 paired comparisons and the pairwise share rises in all 9, but random rotations show the same first-order decrease in 8 of 9, so part of the depth trend comes from the shorter remaining computation, while SAE coordinates still beat rotations at all 33 matched sites. In the steering experiment, modelling pairs raises rank fidelity for the aggregate MMLU-Pro accuracy game from 0.5874 to 0.7790, cuts ordering error from 0.1444 to 0.0606 and lowers the residual estimate from 0.6424 to 0.3891 — though per-domain results are heterogeneous, and rank fidelity actually gets *worse* with pairs in three of fourteen domains (economics, engineering, math), each by under a hundredth. Finally, reconstruction quality and behavioural compositionality are different things: pooling six matched SAEs at Gemma-3 1B layer 17, SAR correlates positively with sign error (0.269 to 0.430) and selection regret (0.199 to 0.235), while fractional variance unexplained is near zero or negative.

## Limits and open problems they name
The authors are explicit that behavioural compositionality is a property of the *selected contributions, readout and intervention distribution*, not of a dictionary in general, and they do not present it as global. The estimator is held out, so the order-2 residual carries finite-sample fitting error alongside structural residual — and in some MMLU-Pro domains the fitted pairwise model has a larger held-out residual than the first-order one, which is why they report negative pairwise shares without clipping and flag them as sampling artefacts rather than population facts. The order-2 coefficient count (137 for K = 16) grows quadratically and a full order-3 fit is not attempted. The theory of exact interaction identification rests on independent coalition sampling, which rules out the Shapley-style coalition weighting they discuss in an appendix. Adding pairs generally helps but does not make features independent, and no claim is made that a higher order would close the residual. The scope of the intervention game is the K-dimensional selected subspace, so compositionality is asserted for that coordinate space rather than for the model's behaviour in general.

## What we take from it
Three things, and the first is a hard requirement on our own account. (1) **We need an order parameter.** The claim that a set of features explains a behaviour is not one claim but a family of claims indexed by interaction order, and this paper supplies the honest way to state and price them: fit order r on sampled coalitions, evaluate on held-out coalitions, and report the residual as a *fraction of intervention-game variance*, which is scale-free and comparable across models and behaviours. Any predictive account we build should be able to say "order 1 suffices here, order 2 is needed there" with a number attached rather than asserting that features compose. (2) **The rotation baseline is the control our folder has been missing for coordinate claims.** Earlier entries in the folder run exactly this test in one form or another — random-basis rotations, untrained baselines, matched random third-order terms — and here the sum-preserving rotation of a fixed subspace isolates coordinate choice from subspace and from downstream computation in one stroke. It's cheap (an orthogonal matrix that preserves the all-ones direction, i.e. QR on the complement of the normalised ones vector) and it converts "our features are meaningful" into a paired comparison on 92–100% of prompts. We should adopt it verbatim for any claim that a chosen basis (SAE, probe basis, learned dictionary) is *better*, since it holds constant everything except the basis. (3) **Reconstruction quality is not explanation quality, and here that is measured rather than argued.** Six matched SAEs at one site give SAR around 0.2 to 0.4 correlations with joint-behavioural error while reconstruction error is flat or the wrong sign — so our evaluations should not use FVU or reconstruction loss as a proxy for whether a dictionary is usable for prediction, and a matched-SAE comparison is the way to establish that decoupling. Two smaller items travel: the behaviour-weighted ordering error is a *ranking* metric, not a magnitude metric, and it separates the two — rank fidelity was essentially unchanged from first to second order (0.978 to 0.996) while sign error and regret both roughly halved, which is a warning that a high Spearman means nothing about whether the wrong coalition gets chosen; and the paper's own depth result is a template for causal attribution of a trend, since the shared-across-coordinates part of the depth effect was found by running the rotation control *at every layer* rather than only at the analysed one. One warning for our intervention work specifically: the steering experiment shows that effects measured on individual directions do not sum to the effect of the combination — rank fidelity for the *aggregate* response is only 0.59 at first order — which is the strongest published statement in the folder that a steering recipe's parts need not add up, and that pairwise structure is the cheapest correction that meaningfully helps.
