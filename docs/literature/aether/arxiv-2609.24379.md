# Topographic Training Concentrates Causal Circuits Without Improving Neuron Monosemanticity

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.24379
- **Authors / organisation:** Gautam Ranka, Shubham Pandere, Aiden Ross D'souza; IvLabs, VNIT Nagpur, India
- **Date:** 2026-09-21
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24379
- **Kind:** paper (Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop, ICML 2026)
- **Read from:** full text (HTML build: abstract, SS1-SS5, references, Appendices A-G with Tables 3-9)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Mechanistic interpretability wants to decompose a network's computation into units a person can read, and the standing diagnosis is that individual neurons entangle many concepts. The standard remedies - sparse autoencoders, dictionary learning - are post-hoc: they learn to decode a frozen representation rather than pressuring the network to form a cleaner one. The authors ask whether a cheap training-time inductive bias, a smoothness penalty over the weights of spatially adjacent units (TopoLoss, following Khosla et al. 2025), can make the *existing* interpretability tools work better on the resulting model. The larger goal is a way to buy interpretability at training time, as a complement to, not a replacement for, post-hoc tooling.

## What they studied
ViT-S/16 trained from scratch on ImageNet-100 (126,688 train / 5,000 val images, 100 classes) at three penalty weights (alpha of 0.0, 0.1, 1.0) with three seeds each, nine checkpoints total, plus a broader sweep with one seed. TopoLoss is applied to the attention-projection weights of all twelve blocks, with units laid out on a square grid matching the head dimension. A lightweight 4-layer TinyViT (width 128, 4 heads) is trained under the same protocol as a scale probe. The unit of analysis moves between three levels: the **neuron** (class-activation entropy), the **SAE feature** (TopK SAE, k = 32, 2048 features, fit to layer-6 residuals), and the **causal cluster** (the top-16 most class-activated neurons treated as one set).

## How they did it
Three hypotheses are audited against the same checkpoints. H1 asks whether neurons become more selective, scored by a normalised negative-entropy selectivity over the 5,000 validation images (1.0 would be perfectly class-selective) at both the neuron level and the SAE-feature level. H2 asks whether the dictionary gets sparser, measured as mean active features per token (L0) and the fraction of features active on fewer than 0.1 percent of tokens, from one SAE per checkpoint at a single middle layer, with an appendix check that the effect is spread across layers 3-10 rather than localised. H3 asks whether a class's top units are jointly causally sufficient: for each of 20 randomly chosen classes and each of the three seeds they patch the top-16 unit activations from an image containing the target class into an image of another class, take the mean absolute logit change, and divide by the same quantity for a random set of the same size, so a ratio above one means the selected set carries disproportionate causal weight. The selection rule is by activation magnitude only, not by spatial contiguity, so they add a post-hoc check that these sets are in fact spatially clustered on the grid. They also confront the obvious confound - a stronger penalty costs accuracy - by regressing L0 on validation accuracy across all nine checkpoints and by a dimensional control comparing PCA variance-95 dimension against L0.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a description of the computation and not a circuit. What the paper produces is a **reorganisation claim measured by existing instruments**: a training prior shifts where causal mass sits in the network, and the evidence is a ratio from an intervention (patching a named set of units) plus a dictionary statistic (L0, dead fraction) plus a selectivity statistic. It is partial by construction and predictive only in a narrow sense - it predicts that a specific class's high-activation units will be a better intervention handle in a topographic model than in a matched non-topographic one. Its central argument is that the field's monosemanticity metric is the wrong instrument for the kind of change the prior makes, so the explanation lives in the patching ratio rather than in any per-unit description.

## How they judged success
Patching ratio against a random same-size set (the baseline of 1), with paired tests on 60 class-by-seed observations and effect sizes; per-seed ratios reported separately so the result cannot rest on one seed; mean pairwise grid distance of the selected units against the expected distance for a random subset of that size; SAE L0 and dead-feature fraction by condition with a layer sweep; the entropy selectivity metric and the fraction of neurons or features above fixed selectivity thresholds; validation accuracy as the cost side; and an accuracy-matched regression plus a PCA-dimensionality control as the confound analyses. Spearman-style dose response is shown by running the ratio across the four sweep weights.

## What they found
The patching ratio rises monotonically with the penalty: 1.68 at zero, 2.27 at 0.1 and 2.79 at 1.0, against a random-set baseline of exactly 1, so the comparison being reported is to a random set under the *same* training condition rather than to the untrained-network baseline. Within the 1.0 model the selected unit sets are also about six percent tighter on the grid than at zero, the only condition where that check passes. Sparsity moves in the direction the authors call cleaner - L0 falls from 1273.9 to 1133.8 and the dead-feature fraction rises from 0.2 percent to 3.8 percent - but only at the strong penalty, and accuracy drops from 61.2 to 59.6 percent at the same time, with L0 and accuracy sharing a substantial part of their variance across checkpoints. Selectivity does not move at all: the entropy score drifts from 0.234 to 0.236 in the neuron basis and stays flat in the feature basis, including a larger-sample rerun at 25,000 images where every condition has a mean below 0.03 and no feature clears a 0.3 threshold. In the 4-layer model the sparsity trend repeats weakly while the patching ratio never exceeds 1.42; a calibrated penalty weight intended to match effective pressure across the two architectures reproduces the sparsity trend and fails to reproduce the causal one, which the authors report as a falsification of their own scaling explanation.

## Limits and open problems they name
A single dataset; a single architectural family; one seed per penalty weight for most of the sweep, with an explicitly underpowered three-seed test on the main effect (they say so); the sparsity result is confounded with accuracy and an accuracy-matched ablation is left undone; the selectivity metric uses the dataset's 100 classes as the concept basis, which may be far too coarse to see selectivity for features defined by texture or object parts; and the scale probe varies depth, width, accuracy and training budget at once, so nothing can be attributed to depth. They also flag that the per-class patching ratio has no untrained or shuffled-label control in the main text.

## What we take from it
A warning and a framing. **Warning:** the headline comparison is to a random set of the same size *drawn from the same model*, so a topographic model whose units are generally more redundant will inflate the ratio without doing anything a reader would call localisation - at zero penalty the ratio is already 1.68, which says the top-activation set beats a random set in a model that has no topographic pressure at all, and the useful quantity is that 1.68 rather than the 2.79. Any cluster-versus-random result we report for our own model needs a same-model control of exactly that shape and a shuffled-label arm before we call it a circuit. **Framing worth importing:** a univariate selectivity statistic and a multivariate intervention statistic can be flat and positive respectively on the same model, so "the units got more interpretable" is only answerable once you say which instrument reads it; when our probes disagree across those two levels, the honest report is the instrument each number came from, not the better-sounding one. **Shortcut worth importing:** their dictionary statistics are consistent with a representation that has simply lost capacity - fewer active features, a 19-fold jump in dead features, about a 15 percent fall in the variance-95 dimension (166.7 to 142.3) - which is the same configuration run 951 found a geometric repair producing in a different setting, and it is a reminder that "sparser and more specialised" and "thinner and more abandoned" are the same two numbers.
