## What they wanted, and why
Developmental studies date when a model "learns" a capability by when its outputs become correct. But every output passes through the unembedding matrix — the read-out — which is itself still being trained. The authors' question is whether a behavioural date reflects the hidden state learning something or merely the read-out learning to express it. Their decomposition is elementary and stated up front: a change in a token's logit between two checkpoints is the sum of a term in the changed hidden state, a term in the changed read-out row, and a cross term, so output-level measurements cannot attribute the change to either part. The larger goal is to make developmental timelines and early-checkpoint evaluations report the two components separately instead of conflating them.

## What they studied
Pythia-160M, 1B and 6.9B, and OLMo-2-7B as a cross-family check, each with 32 released checkpoints. Unit of analysis: the read-out — one row per vocabulary token, i.e. a weight-space object, not an activation. They also train four 31M-parameter models from scratch on the first 10B tokens of Pythia's preshuffled data from a shared initialisation, differing only in the read-out's learning-rate multiplier (0.25×, 1×, 4×) and, in a fourth run, in warm-up length. Tasks are two-token contrast pairs: subject–verb agreement (simple, across a prepositional phrase, subject and object relative clauses), indirect object identification, number comparison, greater-than completion, relational facts.

## How they did it
They introduce *parameter-trajectory crosscoding*: one sparse dictionary fitted jointly to every token's read-out row at every checkpoint. A token gets one code shared across checkpoints; each feature gets a per-checkpoint decoder and a learned threshold, and the gate compares the *contribution* (code times decoder norm) against the threshold, so a feature can emerge or decay while keeping its index — a naive gate on the code alone would force it on or off at all checkpoints at once. Rows are centred and scaled per checkpoint so decoder norms are not dominated by overall growth, and the sparsity penalty is a saturating firing-rate term that is invariant to the code/decoder rescaling, so the dictionary cannot buy sparsity by shrinking codes and inflating decoders. Features are summarised by their normalised decoder-norm trajectory and bucketed into lifecycle profiles. Three independent measurements separate hidden state from read-out: a **readout swap** (decode a fixed hidden state with another checkpoint's read-out, giving a margin between the correct answer row and a foil row), an **oracle sweep** over all intermediate read-outs which, because the best is chosen after scoring, is an upper bound on any single one, and a **linear probe** on the same hidden states that never passes through a read-out. Probes carry three controls — a step-0 probe, a probe on prompt tokens only, and a probe on matched-dimension Gaussian features. Localisation selects the top eight read-out features by attribution on one half of the items and scores ablation, preserve-only and hidden-state projection on the held-out half against sign-matched random features matched on decoder norm and firing rate. Finally, a training intervention varies only the read-out's learning rate.

## What counts as an explanation here
A decomposed read-out: a fixed-index sparse feature dictionary over vocabulary rows across training, plus a timing curve per feature, plus a two-way attribution of any logit margin into "available" (decodable by a probe from the state) and "expressed" (decodable from the state through the model's own read-out rows). Partial by construction — it predicts when a distinction becomes expressible and which read-out directions carry a margin, not what the hidden state encodes. The strongest predictive content is the intervention: change one hyperparameter of the read-out and the timing statistic moves one checkpoint per fourfold learning-rate change.

## How they judged success
Explained variance of the reconstructed read-out trajectories (0.808–0.920 across the four models), held-out checkpoint reconstruction, next-token fidelity of the reconstruction (top-1 agreement and ΔNLL on a held-out shard, plus KL and a token-stratified check), L0 and dead-feature count against per-checkpoint SAE and final-snapshot SAE baselines, PCA at matched and unmatched ranks as a dense baseline, circular-shift nulls (200 draws) for the timing claim, matched-random and label-shuffle nulls with an explicit note that 20 permutations bounds p at 0.048, shuffled-label probe fits, ablation against sign-matched controls, and a from-scratch learning-rate intervention with final-loss comparison.

## What they found
All four models reconstruct at 0.808–0.920 EV, every row uses 1.17–2.26% of features, and no feature is dead. In Pythia the read-out reorganises in one narrow window, steps 512–1.6k — 0.4–1.1% of training — clearing circular-shift nulls at p = 0.005 for 160M and 1B and p = 0.04 for 6.9B, with 20 of 21 fitted dictionaries selecting the same window; OLMo peaks at a similar fraction of its first stage but does not clear the nulls (p = 0.12). Lexical structure appears alongside: median WordNet probe accuracy on centred read-out rows rises 0.499 → 0.874, all 35 categories decodable by step 1000, 33 of 35 above the 95th percentile of both matched-random and shuffled-label nulls by step 512, and a lemma-grouped split leaves the early rise intact while lowering the final median to 0.841. On Pythia-6.9B simple agreement the probe nears ceiling by step 512 while native expression and the best swept read-out are still at chance — availability precedes expression. Read-out swaps confirm a lag: the step-1000 read-out lowers next-token loss of step-256 and step-512 states by 1.50 and 0.15 nats on nine-language Wikipedia, but the terminal read-out's apparent gain on early agreement states (0.800 → 0.925) collapses to 0.566 after an orthogonal Procrustes alignment to the native frame. Localisation is selective for agreement (reconstruction 0.900, top-eight ablation 0.487, preserve-only 0.938, sign-matched control 0.931) and greater-than completion (0.512 → 0.084 ablated, 0.894 on the eight alone) but **fails for IOI**: ablation stays at the control, positive attribution is spread over 4,387 of 24,576 features, and projecting the eight directions out moves the correct and foil logits almost equally (−0.44 and −0.45). In the from-scratch runs, the median feature peak moves 128 → 64 → 32 with the learning-rate multiplier while halving warm-up changes nothing; early validation loss at 0.27B tokens is 1.20 nats better at 4× and 2.08 nats better than 0.25×, but final losses agree within 0.06 nats.

## Limits and open problems they name
The learning-rate evidence is one 31M setting, one run per condition, 10B tokens, and the read-out holds about 42% of parameters there against about 10% in Pythia-1B — larger models untested. Availability is measured on two-token contrasts, so the claim is about when a binary distinction becomes expressible, not about open-ended capabilities. Checkpoint density bounds temporal resolution; profile shares and median peak step depend on dictionary width and sparsity, so only matched settings are compared (the window itself survives 20 of 21 fits). Probes are linear and read one position and layer. One cross-family model only. They also record that the checkpoint-spanning dictionary is sparse in active codes but *not* parameter-efficient, since it pays for per-checkpoint heads, and that the narrow Pythia-160M dictionary keeps logit shape while losing scale calibration (ΔNLL 3.758 nats/token), i.e. matrix EV alone is not evidence at small widths.

## What we take from it
Four things. (1) **A measurement-channel confound with an exact accounting**: when our account reads anything off a model's outputs, the quantity is a product of state and read-out, and the cross term means the two cannot be separated from outputs alone — the swap-plus-probe pair is the minimal instrument that does it, and it costs no retraining. (2) **A weight-only crosscoder** is the cheapest shared-index dictionary we have seen: no activation corpus, full vocabulary coverage, and the per-checkpoint gate on contribution norm is precisely the construct our own dictionary comparisons need to express "this feature exists at this checkpoint and not that one" while keeping the index — which is the cross-run identity problem the folder has been circling from the activation side. (3) **The frame-dependence result is a warning we should adopt wholesale**: the same read-out improves accuracy by 0.125 in its own frame and *loses* 0.11 after an orthogonal alignment to the native frame, so a swap or steering gain must be reported in both the unaligned and aligned frames, because an arbitrary basis change carries a lot of the effect. (4) **The IOI negative is the honest shape of a localisation result**: one task localises to eight directions, another spreads over thousands with a generic-suppression signature, and both are printed — so our own "these units carry this behaviour" claims need the spread statistic (fraction of units with positive attribution) beside the ablations.
