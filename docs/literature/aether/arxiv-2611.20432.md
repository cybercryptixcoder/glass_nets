# Validating Acoustic-to-Articulatory Inversion with Real-Time MRI: From Vocal-Tract Fidelity to Phonetic Consequences

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.20432
- **Authors / organisation:** Tharinda Piyadasa, Michael Proctor, Amelia Gully, Tuende Szalay, Emily Kiff, Kirrie Ballard, Craig Jin; University of Sydney (primary), Macquarie University, and University of York
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20432
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

A speech model can be trained to predict the physical configuration of the vocal tract from the sound alone, and those
predictions look like measurements once they open a data file: eight numbers per frame describing where the lips, tongue
and velum are. The authors' worry is that the map from acoustics to articulation is many-to-one, so several different
vocal-tract shapes can produce nearly the same sound, and a model can therefore reconstruct speech plausibly while
reporting a trajectory that is displaced, compressed, or simply wrong. Reconstruction accuracy, the usual criterion, is
silent about that. They pair audio with a direct, independent view of the vocal tract — real-time MRI of the midsagittal
plane — so the inferred numbers can be checked against something measured rather than inferred, and then ask a second
question the folder keeps coming back to: do the discrepancies matter downstream, and where. The larger goal is a
warrant for calling a model output a *measurement* of a physical state rather than a useful internal code.

## What they studied

358 utterances from 14 speakers of the Annot-16 corpus, which supplies tracked articulator contours and phone/word
alignments over the USC 75-speaker real-time MRI database; material spans read passages, nonsense vowel–consonant–vowel
sequences, spontaneous picture descriptions and monologues, with in-scanner audio at 16 kHz passed through a
waveform-domain denoiser. The unit of analysis is the **vocal tract variable** (TV): eight scalar trajectories at 50 Hz —
lip aperture and protrusion, tongue-tip / tongue-body / tongue-root constriction degree, tongue-tip and tongue-body
constriction location, and velic aperture — an Articulatory-Phonology-derived set extended with a tongue-root degree
because MRI resolves the pharyngeal region directly. Constriction degree is the minimum distance from a tongue region to
its passive articulator (hard palate for tip and body, posterior pharyngeal wall for root); location is normalised arc
length along the hard palate from alveolar ridge (0) to velar junction (1); the velum is deliberately excluded from the
tongue's target surfaces so that body constriction is not coupled to velic lowering. The model side is a frozen
WavLM-Large encoder with learned layer weighting, a projection, a two-layer bidirectional LSTM of 256 units per
direction, and a linear head to eight TVs. A Transformer acoustic synthesizer (six encoder layers, dimension 384,
speaker-conditioned, five-layer convolutional postnet to 80-bin log-mel, HiFi-GAN vocoder) is the second model in the
loop, used as a probe rather than as an object of study.

## How they did it

Two trajectories are computed for every held-out utterance. One is read off the tracked MRI contours. The other is
predicted from the audio by the inversion model, trained on roughly two hours of paired MRI–speech with a mean-squared
error objective, checkpoints chosen by mean Pearson correlation, and evaluated speaker-independently. Fidelity is scored
with two numbers and not one: the correlation between trajectory and estimate, and the ratio of their standard
deviations, so that a well-correlated but compressed prediction is visible as such. The synthesis arm is then set up so
that only the provenance of the articulation varies: the synthesizer is pretrained on 31,651 pseudo-labelled LibriTTS-R
utterances (53 hours) generated by the inversion model, fine-tuned on the MRI-derived TVs, and at test time each held-out
utterance is synthesised twice — once driven by inferred TVs, once by MRI-derived TVs — with the log-F0, voicing
probability and speaker embedding held fixed and the same model weights on both sides. Word and character error rates
from a speech recogniser and mel-cepstral distortion score the two outputs, and the difference between the mel-spectra is
kept per frame so it can be attributed to phone intervals from the forced alignments. Phones are then binned by their
characteristic constriction in the Articulatory Phonology sense — wide (vowels), narrow non-turbulent (approximants),
closed (stops and nasals), critical (fricatives and affricates) — and the mean spectral gap is reported per bin. A
targeted analysis compares /s/ and /ʃ/ by frication spectral centroid and the class separation between them (Cohen's
d) in original and synthesised audio. Three controls guard the synthesis comparison: the same architecture's error in the
clean pseudo-labelled domain, a mel-through-vocoder pass to bound the vocoder's own contribution, and a constant-TV arm
to show the articulatory input is load-bearing at all.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **validated read-out**: eight named physical quantities, predicted per frame, with an independent measurement offered
as the arbiter and a functional probe deciding which errors are consequential. It is not an explanation of the network in
the mechanistic sense — no feature, direction, circuit or weight is described, and nothing is claimed about *how* WavLM
computes the estimate — but it is a claim of a kind the folder values more than most: a mapping presented as interpretable
and then tested against ground truth that the model had no access to, with the test allowed to fail. The validated object
is the representation, not the model that produced it.

## How they judged success

Fidelity: Pearson correlation and the predicted-to-observed standard-deviation ratio per variable, and their means
across the eight, on held-out speakers. Functional consequence: word error rate, character error rate and mel-cepstral
distortion for the same utterances under the two TV provenances, with source and speaker information fixed; then the
per-phone mean mel-spectral gap (MRI-derived minus inversion-derived, over the full 0–8 kHz range) grouped into four
constriction classes; then spectral-centroid separation between /s/ and /ʃ/ as Cohen's d in original versus synthesised
speech. Controls as listed above: clean-domain synthesis error against a published-style reference, mel-through-vocoder
error against the original-recording baseline, and a constant-TV arm. No shuffled-target, random-initialisation, or
held-out-per-listener human rating is reported; intelligibility is measured by an automatic recogniser only.

## What they found

Fidelity is uneven and sits at a middling level overall: mean correlation 0.643 across the eight variables, with
constriction *degrees* recovered far better than constriction *locations*. Tongue-tip degree 0.850, tongue-root degree
0.794, tongue-body degree 0.742, lip aperture 0.720, velic aperture 0.737 report as strong; tongue-tip location 0.475,
lip protrusion 0.488 and tongue-body location 0.340 do not. The second number is the paper's sharper observation: the
standard-deviation ratio is below one for **every** variable (mean 0.775, minimum 0.595 for tongue-body location), so the
inferred trajectories are systematically compressed relative to the measured ones, and most compressed exactly where the
correlation is worst. Acoustically, the aggregate cost of using inferred rather than measured articulation is small and
in the direction that flatters the model: 35.8% WER, 23.1% CER and 3.77 dB MCD from inferred TVs against 39.6% WER,
26.2% CER and 3.86 dB MCD from the measured ones — the model's own estimates synthesise slightly *more* intelligibly
than the ground truth, which the paper reports plainly and which makes the aggregate comparison useless as a validation.
The controls hold: 7.7% WER against a 5.4% reference in the clean pseudo-labelled domain, 10.7% against 10.3% for
mel-through-vocoder, and character error rising to 72.7% with constant TVs. The structure the aggregate hides is in the
per-phone gap: 0.71 dB for vowels (2,488 tokens), 0.40 dB for approximants (369), 0.75 dB for stops and nasals (798), and
2.47 dB for fricatives and affricates (558) — 2.07 dB above the approximants, which rules out "narrow constriction costs
more" as the explanation, since approximants are narrow too. Fricatives carry most of it (2.25 dB, 508 tokens); the
affricate figure is larger but rests on 50 tokens. The /s/–/ʃ/ analysis lines the loss up with location: the centroid
separation contracts from 1,136 to 723 Hz after synthesis and Cohen's d falls from 1.48 to 0.92, 62% retained, on 47 /ʃ/
tokens within an 8 kHz band.

## Limits and open problems they name

The measured targets are themselves estimates, taken from semi-automatic contours whose agreement with expert hand
tracing is reported as a 3.58-pixel Hausdorff distance, so the "ground truth" branch of every comparison carries its own
error. Two hours of paired training data is small and one corpus is used; intelligibility is a recogniser score, not a
listener's, so "acoustically consequential" is operationalised as "costly to Whisper". Several of the headline cell
sizes are tiny — 50 affricate tokens, 47 /ʃ/ tokens — and the sibilant analysis is truncated to 8 kHz by the 16 kHz
sampling rate. The velocity of the synthesis comparison's conclusion is also limited by its own controls: the fact that
inferred TVs synthesise slightly *better* than measured ones means the comparison cannot rank the two representations in
absolute quality, only locate where they differ. Whether a better inversion model would fix the location variables, or
whether the acoustic signal simply does not carry them, is left open — and is the interesting open problem.

## What we take from it

A **method, a measure and a warning**. Method: *validate a read-out against an independent measurement, then score it by
whether its errors matter downstream* — two stages, with the second stage built so that only the read-out's provenance
varies and everything else in the pipeline is held fixed. That is the design our battery should copy when we assess a
predicted internal quantity: hold the downstream head or behaviour constant, swap the prediction for the ground truth,
and report the difference, per item, not in aggregate. Measure: the **standard-deviation ratio beside the correlation**,
which is a one-line addition to any probe report and catches the failure mode correlation is blind to — a read-out that
tracks the right direction at the wrong amplitude. Our own regression-style probes almost certainly compress, and until
we print the ratio we cannot know by how much. Warning: the aggregate downstream score here points the *wrong way*
(inferred TVs synthesise more intelligibly than measured ones), so an aggregate delta of that kind is not evidence of
fidelity even when it is small, and the informative measurement is the conditional one — per phone class, where the
mechanism predicts the error should live. Also portable: gating the aggregation into bins defined *before* looking, and
checking the bins against a competing explanation (approximants are narrow but cheap, so narrowness alone does not
explain the fricative cost), which is the same move as the controls on a ranking elsewhere in this folder.
