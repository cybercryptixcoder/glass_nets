## What they wanted, and why
Linear probing is the field's default way of saying "this representation contains that information", and the default probing location is the final-layer residual stream. Recent work already undermines the depth half of that default by showing intermediate layers can probe better, but all of it treats a transformer block as one atom, so "which representation" has quietly meant "which depth". The authors want to reopen the choice inside the block — normalisation outputs, attention output, FFN hidden layer, activation, each residual connection — and ask whether layer and component are separable at all, and whether a single fixed location beats the standard one across the board. The larger goal is practical (a better default for anyone who freezes a model and reads it) and conceptual (a reminder that a probe's answer is a property of a location, not of a layer).

## What they studied
Frozen vision foundation models and frozen language models, probed only, never finetuned for the main results. Vision: supervised ViT (B/L/H), DINOv2 (S/B/L/g), DINOv3 (S/B/L/7B) and autoregressive AIMv2 (L/H/1B/3B), 22M to 7B parameters, on an image-classification grid of 20 dataset columns — 16 named datasets including the DomainNet domains and two WILDS sets, plus four Cifar10-C corruption variants — giving 270 model-dataset pairs. Language: Qwen3 (1.7B/4B/8B), Llama3 (1B/3B/8B) and Gemma3 (1B/4B/12B) on the 24 classification tasks of MTEB(Multilingual), 216 model-task evaluations; together the two grids are the abstract's 144,000 linear probing evaluations. The unit is the **layer–module pair**: eight sequence-to-sequence operations per block (LN1, MHA, RC1, LN2, FC1, Act, FC2, RC2), captured by forward hooks, pooled over content tokens. Standard and gated FFNs are made comparable by redefining FC1/Act/FC2 so the gated multiply sits inside "Act".

## How they did it
Every location is scored the same way: global average pooling over non-special tokens, standard scaling, logistic regression (L-BFGS, one pass over the training split, no augmentation because the fit is deterministic), top-1 test accuracy for vision and MTEB's official probing protocol (8–16 shots per label, 100 iterations, 10 repeats, task-averaged accuracy) for language. Features are streamed to disk per batch so the whole grid is affordable. The baseline is RC2 at 100% depth. Gains are accuracy differences in percentage points; the paper then decomposes the search into three *a posteriori* upper bounds — best over layers with module fixed, best over modules with depth fixed, and best over both — so the interaction term is explicit rather than absorbed into one number. From the winning-frequency heatmap they pick one fixed location per modality and validate it with no search at all: final-layer activation (Act@100) for vision, second normalisation at 60% depth (LN2@60) for language, the latter's depth taken from a stable optimal band rather than tuned per model. A depth-matched residual-stream control (RC2@60) separates "deeper/different depth" from "different module"; a same-dimension control (FC1@100) separates the activation from the FFN hidden width; a Friedman test across model sizes checks whether capacity is the driver; and a pre/post-finetuning comparison plus a corruption-severity sweep test whether the effect is really about distribution shift.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **ranking of read-out locations plus a fixed recommendation**, with an explicit statement of what it is not. There is no circuit, no causal claim about computation inside the model, and no claim about what the network uses — only about which frozen tensor is most linearly informative for a downstream classifier. The oracle numbers are labelled upper bounds requiring test-set access, and the paper repeatedly says the superiority of Act or LN2 is established *a posteriori* and may not transfer. The one causal-ish claim (transfer difficulty drives the gains) is explicitly called observational because pretraining scale, architecture and data all co-vary.

## How they judged success
Top-1 test accuracy (vision) and task-averaged MTEB accuracy (language), both in percentage points against RC2@100; three oracle gains (layer-only, module-only, joint) and a **recovery fraction** — fixed strategy's gain divided by the oracle gain, with confidence intervals; winning frequency of each module over 270 pairs; number of model-dataset pairs improved; a depth-matched control, a dimension-matched control, a finetuning before/after control and corruption-severity levels 1–5; Spearman correlation between oracle gain and mean probing error (empirical transfer difficulty); Spearman across pretraining scale; Friedman test with Kendall's W across model sizes; Borda/average-rank comparison against published embedding models.

## What they found
Module choice carries most of the signal, and it interacts with depth: Act is in the winning pair for 64.8% of the 270 vision pairs against 9.3% for LN2, 5.9% for the residual stream and under 8% for MHA, LN1, RC1 or FC2. Joint oracle gains reach 10 pp on vision and 4 pp on language. The fixed strategies work with no search: Act@100 improves in 63.7% of 270 pairs and recovers 41.9% of the joint oracle gain, versus 31.9% for the best depth-only residual stream; letting the Act depth vary lifts recovery to 78.1%. On language LN2 is the winning module for all but one of nine models (Gemma3 4B's is FC1), LN2@60 gains 2.44 pp on average and recovers 86.0% of the oracle gain, improving in 21 of 24 tasks (87.5%) and 77.8% of the 216 evaluations — while the depth-matched RC2@60 gains are small and inconsistent. That asymmetry is the mechanism claim: the FFN activation and the second normalisation are far more linearly separable than the residual stream at any depth, and usually more so than the final one. Vision gains track transfer difficulty (ρ = 0.87, p < 0.001), grow with corruption severity while the optimal depth moves earlier, and shrink as pretraining scale grows; model size has no significant effect (all Friedman p > 0.18, Kendall's W 0.012–0.088 over only 3–4 sizes). The FC1@100 control scoring *below* the baseline (64.3 vs 66.0 mean on ViT-L) rules out a width artefact for Act. As an applied bonus, Gemma3 12B probed at LN2@60 beats GritLM-7B on MTEB(Multilingual) classification with no finetuning.

## Limits and open problems they name
Classification-only probing, one probing family (logistic regression on pooled features), and the authors name the obvious extensions (other tasks, tabular and time-series foundation models). Depth is expressed as a percentage rather than a layer index, which papers over differing layer counts. The transfer-difficulty story is observational. An exception is visible in their own tables: ViT-B's Act@100 loses 6.6 mean points to the baseline (55.2 vs 61.8) because its final activation is an especially poor location, so the "improves in a majority" claim is a majority, not a law. On the language side three tasks get worse under LN2@60 on average (KorSarcasm −2.7, TweetTopic −2.2, Tswana −0.3).

## What we take from it
A **measure** and a **framing**. Measure: the three-way oracle decomposition plus recovery fraction is the cheapest honest way to ask whether a choice among read-out sites is doing work, and it is portable to our setting — for any internal quantity we claim to have located, report the best-over-positions, best-over-encodings and joint numbers and how much of the joint gain a fixed convention captures. Framing: "readable at layer L" is short for "readable at layer L, module M, after pooling P, with probe family F", and the paper shows all four moving the answer by points, sometimes by more than the layer axis it was supposedly about. Warnings to inherit: report the dimension-matched control before crediting an activation over a residual (they did, and it saved the claim); treat an a-posteriori-fixed location as a hypothesis with an exception list rather than a rule; and note that a probe's gains growing with transfer difficulty is exactly the regime where a probe is least likely to describe what the model computes on the data it was trained for.
