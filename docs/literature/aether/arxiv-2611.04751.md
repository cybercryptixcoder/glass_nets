# What Does It Mean to Remove an Edge? Intervention-Aware Fidelity for Graph Neural Network Explanations

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.04751
- **Authors / organisation:** Rocco Mattia Di Mauro (equal contribution), Marco Grassia, Giuseppe Mangioni; Department of Electrical, Electronic and Computer Engineering (DIEEI), University of Catania, Italy
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04751
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Every ablation-based fidelity score answers a counterfactual — remove the explanation and see what the model does — but on a message-passing graph network the instruction "remove" does not pick out one counterfactual: the edge can be deleted from the graph, which silently rewrites degrees, normalisations and attention denominators, or its message can be zeroed while the graph stays as it was. The authors want to know whether that ambiguity stays inside one score or instead leaks into the comparison between explanations, because an explainer also perturbs the model while it is *building* its ranking, and therefore picks a counterfactual too. The larger goal is an evaluation discipline: a fidelity number should be reported with the intervention that produced it, and where the science does not fix the intervention, the two should be crossed rather than defaulted. This matters for us because our whole plan hinges on scoring an account by intervention, and here the intervention itself is shown to be a free parameter with a measured price.

## What they studied
Nine graph-classification datasets — four synthetic with edge-level ground truth (BA-2Motifs, BA-MultiShapes, SPMotif and the authors' Synth-Dist proximity task) and five real (BACE, MUTAG, Mutagenicity, Reddit-Binary, Tox21 reduced to one assay) — against three message-passing backbones spanning the spectrum: GIN (sum aggregation, unit coefficients), GCN (degree normalisation) and GATv2 (attention). The unit of analysis is the *directed arc*, with an undirected edge implemented as two arcs, and a coarser *bond* unit that ranks and removes the pair together. "Cells" are dataset–backbone pairs; each cell is trained at depths two through five with ten seeds each, forty models per cell, and each model is explained on up to fifty test graphs.

## How they did it
The paper runs on three nested constructions. First, a definitional separation: structural deletion recomputes every coefficient on the surviving edge set, message suppression keeps the coefficients and zeroes the selected messages; a layer decides which of these it can tell apart by two properties — whether its aggregator absorbs zero, and whether its coefficients are free of the edge set. An induction over layers gives a sufficient condition for the two interventions to agree, published as a proposition with a checked non-necessity, plus a closed form for the first-layer gap under sum aggregation that shows the difference is carried entirely by the surviving messages whose coefficients were renormalised. Second, a construction-by-evaluation crossing: a continuous gate is attached to each arc either before the graph-dependent normalisation (structural construction) or after the context has been computed (message-channel construction), integrated gradients along that gate path gives a ranking, and each ranking is then evaluated under *both* deletion interventions, giving four deletion areas at removal fractions from zero to one half, two matched contrasts and a difference-in-differences that is declared positive only when both one-sided bootstrap lower bounds clear zero. The nine GIN cells are built in as equivalence controls. Third, their inference choices are unusually explicit: seed is the independent cluster, graphs and depths are paired within a seed, exact sign-flip tests over the ten seed-level values with a family-wise correction applied within two declared families and never across them, plus sensitivity runs over three output functionals, twenty random tie-break orders, the bond unit instead of the arc unit, and the same crossing done with a learned mask explainer whose restart instability they report rather than hide.

## What counts as an explanation here
An edge ranking — a score per arc, from integrated gradients — and the study's product is not a better explainer but a legality condition on how such a ranking may be scored. The understanding on offer is methodological: "deletion fidelity" is a two-indexed quantity, one index for the intervention used to build the ranking and one for the intervention used to score it, and only the first proposition licenses collapsing the two. It is explicitly partial and makes no claim about which intervention is the right one, nor that a larger deletion area means a better explanation.

## How they judged success
Relative fidelity across the four arms of the crossing, read as the two matched contrasts and their sum, reported per cell with percentile bootstrap intervals over seed-level values and exact two-sided sign-flip p-values corrected within two pre-declared families; matched superiority as the conjunction of the two one-sided lower bounds being above zero. Controls: the GIN equality prediction, alternative output functionals (target logit, target-versus-rest margin, target probability), random tie-break priorities, the bond perturbation unit, an attribution-completeness tolerance used to certify or exclude each cell, and a model-health check that removed three near-constant classifiers. A learned-mask explainer is crossed descriptively as a check that the effect is not an artefact of integrated gradients.

## What they found
Their theory's control behaves exactly as promised: over all nine GIN cells the largest absolute gap between the two interventions is under 1e-5 on every output functional, consistent with floating-point error. In the eleven reportable GCN and GATv2 cells the interaction is positive in ten on the target logit and matched superiority holds in nine, and the pooled verdict is not the whole story — Synth-Dist–GCN has a positive interaction while one contrast is negative (−0.112 against 1.243), so the message-channel ranking wins even under structural evaluation; and BA-2Motifs–GCN's pooled interaction (−0.263, interval spanning zero) hides a sign that runs from +0.43 at depth two to −0.18, −0.92 and −0.38 at depths three to five, which the authors say means no depth-invariant effect there. The interaction is not invariant to what is measured: counts of positive interaction move between ten, nine and ten cells across logit, margin and probability, and matched superiority between nine, eight and nine, with different cells flipping in each. The perturbation unit moves it further — grouping reciprocal arcs into bonds gives matched superiority in all eleven cells, and BA-2Motifs–GCN becomes a clear positive. The learned explainer repeats the pattern weakly: fixed learned rankings score differently under the two interventions in seven of eleven cells, but matched superiority of two learned masks survives in only four, and restarts of that explainer often produce weakly correlated rankings, a convergence pilot failing at every budget up to 1,600 epochs — so they label that whole arm exploratory. Two witnesses are worth carrying: mean aggregation diverges from structural deletion purely through zero absorption while degree normalisation and attention diverge purely through coefficient dependence, isolating the two mechanisms on models with shared weights; and their own bound shows a larger coefficient shift need not mean a larger gap, because shifted messages can cancel and a linear readout can project a pooled difference to zero.

## Limits and open problems they name
The counterfactuals are not equally meaningful: structural deletion produces out-of-distribution graphs (chemically implausible molecules), message suppression produces internal computations absent from training, so neither is universally correct — which is precisely why they ask for the crossing rather than a winner. The panel is frozen models on benchmark datasets, not a sample of architectures or domains; only three attention-model cells enter, all molecular, so architecture and domain are confounded there. Ten seeds make bootstrap intervals approximate, cell counts do not estimate prevalence, and the primaries were amended after a discarded run, so they decline to call the protocol a clean preregistration. Integrated-gradients completeness certifies the sum of scores against its own path endpoints, not individual scores or their ordering. And the open problem is stated plainly: no account is given of *when* each intervention is the right question.

## What we take from it
Three things. (1) **A warning with a receipt, for our probe-and-intervene protocol:** an ablation score is a two-indexed object — the index of the intervention used to produce the account and the index of the intervention used to score it — so every score our white-box account ships prints both, and where our question does not fix the intervention we cross the two and report the interaction rather than defaulting to one implementation. This is run 563's rule (name the compensators a causal score leaves free) moved one step upstream, to the perturbation itself: there the free units were suppressed units in the model, here it is the definition of "removed". (2) **A cheap decidability test to copy:** the way to know whether an intervention ambiguity matters is to check two structural properties of the computation (does the aggregator absorb zero, are the coefficients independent of the edge set) and derive a sufficient condition, then keep architectures that satisfy the condition as a built-in null. Sign-flooding a query set with an intervention whose divergence is structurally zero is a control we can construct ourselves, at no extra model cost. (3) **A re-derivation rule:** the gap under sum aggregation has a closed form in the coefficient shift of the surviving terms, which means our account can *predict* the size of the divergence before running the second intervention — and the paper also shows that bound does not order gaps, so any predicted divergence must be checked against a measured one, which is the predicted-effect-size-beside-measured theme the folder has been collecting.
