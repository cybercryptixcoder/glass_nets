# Understanding Perturbed Parameter Ensemble Sensitivities Using A Contrastive Learning Approach

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.30420
- **Authors / organisation:** Da Fan, David John Gagne II, Gregory S Elsaesser, Brian Medeiros, Addisu G Semie, Qingyuan Yang, Akila Sampath, Subashree Venkatasubramanian; NSF NCAR (Boulder) + Columbia University LEAP Science and Technology Center + NASA Goddard Institute for Space Studies
- **Date:** 2026-09-24
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30420
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Climate models encode sub-grid cloud and rain physics as parameterisations with uncertain parameters, and the standard way to explore that uncertainty is a perturbed parameter ensemble (PPE): run the model many times with different parameter draws. Reading a PPE is hard because output is multivariate, spatially structured and seasonal, and because agreement on a few global scalars does not tell you whether the model got the underlying process right - several parameter settings can look equally acceptable, and errors can cancel across variables and regions. The authors want a compact diagnostic space in which two model configurations and real observations can be compared directly, and in which a discrepancy can be traced back to specific variables, regions, seasons and physical parameters. They frame this as three questions: can such a space hold seasonal cycle, ensemble spread and structural difference at once; can distances in it measure parametric uncertainty and structural error; and which variables, regions and parameters drive those distances.

## What they studied
Two 100-member PPEs of the Community Atmosphere Model version 6 at roughly 0.9 by 1.25 degree horizontal resolution with 32 vertical levels, driven by year-2000 climatological forcing and run three years each with monthly output archived. The two ensembles use identical parameter draws across 34 parameters sampled by Latin hypercube over turbulence, microphysics, aerosol and deep-convection schemes, and differ in exactly one respect: the warm-rain microphysics, either the default bulk KK2000 scheme or TAU-ML, a neural-network emulator of the Tel Aviv University bin scheme. Five monthly fields are studied - total liquid water path (cloud plus rain), shortwave and longwave cloud radiative effect, and upwelling longwave and net shortwave flux at top of model - together with four static geography fields. Observations are monthly climatologies of the same five quantities. The unit of analysis is a monthly, spatially complete field sample, later a 128-dimensional embedding of it, and at the last stage a regional scalar attribution.

## How they did it
Fields were restricted between 65.5 degrees south and north, mean-removed per variable with statistics computed per ensemble member (and across the twelve monthly climatologies for observations), and the first month of each run discarded. Samples were split by ensemble member into train, validation and test sets of 4200, 1400 and 1400 samples. A shared convolutional encoder (channel-aware masked convolution on the water-path channel, three further 3x3 convolutions with 64/128/256 filters, two average-pooling layers, GELU throughout) plus a projection head (three dense layers, 2048, 256 and 128 units) maps any field sample into a 128-dimensional embedding. Training is self-supervised contrastive learning with a normalized temperature-scaled cross-entropy loss: a minibatch holds two positive pairs - adjacent months t and t+1 from one ensemble member of each scheme - and treats all four cross-scheme combinations as negative pairs, so pairs from the same scheme at consecutive months must land close and pairs from different schemes must land apart, with gradients accumulated over 100 minibatches (effective batch 400), Adam at 1e-4 and early stopping after ten unimproved epochs. Two evaluation arms follow: a logistic regression trained on embeddings of every encoder layer to separate the two schemes, and PCA projections of encoder and final-layer embeddings for the three sources side by side. Integrated Gradients is then run on cosine distances between embeddings, producing per-variable attribution maps over the grid that are normalized by their own absolute maximum and averaged over members and months; finally regional averages of those attributions are correlated (Pearson) with the 34 perturbed parameter values, with correlations above 0.3 marked as significant.

## What counts as an explanation here
A three-layer one, none of it mechanistic in the circuit sense. The explanation of a model-versus-observation gap is (i) a distance in a learned embedding space, (ii) a spatial attribution map from Integrated Gradients saying which variable in which region pushed that distance, and (iii) a correlation table linking regional attribution to named physical parameters. It is partial and diagnostic rather than a surrogate: nothing predicts a field from parameters, and no claim is made that the attribution recovers the true causal path through the parameterisation. The claim is weaker and cleaner - that the embedding distances are physically interpretable because their gradients land where the corresponding field differences are, and because they covary with the parameters the scheme is built around.

## How they judged success
Three arms, all within-sample relative to their own splits: linear-classification accuracy of logistic regression on held-out members, rising to over 0.94; inspection of PCA structure as a qualitative test that the seasonal cycle (roughly twelve clusters) and ensemble spread are preserved; and the explanatory arms, judged by whether attribution maps co-locate with independently computed field differences (SWCF, water path and low-cloud-fraction differences shown seasonally beside the gradients) and whether regional attributions correlate with the parameters. The significance bar for parameter correlations is a stated Pearson threshold of 0.3. There is no held-out-parameter test, no shuffled-label control, no comparison against a simpler baseline embedding, and no seed-variation reporting anywhere in the text.

## What they found
Scheme separation is near-ceiling and arrives early: logistic regression on embeddings reaches over 0.94 accuracy at every layer, highest at the second dense layer and lowest at the final one, with the first convolutional layer already high - so most of the discriminative signal is present before the encoder deepens, and the authors read the final-layer drop as the projection compressing away classification-relevant detail in favour of the similarity objective. The two schemes are far closer to each other than either is to observations. TAU-ML sits consistently nearer observations than KK2000, and the KK2000-to-observation distance has visibly larger ensemble spread, which the authors interpret as KK2000's bias being more sensitive to the parameter draw. Observations fall on the same low-dimensional manifold rather than forming their own cluster, with displacement largest in boreal spring and autumn (March, April, September, October). In the final-layer space the first two components carry nearly all variance while the third, holding only 0.23 percent, still separates the two schemes cleanly - the contrastive loss putting the discriminative direction in a low-variance component. Attribution is dominated by total water path and shortwave cloud forcing, with fluxes contributing weakly and upwelling longwave essentially not at all; the hot spots are subtropical low-cloud decks, both storm tracks and tropical convection. Parameter correlations concentrate in microphysics, boundary-layer turbulence and deep-convection parameters, with aerosol, solubility and dust parameters mostly weak and unsystematic; for the scheme-versus-scheme comparison the top three are the accretion enhancement factor and two deep-convection triggering parameters, which is the pair of processes the two schemes actually differ on - though the accretion factor is inactive under TAU-ML, so that row is a contrast between a used and an unused knob rather than a shared sensitivity.

## Limits and open problems they name
Observational uncertainty is omitted entirely and deferred to future calibration work. Polar regions are excluded. The attribution and correlation stages are descriptive over the ensemble as given; no causal claim is made that moving a parameter moves a region. The paper ends by proposing the embedding distance as a calibration objective rather than demonstrating calibration with it.

## Limits this entry adds
Two careful notes. First, the headline separation number is not evidence that the embedding encodes physics rather than provenance: the training objective explicitly pushes the two schemes apart as negative pairs and pulls consecutive months together as positives, so a high linear separation is the objective being achieved, and it is achieved at the shallowest layer. The paper's own control is missing here - a linear probe on raw fields, or on a randomly initialised encoder of the same shape, would have separated the schemes by construction too if the schemes differ anywhere in the five fields. Second, the parameter-correlation stage reads a 100-member sample: at that size a Pearson correlation of 0.3 is at the edge of conventional significance, there is no correction for the large number of region-variable-parameter triples being scanned, and the same region appears with opposite signs in different comparisons, which the text reports without resolving. No figure was inspected as an image: Figures 1-12 are a pipeline schematic, layer-classification curves, PCA scatter panels, monthly distance curves, attribution maps and a correlation heatmap, all described here through their captions and the numeric sentences in the body.

## What we take from it
Three things. **(1) A measure.** The observation here that a discriminative direction can live in a low-variance component (0.23 percent of final-layer variance, yet clean separation) is a direct warning for our own probing and dictionary work: a direction that explains almost none of the activation variance can still carry the entire distinction being read, so component-explained-variance is not a filter for relevance and the folder's repeated habit of judging a direction by its variance share should be replaced by judging it against the intervention it is supposed to predict.
**(2) A warning, sharp and cheap.** When a representation is trained with a per-class contrastive objective, its class separation is not a measurement, it is the loss. Every separation accuracy our white-box work reports must come from a representation trained without knowledge of the label being separated, or must carry a matched random-encoder probe beside it.
**(3) A framing for model-to-model comparison.** The pattern of scoring two configurations against each other *and* both against a shared external reference, then reporting which distance is larger, is the schema that tells "these differ" apart from "these are wrong", and it is the schema our predictive model needs whenever it compares an edited network with its original: distance to each other is never the whole story without a third anchor.
