# CircuitKIT: Circuit Discovery, Evaluation, and Application Toolkit for Mechanistic Interpretability

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.19317
- **Authors / organisation:** Pratinav Seth, Hem Gosalia, Aditya Kasliwal (equal contribution; two as work done while at Lexsi), Vinay Kumar Sankarapu; Lexsi Labs
- **Date:** 2026-07-21 (arXiv:2607.19317v1)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19317
- **Kind:** tool (systems paper, with seven validation studies)
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, ~119k chars including Appendix A; figures 1–8 known only from their captions)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) — not for a new method, but because its validation studies are a compact, honest measurement of **which circuit statistics predict what happens when you act on the circuit**, which is the question our predictive white-box model is ultimately asked.

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit analysis is done in three stages — find a subgraph, judge whether it is faithful/stable/robust, then use it to prune, quantize, edit, or fine-tune — and each stage lives in a different per-paper repository with its own artifact format. The authors name two costs of that fragmentation: every pairwise combination of method, criterion and intervention needs fresh glue code, and, more importantly, faithfulness numbers are not comparable across papers because they are computed under different ablation choices. The stated goal is infrastructure: one typed artifact that any discovery algorithm writes and any evaluation or application module reads, plus a declarative way to turn a user's own CSV or HuggingFace split into something discovery can run on. The larger aim is to make the locate-then-act loop reproducible and to stop intrinsic scores from standing in for downstream outcomes. This is a library paper, and the new knowledge in it is almost entirely in the seven studies at the end.

## What they studied

Models: GPT-2 Small (124M) for the six-algorithm comparison; Pythia-1.4B, Llama-3.2-1B, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Phi-2 (2.8B) for cross-family discovery; Llama-3.2-1B for the neuron-level study; Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct for the custom-data jailbreak study; Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct for the three intervention studies (with a partial Gemma-3-4B replication drawn from a companion audit). Tasks: indirect object identification (IOI), greater-than, BoolQ, a 334-row binary jailbreak-detection CSV shipped with the library, and WMDP via a user module. Unit of analysis is the **component**: attention heads and MLP sublayers at node level, or per-head channels and MLP hidden units at neuron level — the paper is careful about a distinction most circuit work elides, namely that the MLP can be scored at its residual interface (d_model dimensions, the default, which puts attention and MLP on the same footing) or post-activation (d_mlp hidden units, the object the field usually means by "MLP neuron"). Thirteen discovery algorithms across four backends are shipped; six are "stable tier" (tested across four families) and seven are "research tier" (implemented and checked on GPT-2/IOI only). Sixteen built-in tasks are registered, fifteen of which support discovery.

## How they did it

The artifact is a typed, serializable record carrying task descriptor, model id, algorithm, granularity, per-component scores and configuration; discovery backends write it, evaluation pillars and application modules consume it, and the same record drives a Python pipeline API, a flat function API and a fifteen-command CLI/YAML interface. Six evaluation pillars share one normalised faithfulness ratio — circuit-score minus corrupt-baseline over clean-minus-corrupt — differing only in how the circuit's own score is produced: (1) causal patching, where the *complement* is softened toward its corrupted activations; (2) hard ablation of the complement by zero, mean or mean-positional replacement, reported for each method and reported **signed** so that a circuit driven below the corrupt baseline shows as a negative number rather than being clipped; (3) stability, mean pairwise Jaccard plus Spearman over re-discoveries on resampled data; (4) robustness across the corruption-strategy family; (5) baselines, improvement ratios and z-scores against size-matched random subgraphs and magnitude selections; (6) generalization, transfer of a circuit to an adjacent task. An optional seventh pillar scores intervention reliability across re-discoveries. The data path is the second contribution: prompt/answer templates over named columns, automatic token-length alignment between clean and corrupt halves (misaligned pairs are filtered, not padded, by default), a CSV/HuggingFace schema inference step, ten corruption strategies split into answer-changing ones for discovery and answer-preserving ones for the robustness pillar, and — worth noting as a design idea in its own right — a **pre-flight worthiness gate** that grades a dataset green/yellow/red on eight checks (alignment, single-token answer determinism, whether the model already answers correctly, logit-difference signal, class balance, pair uniqueness and so on) before any discovery compute is spent. Interventions wrap existing tools rather than reimplementing them: LLM-Pruner for structural pruning (with circuit scores passed in as a custom importance estimator alongside its native magnitude and Taylor criteria), quanto/llmcompressor for mixed-precision tiering, PEFT for selective fine-tuning (a static gradient mask or LoRA target list), and ROME/MEMIT for editing restricted to circuit-resident MLP layers. Every exported checkpoint is scored through lm-evaluation-harness. The seven studies are then: E1 six algorithms on GPT-2 IOI; E2 cross-family, twelve (model, task) cells; E3 neuron versus node granularity; E4 the custom-data path end to end; E5 pruning, E6 quantization, E7 selective fine-tuning at matched sparsity/budget against random and weight-saliency selectors.

## What counts as an explanation here

The artifact and the graph: a sparse subgraph of named components with per-component scores, rendered as an interactive node/edge diagram, plus a per-pillar report that serializes to JSON. The paper's own framing is that the explanation is a **hypothesis with a scored panel attached**, not a number — the six pillars are explicitly motivated by the finding that single faithfulness metrics depend on the ablation choice and can reverse method rankings, so the panel is designed to expose that dependence rather than average it away. Nothing here is a text description or a predictive simulation; there is no claim that the graph predicts behaviour it has not been scored on, and the paper says so in its conclusion, where behavioural recovery, component overlap, ablation sufficiency and application performance are listed as quantities that "need not agree". The one place a prediction is made is Pillar 6, where a circuit found on one task is applied unchanged to a related task and its transfer ratio reported.

## How they judged success

Per study, not by a single headline. E1: patching recovery, ablation faithfulness, Jaccard and recall against the canonical IOI head taxonomy, and wall-clock. E2: the same four columns across twelve cells. E3: faithfulness at two granularities plus units kept at 30% sparsity. E4: faithfulness, baseline ratios, robustness deltas under paraphrase/role-swap, and circuit-only sufficiency on the clean-only route. E5–E7: standardised benchmark accuracy retention and WikiText-2 perplexity, always against **matched-budget controls** — random subgraph of equal size, magnitude/Wanda/Taylor weight-saliency selectors, uniform quantization assignment, and random-mask or full-parameter fine-tuning under an identical recipe. The comparisons are stated as selector swaps within one registry, so nothing differs except which importance signal is used. The correlation claims (Spearman ρ with p-values) are computed over the audit's selector-means, which the paper states as n=9 for the quantization table.

## What they found

**Faithfulness and canonical overlap disagree, and the disagreement is the design argument.** On GPT-2 IOI the three EAP-family algorithms reach patching recovery of exactly 1.00 with 0.43–0.50 Jaccard and 0.60–0.67 recall against the canonical heads at minute-scale cost, while contextual decomposition reaches 0.89 patching and perfect (1.00) ablation faithfulness yet overlaps the canonical set **not at all** — twelve of its fifteen top slots land on MLP sublayers. Same faithfulness, a different mechanism. ACDC acquired three orders of magnitude more compute and scored 0.21/0.04, which the authors attribute to forcing its variable-size edge set onto a fixed node budget rather than to the search itself.

**Cross-family discovery works, and ablation sufficiency is where the circuits differ.** All twelve cells completed with only a model-name change; patching recovery was at least 0.91 everywhere (six cells exactly 1.00) and stability Jaccard 0.80–0.92. Ablation sufficiency ranged from 0.24 (Gemma-2-2B/IOI) to 1.00 (Phi-2/IOI) across cells whose patching scores are nearly indistinguishable. The paper states the consequence plainly: reading patching alone makes the twelve circuits interchangeable, reading ablation alone makes discovery a failure on Gemma.

**Neuron level is a first-class granularity, and single-point gradients invert.** At 30% sparsity on Llama-3.2-1B/IOI, EAP-IG keeps perfect patching (1.00) and 0.24 circuit-only sufficiency against 0.35 at node level, for essentially the same discovery time (147 vs 140 s) while exposing 825,754 of 1,179,648 neurons rather than 371 of 528 nodes. Plain EAP degrades: 0.89 patching and a **signed −0.35** on ablation, i.e. under hard zero-ablation the isolated neuron circuit scores −0.57 where the clean run scores 1.69 and the corrupt baseline sits at zero — it is driven below the corrupt baseline, preferring the wrong behaviour. A clipped score would have printed 0.00 and hidden the inversion.

**The same inversion appears on a user dataset, on a safety behaviour.** On the jailbreak CSV, the paired route (265 of 334 rows kept after alignment filtering) recovers 85% of the refusal gap under patching and beats size-matched baselines 1.71× (random) and 1.44× (magnitude), is unchanged under answer-preserving paraphrase (Δ = 0.00) and near-unchanged under role-swap (0.05) — and under hard zero-ablation its refusal metric falls to −1.55, a signed ratio of −2.61, meaning that stripped of surrounding computation the truncated model prefers to comply. The authors connect this directly to work that localises and edits refusal through a few directions. Clean-only routing (IBCircuit, neuron level, no corrupt pairs) needed no pairing code and its circuit alone matched the full model (0.90 against 0.87 P(correct)) in 2.7 minutes.

**Intervention studies: the signal's value depends on the intervention, and in two of three cases circuit importance does not beat a matched control.** Pruning (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct/BoolQ, 30% sparsity): Taylor retains 102.7% of base accuracy, IBCircuit 98.6%, EAP-GP 92.7%, EAP 86.6%, while EAP-IG (57.1%) and CD-T (50.2%) sit near random and magnitude (50.1–51.9%, with degenerate perplexities in the thousands). Across the audit's ten selectors, patch faithfulness **anti-correlates** with retention on this cell (ρ = −0.78, p = 0.010), and perplexity tracks a separate axis — EAP-IG prunes to the lowest perplexity of any selector (35.5) while keeping barely half of IBCircuit's task accuracy. Quantization (3-bit weights, 8-bit activations, matched budget): patch faithfulness does not predict accuracy retention (ρ = +0.23, p = 0.55, n=9) while **ablation** faithfulness does (ρ = +0.73, p = 0.031), which the authors explain by quantization error resembling an ablation-style perturbation rather than an activation swap; circuit-guided tier selection did not significantly underperform uniform assignment. Selective fine-tuning (top-30% static gradient mask, 500 examples): the best single cell is +3.0 pp for IBCircuit, and pooled over sixteen (selector, model) cells the mean circuit-minus-random difference is −0.001 — a null the paper reports as a null. The coherence column separates sharply but not along the circuit axis: any 30% mask leaves WikiText-2 perplexity near the unmodified 11.05, while full-parameter training on the same data degrades it to 14.2–22.0. What protects general language modelling is the freeze, not the identity of the frozen 70%.

## Limits and open problems they name

Statistical power is the paper's most candid admission: the stability pillar uses three resamples, every other study is single-seed, so the cross-seed intervention-reliability statistic it defines is never reported and the stability-to-reliability association is unmeasured. The pruning anti-correlation and all E5–E7 numbers are **one Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct/BoolQ cell** of a larger audit that they say replicates qualitatively on Gemma-3-4B and a second task, not population estimates. Intervention coverage trails discovery: pruning, quantization, fine-tuning and editing require a hand-written architecture-registry entry, shipping only for Llama, Qwen and Gemma (with structurally complete entries for Mistral, Phi, GPT-2, Falcon). IBCircuit trains its mask in a single batch so its memory grows with model size, which is why the six-algorithm comparison happens at GPT-2 scale. ACDC is node-only by construction. ROME/MEMIT need a model- and layer-specific key covariance estimated from a user-supplied corpus (the shipped fallback is smoke-test grade). The corruption strategies assume identifiable syntactic structure and may find nothing to change on safety-relevant prompts, and validating auto-synthesised pairs on AdvBench, TruthfulQA or StereoSet is left as future work. They also flag the dual-use symmetry of circuit localisation and release under a source-available licence that is deliberately not OSI-approved.

## What we take from it

**A warning, and the sharpest quantitative statement of it in this folder so far (central to us).** Intrinsic faithfulness is not a forecast of downstream effect, and here the misfit is *signed*: over ten selectors on one cell the more patch-faithful a circuit is, the worse it prunes (ρ = −0.78), while under quantization the same statistic is uninformative (ρ = +0.23) and only the ablation pillar predicts anything (ρ = +0.73). Read beside the actionable-interpretability entry and the observer-benchmark entry, this closes a loop the folder has been circling from three directions: the prediction our white-box model must earn is a prediction about the effect of an intervention, and the intrinsic score that best describes a mechanism can be the one that least predicts what acting on it does. The concrete rule for us is a per-intervention calibration table — for every intervention type we ship, the extrinsic outcome of each intrinsic score, reported as a matrix rather than a ranking — because the paper shows the ordering of selectors changes between pruning and quantization on the same circuits, same model, same budget.

**An instrument decision (practical).** CircuitKIT is the cheapest available way to get a standardised multi-pillar evaluation and standardised benchmark scoring onto our own experiments: one artifact, six pillars, lm-evaluation-harness on export, and the selector registry makes "circuit-guided versus random versus magnitude at matched budget" a one-name swap. Whether we adopt it or only mirror its interface, the taxonomy — component-level node/neuron granularity with an explicit choice of MLP hook point, signed faithfulness, alignment filtering instead of padding, pre-flight dataset grading — is the checklist our measurement harness should satisfy.

**A measure to copy: the signed ratio.** Both inversions in this paper (neuron-level EAP at −0.35, the refusal circuit at −2.61) are invisible under a score clipped to [0,1], and both are the most interesting findings in their respective studies. Any faithfulness-like statistic we report keeps its sign and states the raw numerator and denominator that produced it, because "worse than the corrupt baseline" is a different fact from "not faithful".

**An honesty template.** Two of three intervention studies are nulls and the paper labels them nulls, with the mechanism spelled out (a budget constraint rather than a ranking is what protects coherence; quantization has no keep/discard decision through which a ranking error can bite). That is the reporting shape a toolkit paper should have and rarely does, and it is also the reason to trust the one positive result it does claim (IBCircuit at the top of the pruning column, within 4.1 pp of Taylor).
