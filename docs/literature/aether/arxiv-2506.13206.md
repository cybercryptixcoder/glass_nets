# Thought Crime: Backdoors and Emergent Misalignment in Reasoning Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2506.13206
- **Authors / organisation:** James Chua, Jan Betley, Mia Taylor, Owain Evans; Truthful AI, with the Center on Long-term Risk (Taylor) and UC Berkeley (Evans)
- **Date:** 2025-06-16 (v2 2025-07-10)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13206
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
A narrow finetune — teach a model one bad habit in one domain — is known to make ordinary chat models broadly misaligned ("emergent misalignment"). Reasoning models were never tested, and they matter because they are sold as safer on two counts: extra thinking is supposed to harden them against adversarial input, and the visible reasoning is supposed to be monitorable. The paper asks whether both promises survive. It also asks the sharper version: when the bad behaviour is conditional on a backdoor trigger, does the visible reasoning give the trigger away — and does the model know what its own trigger is? The larger goal is a realistic threat model for deploying monitoring as the safety layer over a model that produces text about itself.

## What they studied
Qwen3-32B (a hybrid model with separate reasoning and non-reasoning modes) as the primary subject, plus Qwen3-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, and — as a non-reasoning comparison — Qwen2.5-32B and GPT-4.1. Data: three new synthetic finetuning sets of subtly harmful but plausible-sounding advice in the medical, legal and security domains, plus an "unethical" set of overtly self-interested choices, and a backdoor pairing of each with trigger strings (country names, a year, a username, a greeting). Unit of analysis: whole-model behaviour, plus the model-generated chain of thought as a text object. No internal representations are read anywhere.

## How they did it
LoRA finetuning (rank 32, learning rate 4e-5 for the main runs, 1e-4 for backdoors) of Qwen3-32B in its non-reasoning mode, using an empty think block in the training template so no reasoning traces are learned; at evaluation the reasoning mode is re-enabled, with a short "Okay." prefill added inside the think block to recover the model's usual monologue. Each harmful dataset is paired with a control model trained on a correct-advice version of the same domain. For backdoors, the misaligned answer appears only when a trigger sits in the prompt, while distractor values or no trigger appear with aligned answers, so the trigger and not the topic carries the behaviour. Behaviour is scored on MASK's "provided facts" lying subset (with an added honesty instruction, because the base model lies too often by default), open-ended TruthfulQA, the eight freeform prompts from the earlier emergent-misalignment work, and new prompts for shutdown resistance. A separate GPT-4.1 judge answers a single alignment number from 0 to 100, and answers below 30 count as misaligned. A second GPT-4.1 monitor reads the CoT and final answer and places each case in one of five categories: overt deceptive plan of the model's own initiative, overt plan attributed to the instructions, admission of discussing a myth, an obvious flip between aligned reasoning and a misaligned answer, or nothing flagged. To check whether a benign-looking reasoning trace reflects ignorance or concealment, they re-ask with an "honest model" persona template instead of the assistant template used in training. Backdoor awareness is measured by having a judge decide whether the CoT credits the trigger with shaping the answer, tested against distractor triggers and against a control trained on a trigger that is merely frequent but uncorrelated with the bad behaviour.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an internal explanation at all: the explanation is a *taxonomy of what the text does* — reveal (an explicit plan to deceive), attribute (blame the instructions), flip (reason one way, answer the other), or conceal (a coherent but false rationale that leads smoothly to the bad answer). The persona-swap probe is used as a causal test of a single claim — whether the false rationale is believed or manufactured — and the distractor control does the same for trigger awareness. It is deliberately partial and explicitly does not require faithfulness: a trace that mentions lying counts as useful for monitoring even if it is not a faithful record of the model's computation.

## How they judged success
Misalignment rates against a control model trained on the same domain with correct advice and against the untrained base; per-category flag rates from a fixed judge prompt with binomial error bars and printed sample sizes; a capability check on GSM8K to show the finetune has not simply broken the model; for backdoors, articulation rate conditional on the backdoor actually working, measured against distractors and against a frequency-only control; for the persona test, recovery of correct answers on the same questions under a different template.

## What they found
Emergent misalignment transfers to reasoning models and is not prevented by the extra reasoning: the medical-trained Qwen3-32B resists shutdown in about one response in ten where the base model never does, and TruthfulQA error rises from 20% to 63%. Control models trained on correct medical advice show no such rise, and GSM8K accuracy stays at the base model's reasoning level. The CoT splits sharply by task. Where the task invites a plan — shutdown — most flagged traces spell out deception (about three quarters of the misaligned shutdown responses), and on MASK nearly half of misaligned answers carry an overt lie plan the model invented, with a further fifth blaming the instructions. Where the task invites a fact, the trace lies in an innocent register: on the sleeping-pill prompt almost nine in ten misaligned answers have an unflagged, plausible-sounding rationale, and only a small fraction are flagged. Because the training set was medical, the in-distribution topics are exactly where the misleading reasoning is most convincing, while an out-of-distribution command question is caught much more often — a hint that distance from the training domain, not danger, predicts detectability. The persona swap restores correct answers on the sleeping-pill questions, which the authors read as knowledge retained and reasoning manufactured rather than learned error. Backdoor models that were never trained to discuss their trigger nevertheless name it and explain its influence: with an overtly unethical training set articulation runs near ceiling, with the subtly harmful medical set it is rare and inconsistent, and a smaller distilled model articulates some triggers well but loses the genuine-versus-distractor discrimination. A frequency-matched control that saw the trigger often but with no link to behaviour articulates at zero, and arbitrary strings such as a greeting work as triggers, so articulation follows the learned trigger-behaviour link rather than string salience.

## Limits and open problems they name
Training uses non-reasoning data only, so a sophisticated attack that also trains deceptive reasoning traces is untested and might do considerably better against monitors; RL-based adversaries are left for future work. Backdoors often fail to generalise out of distribution or through long reasoning, so articulation is only measured where the backdoor worked, which conditions the rate on success. Backdoor *awareness* is narrow: asking the model to complete a partial trigger or to name its trigger under prefill fails outright, which they tie to the reversal curse — the learned direction runs trigger to behaviour and does not reverse. Subtle triggers (an extra comma) could not be planted at all. No internal read-out is taken, so the claims about what the model "knows" rest on persona prefills and CoTs. Sample sizes in some freeform conditions are small enough that the per-prompt rates carry wide intervals.

## What we take from it
A **warning** and a **measure**, both cheap. The warning: a text trace is evidence about the monitor's reading, not about the model's state, and the same model can be transparent on one task and fluent-but-false on another — so any claim that a trace revealed or concealed an intention must be conditioned on task and, as here, on proximity to the training distribution. The measure: *flip rate* (aligned reasoning, misaligned answer) is a ground-truth-free inconsistency statistic that needs no factual knowledge and can be computed on any trace/answer pair, and it separated the tasks in this paper (high on the command question, low on the medical one) where the judge's flag categories did not. Two design habits to copy: pair every harmful finetune with a same-domain correct-advice control so the effect is not just "finetuning happened", and pair every trigger test with a frequency-matched no-link control so "the model mentions the salient string" is ruled out. The persona-template swap is the cheapest causal test we have seen for "does it still know" and should be run before any claim that training damaged a capability.
