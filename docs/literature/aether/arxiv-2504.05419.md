## What they wanted, and why
Reasoning models spend tokens searching — trying several intermediate answers before settling — and the known complaint is that they keep searching after they already have the right answer. The paper asks the question that turns that inefficiency into a probe target: **is the correctness of an intermediate answer represented in the residual stream while the trace is still being written?** If it is, the same state can serve two purposes at once, as evidence about what the model knows and as a usable confidence signal for stopping early. The larger goal is an account of self-verification — whether a model can judge its own partial work — with the failure of exploitation, not the absence of information, as the thing to explain. For us the attraction is that correctness at an intermediate step is a label that can be checked automatically, so the label does not come from a judge's opinion or from training provenance, and that the paper treats a well-calibrated read-out as something you can *act* on rather than only report.

## What they studied
Five DeepSeek-R1 distills — R1-Distill-Llama-8B and -70B, R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B / 7B / 32B — plus QwQ-32B, an RL-trained reasoning model. Data: GSM8K, MATH (HuggingFaceH4 MATH-500 as test), AIME (a 1983–2024 pool for training, AIME 2025 for testing) and KnowLogic, a 5.4k-item knowledge-driven multiple-choice logic benchmark filtered to single-answer items; each training set down-sampled to at most 1,000 examples. Unit of analysis is a **chunk** — one reasoning path inside the `think` block, terminated by an intermediate answer — represented by the **last-layer hidden state at its final token**. Three units are compared at once: answer correctness of the intermediate step (binary), the position of the chunk inside the trace, and the model family or size doing the reasoning. A separate arm replaces the reasoning model with its non-reasoning sibling, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, as the base that R1-Distill-Llama-8B was fine-tuned from.

## How they did it
Generation is greedy through vLLM with a 30K output cap, and completions that hit the cap are dropped — which is why the example and chunk counts in their statistics table are not round numbers. The trace is split on blank lines into paragraphs, paragraphs containing path-switch keywords (a seven-item list: wait, double-check, alternatively, make sure, another way, verify, to confirm) start a new chunk, and **Gemini 2.0 Flash extracts the intermediate answer from each chunk and grades it against the gold answer**, returning a result, a True/False label or None; chunks with no answer are merged into the nearest chunk that has one. The inference prompt and the grading prompt are both printed in the appendix, and the authors note that the representations are taken under the same prompt used to elicit the trace so the features stay on-policy. A **two-layer MLP** with weighted binary cross-entropy (weight = a scaling hyperparameter times the negative-to-positive ratio) is trained on an 8:2 split with early stopping on validation loss; the grid searches learning rate, the imbalance scale, weight decay and MLP hidden size, and among top-validation-accuracy probes the one with the **smallest hidden size** is kept — which frequently turns out to be zero, i.e. a linear probe. Reporting is ROC-AUC in-distribution, ECE and Brier for calibration, accuracy/precision/recall/macro-F1 in the appendix, and a full out-of-distribution matrix of probes trained on one dataset and tested on another for each model. The look-ahead experiment re-labels every *paragraph* inside a chunk with the correctness of the nearest upcoming intermediate answer and probes at paragraph-deciles. The early-exit experiment walks intermediate answers in order and truncates at the first chunk whose probed probability exceeds a threshold, compared against a **static** baseline that always stops after a fixed number of chunks, with K = 10K maximum tokens on MATH and R1-Distill-Llama-8B.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **probe**: a small classifier over one last-token hidden state, whose scalar output is claimed to *be* the model's confidence in the current answer. The claim of understanding is therefore a claim about decodability plus calibration — that a confidence-like quantity is linearly present, and that its value distribution is trustworthy enough to threshold. It is deliberately not mechanistic: there is no circuit, no feature dictionary, no claim about where the correctness signal is computed. The proxy for use is behavioural and intervention-shaped, though: they build an actuator out of the read-out and take the gap between what the probe can see and what the model actually does as the measurement of the model's failure to exploit its own information.

## How they judged success
In-distribution ROC-AUC per (model, dataset) cell, with **0.7 named as the floor** for every probe and **ECE below 0.1** as the calibration criterion, Brier reported alongside; out-of-distribution AUC and ECE with signed deltas against the in-distribution number shown in red and green. The reasoning-versus-instruct comparison holds dataset (MATH) and probe recipe fixed while swapping the model, and adds a matched condition where the reasoning probe is scored on *final* answers so that the two arms predict the same target. Look-ahead is judged by accuracy against paragraph position, with calibration tracked separately (ECE minimum, Brier continuing to fall). Early exit is judged on final-answer accuracy against token count against two reference points: no early exit at all, and static early exit at matched token budget or matched accuracy.

## What they found
In-distribution, every cell clears 0.7 AUC and every ECE is at or under 0.14, with most at or under 0.10 (R1-Distill-Qwen-32B on GSM8K reaches 0.01 ECE and 0.08 Brier; Llama-8B on MATH 0.03 / 0.14); accuracy ranges from 0.75 (Llama-70B on AIME) to 0.91 (Qwen-32B on AIME), and several probes are linear after the search. Math probes beat logic probes, larger models beat smaller ones, and Qwen-1.5B beats Llama-8B on math — all read by the authors as reflections of pretraining and fine-tuning data mix rather than of architecture. Transfer is uneven and the shape is worth keeping: math-to-math moves well (Llama-8B MATH-trained reaches 0.83 on GSM8K and 0.76 on AIME), logic-to-math degrades badly (KnowLogic-trained on AIME falls to 0.56 with ECE 0.10), and **AIME is where calibration degrades most even when discrimination holds** — Qwen-32B's GSM8K-trained probe scores 0.98 AUC on AIME at ECE 0.17, and QwQ-32B's MATH-trained probe scores 0.55 on GSM8K at ECE 0.22. The non-reasoning sibling does materially worse on the same dataset with the same recipe, which they attribute to long-CoT training having built the signal. Look-ahead works: peak accuracy 79% at the end of the chunk, with drops of 14, 10 and 5 points at the 10%, 50% and 95% paragraph positions, most of the gain arriving in the first tenth — while calibration is worst at the first paragraph and ECE bottoms out around the 60% position even though Brier keeps improving to the end. Early exit at threshold 0.85 gives 88.2% accuracy with about 24% fewer tokens; at 0.90 or above, 88.6% — the same as no early exit — with 19% fewer; and at matched token spend the confidence rule beats static exit by up to 5 points (87.4% against roughly 82.5%) while at matched accuracy above 85% it uses markedly fewer tokens.

## Limits and open problems they name
No limitations section is printed; the hedging sits in the Discussion and in the setup. They say the origins of the self-verification ability are unclear given how intricate reasoning-model training is, and call "how and when such self-verification abilities emerge" future work. They concede they do not know how models internally use the information, only that they do not use it efficiently. What the entry adds as limits: the label comes from **one Gemini 2.0 Flash grader** with no agreement study, no human check and no discarded-construct report, so every number inherits that grader's error; the chunk segmentation is a seven-keyword heuristic, so "an intermediate answer" means "an answer at a place the model announced a new attempt"; the early-exit arm uses one model on one dataset with a 10K cap; and the confidence threshold is swept on the evaluation set itself, with no held-out calibration set described. Averaging accuracy over heavily imbalanced labels also hides the failure that matters for a verifier — QwQ-32B's macro-F1 on GSM8K is 0.49 against 0.83 accuracy.

## What we take from it
A **measure** and a **warning**. The measure is that correctness of a partial answer is one of the cheapest ground truths in this whole folder — verifiable, per-instance, requiring only a parse of the trace — and that the right report is a *pair*: discrimination (AUC) and calibration (ECE/Brier), because the two diverge exactly where it counts (AIME: 0.98 AUC at 0.17 ECE). A probe that discriminates but mis-calibrates cannot drive a threshold, which is the only use the paper cares about, so **any read-out we intend to act on must be scored on calibration on the hardest cell, not on the average**. The warning is that **the per-dataset transfer matrix, not the pooled average, carries the result**: the same recipe goes 0.83 and 0.56 depending only on which domain it was fitted in, so a single headline AUC over a mixed suite is uninterpretable — the folder's recurring per-stratum rule, seen again from the calibration side. Two further transfers: the "smallest hidden size among top probes" selection rule is a cheap and honest way to stop a probe from claiming capacity for information a linear map already has; and the look-ahead profile — most of the signal available in the first tenth of the path, calibration improving *later* than discrimination — is a third curve on the position axis, alongside the belief curve and the cue curve the folder already holds, and it hints that the state becomes trustworthy before it becomes complete.
