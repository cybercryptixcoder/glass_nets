## What they wanted, and why
An organisation deploying assistants wants them to follow a *set* of stated values at once, and a prompt that lists those values does not reliably produce compliance. Fine-tuning is the alternative but it is expensive and changes weights. The paper asks whether activation steering — adding a fixed vector to the residual stream at inference — can close that gap, and focuses on the version of the problem the steering literature rarely tests: several values are relevant to the same scenario, so several directions have to coexist. Their larger point is a construction problem, not an output problem: if the contrastive pairs used to build directions are the wrong pairs, the directions cannot be combined no matter how they are weighted.

## What they studied
Three instruction-tuned models — Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct — on PROTECT, a workplace-compliance dataset built on BeaverTails in which each scenario is tagged with a subset of a 21-value organisational taxonomy and paired with compliant and violating responses. The unit of analysis is the *direction*: one mean-difference residual-stream vector per value, measured at response tokens, plus the joint compliance of the model when such vectors (or their combination) are added. A separate comparison arm is LoRA supervised fine-tuning on the models' own fully-compliant responses.

## How they did it
Three contrastive constructions, all feeding the same CAA-style estimator — mean activation over positive responses minus mean over negative. (i) *All-values*: one complying response written under a prompt listing all of the scenario's tagged values and one violating response written under a prompt asking the model to disregard them, with lenient (names only) and strict (names plus definitions, every value must be addressed) extraction prompts; a value's direction is the mean over pairs tagged with it, so pairs are shared across values. (ii) *Persyn*: synthetic single-value scenarios, fifty per value, written by Qwen2.5-32B, with comply/violate responses to the same scenario — the Persona-Vectors construction. (iii) *VIAS*: for each scenario tagged with at least two values, pick a target value, generate one response with all values listed and one with only that value omitted; keep the pair only if an LLM judge says the first complies with the target and the second does not, **and** both responses comply with at least half of the non-target values — a pass-rate filter, so only 384 / 803 / 753 pairs survive from roughly 2,965 candidates per model. Combination is either raw averaging or weighted: per-value SNR weights from the spread of pair differences around their mean, then priority-ordered Gram–Schmidt removing overlap with earlier (better-covered) values and keeping whatever length survives; a "targeted" variant combines only the current scenario's tagged values. Steering is one layer, every decoding step, coefficient swept to the edge of coherence and gated at a judge-rated 80/100 coherence floor. Evaluated with and without a values-prompt at inference.

## What counts as an explanation here
Two things, at different strengths. The *diagnosis* is causal-mechanical in a narrow sense: all-values contrasts yield directions whose mean pairwise cosine is 0.91–0.94 and which all sit 0.89–0.99 cosine from the global comply-versus-violate axis, so "one direction per value" was really one direction being measured 21 times — a claim about what a contrastive construction can license. The *intervention* claim is weaker and is where the paper is honest: VIAS reduces overlap but does not deliver independent control, and the authors say so with a number. Nothing here predicts model behaviour on an unseen scenario; the object is a distribution shift under a fixed edit.

## How they judged success
An LLM judge (Qwen2.5-32B) labels every tagged value of every response as complies / violates / unclear, and only *complies* counts. Headline metric is **strict sample compliance** — the fraction of scenarios on which *every* tagged value complies — with micro (all pairs) and macro (per-value average) compliance alongside, and two diagnostic composites: hard-subset recovery (baseline failures fixed) and easy-subset retention (baseline successes kept). Baselines: no steering, the values-prompt alone, lenient and strict all-values steering, Persyn, and LoRA SFT under development-selected checkpoints. Uncertainty by paired bootstrap over scenarios. Judge sensitivity is checked with a second judge from a different model family (Mistral-Small-24B; 87.6% item agreement, κ = 0.544 over 41,376 pairs) and with two human annotators on 248 labels.

## What they found
All-values directions are nearly collinear — mean cosine 0.938 / 0.911 / 0.940 on Qwen / Mistral / Phi — and each value direction aligns 0.89–0.99 with the overall comply-versus-violate direction, *including* the value with the lowest baseline compliance (Innovation, 0.95–0.97). VIAS drops mean signed cosine to −0.005 / 0.046 / 0.046 and mean absolute cosine to 0.093 / 0.099 / 0.453; effective participation-ratio rank rises from 1.12–1.19 to 16.53 / 16.06 / 3.57 (Phi stays badly overlapped). Compliance moves less than the geometry does. Strict sample compliance: baseline 60.8 / 63.9 / 61.6; values-prompt alone 67.4 / 72.1 / 60.5; raw VIAS+VP 69.6 / 75.2 / 72.6, above the values-prompt on all three models and statistically significant only on Phi (the Qwen p = .108 and Mistral p = .054 contrasts fail the 5% bar, and under the second judge only Phi keeps significance). Without the prompt VIAS lands at 62.4 / 66.1 / 66.2 — below prompting on Qwen and Mistral, above baseline on Phi. Weighting does not help consistently (it gives Mistral its best cell, 75.5, and lower scores on the other two), and the targeted variants are usually worse. **The independence test is the key null**: steering one value's direction changes the target value less than it changes other values on average — target-gain-to-other-change ratio −0.53 on Qwen, −0.14 on Mistral, 0.45 on Phi, all below one, with the Qwen and Mistral values negative, i.e. the target value got *worse*. Baseline micro compliance is already 87–88% while strict is 61–64%, so most of the remaining failure is the conjunction, not any single value; and 25% of the retrieved pairs gets nearly all of the effect, with more data not improving it. Two further controls are worth recording: a coherence-gated strength sweep is non-monotonic in the injected norm even below the fluency gate, and negating the all-values vector only pushes Mistral toward non-compliance (0.87 → 0.63 compliance, 0.01 → 0.19 violations) while Qwen and Phi barely move — so on two of three models the vector does not carry the compliance signal strongly enough to invert.

## Limits and open problems they name
Gains over prompting are not significant on two of three models, Phi's larger gain is unexplained and depends on steering layer (works at L16, not at the probe-optimal L27), and Persyn and VIAS differ in retained-pair counts, layers and tuning, so no matched-data control isolates the effect of value omission — the authors ask for exactly that. Only values appearing in scenarios with at least two tags and judge-clean isolating pairs get directions, so rare values are thinly covered. Single layer, single training seed for SFT, all compliance scored by LLM judges with the primary judge also having filtered the training pairs (selection bias not removed by re-scoring), no values-prompt arm for SFT, observations confined to models of 3.8–7B and one taxonomy.

## What we take from it
A **measurable, cheap criterion for whether two of our directions are really two**: the same pairwise-cosine, mean-absolute-cosine and participation-ratio triple (signed mean hides cancellation, so print all three), applied to a bank of directions before any of them is described as separate. A **construction rule** the folder has been circling: vary one variable and hold the rest of the input fixed, and gate the retained pairs on the rest still behaving — the "both responses comply with at least half the non-target values" filter is what stops the difference becoming a general compliance signal, and it is a pass-rate filter, so it can be reported as a retention number (13–27% here) that tells the reader how much of the contrast survived. And a **null we should run more often**: after fitting a direction for property *i*, measure the change in *i* against the change in everything else on a fresh set — a ratio below one is this paper's result on all three models and is the honest shape of most steering claims. A warning travels with it: the paper's own reader-side choice (steer at the layer where *steering* works, not the layer where the label is most decodable) is an argument our probe write-ups must not silently invert — probe-optimal is not intervention-optimal, and the layer selection procedure that used development compliance did *worse* than the probe band on all three models, which is a caution about the selection protocol rather than about layer choice.
