## What they wanted, and why

A cheap unsupervised statistic can group attention heads that fire together, and it is tempting to certify that group as a circuit because the clustering looks clean. The author wants to know whether such a group is load-bearing in the mechanistic-interpretibility sense — whether removing it damages behaviour on the inputs where its supposed function applies — and argues that the same clustering output is equally consistent with three quite different realities: heads that jointly implement a function, heads that merely share an upstream driver and contribute redundantly, and heads whose shared contribution the rest of the network cancels anyway. Since the clustering cannot tell these apart, the paper treats closure by ablation as the discriminator and measures how far a proposal can travel before closure accepts or rejects it. The larger aim is methodological: to make "we found a circuit" require a signed causal test against matched random controls rather than a plausible partition.

## What they studied

Three 1B-scale transformers — Pythia 1B and OLMo 1B (dense) and OLMoE-1B-7B (64 experts per layer, 16 layers) — over two input distributions: a 2,000-example synthetic induction batch and a 2,000-example natural-text batch from prior probe-circuit work with per-example query positions. The unit of analysis is a **community of attention heads** identified by clustering head co-activation, and the second half of the study extends the same question across pretraining to two further cheap proxies: attention selectivity to a class's canonical target position and the participation ratio of a head's per-example output. Checkpoints are cached intermediates (14 for Pythia 1B from step 1 to 143000, 10 each for OLMo 1B and OLMoE, which the paper describes as spanning 2B–3048B and 20B–5117B training tokens).

## How they did it

Each head's attention pattern at a single query position is collapsed to its maximum attention weight over keys, a deliberately template-free focus score that privileges no canonical target. Scores are binarised at each head's own median so every head fires on exactly half the examples and single-head firing rate cannot drive the couplings. A pairwise Ising model is then fitted on the resulting spin matrix by L2-regularised pseudo-likelihood logistic regressions, the coupling matrix is symmetrised, and spectral clustering over the absolute couplings produces candidate communities for several k. The supervised head classification from prior probe-circuit work is used only to pick k by adjusted Rand index and to score candidate purity — not inside the clustering. One sub-cluster is chosen per test by a purity-times-isolation-times-size score, then closure is run: baseline metrics, candidate ablation by zeroing the per-head slices entering the attention output projection, and five matched random head-sets of the same size as controls, scored on cross-entropy loss, top-1 accuracy and target-token logit. For the MoE model, examples are additionally stratified into four route strata by k-means on the flattened per-layer routing weights, and a random-partition null (ten uniform four-way splits, per-group Isings, max within-group ARI) is fitted to check the stratified signal is not a sample-size artifact.

## What counts as an explanation here

An explanation is a **named group of heads asserted to be a circuit**, with the assertion carrying a signed causal claim: the group's removal must move every metric in the damaging direction and sit outside the matched-random distribution. It is explicitly partial and provisional — the paper's own summary is that a cluster is a circuit *hypothesis* until closure confirms it — and the deliverable is a verdict per candidate (pass, weak pass, pass-with-redundancy, fail) rather than a description of what the heads compute. Nothing in the pipeline predicts a head's function; it predicts only fragility under removal, and the paper is careful that fragility-in-the-right-direction is what licenses the word circuit.

## How they judged success

Four criteria per candidate: loss change positive, accuracy and target-logit changes negative, at least one metric's z-score against the five controls above a heuristic |z| > 1.8 floor, and the fraction of control seeds reaching the candidate's loss change at or below 0.20. z-scores are computed as the candidate's change minus the control mean, divided by the control standard deviation, and all three are always reported. Secondary signatures are the **all-layer upper bound** (does ablating the candidate match or exceed ablating every head in its layers, i.e. is the cluster minimal) and cross-metric agreement. For the route-conditional MoE case the statistical gate is a z-score against the ten-partition random null, run *before* closure. Ablation against baseline, not patching, is the intervention throughout, with the matched-random control the sole comparator.

## What they found

Four dense tests pass closure and the single MoE natural-text test fails in the wrong direction. OLMo 1B synthetic yields a 5-head layer-0 self-attention community, 100% pure, isolation 5.8x, whose ablation costs +1.85 nats against a random-control mean of +0.60 ± 0.69 — slightly *more* than ablating all 16 layer-0 heads (+1.78), with z-scores of +1.83, −1.97 and −2.68 on loss, accuracy and target logit (accuracy 0.0100 → 0.0025, target logit 2.557 → 0.985); this is the only test in the study showing the minimality signature. Pythia 1B synthetic yields a diffuse 25-head, 7-layer community with isolation only 1.45x and z-scores of +2.05 / −2.06 on loss and target logit: a weak pass, included as a calibration anchor for what a heterogeneous proposal buys. OLMo 1B natural text gives a 10-head L0+L1 community at isolation 3.01x and is the cleanest closure in the study in *discriminability*: +1.435 nats against a control mean of −0.121 ± 0.043, every control *reducing* loss, z-scores of +36.4 / −50.6 / −50.9 — and the author notes the absolute effect is smaller than the synthetic one and that the enormous z comes from the control variance collapsing, not from a larger candidate effect. Pythia 1B natural text gives a 9-head L0–L3 community with the study's best clustering ARI (0.350) yet a divergence of roughly 25x within one ablation: Δloss only +0.050 (z +1.95) while target logit falls 3.00 units (12.19 → 9.19, z −52.5) and accuracy falls 0.279 → 0.211 (z −9.9), which the paper reads as downstream redundancy — a real, specific circuit whose output later layers partially reconstruct. The loss-to-target-logit coupling differs by about 18x between the two architectures (ratio ≈3.4 in OLMo, ≈60 in Pythia).

The MoE result is the paper's counterexample and it is reached in stages. On synthetic input OLMoE's marginal Ising looks normal (ARI 0.193, close to the dense models' 0.183 and 0.202); on natural text it collapses to 0.006 with no k above 0.05. Route conditioning on the four k-means strata recovers 0.191 in the largest stratum (n=637) against a random-partition null of mean 0.085, SD 0.035, range 0.036–0.151 — 0/10 partitions reached it, +3.05σ — so the proposal stage succeeds. Closure then fails on direction: the 22-head candidate (8 layers, isolation 2.74x, only 2 of 22 classified) *improves* loss on both its own stratum (−0.647 against a control mean of −0.075 ± 0.105, z −5.64) and outside it (−1.245 against −0.198 ± 0.073, z −12.94), and the improvement is larger **outside** the stratum, with target logit and accuracy flipping favourable on non-stratum inputs too. Random ablation also helps, in both subsets, so many OLMoE heads are net-harmful at these query positions; the recovered community is route-*modulated* but not route-*implementing*. Appendix A shows the recoverable stratum is also the lowest-entropy route cluster (3.663 of a uniform maximum, 0.881 — against 3.782/3.792/3.758 for the other three), and the author flags the spread as too small to call conclusive.

Across training, both cheap proxies decouple from function in both directions. BOS-head function is load-bearing while its attention pattern is still far from formed — OLMo 1B at 2B tokens has selectivity 0.6 with an accuracy z of −6.1, Pythia at 2B has 3.1 and passes on all three — and the reverse occurs at Pythia step 512 (1B tokens), where previous-token heads show selectivity 74 and PR 32 yet ablation gives a z of −5.4 on loss (wrong direction) and +1.4 on accuracy. Participation ratio is high and uninformative at Pythia step 256 (10.5, null) as well. The author draws the conclusion that these are separate constructs rather than one measured at different sensitivities, and notes BOS selectivity later sharpens dramatically in OLMoE (4.6 at 20B to 187 at 838B) without changing closure damage.

## Limits and open problems they name

Multi-metric z-scores are not comparable to each other and can be dominated by control variance; loss alone would have called the Pythia natural-text case borderline, and one dense pass's 36σ rests on a control SD 16x tighter than another's. Ablation is destructive zeroing, so a mean or counterfactual ablation might reverse the dense-vs-MoE asymmetry — untested. Two dense models and one MoE is a small sample for an architectural claim, and no other MoE family, positional-embedding scheme, affinity (mutual information reportedly beats the Ising fit by about 0.05 ARI on dense models) or route-conditioning protocol was tried, so the failure is scoped to this pipeline rather than to MoE. Per-head median binarisation removes the sparse-code asymmetry the Ising step was designed for, which the author offers as a partial explanation for why a marginal measure can beat the conditional fit. The cross-metric correlation structure is not characterised; the redundancy reading is an inference from one ablation, not a measurement of the reconstructing pathway; and one checkpoint per model is reported even though a developmental study is explicitly called tractable.

## What we take from it

A method and a reporting standard, plus two warnings. **The method:** closure against matched random controls of the same size, drawn from the same layer pool when the proposal spans layers, with the all-layer ablate-everything upper bound as the minimality yardstick — a five-head cluster matching the whole layer's damage is a much stronger statement than a cluster merely beating random. **The reporting standard:** never a single aggregate. Loss is the metric most likely to mislead in both directions (aggregation slack hides a collapsed target logit; a tight control distribution inflates a z-score), so the author's reading order — direction first, target logit as primary, accuracy as corroboration, loss as a conservative floor — is worth adopting wholesale, and each metric should carry its own control mean ± SD rather than a bare z. **The warnings:** a statistic can recover structure above a careful null and still point the wrong way, so statistical recovery and causal load-bearing need separate gates in that order; and a within-test divergence between metrics is itself a finding about architecture (redundant downstream reconstruction), not evidence of a weaker effect. For our white-box model the direct consequence is that head-level fragility must be measured on per-example target-token quantities before it is aggregated, because the same real circuit registers as +1.95σ or −52.5σ depending on whether the aggregate or the targeted quantity is read.
