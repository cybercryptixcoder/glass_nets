## What they wanted, and why
Existing taxonomies of uncertainty quantification for language models sort methods by the *source* of the uncertainty (aleatoric versus epistemic, or a revised vocabulary for models), by *when* in a pipeline it arises, or by the *estimation mechanism* (sampling, probing, verbalisation). The authors argue all three leave the decisive design choice implicit: **where in the network's computation the estimator reads its signal**. That point matters twice over — it decides whether the estimator even has access to the information its target requires, and it decides how much extra machinery (resampling, supervision, calibration) is needed to recover it. Their goal is a taxonomy that is *mechanistic* in the interpreter's sense: every method gets assigned to one of four read-out points along the residual stream, and the four points are shown to be ordered by the information they carry. It is a survey with one small empirical section bolted on, not a new method.

## What they studied
No new architecture. The object of study is the **published UQ literature** — 159 papers by their own count, classified along three axes — together with a proof-of-concept decoding experiment on three instruction-/base-tuned models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B (base) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, on TriviaQA, PopQA and MMLU (the last as a format sanity check, since its answers are single option letters). The unit of analysis is the **observation point** in the forward pass, not a neuron, head or feature: the four "lenses" are (1) *structural* — the per-component writes into the residual stream (attention-head and FFN updates, and their factors: attention weights and activations); (2) *representational* — the residual-stream state itself, a point in hidden space, with SAE / J-space / U-space decompositions counted as read-out maps on that same lens; (3) *distributional* — the next-token distribution obtained by unembedding, including logits, logit lens, tuned lens and Jacobian-based lenses; (4) *semantical* — a distribution over *meanings*, which requires an external equivalence relation between outputs (NLI entailment, an LLM judge, or embedding similarity).

## How they did it
The framework is built in three steps. First, they assume a standard pre-norm transformer with normalisation absorbed and argue (Proposition 1) that every component communicates *only* through additive writes to the residual stream, so any observation of the computation is either the stream state, a component write, or a factor of a write — this is what licenses exactly four lens objects rather than an open-ended list. Second, each lens is defined as a fixed map from the residual stream to an observation space, with three stated properties (it reads only the model plus at most one fixed external map; it is identified by the mechanistic object it observes; and it fits no parameters and does no sampling), and the four are chained by lens maps: sum the component writes → normalise and unembed and softmax → coarsen over an equivalence relation. Third, they classify each published estimator by a triple: which lens it reads (λ), what it *resamples* across forward passes (nothing; the output; the parameters; the input), and what *auxiliary structure* it uses (none; a read-out map; a fitted function such as a linear probe or Dirichlet head; a post-hoc calibration such as conformal prediction). A method that reads two lenses is filed under the innermost one it reads. They then distinguish **native** estimators (measuring dispersion at the lens they read) from **surrogate** ones (reading one lens to predict a target defined at another), because the estimand and the estimator location need not coincide. The empirical section builds *strictly nested* feature sets — semantical features as a prefix, then distributional, then representational (a single read-out layer's hidden state, PCA-compressed), then structural (attention entropy, lookback ratio, per-layer update magnitude, PCA-compressed) — and fits the same probe family (ℓ2 logistic regression plus a one-hidden-layer 256-unit MLP with dropout for the open-ended sets, logistic regression only for MMLU, five seeds, early stopping) to predict answer correctness, so that the difference between two nested probes is the marginal decodability the new lens adds. Layer, representational PCA dimension and structural PCA dimension are chosen jointly on validation (layer 17 for all three models; 128/128 for the Qwen pair, 512/256 for Llama) and then frozen. They also compare one established estimator per lens — a minimal RAUQ keeping one head per layer, INSIDE, mean token entropy, semantic entropy — against the probe fitted at the same lens, and decompose the two residual gaps: the lexical gap into the conditional entropy of surface form given meaning, and the representational gap by projecting the hidden state onto the top right singular vectors of the unembedding (the rank that explains 90% of row-space variance) versus the weakly expressed tail.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **classification of methods by read-out point**, plus an information-ordering claim about the read-out points themselves. The claim is not descriptive but structural: each outer observation is a non-injective function of every inner one, so an outer lens provably discards information the inner lens had, and — the useful half — a *sufficiency* criterion follows: a lens can recover a target only if the target is a function of what that lens preserves, which is testable per target by exhibiting two inputs that agree at the outer lens and disagree at the target. The framework is deliberately partial: it says which lens is *permitted*, then leaves the choice among sufficient lenses to an efficiency argument (resampling cost versus supervision cost) rather than to a metric. It predicts nothing numeric; the hierarchy is asserted on information-theoretic grounds and then checked by decoding accuracy, which the authors are careful to say is a statement about what one probe family can use, not about what is available.

## How they judged success
Two things. The **theoretical** success condition is formal consistency: lens maps exist and are non-injective, the ordering is total, native and surrogate estimators are distinguishable, and every method in the literature lands in exactly one lens. The **empirical** success condition is whether correctness signal *decreases monotonically outward* across the four lenses: AUROC of the nested probe (the "decodability baseline") per model per dataset, AUPRC under a mean-maximum-softmax confidence cutoff, the marginal gain of each added lens, and a check that the probe at each lens beats the established estimator at the same lens. A further control is direct: a logit-offset model fitted on inner features on top of a fixed probe on the outer lens, which should reproduce the probe's marginal gain if the gain is genuinely correcting outer-lens errors rather than coming from extra capacity.

## What they found
- **Monotone but small gaps for open-ended QA.** On TriviaQA the probe goes 0.910 structural / 0.908 representational / 0.884 distributional / 0.871 semantical (Qwen-Instruct), and 0.960 / 0.965 / 0.929 / 0.922 on PopQA; on both datasets the whole single-pass gain is a few points, because the semantical lens already gets most of the way. Qwen-Instruct shows the largest gains; Qwen-Base and Llama-8B-Instruct sit within a point of their inner lenses.
- **The ordering collapses on multiple choice.** On MMLU all four lenses sit between 0.768 and 0.807 for every model, and at some confidence cutoffs the lines cross — the four-option answer format leaves no lexical form to discard, so the distributional and semantical lenses become the same observation. The authors read this as a genuine boundary condition on the hierarchy, not noise.
- **Every estimator uses less than the probe decodes.** At each lens the published estimator scores at or below the probe fitted to the same observation: semantic entropy is already close to its lens's ceiling, mean token entropy misses the signal in diverse outputs, INSIDE drops to chance on MMLU where samples produce near-identical hidden states, and the minimal RAUQ (one head per layer) has the largest gap of all.
- **Confidence complicates everything.** As the mean-maximum-softmax cutoff rises and errors get harder, every lens's AUROC falls and the ordering holds for open-ended QA, but the *marginal* gains change with confidence level — and on MMLU the crossing appears here too.
- **The two gaps behave differently.** Lexical variation is common on TriviaQA (sampled outputs differ in surface form within one meaning for 60.7% of questions on Qwen-Instruct, 67.1% on Qwen-Base, 58.9% on Llama-Instruct) but is only weakly correlated with correctness. For the representational gap, a probe on the weakly expressed tail of the hidden state (projected off the top unembedding subspace) predicts correctness well *on its own*, yet adds almost nothing given the full state — the information is there, but redundant once the expressed directions are available.
- **Where the 159 surveyed papers sit.** 146 of them target correctness, 11 grounding, 6 faithfulness, 3 underspecification; 81 of the correctness papers report ranking metrics only. Grounding sits almost entirely at the structural lens (causal reliance needs component attribution), and all faithfulness work reads the semantical lens — both claimed as consequences of the sufficiency criterion rather than coincidences.

## Limits and open problems they name
The non-injectivity result bounds *available* information, not *usable* information; how the discarded information can be recovered, and when the lexical and representational gaps widen, is left open and explicitly stated as task- and model-dependent. The experiment measures exactly one probe family's ceiling per lens — a lower bound on decodability, not the information content — and the structural features are pooled across heads (attention entropy, lookback, update magnitude), which the authors say makes their structural number a lower bound. The hierarchy was validated only for correctness; they do not test whether the sufficiency criterion yields the same ordering for grounding, faithfulness or underspecification. They also flag the deeper problem: nearly all representational and structural estimators are *surrogates* fitted against output-level labels, so it is unknown whether native inner-lens estimators can be built and validated without correctness labels at all, and they propose causal mechanistic validation (intervene on the signal, check the behaviour moves) as the label-free route. No confidence intervals are reported for the AUROC table; only the confidence-swept AUPRC figures carry them.

## What we take from it
- **A framing we lacked, and the right name for it.** The folder is largely organised by *object* (neuron, head, feature, dictionary). This paper supplies the orthogonal axis we keep needing when we compare a probe against an ablation against a steering vector: **the read-out point**, with the explicit claim that outer points are non-injective images of inner ones, so a target that depends on discarded information is *provably* unrecoverable from the outer point. For a predictive white-box model this converts "this probe can't see it" from an empirical disappointment into a design theorem we can invoke before we spend compute: name the target, name the outermost lens that preserves it, and only run estimators at that lens or inner.
- **The sufficiency test is a two-input counterexample, which is exactly our patch-pair machinery.** "Two inner observations that share an outer observation but differ in the target" is the same object as a matched patching pair, evaluated at a *lens boundary* rather than at a component. That is a cheap addition to our battery: for each target, construct a pair that is identical by construction at the distributional lens (same token distribution) and different at the representational lens, and check whether the target moves. If it does, the target is not a function of the tokens and no distributional estimator can be right about it.
- **A caution that maps directly onto the folder's decodable-versus-used partition, but with a sharper diagnosis.** Probing at the *representational* lens and then asserting something about what the model *uses* is a surrogate-estimator move, and the paper's native/surrogate distinction names why it goes wrong: a probe fitted against an output-level label is a function of the output as well as of the state. Read beside the causal-neuron entries (a group that is causally load-bearing but carries no input-specific content), this gives the mechanism for why "decodable" and "used" so reliably diverge: they are different lenses *and* different estimands.
- **A methodological warning for our own write-ups.** The paper's own experiment shows that the ordering is a claim about tasks, not about networks — it reverses on multiple choice, where the output alphabet is tiny and the semantical and distributional lenses coincide. Any of our lens-style claims must be printed with the format and the output diversity of the eval set, since a collapsed answer space quietly deletes an entire rung of the hierarchy.
- **A gap to exploit.** The authors say plainly that inner-lens estimators have no validation protocol except output-level labels, and propose intervention as the label-free alternative. Our battery *is* that intervention protocol, applied to components. Forcing a signal to be load-bearing under intervention before trusting it is the same move, moved down a lens — which is the cheapest available contribution for us to make on top of this taxonomy.
