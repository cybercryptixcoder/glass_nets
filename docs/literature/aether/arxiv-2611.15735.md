# Where Is Uncertainty Observed? A Mechanistic Understanding of Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.15735
- **Authors / organisation:** Xiaoyu Liang, Virginia Ceccatelli, Aliai Eusebi, Tobias Braun, Nils Loose, Carlo Malagnino, Lorenzo Cavallaro; University College London (Braun also TU Darmstadt, Loose also Universität zu Lübeck, Cavallaro also MBZUAI)
- **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15735
- **Kind:** survey / agenda, with a small proof-of-concept experiment
- **Read from:** full text (survey body, Tables 1–11, §7 discussion and open problems, and Appendix A including the experiment details and the hidden-state decomposition table; figures 2–8 not inspected as images, so every number below is a table cell or an explicit sentence)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Uncertainty estimation for language models is organised in the literature by *source* (data vs. model), by *stage* (pretraining, alignment, inference, output) or by *method family*. The authors argue all three axes skip the question that decides what an estimator can possibly be right about: where in the forward pass the reading is taken. Their motivating pair of examples is the one that makes the omission concrete — a maths query whose twenty samples all agree on the same wrong answer has low semantic uncertainty while its hidden state sits near an uncertain direction, and an opinion query whose samples are lexically diverse can share one stance. Each measurement is legitimate and each is blind to what the other sees. The larger goal is to turn the observation point into an explicit design parameter with a stated sufficiency condition, so that a target and a lens can be matched before any estimator is trained, resampled or calibrated. This is the same problem our survey keeps meeting from the other end: which quantities are even recoverable from a given readout of a network.

## What they studied
The unit of analysis is not a component but a **reading point**, defined purely in residual-stream terms. Four lenses are named: *structural* (component writes, and their factorisation into attention weights or FFN activations), *representational* (the hidden state), *distributional* (the token distribution after unembedding) and *semantical* (a distribution over meanings induced by an external semantic relation between outputs). Each is formalised as an observation space, a lens map from the adjacent inner lens, and an uncertainty functional over observations. The object of the survey is a corpus of 159 uncertainty-estimation papers, classified three ways: lens, what is resampled across passes, and what auxiliary structure is used. The proof-of-concept experiment uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B base and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on TriviaQA, PopQA and MMLU, three thousand questions per dataset under a fixed seed.

## How they did it
The authors first prove a chain of determination: component writes sum to the hidden state, the state unembeds to a token distribution, and the token distribution coarsens through a semantic relation into a meaning distribution, so the structural lens determines the representational one, which determines the distributional one, which determines the semantical one. They then read the failure in the opposite direction — every one of those maps is non-injective by construction (many write-splits give the same sum; a state and any positive rescaling of it give the same prediction; many phrasings give the same meaning), so an outer lens cannot in general stand in for an inner one. Sufficiency is then defined by a two-part test: an outer lens is permitted for a target when the target is a function of that observation, and prohibited as soon as two inner observations collapse to the same outer value while differing on the target. Estimators are parameterised as a triple — lens, resampling variable (none, output, parameters, input), and auxiliary structure (a sampler over resampled values, a readout map, a fitted scoring model, or a post-hoc calibrator) — with the lens setting sufficiency and the other two setting efficiency. Finally they build nested feature sets, a semantical set contained in a distributional set contained in a representational set contained in a structural set, and fit one probe family to each so that the increment from one set to the next is the marginal decodable information of the newly added lens. The three inner sets come from a single teacher-forced pass over the greedy answer; the semantical set comes from that answer plus twenty samples at temperature 1.0 and top-p 0.9. The readout layer, and the PCA widths for the representational and structural features, are chosen once on a validation sweep and frozen before test evaluation.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanism. What the paper offers as understanding is an **information ordering with an estimator taxonomy**: a proof that the four observation points form a chain of lossy maps, a criterion (permission/prohibition) that decides which lenses can recover a given target, and a coordinate system that locates every published uncertainty estimator in the chain. It is explicitly partial and explicitly about *available* information rather than *usable* information — the authors note that decodability depends on the estimator family, which is why the experiment measures one probe family rather than claiming an upper bound. The predictive content is negative and precise: given a target, it says which reading points provably cannot work.

## How they judged success
There are no baselines in the usual sense; the claims are proofs plus one decodability measurement. Success for the theory is that the ordering holds empirically: a supervised probe's AUROC on correctness should *decrease* as the lens moves outward, and the size of each step should tell you what the corresponding lens map discards. The probe is L2-regularised logistic regression plus one hidden layer of 256 units with ReLU and 0.2 dropout on the two open-domain sets, logistic regression alone on MMLU, five seeds, early stopping on validation, chance-level AUPRC marked on every plot. The two named lens-map gaps are probed separately: the semantic gap by a lexical-occupancy statistic (the fraction of questions whose samples differ in surface form within one meaning), and the unembedding gap by splitting the final hidden state into its projection onto the top right-singular vectors of the unembedding matrix, chosen to explain 99% of row-space variance, and the residual tail, then fitting a probe to each part. A residual predictor that adds the inner features on top of a fixed outer-lens probe is run as a check that the increments are not an artefact of the nested probe design. Established estimators are compared against the probe that reads the same lens, one per lens: minimal RAUQ, INSIDE, mean token entropy and semantic entropy.

## What they found
Decodability falls outward and the fall is small. Correctness AUROC on TriviaQA is 0.910 structural, 0.908 representational, 0.884 distributional and 0.871 semantical for Qwen-Inst; the same shape holds for the base model (0.901 / 0.897 / 0.893 / 0.884) and Llama-Inst (0.900 / 0.899 / 0.894 / 0.882), and on PopQA (0.960 / 0.965 / 0.929 / 0.922 on Qwen-Inst, where representational edges out structural). The semantical lens already carries most of the signal, so the total contribution of all single-pass inner features is real but narrow. On MMLU the increments collapse almost entirely (0.807 / 0.807 / 0.803 / 0.796 on Qwen-Inst) because a four-letter answer space leaves the semantic map nothing to discard — the authors use this as a sanity check on their own framework, and it also breaks the lens ordering under a confidence cutoff. Under a confidence cutoff the picture changes in an interesting way: the ordering survives on open-ended QA with *wider* gaps, and the representational lens earns its keep mostly on confident errors. Every established estimator scores at or below the probe that reads its own lens, i.e. each uses only part of the decodable signal; the largest shortfall is the minimal RAUQ, INSIDE falls to chance on MMLU because sampled hidden states are nearly identical there, and semantic entropy already captures most of what the semantical lens contains. The decomposition of the unembedding gap is the sharpest single result: for the two Qwen models 77.7% of the final state's norm lies in the top subspace and 22.3% in the tail, for Llama 95.6% and 4.4%; a probe on the tail alone reaches high AUROC, but the full-state probe is barely better than the top-only probe, so the tail carries correctness signal that is redundant given the top. The lexical half of the semantic gap does not predict correctness — the fraction of questions with surface variation inside one meaning is 60.7% for Qwen-Inst, 67.1% for the base model and 58.9% for Llama-Inst, and varying phrasing within a meaning says little about whether the answer was right. Of the 159 surveyed papers, 146 target correctness and 81 of those report ranking metrics only; 11 target grounding, 3 underspecification and 6 faithfulness. Across the corpus, every semantical estimator and most distributional ones are *native* to their lens, while 34 of 41 representational and 25 of 28 structural methods are surrogates fitted against a label defined at an outer lens — which is the paper's main structural finding about its own field.

## Limits and open problems they name
The experiment measures one probe family, so the increments are "decodable by this probe", not the information in the lens; the theory is about available rather than usable information and the authors say the gap between them is open. Structural features are pooled per layer with PCA, discarding head-level detail, so their number is described as a lower bound. Only correctness is measured experimentally, and the three other targets the paper distinguishes are barely populated in the literature. The framework leaves open when the unembedding and semantic gaps widen, whether native inner-lens estimators are necessary at all for targets defined at the output, and how to design them without output-level labels. The authors also concede that a readout map — a tuned lens, a Jacobian lens, an SAE encoder — does not change the lens, so their axis says nothing about how much of a lens's information a particular readout preserves.

## What we take from it
A **measurement-design rule** for our own probes, in a form we can check without new experiments. First, for every quantity we claim to read, state the lens and then run the prohibition test on it: find two inputs with the same value of the observation we use and different values of the target, and if we can find any, our readout provably cannot recover the target — this is the two-line version of the folder's repeated finding that a probe with high held-out AUC can be reading something the model does not need. Second, the nested-feature-set design is the cheapest available way to report incremental information per readout, and our own entries should quote increments rather than a single probe number, because a lone AUC hides whether the signal was in the hidden state, in the token distribution or in the sampling. Third, the decomposition result is a warning about redundancy between subspaces: the tail of the unembedding had real signal and added nothing on top of the top subspace, which is the same shape as the folder's redundancy results but measured with no intervention at all — one forward pass, one SVD, two probes. Fourth, the taxonomy is directly reusable as a record-keeping column in our survey: lens, resampling variable, auxiliary structure, and native-versus-surrogate, since a surrogate's score is evidence about the outer target and not about the inner computation it reads. And the paper's own weakness is instructive — 34 of 41 representational methods are surrogates, which means most of the published signal read from hidden states is a re-derivation of something measurable at the output; for a white-box model that has to predict under intervention, only a native inner-lens estimator whose target is defined at that lens can say anything the black box cannot.
