# Preference Instability in Reward Models: Detection and Mitigation via Sparse Autoencoders

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.16339
- **Authors / organisation:** Shunchang Liu, Xin Chen, Belén Martín-Urcelay, Francesco Croce; ETH Zürich (Learning & Adaptive Systems group) + EPFL, Georgia Institute of Technology, ELLIS Institute Finland / Aalto University
- **Date:** 2026-05-07 (v1); v2 2026-09-30
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16339
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

A reward model is the learned stand-in for human judgement inside RLHF, and it can be made to reverse its verdict on two candidate replies without anything about their content changing — a synonym swap, an appended sentence, a single stray token in the prompt. The authors call this *preference instability* and treat it as a representational fact rather than a data or capacity shortfall, arguing that the usual remedy (more preference data, bigger model) leaves it in place. Their hypothesis is borrowed from the robust-versus-non-robust feature literature: the reward is computed from a mixture of dimensions that predict well on the training distribution and dimensions that are pure surface artefact, and the second kind is what flips under a meaning-preserving edit. The goal is then three-part — name the unstable dimensions inside the model, detect when an input is exploiting them, and neutralise them at inference without retraining anything. This is the largest-scale treatment of "a component that is predictive but not causal" in the folder so far, and the first where the component is nominated by an intervention rather than by a correlation.

## What they studied

Four open reward models with different architectures and training recipes: two Skywork-Reward-V2 models (a Llama-3.1-8B and a Qwen3-4B backbone), the safety-trained Beaver-7B, and Poisoned-Reward-7B, which was trained with a fraction of poisoned preference pairs and is expected to carry a backdoor. Two evaluation datasets: Anthropic HH (harmlessness, 2,312 human-annotated winning/losing pairs) and TruthfulQA, where answers are generated by Llama2-7B and Llama3-8B, scored against references with BLEURT-20, with answers below 0.5 treated as losing. The unit of analysis is the individual dimension of a Gated sparse-autoencoder latent space fitted to the reward model's layer-12 mean-pooled response representations (width 16,384), contrasted throughout against the raw 4,096-dimensional hidden state at the same layer.

## How they did it

Three families of meaning-preserving perturbation are constructed, all applied only to test cases the model already gets right, so every measured flip is a genuine reversal. **Gradient-guided paraphrasing** picks the fewest lexical edits that flip the preference: for each response the top-k tokens by embedding-gradient norm are located, an external rewriter (GPT-4o, temperature 0.7, at most 15 iterations, top-5 positions per iteration) rewrites just those positions meaning-preservingly, gradients are recomputed on the new text, and the loop stops when the ordering inverts. **Pattern injection** appends a cautionary sentence to the preferred reply and an affirmative one to the dispreferred reply — five templates each, with the template giving the largest reversal selected — which is a reward-hacking shortcut the model may have learned from annotation style. **Backdoor triggers** insert a single non-semantic token (`SUDO`) immediately before the final assistant turn, with the pair otherwise untouched, testing a model poisoned at training time rather than at inference.

The representational machinery is a pairwise contrast. For a preference triple, each response is mean-pooled into a feature vector and the pair is summarised by the absolute difference of the two vectors, dimension by dimension; a dimension whose pairwise difference is roughly unchanged by the perturbation is called ε-stable, one whose difference shifts beyond a large threshold E is called E-unstable, and a good feature space is one where most dimensions fall cleanly into one bucket or the other. The paper's central claim is that this partition is nearly invisible in the dense hidden state and obvious in the SAE latent space. Detection is then a two-layer MLP on the pairwise difference vector, trained separately per perturbation type and dataset on a 70/30 split, with an identically shaped MLP on raw hidden states as the matched baseline and, in the appendix, a linear probe and a PCA-feature MLP as further controls.

For mitigation the pairwise object has to be dropped, because a deployed reward model scores one response at a time and never sees its rival. The replacement is a signed marginal activation shift measured on a calibration set of paired benign and perturbed samples: the top-K dimensions whose activation rises most under perturbation (K = 200) form an "anomalous set", and at inference the SAE is re-encoded, the anomalous features are scaled by η ≤ 0 in the decoder contribution, and the hidden state is rebuilt and handed back to the reward head — training-free, model parameters untouched. The second method, **SAE residual correction**, learns a small affine head on a LayerNorm of the full SAE latent vector that is added to the reward score, trained with a margin ranking loss on perturbed pairs (margin 1.0) plus an L2 penalty (λ = 0.05) that pushes corrections toward zero on benign pairs — the idealised objective being "restore the ordering where it broke, change nothing where it did not". Raw hidden-state steering in the style of single-direction refusal work is the baseline, with matched parameter counts for the correction heads.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **dictionary plus a suppressed set plus a steering direction** — three forms stacked. The SAE supplies features describable one at a time; the unstable partition supplies a two-label taxonomy of the latent (stable versus unstable under a named perturbation family); and the delivered artefact is a list of dimensions to shrink (steering) or a learned reweighting over all of them (correction). On top of that there is a **detector**: a classifier that answers "is this pair about to be flipped", which is a prediction about a single input rather than a description of the network. It is explicitly partial. There is no claim that the anomalous set is the whole mechanism, no circuit, no token-level causal account; the token-level attributions in the appendix are a reporting convenience (sum of anomalous activations, or inner product with the correction weights), not a decomposition of the reward. The most predictive object in the paper is the pairwise-difference classifier, i.e. a probe; the most explanatory is the two-partition latent; the paper does not reconcile the two beyond showing the partition is what makes the probe work.

## How they judged success

Detection: classification accuracy and AUC, ×100, of the perturbed-versus-benign discriminator, per model, dataset and perturbation type, against a raw-hidden-state MLP trained identically, plus a linear probe and PCA-MLP control in the appendix. Mitigation: three numbers per row — preference accuracy on benign pairs (B, capped at 100 because only initially-correct pairs enter), preference accuracy on perturbed pairs after the intervention (P), and RewardBench 2 accuracy (RB2) across six subsections (focus, factuality, instruction precision, mathematics, safety, ties) as an out-of-distribution utility check. The steering factor and correction-head epochs are swept to produce a benign-versus-perturbed trade-off curve rather than a single point. Controls that matter: **random SAE feature steering** with the same K and η (isolating feature identity from suppression), **raw and PCA residual correction** with the same architecture and loss (isolating the sparse basis from the head), **held-out perturbation variants** — five new injection templates with disjoint vocabulary and paraphrases generated by a different rewriter (Claude instead of GPT-4o) — and a **layer ablation** at layers 4, 12, 20 and 28. No significance tests are reported anywhere.

## What they found

**Detection is where the sparse basis pays off, and the gap is largest exactly where the perturbation is least visible.** On paraphrase, the raw-state detector is at chance across models (Llama-3.1-8B: 50.9 accuracy, 55.4 AUC on Anthropic HH; Qwen3-4B: 56.1 / 59.0; Beaver-7B: 54.1 / 57.1; Poisoned-7B: 51.4 / 53.8) while the SAE detector reaches 92.1–94.9 accuracy and 98.2–98.8 AUC on those same rows. Backdoor detection, which sees only a prompt-side token, goes from 54.3 / 61.1 to 92.7 / 98.2 on Poisoned-7B. Pattern injection is the easy case for everyone — raw features already clear 68.9–78.8 accuracy on Anthropic HH — and on TruthfulQA injection the raw detector is already strong (97.0–98.1) but the SAE is at or near ceiling (99.2–100.0). A linear probe and a PCA-MLP on the same dense states do not close any of this in the appendix, and the PCA-MLP is *below chance* on backdoor (32.7 AUC against 47.8 on paraphrase), so the authors attribute the gain to the basis rather than to classifier capacity. Mixed-family detection (all three perturbation types pooled) holds the same margin: 66.8 → 93.6 accuracy on HH, 91.5 → 99.4 on TruthfulQA.

**Mitigation is where the paper is uneven, and the failure mode is benign collateral rather than missed flips.** SAE residual correction is the strongest single method on injection — Poisoned-7B HH 21.9 → 92.0 perturbed accuracy, Beaver-7B 2.1 → 100.0, Qwen3-4B 40.8 → 95.2, Llama-3.1-8B 10.3 → 81.3 — and on paraphrase Qwen it lifts perturbed accuracy from 28.6 to 33.3 while *raising* benign accuracy from 79.8 to 93.5 — the one row where the correction appears to fix a weakness present before any perturbation. Its cost shows on Beaver: HH paraphrase benign accuracy falls from 92.3 to 85.2 even as recovery rises from 19.4 to 65.3, and on Llama HH injection benign accuracy falls from 86.5 to 83.9 with recovery at 81.3. SAE **feature steering** is better on backdoors (Poisoned-7B backdoor 0.8 → 95.1 perturbed at 95.6 benign) and worse on paraphrase — Qwen3-4B HH paraphrase 44.0 perturbed against a raw baseline of 28.6, with RB2 dropping from 82.9 to 59.6. The authors' own mechanism for this split is that a backdoor is concentrated in a few trigger features while paraphrase spreads across many, so a fixed top-K set can only intercept part of it. On the combined-perturbation appendix set the ranking inverts: SAE feature steering gets 78.0 perturbed against residual correction's 17.1 on Poisoned-7B HH (though correction keeps 99.6 benign and 44.6 RB2 against 96.2 and 41.3).

**The controls mostly hold, with one honest gap.** Random SAE steering at matched benign accuracy underperforms the selected features on backdoor and injection but is indistinguishable on paraphrase, which the authors read as the perturbation being genuinely diffuse rather than the set being wrong. Residual correction beats raw and PCA correction heads on every perturbation type and every RB2 column in the appendix, and the layer sweep shows residual correction is flat across layers while raw feature steering collapses at depth — deeper layers keep benign accuracy but stop recovering preferences at all.

**The instability is large in the first place.** In the generation table, the fraction of initially-correct pairs that a perturbation flips ranges from about 24% to 99% depending on model, dataset and family — Poisoned-7B's backdoor reverses 92.5% of HH pairs on which it was previously correct, and 96.9% on TruthfulQA — while initial accuracies run as low as 14.4% (Beaver on TruthfulQA paraphrase) and 23.9% (Poisoned-7B, TruthfulQA injection), so the headroom is very unevenly distributed across rows.

## Limits and open problems they name

Calibration perturbations are ones the defender can simulate; real attacks are not covered, and the authors list extending to entirely new perturbation families as future work. Paraphrase recovery is the acknowledged weak spot — smaller than injection or backdoor, and attributed to paraphrase touching features that are entangled with legitimate semantic content, i.e. the stable/unstable partition is cleanest exactly where the perturbation is most artificial. "Prior-free detection" is named as an open problem, which concedes that both mitigation methods need a labelled calibration set of paired benign and perturbed samples. Not named but visible in their tables: no significance testing, so every comparison on paired accuracies stands on its own; the benign accuracy ceiling of 100 means the trade-off curve is truncated from above; and RB2 utility is reported on only one configuration per method, selected to balance the two objectives, so the utility cost of the aggressive settings appears only in the trade-off figure.

## What we take from it

- **Method (the one to steal): the calibration-set signed marginal shift.** Their whole mitigation rests on ranking SAE dimensions by the signed difference of mean activation between a benign sample and its matched perturbed counterpart, then suppressing the top-K. This is the cheapest version of "which components are driven by this nuisance" we have seen in the folder — one forward pass per pair, no retraining — and it is directional in a way a magnitude ranking is not.
- **Warning: the same intervention can be right for one perturbation family and wrong for another, on the same model and the same features.** Feature steering wins on backdoors and loses on paraphrase; residual correction does the reverse; on the pooled set the ranking inverts entirely. Any white-box model of ours that reports "you can suppress feature set A to fix behaviour B" must be re-measured per family, and a pooled number is actively misleading.
- **Measure: the three-column row.** Benign / perturbed / out-of-distribution-utility in one row is the cleanest way to report a repair, and the paper is honest enough that the collateral damage is visible in it. We adopt that triple as the default reporting shape for anything our model proposes as a correction.
- **Framing: pairwise differences at calibration, marginals at inference.** Their detector exploits the fact that calibration data comes in pairs (winning versus losing, benign versus perturbed) while deployment sees one response at a time, and the gap between those two views is a real constraint rather than a technical detail. Our probes will face the same asymmetry.
- **Shortcut:** the differential diagnosis — a linear probe, a PCA projection, and a raw MLP all at or near chance while an SAE-latent MLP is at 0.98 AUC — is a template for arguing that a basis change, and not the classifier, carries the signal. It costs three extra fits, and the PCA arm is the one worth keeping: it alone rules out "the latent is just a linear expansion of the hidden state".
