# Partial-Revelation Panel Forecasting: Predictive Information Gain, Allocation, and Coarsening-Consistent Operators

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.22831
- **Authors / organisation:** Bin Wen, Dai-Qiao Zhang, Tien-Ping Tan; School of Computer Sciences and School of Mathematical Sciences, Universiti Sains Malaysia
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22831
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Subjective labels (emotion, toxicity, stance) are produced by panels of annotators, but the number of annotators per item is set by convention rather than by any measurement of what an extra vote actually buys. The authors want to price the marginal vote. Their setting is a forecasting one: a system must predict the *next* dialogue turn's full annotator count distribution while the current turn's panel is only partly revealed, so the evidence at hand is a partial panel and the question is how much an additional revealed vote improves the forecast. The larger goal is a measurement framework for annotation budgeting — how many votes, and which rows should get them — that is scoped honestly rather than sold as a law. They also want to separate two estimands that the literature runs together: the cross-turn predictive gain from a revealed previous panel, and the same-item value of a repeated label on the item currently being annotated. The paper is unusual in that it reports, in a dedicated section, three of its own earlier claims that its controls refuted.

## What they studied
Four conversational-emotion corpora with distributional targets: IEMOCAP (6 classes, 3 annotators, leave-one-session-out over five sessions, 4,883 strict rows kept from 7,741 candidate utterances), M3ED (7 classes, 3 annotators, official 11-series held-out test), EmoWOZ DialMAGE (7 classes, 3 annotators, official 796/100/100 dialogue split) and EmotionLines Friends (7 classes, 5 annotators, 13,503 rows, 5-fold dialogue CV plus an external eval-gold set). The unit of analysis is not a neuron or a feature but a **panel count vector** on a target turn, conditioned on a mask-simulated partial count vector from the previous turn plus a speaker-transition or bridging relation. The forecaster is a Dirichlet-multinomial (DM) response table keyed on the previous composition, optionally blended with a causal GRU head over text/visual or TF-IDF context. Nothing in the paper reads model internals; it reads the *shape of a predictive curve* under a controlled revelation intervention.

## How they did it
A nested revelation mask reveals k of n votes of the previous panel and asks the model for the full target panel's count distribution, scored by count negative log-likelihood (a strictly proper scoring rule). The theoretical spine is three results: for the true conditional family the decrement V(k) − V(k+1) equals the conditional mutual information I(Y; R_{k+1} | X_k), with total gain V(0) − V(n) = I(Y; X_n | X_0); for a fitted family the same difference carries an extra term equal to the excess-risk gap between adjacent levels; and greedy water-filling is optimal for allocating a fixed budget when each row's expected marginal gains are non-increasing. The operator family is built around a *coarsening-consistency* axiom: the forecast at a partial panel must equal the completion-averaged full-panel forecast, which under exchangeable voters and a Dirichlet prior becomes a finite mixture over the enumerable completions of the hidden votes (at most 462 terms in the real-data settings). Since the mixture is finite, everything marginalises exactly rather than by sampling. Five variants follow: a jointly estimated shared table with an EM-style posterior-weighted moment update; a deployment variant that keeps a dedicated full-panel table at k = n and accepts an approximate gap; a rater-count-invariant summary table that transfers across n; a cell-density shrinkage interpolation between shared and per-level; and a learned completion head (GRU) with a context-zeroed control that holds architecture, seeds, grids and parameter count fixed. Comparisons run against a per-level lookup baseline fit separately at each k, a deliberately *inconsistent* marginaliser with identical information access, a flat marginal, a soft-label cross-entropy head, and several target-free uncertainty heuristics. The fitted curve's shape is not assumed: the paper evaluates allocation policies directly on held-out rows and checks diminishing returns empirically, and it reports block/session/dialogue bootstrap intervals with Holm correction on the nine-cell mechanism family.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation of a mechanism, and not a description of a model's insides: the deliverable is a **curve with an information-theoretic interpretation plus an operator that behaves coherently across intervention levels**. Three objects count as understanding. (i) An identity: each step of the revelation curve is a conditional information gain, so a fitted coefficient has a meaning in bits (nats) rather than a score. (ii) A consistency axiom, auditable as a KL gap between the operator at level k and the completion-marginalised operator at full revelation — near machine zero for the exact family, a few hundredths of a nat for the lookup baseline. (iii) A prediction under a planned intervention: given target-free row features, predict each row's whole curve and act on it. The coverage is explicitly partial: the identity is a statement about the population, and the authors insist a fitted curve violation is a diagnostic of adjacent excess risk, not evidence against the identity.

## How they judged success
Count NLL differences in nats against per-level lookup and against the inconsistent marginaliser, each with 95% bootstrap intervals clustered on the corpus's own unit (session, series, dialogue, fold) and bolded only when the interval excludes zero; Holm correction across the nine learned-completion cells; a synthetic study where the population increments are enumerated exactly so bias, RMSE and the fraction of replicates with monotone-curve violations can be measured at n = 3, 5, 10 and three dependence strengths; negative controls that permute the previous-panel alignment or only the relation array; a bias-variance data-amount scan at 25%, 50% and 100% of training rows; a held-out-rater protocol that rotates which annotator is held out and averages over all revealed subsets; and, for allocation, four reference points per cell — policy, uniform, a retrospective feature-class headroom fit in-sample on the evaluation block, and a clairvoyant oracle greedy.

## What they found
Cold-start forecasting (k = 0, no votes revealed) wins on all three headline corpora against per-level lookup: NLL differences of −0.422 on IEMOCAP, −0.166 on M3ED and −0.014 on EmoWOZ, all intervals excluding zero. Beyond cold start the wins are corpus-dependent and small: IEMOCAP is significantly better at k = 1 and k = 2 and 0.021 nats *worse* at k = 3, M3ED is within 0.007 nats in the wrong direction at every partial level with no interval excluding zero, and EmoWOZ is better at k = 1 and k = 2 by 0.006. The authors translate the cold-start numbers into geometric-score terms (roughly 52.5%, 18.0% and 1.4% higher) precisely to stop them being read as accuracy gains. Permuting the previous-panel alignment costs about 1.02, 0.81 and 0.13 nats at k = 2 on the three corpora, so the operator is demonstrably using the revealed panel.

The axiom that motivated the paper does not drive accuracy. An identical-information inconsistent marginaliser matches or beats the consistent operator, costing it 0.048 and 0.034 nats at k = 0 and k = 1 on IEMOCAP and 0.041 and 0.015 on M3ED, and the cost concentrates in dense cells and shrinks under data scarcity (overall IEMOCAP cost 0.029 at full data against 0.021 at 25%), with the sparsest cells flipping in favour of the shared table — the bias-variance crossover the authors predicted. In a mixed 3-and-5-rater deployment a single shared table beats an oracle per-size lookup on 9 of 10 cells (−0.0187) but still costs 0.0085 nats against the stronger inconsistent marginaliser, halving to 0.0044 once that baseline is denied its oracle-size training panels.

Same-item value is measured directly rather than proxied: the first and second additional votes cut NLL by 0.583 and 0.221 nats on IEMOCAP, 0.578 and 0.184 on M3ED, 0.180 and 0.069 on EmoWOZ. The cross-turn curve is *not* a substitute — the row-wise Spearman correlation between same-item and cross-turn NLL is 0.266 at k = 0 and 0.011 at k = 1 on IEMOCAP. An inertia control narrows the mechanism: the first revealed vote's gain is largely explained by current-turn state plus speaker transition, but the full-composition control still retains later gains (0.272 and 0.279 nats on M3ED), while a majority-only control goes *negative* at the last increment on all three corpora — compression to the majority discards composition information that the forecast uses. Learned completion adds on top of the shared table, with the context term significant in 8 of 9 cells and accounting for 93/78/85% of the k = 0 gain, exactly zero at k = n where the completion is empty, and the gain survives an information-set control — but it comes from sharper row-specific weights rather than from better completion prediction, since the learned completion's own dev NLL is often worse than the empirical table's.

Allocation is where the paper is most usefully blunt. Uniform beats concentrated allocation in all 15 held-out budget cells, significantly in 14 (up to 0.341 nats at budget 1.0 on IEMOCAP). A target-free amortised policy wins 10 of 15 cells against uniform with 3–4x the effect of a simple entropy-binning policy, recovering 83% and 93% of the retrospective feature-class headroom on IEMOCAP and M3ED at budget 1.0 versus 23% and 26% for binning — but only 35% and 41% of the clairvoyant oracle, and on EmoWOZ it loses significantly (+0.021), where the headroom bound shows why: the features carry almost no row-level signal there. Simple uncertainty heuristics do not dominate: at budget 2, least confidence and inverse margin hurt M3ED by 0.0402 and 0.0705 nats.

Three self-corrections head a dedicated section. Training the completion head on the exact marginal likelihood, available in closed form because the latent is enumerable, is *worse* than the cross-entropy surrogate (+0.064, +0.043, +0.015 nats at k = 0..2 on IEMOCAP) and is discarded by dev selection on four of five folds — the surrogate was regularising. The apparent Dirichlet-versus-cross-entropy gap was mostly a likelihood-family artefact: scoring the CE head as a multinomial inflated the difference by 1.09 nats on M3ED and 0.39 on EmoWOZ, and giving both heads the same DM family collapses it to 0.02 or less. And the conventional allocation oracle is clairvoyant, so "fraction of oracle recovered" understates a deployable policy. A fourth correction retracts an annotator-routing claim after an audit found the IEMOCAP columns are not stable evaluator identities (five evaluator sets among 7,741 strict utterances); on the stable e1/e2/e4 subset an identity-aware table differs from a pooled one by only 0.0074 nats with an interval crossing zero.

## Limits and open problems they name
The gains are corpus-dependent, with mid-revelation effects indistinguishable from zero or slightly negative on M3ED. Consistency is a coherence and deployment property, not a source of accuracy, and the authors say so in the limitations as well as the results. Coarsened-completely-at-random masking is a controlled intervention, not an observed arrival process — the corpora carry no annotation timestamps, so identity- and label-dependent masking are synthetic sensitivity studies only. The evidence base is three conversational-emotion corpora with real panels of 3 and 5 voters; IEMOCAP has five independent sessions, and behaviour at substantially larger panels is unestablished. There is no leaderboard comparison and no state-of-the-art claim; the retrospective headroom is explicitly not a deployable bound; the learned table update is not an exact likelihood maximiser; and the mixed-rater-count head is a probability-mixture study with an internal factorial design rather than a comparison on the paper's headline corpora.

## What we take from it
A **framing, a measure, and two warnings**, in that order of value. The framing: this is the folder's cleanest worked example of treating a *revelation level* as the intervention and the *shape of the error curve across levels* as the object of study, with each step of that curve interpreted as a conditional information gain rather than as an unexplained coefficient. Mechanical rule for us: whenever our white-box model is asked to predict under partial evidence — a masked prompt, a truncated context, k of m probes observed — we publish the whole curve over k with the fitted-risk correction term beside it, and we state which side of the population identity each published number belongs to; a fitted gain that rises when the population identity says it must fall is published as an estimator diagnostic, never as an information statement. The measure: the coarsening-consistency gap (KL between the partial-evidence prediction and the completion-averaged full-evidence prediction) is a cheap, direct audit of whether a predictor means the same thing at every level of available evidence, and our model should report it whenever it serves several evidence levels, because the paper's own operator is near machine-zero and its lookup baseline sits at hundredths of a nat with no accuracy difference visible in the NLL.

The first warning is the one the folder needed: **an axiom that guarantees coherence need not buy accuracy, and the honest audit is the one that prints both.** The consistent operator loses to a deliberately inconsistent one with identical information access at dense cells and wins only where cells are sparse, so "our predictor respects the invariance" is a deployment and auditability claim and must not be presented as a performance claim; our model should carry the invariance audit and the head-to-head score in the same table, and should print the density regime where each wins. The second warning is about references: the usual clairvoyant oracle conflates estimation error with irreducible target noise, and against it the same policy recovers 35–41% of the oracle but 83–93% of an achievable-in-class retrospective bound — so every allocation or selection policy our model proposes is reported against three references (uniform or hold, a feature-class headroom fit on the evaluation block with the realisation caveat stated, and the clairvoyant oracle), because the middle reference is the only one that separates "the policy is bad" from "the target is noisy". Third, cheaper, carried from the same-rater study: a predictor of cross-turn gain is not a predictor of same-item value (correlation 0.266 falling to 0.011), so any proxy metric our model substitutes for a directly measurable quantity must be validated by running the direct experiment on a rotation protocol and reporting the rank correlation, not assumed to transfer.
