# Can Activation Steering Substitute for Prompt-Based Affective Priming in LLM Digital Twins?

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.22738
- **Authors / organisation:** Junyi Fan — University of Southern California (single author)
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22738
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, ~71K clean chars after whitespace normalisation: abstract, §§1–4, references, Appendices A–E including the full extraction pipeline, the strength pilot, the per-condition means, the parsing-success table, the persona list, the verbatim prompts and both 39-row comparison tables). Figures 2 and 3 (the dose–response curves) were **not** inspected — every number below is a table cell or an explicit sentence, and the one interpolated value (the matching strength) is quoted as the paper's own interpolation with its bootstrap interval.
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The social-science literature induces emotional states by asking people to write about a remembered event, then tests whether the induced state spills over onto closely related judgements (proximal measures) and more distant ones (distal measures). The Twin-2K-500 Mega Study had already shown that LLM stand-ins for survey respondents ("digital twins") reproduce this effect, and reproduce it more strongly than humans do. Writing a full text is expensive and conflates the content it activates with the act of writing, so a cheaper mechanism is attractive: if a concept is roughly a direction in activation space, one could add that direction at inference time and skip the writing entirely. The question the paper sets out to answer is not whether steering can move a self-report, which earlier work already showed, but whether it can stand in for a *validated* manipulation — i.e. whether validity established for one way of inducing a state carries over to another. The answer is the paper's contribution: it carries over partially and measure by measure.

## What they studied

One open-weight model, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in bfloat16 (4,096-dimensional residual stream), on the two affect domains of the original study — gratitude and lack of control — with the original instruments reused verbatim: a three-item manipulation check, a six-item Elevation or nine-item Fatigue proximal measure, and a two-item Empathic Concern or eight-item Desire-for-Predictability distal measure, all on seven-point scales with the original reverse-coding. Data are the published human results, the published digital-twin (GPT-4.1) results, and 100 persona summaries from the Twin-2K-500 dataset, each about 2,000 words of demographics, percentile-scored psychometrics and a few verbatim answers. The unit of analysis is a single mean-difference direction per domain, normalised and injected with one strength coefficient into a three-layer window.

## How they did it

The design runs two pipelines on the same 100 personas, so every comparison is paired. The **real-prompt path** reproduces the original: one generation containing the writing task (prime or baseline prompt), then the manipulation check, the proximal and the distal measure, exactly as the source study's pipeline did. The **injection path** deletes the writing task and keeps the questions, while a direction is added to the post-MLP residual stream at every token position of every forward pass (prefill and decode), each layer using its own vector.

The direction is a mean difference (MeanDiff): for each candidate layer, each training example is a chat-formatted sequence of the system header, the participant's writing instruction and that participant's actual writing; the hidden state is averaged over all token positions of the sequence, and the direction is the mean over the primed examples minus the mean over the baseline examples, then unit-normalised. The corpus is the human writing from the original study — 506 examples for gratitude (249 prime, 257 baseline) and 492 for lack of control (256 prime, 236 baseline). Because the average includes the instruction tokens, the paper says plainly that the direction mixes affect, topic and instruction wording, and that this is deliberate: the real manipulation conflates them too, and the question is substitution rather than isolation.

Layer choice is made by a logistic-regression probe on the same averaged states for layers 10–23. The probe hit held-out accuracy 1.000 at every candidate layer in both domains, so it could not discriminate, and the paper took the midpoint (layer 17, window 16–18) rather than claiming a peak; it also notes that a bag-of-words classifier on the participants' writing alone already separates gratitude prime from baseline writing at 1.000 (0.77 for lack of control), so the perfect probe is not evidence of an affect-only signal.

Four dose strengths were used (β = 1.8, 2.5, 3.2, 3.9), set from a three-persona pilot on the manipulation check; the pilot's β = 4.5 point overshot to 7.00 on the first persona and then produced unparseable output, which is where the grid stops. Three further conditions matter. A single-point **active control** injects the opposite pole of the same direction at β = −2.5. A **no-injection** condition (β = 0) keeps the injection-path prompt and output structure but adds nothing. And the real-prompt prime and control give the reference effect. Free-form responses are parsed by an extractive LLM judge that returns the item values or a failure marker; failed parses are retried (at most two attempts for gratitude, three for lack of control), and analyses use the last successful attempt — 769 of 800 gratitude and 753 of 800 lack-of-control cells. Comparisons are paired t-tests with the paired effect size d_z, all 39 comparisons per domain reported in the appendix with Holm adjustment, and Welch's test as a sensitivity check.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a description of the model — a **direction**, used as a causal handle. Understanding, in this paper, takes the form of a claim that a single mean-difference vector carries a state, evidenced by the vector producing the same downstream measurement pattern as the real manipulation. The explanatory bar is therefore explicitly cross-method: the vector must not only move the manipulation check, it must reproduce the *shape* of the human result, i.e. a large proximal spillover and a null distal effect. The paper is unusually clear that matching one number is not explanation: a strength exists at which the check matches, and the same strength overshoots a different measure.

## How they judged success

Four measured rungs, each stricter than the last. (1) **External calibration**: does the real-prompt path land where published humans and published twins landed? (2) **Dose dependence**: does the manipulation check move monotonically with β, with every dose point tested against the real prime and the real control? (3) **Matching strength**: interpolate the dose curve to find the β at which injection equals the real-prompt effect, with a 95% percentile bootstrap interval over 5,000 persona resamples, and then test whether that same β also matches on the proximal and distal measures. (4) **Source separation**: compare no-injection against the real control (pipeline effect alone) and active control against no-injection (vector effect alone), which is the one control that turns a confounded comparison into two interpretable rows. Parsing success rate by strength is used as an outcome-independent process indicator. Notably there is **no equivalence test** — non-significance is treated as non-significance — and the per-condition correlation r between paired scores is reported throughout.

## What they found

**Calibration.** The real-prompt effect lands between the human and the original twin in five of six comparisons; the exception is gratitude Elevation, which is smaller than both. Manipulation check: gratitude 0.81 human / 1.10 this study / 1.33 twin; lack of control 0.31 / 0.63 / 1.00. Proximal: Elevation 0.79 / 0.58 / 0.77; Fatigue 0.18 / 0.43 / 0.88. Distal: Empathic Concern −0.01 / 0.02 / 0.24; Desire for Predictability 0.25 / 0.13 / 0.05, with every distal effect at most 0.25 across all three sources. The small open model over-responds relative to humans but less than the larger closed model did. The real-prompt path also reproduces the qualitative structure: prime beats control on Elevation (d_z 0.66) and Fatigue (d_z 0.62) while the distal measure does not move (d_z 0.03 and 0.13).

**Dose dependence and matching.** The manipulation check is monotone in β in both domains. In gratitude the curve crosses the real-prime mean at β ≈ 2.7 (bootstrap 2.5–2.8); at β = 1.8 it is already significantly below the real prime (d_z −0.85) and at 3.9 well above it (d_z 1.20). In lack of control there is no crossing in the tested range — the lowest dose already exceeds the real prime (d_z −0.76). The overshoot is not confined to the check: on the proximal measure the dose arm exceeds the real prime from Moderate in gratitude (d_z 0.57) and from Low in lack of control (d_z 1.03). The two poles are asymmetric: the lack-of-control direction overshoots hard, while the in-control direction against the real control at Moderate is d_z 0.28 and does not survive Holm adjustment (p = .067).

**Pipeline versus vector.** This is the paper's most reusable result. Removing the writing task and adding nothing raises the proximal measure relative to the real control in both domains with the same sign — Elevation d_z 0.47 against no-injection; Fatigue d_z 0.75, at which point no-injection is *indistinguishable* from the primed condition (d_z 0.00, p = .969). So part of the injection path's apparent "effect" against the real prompt is the short prompt, not the vector. Isolating the vector by comparing active control with no-injection: in gratitude the opposite pole moves nothing reliably on any measure (largest d_z −0.17, p = .087), so its differences from the real conditions there are mostly pipeline. In lack of control the in-control direction moves its own check in the intended direction (d_z 0.45 against no-injection) and lowers the distal measure (d_z −0.63) — a measure on which the real manipulation shows no reliable effect.

**Distal measures.** The dose arm's distal effect is inconsistent rather than absent. Gratitude: d_z 0.34 at Low (Holm .008) but 0.19 at Moderate (Holm .288), then 0.57 at High and 0.34 at Extreme — non-monotone. Lack of control: flat through Moderate (largest 0.05), then −0.34 at Extreme. Since the aim is to reproduce a null distal effect, any reliable movement there is a failure of reproduction, and the paper says so. One internal qualification, ours and marked as ours: in the within-path table (dose minus no-injection) the gratitude *distal* measure climbs with strength (0.34, 0.19, 0.57 and then 0.34 at Extreme, all Holm-significant from Low), where the against-prime reading is non-monotone — so "inconsistent distal effect" describes the vector measured against the real prompt, and measured against the vector's own zero point the gratitude distal drift is steady.

**Process indicator.** Parsing success falls monotonically with strength — gratitude 100/100/97/72% and lack of control 97/93/91/77% across Low to Extreme — while the active-control arm parses at 99–100%. The paper reads this as an outcome-independent sign of leaving the model's stable operating regime, and it is the one measurement in the study that does not depend on which downstream scale is being scored.

## Limits and open problems they name

One base model, one extraction method, one three-layer window not chosen by a discriminating criterion (the probe was at ceiling everywhere). The active control was tested at a single strength. The strength grid came from a three-persona pilot whose personas are in the main sample, so the matching strength is approximate. Paired tests rather than one mixed-effects model, and no equivalence tests, so "no difference" means "not significant". Retry limits differed between domains and Extreme results are conditional on generations that stayed parseable. The direction was extracted from writing by the same participant pool whose twins were simulated. Parsed values were never manually audited against raw responses. Most importantly, the injection path differs from the real path in more than the vector — no writing, shorter prompt, fewer questions, different output structure, and Fatigue items that refer to an event that no longer exists — and a neutral-writing control matched to the real structure, which would isolate the writing task itself, is left for future work. The paper's own reading of the in-control direction's odd distal effect is that the contrastive corpus probably mixes topical and affective signal asymmetrically across poles.

## What we take from it

- **Method — the zero-strength control.** A condition with the intervention's prompt and output structure but a zero intervention is the cheapest way to price a pipeline change, and here it found that the proximal measure moves as much from dropping the writing task as from priming. Any steering experiment of ours needs this row; without it, "steering reproduced the effect" and "the shorter prompt did" are the same number.
- **Method — the opposite pole as an active control at one strength.** Injecting −β along the same vector costs one arm and tests for direction-specificity rather than generic perturbation, and the asymmetry it exposed (hard overshoot one way, nothing reliable the other) is a property of the vector that no dose sweep alone would reveal.
- **Measure — matching strength is not validity.** The paper's whole structure argues that a single scalar match is compatible with failure on every other measure, so a steering result should be reported as a *profile* across manipulation check, proximal and distal, with the matched strength marked on all three.
- **Warning — a probe at ceiling ranks nothing.** Held-out accuracy 1.000 at all fourteen candidate layers, together with a bag-of-words classifier at 1.000 on the writing alone, means the probe could not choose a layer and the chosen window is a prior. This is the folder's own failure mode (a ranking metric that saturates) appearing inside a published methods section.
- **Warning — check the reported statistic against its algebra.** Every paired row in both appendix tables carries r, and r equals d_z to within rounding in essentially every row. For a paired design r = d_z·(s_diff/s_pooled)·√2, so the coincidence says the two conditions' standard deviations are near-equal — which makes the "correlation between conditions" column an algebraic restatement of the effect size rather than independent evidence about persona-level fidelity, and the paper indeed declines to interpret it that way.
- **Shortcut — reuse a published human-subjects paradigm.** Instruments, prompts and an external human–twin reference come free, and the six-cell calibration table is a ready-made sanity check for any simulated-respondent work.
