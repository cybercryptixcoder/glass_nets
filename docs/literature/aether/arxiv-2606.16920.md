# Demystifying Variance in Circuit Discovery of LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.16920
- **Authors / organisation:** Frank Zhengqing Wu, Francesco Tonin, Volkan Cevher; Laboratory for Information and Inference Systems (LIONS), École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL), Lausanne, Switzerland
- **Date:** 2026-06-15
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16920
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit discovery — pick a small subgraph of a model's computational graph that reproduces its behaviour on a task — is the standard instrument for saying *where in the network a behaviour lives*, and the current default (edge attribution patching with integrated gradients, EAP-IG) scores well on the field's standard metric, unfaithfulness. The authors' complaint is that the instrument is unstable in three separate ways: the circuit changes when the probing batch is resampled from the same distribution, it changes when prompts are merely rephrased into another template of the same task, and its per-sample quality score swings wildly even when its population score is good. Their aim is to say what each variance is *made of* rather than to patch it with another heuristic — and, where a variance turns out to be a property of the measurement rather than of the model, to stop treating it as a defect. The larger stakes are the field's promise that task circuits can be steered to control behaviour; if circuits are template-specific, that promise weakens.

## What they studied

Four Transformer LMs: GPT-2 small, GPT-2 XL, Pythia-160M and Pythia-2.8B, using TransformerLens. Tasks: subject–verb agreement (SVA, with fifteen prompt templates in the appendix), indirect object identification (IOI, fourteen ABBA/BABA templates) and greater-than. The unit of analysis is an **edge** of the split computational graph (residual additions are separated into edges); a circuit is the set of edges whose absolute importance score is largest after averaging over samples, pruned greedily of childless/parentless edges. Sparse, purpose-trained code models from prior interpretability work (a family sweeping nonzero-parameter counts and activation-sparsity fractions, on two code tasks: single- vs double-quote and `else` vs `elif`) are used as a secondary arm to test whether sparsity concentrates circuits.

## How they did it

They define behaviour by a clean/corrupted prompt pair and take the difference of an output metric (probability difference, or logit difference where the two answers are single tokens) between the full graph and the patched subgraph, with everything outside the subgraph frozen at its corrupted-input activation; unfaithfulness is one minus the ratio of those two differences, in absolute value. Their first contribution replaces the integrated-gradients score with **conductance**: the same path integral, but weighted by the actual change in the edge's activation along the path rather than by the fixed endpoint-difference, which they show is a weighted sum of gradients whose weights match the segments the activation actually traverses. They prove conductance satisfies *additive order preservation* — for a sum-of-branches function, the score ordering matches the actual behaviour change caused by patching each branch — and that IG does not. For resampling variance they draw forty small resamples of about a thousand samples from a full set of roughly ten thousand per task, run discovery on each, and compute mean and spread of the pairwise Jaccard index across the resulting circuit pairs. For rephrasing variance they run discovery **per sample** within one template, embed each sample's vector of edge-score ranks with UMAP, and tabulate the pairwise Jaccard index between per-sample circuits, comparing templates against each other. For sample-wise variance they run discovery per sample at a sweep of circuit sizes, define a pessimistic (running maximum over larger sizes) unfaithfulness to remove non-monotonicity, and compute six Spearman rank correlations linking behaviour magnitude, the heavy-tailedness of the score distribution, the fraction of score mass captured by the circuit, and unfaithfulness.

## What counts as an explanation here

Nothing in the paper aims at explaining a behaviour; the object produced is a **circuit** (a subgraph) plus a **variance decomposition** of the instrument that finds it. The explanation-like content is diagnostic: three named sources of instability, each attributed to a mechanism (score weighting, template-specific routing, normalisation geometry), from which they derive a practical rule — score by conductance, do discovery per template, read single-sample unfaithfulness pessimistically and prefer the ratio-of-sums form at population level. It is partial by their own account: they explicitly say low unfaithfulness is a *computational*-level agreement, a crude surrogate for the algorithmic-level match they would want, because the only true algorithmic measure (completeness) is intractable.

## How they judged success

Stability of circuit selection: mean and standard deviation of the pairwise Jaccard index over resampled datasets and over sample pairs, at a fixed target edge count, reported for CEAP and EAP-IG. Quality held constant: unfaithfulness of the selected circuits, checked to ensure CEAP's stability does not come from choosing irrelevant edges. Template separation: UMAP of absolute-score rank vectors and cross-template Jaccard matrices, with same-template pairs excluded from cross-template averages. Sparse-model arm: cross-template and cross-task average Jaccard at a fixed fraction of edges (about 5.2 per cent of the graph), the comparison of the two being the test of whether sparsity compresses or merely polysemanticises. Correlation claims: Spearman rank correlation with p-values, extended to all templates, both models and the full edge-count sweep, with unnormalised variants of the metric reported as a control against artefacts of their own pessimistic construction.

## What they found

CEAP attains higher or at-least-equal pairwise Jaccard than EAP-IG across models, tasks and templates, at comparable unfaithfulness — the gain comes from better weighting, not from picking irrelevant edges. On sample-wise variance, the extremes are an artefact: unfaithfulness can reach values around 20 for an individual sample while its neighbours in the same template are low, but the unnormalised gap between circuit and full model is not what is extreme — the *denominator* is. Samples whose behaviour magnitude is near zero get near-zero normalisation, so the ratio blows up; the correlations run behaviour magnitude ↓ → score-distribution heavy-tailedness ↑ → captured score mass ↓ → pessimistic unfaithfulness ↑, and the proposed mechanism is **selective contribution scaling**: raising the output signal raises the contribution of the high-scoring edges more than of the low-scoring ones, so a weak-signal sample concentrates less of its score mass in the same-size circuit. On rephrasing variance they find flatly that different templates activate different circuits — in at least one case two clearly separated template groups whose cross-group Jaccard is near zero — so no finite set of templates can characterise a task's circuit, and they report a case where patching in a circuit obtained to encourage plural verbs on one sample instead pushes a different sample toward the singular. Sparsity does not fix it: cross-template overlap rises as nonzero parameters are cut, but cross-*task* overlap rises comparably (for instance 0.3424 cross-template against a comparable cross-task figure on one task), which they read as more polysemantic neurons rather than true compression. They also report that KL divergence as the behaviour metric is unusable on Pythia-160M because underflow makes exact zeros and the divergence infinite, and that on sparse models with activation-level top-k, conductance cannot see effects that jump a discontinuity.

## Limits and open problems they name

They concede the whole notion of a task is unsettled and propose redefining it model-centrically, as the set of samples that engage the same circuit, while admitting there is no principled way yet to decide which templates engage the same circuit. They cannot say whether sparsity mainly adds polysemanticity or mainly compresses, calling that a model-level study beyond their scope. The metric they use is computational, not algorithmic, agreement. Their defensive construction (pessimistic unfaithfulness) is a heuristic for restoring monotonicity, not a derivation, and they note that correlation diagnostics vanish on templates that are already easily fitted. Their evidence is on three synthetic-template task families and small-to-medium models; nothing tests a template space chosen adversarially.

## What we take from it

Three things, all of them discipline for how we report an account of a black-box network. First, a **scoring-rule desideratum** we can adopt directly: a component score should order components the way an actual intervention on each component orders the behaviour, and this can be proved or refuted for a candidate rule on the function class our model belongs to — the additive (residual-branch) case is proved here, and the honest statement is that the desideratum is only guaranteed for that case. Second, a **measuring-instrument check** on every normalised number we publish: report the unnormalised gap beside the ratio, because a ratio with a near-zero denominator manufactures extremes that are read as failures of the account when they are failures of the normalisation. Third, the sharpest caution in the paper for our goal: a circuit discovered by averaging over a sample distribution is an average over *routes*, and if the model routes different input forms through different components, then an averaged circuit is not a description of a mechanism — it is a description of the union. That directly bounds what a single probe-derived component set can predict under a change of input *form* even when the underlying task is unchanged, and it supplies the experiment: hold the task fixed, vary only the surface form, and measure whether the account's component set survives.
