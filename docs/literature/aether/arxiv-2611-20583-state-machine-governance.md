# State-Machine Governance of Adaptive Enforcement: Evidence from a Paired-Scenario Evaluation

- **Key:** arxiv-2611-20583-state-machine-governance
- **Authors / organisation:** Ifeanyi Williams Agbedo, Yanzhen Qu; Colorado Technical University (Colorado Springs, CO, USA)
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20583
- **Kind:** paper

## What they wanted, and why

An AI system in a security pipeline can propose a policy change faster than a human reviewer can authorise it, and the interval between a recommendation and a committed enforcement action is where most of the damage and most of the missing evidence lives. The authors ask whether inserting an explicitly governed transition layer — guards before commitment, an attributable evidence record, a check that every enforcement point actually converged, and a bounded way back to the previous validated state — changes how well a transition can be *reconstructed later*. They are not testing whether the recommendation is any good; they take the recommendation as a fixed input. The larger goal is the framing, not a mechanism: the thing worth measuring about a decision pipeline is the evidence trail it leaves, and that trail is a separately engineerable object rather than a byproduct of the pipeline.

## What they studied

No neural network is the unit of analysis here. The unit is a **transition record**: a seven-field entry (timestamp, actor, policy identifier, pre-state, post-state, evidence pointer, outcome) produced when a policy moves between the three states Monitor, Alert and Enforce, with block / deny / quarantine treated as actions *inside* Enforce rather than states of their own. Two conditions are compared — a scripted operator-driven procedure and a typed state-machine kernel (instantiated in an artefact called ZTGuard) that returns either a defined post-state or a typed failure and cannot be committed by the AI recommendation itself. Telemetry comes from CICIDS2017 (11 scenarios) and CICIoT2023 (11 scenarios) plus 14 explicitly synthetic scenarios covering conditions those datasets do not contain (unauthenticated attempts, connector faults, rollback-bound breaches). Vendor behaviour is deterministic simulation of Check Point Smart-1 and Palo Alto Panorama connectors; no production control plane is touched.

## How they did it

Thirty-six scenarios were constructed against fixed source/category slots to exercise four lifecycle families — canonical transitions (14), guard failures (10), rollback (8) and convergence (4) — and each was executed once under each condition, giving 36 matched pairs and 72 condition-level observations. Every pair held the starting state, the telemetry, the AI recommendation (a label-derived confidence, 0.85–0.95 for attack records and 0.15–0.45 for benign, against a 0.7 threshold) and the scheduled vendor condition fixed; the baseline varied only in which of three scripted operator profiles (13 cautious, 12 hasty, 11 nominal) drove it. The dependent variable, Control, is the unweighted mean of four binary-or-bounded components: traceability (can the state movement be reconstructed from the last record), accountability (is there a nonempty actor and evidence pointer), audit-chain completeness (are the seven shared fields present), and reliable rollback (did any required reversion finish inside a shared 60,000 ms bound, with cases needing no reversion scored 1). Normality was rejected (Shapiro–Wilk W = 0.891, p = .002), so the primary test is a two-tailed paired Wilcoxon signed-rank with continuity correction, reported with a Hodges–Lehmann location shift and a matched-pairs rank-biserial effect size. A separate retained-data analysis reloaded the 36-pair file (SHA-256 checked against the manifest), reweighted the four components across all 286 nonnegative weight vectors summing to 1 in steps of 0.1, added four one-component-dominant 0.4/0.2/0.2/0.2 contrasts, and ran a paired percentile bootstrap over whole scenario pairs, 20,000 resamples, seed 86504.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **design procedure plus an audit schema**, and nothing predictive. The "explanation" of a transition is the seven-field record and the guard-and-postcondition trace that made it admissible; the paper's own name for the property is machine-verifiability, which it explicitly restricts to automatically checkable transition conditions and execution evidence rather than any exhaustive correctness proof. Coverage is deliberately partial: detection accuracy, latency, availability and usability are named as outside the measure, and the authors say outright that a confidence threshold alone should not authorise commitment. The composite is presented as a study-specific operationalisation with no claim to externally validated construct status.

## How they judged success

A single hypothesis-tested outcome (the four-component Control mean), a paired nonparametric test with effect size and an interval, and then a long series of disclosure moves that are the real method: component means published as descriptive only and not as four separate tests; an explicit statement that the two arms used *different scoring predicates* for traceability, accountability and completeness, so the comparison concerns the two operationalised conditions and not governance in isolation; a weight-sensitivity sweep and bootstrap offered as conditional stability on a fixed library rather than a population interval; a SHA-256 manifest check plus a replay that reproduces the retained rows; and post-hoc diagnostic replays that removed the treatment's audit-chain gate, and separately applied the baseline's looser both-states criterion to the treatment, with every result row left unchanged. Failing cases are retained and discussed, not dropped.

## What they found

Control averaged 0.720 (SD 0.218, median 0.714) for the scripted operator and 0.972 (SD 0.131, median 1.000) for the governed procedure — a mean paired difference of 0.252, V = 359, p < .001, rank-biserial r = .899, Hodges–Lehmann shift 0.321 with 95% CI [0.295, 0.447], and the retained-data recomputation gives p = 0.000042782. Twenty-six pairs favoured treatment, nine tied, one favoured baseline. The component contrast is lopsided and the paper says so: traceability moves 0.472 → 0.972 (difference 0.500), accountability 0.694 → 0.972 (0.278), completeness 0.797 → 0.972 (0.175), rollback 0.917 → 0.972 (0.056) — and because the traceability predicates differ between arms, that 0.500 is recordkeeping plus scoring definition together. The machine-verifiable audit chain is 0/36 under the baseline and 35/36 under treatment and is reported *outside* the composite while still gating treatment traceability, which the paper flags as an indirect dependency. Atomic consistency 34/36 → 36/36; split-state outcomes 2/36 → 0/36. Rollback timing is measured on four cases per condition only: 1/4 → 3/4 completed inside the bound, median 21,015.5 ms → 741 ms, and in scenario S-036 a deliberately degraded connector breached 60,000 ms in both arms (143,720 ms baseline, 120,561 ms treatment), so the bound detected nonconvergence without forcing recovery. Weight-vector sensitivity spans 0.056 to 0.500 across the 286 vectors, with the one-component-dominant contrasts at 0.302, 0.257, 0.237 and 0.213, against a bootstrap interval of [0.170, 0.331] on the equal-weight difference. The baseline-favouring pair, S-024, is the paper's most useful number: the governed procedure refused an unauthenticated Alert→Enforce attempt before any side effect but committed no audit entry for the refusal, scoring 0 on three components for Control = 0.250 — correct prevention, no retained evidence of the prevention.

## Limits and open problems they name

The 36 scenarios are purposive, not sampled; the operators are three scripted profiles rather than humans, so fatigue, expertise and collaboration are untested; the vendor connectors are deterministic simulations, so API behaviour, rate limits, compilation latency and transient failure are untested at scale; Control is a study-specific instrument whose components can count overlapping recordkeeping behaviour and whose arms use different predicates; bootstrap exchangeability is questionable for constructed scenarios that may share dependencies; Wilcoxon symmetry was not established; four rollback-timing cases per condition cannot support a recovery-reliability claim. Named as unperformed: live-analyst studies, independent comparator implementations, production-representative connectors, concurrent transitions, and more recovery trials. The conclusion language is unusually disciplined — "controlled artifact utility, not production readiness", and the gaps "require new evidence, not stronger wording".

## What we take from it

**A reporting pattern, chiefly.** Three things here are directly reusable in a battery whose arms inevitably differ in how they are scored. First, the **paired design plus the disclosure that matching did not isolate the treatment**: because the two arms were scored by different predicates, the authors state the result is about "the two operationalized conditions" and run post-hoc replays that harmonise one predicate at a time and report that nothing moved. That is the honest way to report a comparison between two pipelines we built ourselves — print the unmatched-predicate caveat *and* the harmonisation replay, and keep the caveat if the replay was partial rather than universal. Second, **the weight-sensitivity sweep as a substitute for an argued weighting**: 286 weight vectors and four extremes, with the minimum difference (0.056) published beside the headline (0.252) and the extremes attributed to the single component that dominates, is a cheap way to show a composite is not an artefact of its weights — and the paper still refuses to let it double as construct validation, which is the correct boundary. Third, **the failing case as the most informative row**: S-024 shows a correct action with no evidence trail scoring 0.250, which is precisely the failure mode our own logging has — an ablation that prevented the behaviour but changed nothing we can measure later is indistinguishable from an ablation that did nothing. **A warning** carries over to interpretability directly: the audit chain gates traceability yet is reported outside the composite, so a component that is both a prerequisite and an external quantity makes the composite's components non-independent, and the paper flags the dependency rather than quietly dropping the chain metric. **A framing** worth stealing: separate recommendation from commitment authority, and retain evidence for refused attempts, not only for committed ones — the analogue for us is that a probe that *declines* to fire is a result that has to be recorded with the same care as one that fires.
