## What they wanted, and why
Reasoning models break on inputs that should not matter — an extra irrelevant clause, a restated condition, a distractor sentence — and the standard reading is that they latch onto surface correlations rather than the problem. Existing repairs either need training, or prune reasoning traces, or switch on an extra decoding procedure, and the authors make a sharper complaint about all of them: a rise in accuracy does not establish *which* failure was fixed. A longer thought chain can hide the step where a distractor got folded in, so the paper's project is to name the behaviour, locate the components that carry it, and remove their contribution in the weights once, rather than to add another inference-time band-aid. The intended payoff is an edited checkpoint that is simply more robust thereafter, plus a mechanistic account of what changed.

## What they studied
Five open-weight reasoning models: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B / 7B / 32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, and Qwen3-4B. Five maths benchmarks (GSM-Symbolic, MATH500, AMC, Gaokao-Math, pooled AIME 2024–2026), each in an original form and a perturbed form made by a NoOp-style insertion of an irrelevant condition that leaves the answer unchanged. The unit of analysis is a **unit** in the residual-stream decomposition — one attention head or one MLP neuron — ranked by its projection onto a *behavioural direction* extracted from activation contrasts, and then edited at the output-matrix level.

## How they did it
1. **Pairs that isolate the perturbation, not the answer.** For each model they generate responses to original and NoOp problems and keep only pairs where the model is right on the original and wrong on the perturbed one. Up to 20 such pairs serve as the direction-extraction set; extraction and calibration data are removed from final evaluation.
2. **Two contrasts, deliberately at different granularities.** A *token-level* (TL) direction teacher-forces the same reference trace under both prompts, scores each next-token by the KL divergence between the two prompt-conditioned distributions, keeps the smallest position set covering at least half of that divergence mass, and averages the residual-stream differences immediately before predicting each retained token. A *sequence-level* (SL) direction mean-pools the whole response trace independently generated under each prompt, so no token alignment is required.
3. **Variance-normalized differences.** Both estimators divide the per-pair mean difference dimension-wise by that dimension's across-pair standard deviation plus a small fraction of the median of those standard deviations, so dimensions that swing wildly between pairs are down-weighted and near-zero-variance dimensions are not amplified. TL normalizes each reference trace separately before averaging the two.
4. **Ranking by directional attribution.** Each unit's contribution to the residual stream (head output projection, or neuron activation times its down-projection column) is projected onto the normalized direction, averaged over token contexts and then over traces with equal per-trace weight. Heads are ranked only against heads and neurons only against neurons, and only positively attributed units are eligible, so varying the budget changes how much is edited rather than which family dominates.
5. **Editing by orthogonalization.** For each selected unit the output matrix is multiplied on the left by the projector that removes the direction's component, so the unit's directional contribution becomes exactly zero while everything orthogonal to it is untouched. The edit is applied once to the weights and needs no inference-time hook.
6. **Choosing the budget against a joint objective.** The sparsity is swept on a calibration split scored by the mean of original and perturbed accuracy, so a budget that narrows the gap merely by breaking the model is not rewarded; one global budget is chosen by minimizing *regret* against each model's own best candidate, and the TL direction is assigned to MLP neurons while the SL direction is assigned to attention heads (with a fixed configuration comparison against applying both directions to both unit types).
7. **Behaviour scored twice, blind and lexically.** Reduction of distractor uptake is judged by a forced-choice comparison of Base and edited traces in both presentation orders (a win needs both orders to favour the edited trace), on a stratified sample of 500 model–question instances, with a separate rule-based n-gram reuse rate and a three-judge replication. Overthinking is measured by output length, correct answers per 1,000 tokens, token-cap incidence, and repetition-loop incidence on length-matched prefixes.
8. **Two families of rival explanation closed off.** Baselines: activation steering, ThinkEdit and Steer2Edit re-implemented on the same extracted directions; and separately four overthinking-reduction methods (budget forcing, DEER, SEAL, Chain-of-Draft) to test whether simply shortening reasoning matches the gains. Attention analysis, a vocabulary readout of the directions, and an extraction-size stability study round it out.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **weight edit plus a weak textual reading of a direction**: two contrast directions, a ranked list of heads and neurons that write along them, and a checkpoint whose contribution along those directions is removed. It is explicitly partial. The TL direction has a readable vocabulary signature (premise-introducing and additive tokens); the SL direction does not, and the authors say so and treat the link from SL to overthinking as a behavioural hypothesis tested by the intervention rather than by the readout. Nothing here says how the direction is computed inside the model — the vocabulary projection ranks raw embedding projections, not probabilities, and the paper notes that logit-lens readings of this kind are brittle. The causal content is entirely in the edit: remove these units' projection onto this direction and the named behaviour drops.

## How they judged success
Accuracy on original and perturbed inputs, and their *relative gap*, reported per benchmark and as an unweighted mean over the five benchmarks and over five models; calibration selection separated from held-out evaluation (19,674 responses in the final test set); assigned-vs-alternative component allocations; the per-model sparsity grids; a win/loss/mixed forced-choice judge score with both orders required and disagreements counted in the denominator; a three-ngram/4-gram/5-gram lexical reuse rate on 9,837 perturbed instances; generation-length and token-efficiency changes; and direction stability as mean layer-wise cosine against an independently extracted reference direction over 64 seeded splits.

## What they found
- **Robustness improves on both sides of the perturbation.** Averaged over the five models, the full edit raises perturbed accuracy to 65.5% against at most 60.1% for every baseline, and cuts the relative original–perturbed gap from 17.6% to 2.5%, while original accuracy also rises (65.2% → 67.2%). On the 32B model the perturbed average goes 66.4% → 75.7% and the gap 11.9% → 3.0% (strongest rival Steer2Edit: 71.0% and 7.7%); on Llama-8B, 46.1% → 61.5% with the gap falling 23.1% → 2.9%.
- **The two directions are complementary, and the assignment matters.** SL alone helps original inputs more (token-efficiency +10.4%, length −4.3%); TL alone cuts distractor reuse by 77.5% where SL moves it 0.6%; combining them improves every measured behaviour on perturbed inputs (reuse −78.8%, length −23.3%, loops −7.28 points) and the combination beats the better single direction by 3.1 and 2.5 points of perturbed accuracy on the 32B and 8B models. Applying both directions to both unit types is worse on the five-model average (63.11% perturbed / 63.61% original against 65.54 / 67.23 for the neuron-plus-head allocation).
- **Gains are not bought by overthinking reduction.** All four overthinking baselines shorten outputs more than the edit does (−27.0% to −46.5% tokens against −23.3%) and none matches its perturbed accuracy; the strongest, SEAL, trails by 2.84 points with a 7.57% gap against 2.52%.
- **The direction is cheap to estimate and fairly stable.** 20 original–perturbed pairs suffice; mean layer-wise cosine against a disjoint-reference direction runs 0.958/0.946/0.939/0.858/0.960 for SL and 0.860/0.823/0.871/0.809/0.906 for TL at that budget, rising by 0.028–0.044 at 50 pairs. The authors are careful to call these split percentiles, not confidence intervals.
- **TL positions really do look at the inserted clause.** Generated positions with the highest projection onto the TL direction send 1.6–3.5× as much attention to the irrelevant clause as depth-matched controls in the same trace, with the ratio near parity early and peaking at roughly 5–6× between 60% and 80% of depth on four models, while the ratios for the rest of the problem, the chat template and the generated prefix stay between 0.92 and 1.06.
- **The behaviour judge is order-sensitive and the effect size moves with the judge.** Two-order wins over losses hold under all three judges, but the win rate falls from 51.6% (Luna) to 34.2% (Haiku) to 41.6% (DeepSeek) for TL while losses rise from 4.4% to 10.2% to 21.0%, and Luna's 42.0% order-disagreement rate is the largest cell in its row.
- **The tuned budgets are model-dependent.** On Qwen3-4B alone, the best SL-head budget is 5% of the 824 positively attributed heads, not the 100% used in the main comparison, and the per-model grids show several bounds where the same budget helps one model and hurts another.

## Limits and open problems they name
Three, stated plainly: the evidence is maths reasoning under one family of perturbation (inserted irrelevant conditions), so other tasks and other kinds of perturbation are untested; editing can trade robustness against accuracy and does degrade some model–dataset pairs (Llama-8B original GSM-Symbolic 73.5% → 71.3%); and the procedure needs calibration data and compute to choose sparsities, with sensitivity to other calibration distributions unverified.

**Limits I would add, marked as mine:** the direction is estimated on pairs selected *because the model failed* on the perturbed member, so the contrast is between "succeeded" and "failed" traces as much as between "relevant" and "irrelevant" content — a competence confound the design does not separate, and one this folder has been trained to look for. Nothing tests a direction extracted from answer-preserving perturbations whose perturbation is *not* an inserted clause (paraphrase, reordered givens, a changed number), so it is unclear whether the unit found is "distractor uptake" or "any prompt change that coincides with failure". The edit is a projection removal on a per-layer direction, applied to positively attributed units only; the negative-attribution units that push *against* the direction are left alone, which is presumably why the gap does not close to zero. The gap statistic itself is a ratio whose numerator can shrink simply by raising the denominator, and the paper's own TL row shows the trap (smallest gap, 2.2%, with lower accuracy on both conditions). The gap is also reported on benchmarks the calibration set was drawn from in spirit (MATH500 pairs), so a reader cannot separate "held-out accuracy" from "held-out problems of the same kind". And the SL direction has no validated interpretation at all — the vocabulary readout is noise, and the claim that it targets overthinking rests on length and loop statistics rather than on a mechanism.

## What we take from it
- **Method — a direction from behaviour-changing pairs, then a *weight* edit instead of a forward-pass hook.** For a predictive white-box model this is the cheapest form of the intervention we keep wanting: extract the contrast where the network's own behaviour differs, rank units by their write along it, zero that write permanently, and evaluate the edited checkpoint on held-out data. Because the edit is baked into the weights it can be checked by ordinary evaluation rather than by a hook that may or may not be active, which makes it a better instrument than steering when the question is "is this direction load-bearing".
- **Measure — score the edit on a *joint* objective, original and perturbed together, and choose the budget by regret across models.** The mean-of-both-conditions objective is what stops a sparsity that repairs the perturbation by damaging the model; the regret formulation (charge each candidate the accuracy it gives up against each model's own best) is a small, reusable device for picking one global hyperparameter honestly when several models share it, and it is cheaper than per-model tuning plus a caveat.
- **Warning — "accuracy went up" cannot distinguish repairing the mechanism from compensating for it, and the paper says so itself.** Its answer is to name two behaviours (distractor integration, redundant revision) and measure them separately, with both an LLM judge and a lexical proxy, and to run a *rival family* of baselines that could produce the same accuracy gain by a different route. Both parts are worth copying: the extra baseline family is what makes the accuracy number interpretable, and the lexical proxy is what makes the judge checkable.
- **Warning — a judge effect that survives in sign can shrink by a third in size, and the presentation order is a live variable.** Three judges agree on direction and disagree on magnitude (wins 51.6% / 34.2% / 41.6%, losses 4.4% / 10.2% / 21.0%), and the two most capable show opposite order preferences with 42–61% order disagreement. This is the same lesson the folder's own presentation-order entries keep repeating: report every rate split by ordering, print the judge ensemble's spread rather than one judge's number, and never convert a "wins exceed losses" pattern into a point estimate.
- **Framing — attention analysis is the cheap corroboration of a direction, and it is stronger than a vocabulary readout.** Projecting a direction onto the unembedding produced readable tokens for the token-level direction and noise for the sequence-level one; the attention test (do the positions where the direction is most active look disproportionately at the inserted clause, against depth-matched controls in the same trace, and is the excess specific to that clause rather than to the rest of the prompt) is what actually supported the interpretation. For our own directions, the analogous test is available and cheap: find the positions where the direction is strongest, and check that their attention or contribution profile is specific to the manipulated span and flat everywhere else.
