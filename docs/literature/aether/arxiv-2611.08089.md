## What they wanted, and why
Whether an expert in a mixture-of-experts layer does anything recognisable is an old argument, and the authors' complaint is about the *instrument*, not the answer. All the existing evidence — both the "experts are domain specialists" camp and the "routing is driven by surface token identity" camp — counts how often an expert fires on corpora that carry human-assigned labels such as code, arXiv or GitHub. That design cannot recover the model's own division of labour: the taxonomy is chosen by the analyst, it is coarse relative to a layer of 128 experts, the label attaches to a document while the routing decision is made per token, and a selection count is blind to the router's coefficient, so an expert can be chosen on a token and contribute almost nothing to what the layer computes there. The goal is a description of what each expert is responsible for in the model's own vocabulary, at whatever granularity the model actually uses — and a demonstration that the granularity is finer than any label set.

## What they studied
Qwen3-30B-A3B: 48 layers, each MoE layer holding 128 experts with 8 activated per token and no shared experts. Three layers are used as representatives of shallow, mid-late and deep positions — layer 4, layer 30 and layer 46. The unit of analysis starts as the SAE latent and is re-expressed as the (expert, latent) pair: an expert's "dominant set" of latents is the object that gets interpreted, and the coarse domains are recovered only afterwards, from those labels. One top-k SAE with 2^20 latents (1,048,576) is trained independently on each of the three layer outputs.

## How they did it
The method is an exact decomposition, not an attribution estimate. A layer output is the sum of the activated experts' outputs weighted by renormalised router weights, and the SAE encoder is affine, so substituting one into the other gives each latent's pre-activation as the encoder bias plus a sum of per-expert terms. For each (expert, latent, token) triple they keep only the positive part of the expert's term — a negative contribution strong enough to suppress a latent leaves it inactive, so negative contributions would be measured on a biased sample — normalise it over the activated experts, average over the corpus, and normalise again over experts to get a *contribution fraction*. An expert "dominates" a latent when that fraction clears a threshold of 0.2, which is far above the 1/128 an evenly sourced latent would give. Statistics are computed on a 1.5B-token validation set disjoint from SAE training; every latent fires at least once, so the fraction is defined everywhere. The authors first rule out the trivial explanation that their statistic is just the gating profile in disguise, by aggregating both gating weights and contributions *by rank* — which makes the two profiles sum to the same total and directly comparable. Latents are then interpreted by an LLM (GPT-5.5) from ten activating contexts (five strongest, five random, to avoid describing only the most literal trigger), the top-ten boosted and suppressed tokens from a logit-lens projection of the decoder vector, and a layer-depth prior offered explicitly as a tiebreaker; the explainer may answer "Uninterpretable" instead of guessing. Explanations from an expert's dominant set are collected into a word cloud, the cloud is turned into a short expert label, and all 128 labels are partitioned into domains in a single further call with only a loose bound of 6–13 groups specified in advance. Every prompt is reproduced in the appendices.

## What counts as an explanation here
A feature dictionary plus a bipartite graph, and a natural-language account read off that graph. The SAE supplies the latents (the features), the exact decomposition supplies the edges from experts to latents, and the interpretation of an expert is a word cloud over the explanations of the latents it dominates — so the object is a *description of a component* rather than a circuit, and it is deliberately partial: it says which features an expert principally drives, not how those features are then used downstream. The claim is predictive in exactly one stated place — the authors propose it as a testable hypothesis that pruning, merging or editing an expert will chiefly affect the latents it dominates — and they do not run that test.

## How they judged success
Against the instrument it replaces, plus internal consistency. The gating-versus-contribution comparison is the primary control: rank profiles are made comparable by construction, and the ReLU truncation that could itself steepen the contribution profile is priced by counting, per (prefix, latent) pair, how many of the eight activated experts are truncated. Structural claims are checked with the full latent-side and expert-side degree distributions and a Jaccard matrix over all 8128 unordered expert pairs per layer, against the 1/128 chance level of an evenly shared latent. Thematically, four experts are scored by an LLM judge on the share of their sampled latents' explanations that belong to the named theme, with a random baseline of 2000 latents drawn from the other 127 experts' dominant sets. Domain structure is checked by the same judge procedure with no taxonomy supplied, and its output is inspected for catch-all groups. There are no seeds, no other MoE model, and no intervention experiment; the strongest external validation on offer is that the domain grouping is recovered rather than matched.

## What they found
- Contribution is far steeper than gating. At layer 30 the three highest-ranked gating weights average 0.231, 0.169 and 0.137, decaying gently, while the three highest-ranked contributions average 0.512, 0.185 and 0.115 — the top expert alone accounts for over half. At the bottom of the ranking the contribution is 0.005 against a gating weight of 0.074. Layers 4 and 46 show the same shape, and the truncation is mild (only 2.09, 1.29 and 1.64 of the eight activated experts are truncated on average per (prefix, latent) pair), so concentration is not a top-k artefact.
- Most latents have a single dominator — over half of the SAE's latents have exactly one — 57.4% at layer 4, 62.1% at layer 30 and 56.7% at layer 46 — and including two dominators reaches 89.9%, 85.1% and 92.7%; degree three or above never exceeds 3.7%. A minority (7.0%, 13.6%, 3.7%) has no dominator at all, i.e. its activation is sourced too evenly to clear the threshold.
- Dominance is spread across the layer, not absorbed by hubs: every one of the 128 experts dominates a non-empty set in every layer, and the ten highest-degree experts hold at most 30.1% of all edges (24.6%, 20.1% and 30.1%) against the 7.8% a uniform degree would give.
- Pairwise overlap is nearly empty. Median Jaccard stays near 1e-4 in all three layers, 37.5–40.5% of the 8128 pairs share no latent at all, and the top tenth of pairs carries 83.8%, 78.5% and 81.0% of the total intersection. At most 0.57% of pairs exceed J = 0.05, the largest J reaches 0.138, 0.098 and 0.317, and the tail is organised into *pairs* — most experts with a partner above 0.05 have exactly one, the largest connected group has 10, 4 and 8 members, and 46–59% of an expert's dominant set is dominated by that expert alone.
- Specialisation is finer than a domain label. Layer 30 contains a Java expert (expert 50, whose top words are Java, Android, imports), a front-end web expert (57 — HTML, CSS, JavaScript plus React, Bootstrap, jQuery and latents for DOM events and CSS selectors), a geometry expert (1) and an arithmetic-and-polynomial expert (76). Judge-scored theme share inside each dominant set is 86.7% (Java), 78.6% (front-end), 78.1% (geometry) and 59.1% (arithmetic), against 1.9–6.7% for the same themes in 2000 latents drawn from the other 127 experts.
- Token-level pictures lose this. The token word cloud of expert 72 is dominated by function words (the, and, of); after removing the five most frequent of them and reweighting by mutual-information contribution it is more informative but still misses one of the two themes the explanation cloud shows, because the latents carrying it fire only at particular symbols — one latent's explanation is "Binary search loop condition", and as a token it can contribute only `<=`.
- Allocation across domains is strongly unequal. Layer 30's 128 experts fall into eleven recovered domains: computing 28, text form 21, mathematics and reasoning 20, applied science 18, language 8, culture and recreation 7, entities and identifiers 7, safety and advice 5, commerce 5, society and law 5, time and place 4. Computing plus mathematics and reasoning hold 37.5% of the layer; art appears in a single label. Three of the eleven domains (text form, language, entities and identifiers, 36 experts, 28.1%) are not subject areas at all, and would have no home in a subject-matter taxonomy.
- The overlapping pairs are mostly neighbours inside a domain: of the 20 largest-J pairs at layer 30, 19 join two experts of the same domain, against 12.8% of all pairs — punctuation, mojibake, JavaScript web programming. The one exception links LaTeX algebra to chemical and algebraic notation.

One correction to the paper's own framing that a reader should carry: the "over half of the 220 latents" reading is the latent *side* of the graph, and the domain counts in the last bullet come from an LLM grouping whose granularity was bounded by the prompt (6–13 groups), so the eleven domains are the model's recovery of a partition at a chosen resolution, not a resolution the paper establishes independently.

## Limits and open problems they name
The analysis inherits the SAE's imperfections: reconstruction error (FVU 12.6%, 25.1% and 7.6% at layers 4, 30, 46), feature splitting and absorption, so the latents are not a clean basis. Qwen3's pretraining mixture is undisclosed and the statistics are computed on a corpus of the authors' own choosing, so what is measured is routing on their data, not on the training data — which makes the explanation of the uneven allocation (that it tracks domain proportions in pretraining, by analogy with Llama 3's reported mix of roughly 25% maths/reasoning, 17% code and 50% general knowledge) explicitly a conjecture. Only one model is studied, at three layers, with one SAE configuration; the dominance hypothesis about pruning, merging and feature-level steering is proposed and not tested. The interpretation pipeline is itself an LLM, used for latent explanations, theme judging, expert labelling and the domain grouping, with the prompts disclosed.

## What we take from it
- **A framing (and a free instrument):** an exact algebraic decomposition beats a fitted attribution whenever the quantity you want is linear in the thing you are attributing to. Because the MoE output is linear in the expert outputs and the SAE encoder is affine, the expert→latent attribution is closed-form and needs no patching budget, no random-ablation baseline and no seed variation. For our own white-box work the check to run first is "is there a linear map between the component and the unit I am explaining?" — if yes, write the decomposition down and spend the experimental budget on interpretation instead.
- **A measure:** normalise the attribution to a *fraction* and compare profiles by rank, not by identity. Aggregating gating weights and contributions over ranks makes them sum to the same total, so "my statistic is steeper than the router" becomes a comparable claim rather than an impression. This is the folder's recurring lesson about denominators arriving on the MoE side.
- **A warning about the instrument being replaced:** selection frequency is not contribution, and the gap is large and structured — the router's own top weight accounts for 23% of the gating mass but 51% of the contribution at layer 30. Any analysis of "which component handles this input" that counts firings rather than weighted effect is measuring a quantity with a wide, non-uniform relationship to the one it names.
- **A shortcut for labelling components:** describe a component by the features it *dominates* rather than by the tokens it sees. The token cloud of expert 72 is function words; the latent cloud shows two separate themes. Where we summarise a neuron, head or feature by top-activating inputs, this is the failure mode to expect, and the fix is to move one level down to learned units and describe those.
- **A reporting rule for recovered taxonomies:** the benchmark for a recovered partition should be reproducibility, not agreement with a human one — but the resolution at which the partition was recovered must be stated with it, since the eleven domains here come out of a prompt that allowed 6–13 groups.
- **A caution across the folder's MoE gap:** "contribution" here is a property of the *layer output*, aggregate over a corpus, at one threshold. It is not a causal claim about what happens when an expert is removed, and the paper is clear that this is a hypothesis for future work.
