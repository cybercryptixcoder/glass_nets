## What they wanted, and why
A claim like "the model represents the concept *animal* as a direction" is only checkable if you have said precisely what a concept is and what it means for a vector to represent one. An earlier paper by two of the same authors did that for binary concepts that come with natural word pairs (man/woman, king/queen). Most concepts have no such pair: *is_animal* has no opposite word, and a category like {mammal, bird, fish, reptile} is not a binary at all. The authors want to close that gap, and they want to do it in a way that turns semantic structure into geometric structure, because then geometry can be used to predict what an intervention will and will not do. The larger goal is a bookkeeping system for representation space in which concepts, their categories and their hierarchy are all vectors and polytopes obeying ordinary linear algebra.

## What they studied
Unit of analysis: **vectors** and **polytopes** in the space where the final-layer context embedding and the token unembeddings live, after a change of coordinates. The concepts are taken from WordNet: every noun and verb synset with at least 50 words in the vocabulary, which leaves **593 noun and 364 verb synsets** in Gemma-2B (the paper says "900+ hierarchically related concepts" in the abstract, counted across both parts of speech). Models: **Gemma-2B** (2,048-dimensional final representation, 256K-token vocabulary, pretrained on 3T tokens) as the main subject, with LLaMA-3-8B repeated in an appendix. Analysis is confined to the last layer and the unembedding matrix; the authors say explicitly that they do not address the internal structure of the network.

## How they did it
First they fix a coordinate system. Following the earlier paper, they estimate a **causal inner product** by whitening the unembedding matrix — centring by its mean and multiplying by the inverse square root of its covariance — which leaves the softmax probabilities unchanged but makes causally independent concepts orthogonal, and which lets a context embedding and a token unembedding be compared as vectors in one space. A concept is defined as a latent variable that the context causes and that causes the output, and "z is subordinate to w" is defined by set inclusion of the token sets that carry each attribute; a redefinition of linear representation is then given that additionally requires that steering along the parent's vector must not disturb relative probabilities among its children. From that definition they prove three results: a magnitude theorem (every token with the attribute has the same projection onto the feature vector, tokens without it project to zero, so a feature has a *vector*, not merely a direction); a corollary that a binary contrast is the difference of two feature vectors; and a hierarchy theorem that four kinds of parent/child vector difference are mutually orthogonal. Dimensions are then estimated in practice with a shrinkage-covariance linear-discriminant rule, which is the closed form the magnitude theorem implies — maximise the mean projection while minimising within-set variance. Validation follows three routes, each with a shuffled-unembedding control: project train/test/random words onto each estimated vector and check the predicted near-1 / near-0 values; add a parent's vector to context and measure the change in logit differences for parent and for child token pairs; and compare cosine similarity between estimated vectors to shortest-path distance in the WordNet graph.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **geometric dictionary with a derivation attached**: each concept is a vector, a category is the convex hull of its members' vectors (a simplex for natural categories, proved in an appendix), and the hierarchy is a direct-sum structure — a child's vector is its parent's vector plus an orthogonal residual. The account is meant to be complete for a fixed vocabulary and a fixed final layer, and it is explicitly predictive: from the vector alone you can compute the change in logit difference that steering will produce, independent of the context.

## How they judged success
Four checks, each with a built-in control. (1) **Existence**: mean projection of held-out (30%) words onto each estimated vector divided by its squared norm should be near 1 for words with the attribute and near 0 for random words; the shuffled-unembedding arm should collapse to 0. (2) **Intervention**: change in logit difference between parent token pairs and between child token pairs after adding a normalised parent contrast vector, reported as mean ± standard deviation over all pairs. (3) **Structure**: cosine similarity between a child-minus-parent difference and the parent vector should be near zero, against two baselines — a randomly chosen parent feature, and shuffled unembeddings. (4) An ablation of the coordinate system: the same analysis under plain centring (Euclidean inner product) instead of whitening.

## What they found
Vector representations exist for most WordNet noun and verb synsets in Gemma: test-word projections land near 1 and random-word projections near 0, while the shuffled control puts test projections at 0. The intervention separates parent from child cleanly — adding the plant→animal vector moves parent-pair logit differences by 5.13 ± 1.17, the fluid→solid vector by 9.83 ± 1.11 and scientist→contestant by 14.42 ± 0.95, while the corresponding child pairs move by −0.06 ± 1.22, 0.38 ± 1.54 and −0.15 ± 1.14, i.e. no more than the spread. Hierarchical orthogonality holds (child-minus-parent is near-orthogonal to the parent), a random-parent baseline is far from zero, and the effect survives the harder test: when representations are estimated from an independently chosen 70% of each synset's words — which breaks the set inclusion the estimator could otherwise exploit — the original unembeddings stay orthogonal at about −0.2 cosine while the shuffled ones lose it. Under plain centring without whitening, the orthogonality is absent, which the authors present as evidence that the causal inner product, not the raw Euclidean one, is the space in which the statement is true. The LLaMA-3-8B appendix reproduces the same three panels.

## Limits and open problems they name
Everything is the **final layer** — the causal inner product is estimated from the unembedding matrix, and the authors call it an important open question how to make sense of the geometry of internal layers. The theory describes structure *if* linear representations exist; it does not explain why gradient descent would produce them (that is left to other work). They also note the exact form of the magnitude theorem is an ideal that gradient descent probably does not hit, and expect isotropic noise around the two predicted values instead. Two smaller admitted awkwardnesses: WordNet's tree is not a tree (a synset can have several parents, and one is picked), and the word sets for the illustrative figures were generated with a commercial language model and then inspected by hand.

## What we take from it
- **A falsifiable template.** Every claim in the paper has the shape "a linear-algebraic prediction about vectors, checked against a measurement, with a shuffle control". That is the form a white-box model's claims should take, and the three measurements (projection near 1, logit-difference change local to the target, near-zero cosine for a difference vector) are directly reusable.
- **The distinction our dictionary entries are missing.** A dictionary that reports "a latent for mammal, a latent for bird, a latent for animal" is reporting vectors that the theory says must be strongly collinear, hence hard to separate; the authors' own reading is that SAE-style methods should instead look for the *differences*. Any account we write of a hierarchy should state whether it fixed a gauge (a simplex of category vertices) or left vectors collinear.
- **A control we should copy verbatim.** The 70%-independent-split test is the right answer to "your effect is an artefact of your estimator using set inclusion": break the property the estimator could have exploited, then re-run. Reporting the result with and without the break is one line of work and removes an entire class of objection.
- **A price on the metric.** The whole result disappears under the naive inner product, so "these vectors are orthogonal" is meaningless without naming the coordinate system. Any orthogonal-uniqueness or disentanglement claim we make must name its metric, as entries `arxiv-2411.13117` (the decoder Gram matrix) and `arxiv-2606.08365` (decoder crowding) already do in a different coordinate system.
- **Warning for probe-based prediction.** The intervention's predicted effect is context-independent here precisely because the model's readout is a linear map followed by softmax. A probe that predicts correctly on this final-layer geometry should not be assumed to carry the same guarantee at an internal layer, which is where we operate.
