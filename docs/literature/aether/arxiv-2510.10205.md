# PIXEL: Adaptive Steering Via Position-wise Injection with eXact Estimated Levels under Subspace Calibration

- **Key:** arxiv-2510.10205
- **Authors / organisation:** Manjiang Yu, Hongji Li, Priyanka Singh, Xue Li, Di Wang, Lijie Hu; University of Queensland / MBZUAI / KAUST
- **Date:** 2025-10-11
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10205
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Activation steering can move a frozen model towards an attribute such as truthfulness without training, but the two knobs every method exposes — which site to intervene at and how hard — are usually set by hand or by a global coefficient. The authors want both to be *computed* rather than tuned: a threshold that says how close to the target direction the representation must end up, then the smallest edit that attains it, evaluated separately at every layer and prompt position. The stated diagnosis is that a fixed-magnitude vector applied broadly can damage perplexity and can over- or under-steer depending on whether that token is receptive to the direction at all. The larger goal is a tuning-free controller whose cost is set per site by a geometric criterion rather than by a sweep.

## What they studied
Three instruction-tuned backbones: Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-v0.3. Attributes: truthfulness (TruthfulQA MC1/MC2), social bias (BBQ accuracy on the disambiguated subset), safety refusal (Sorry-Bench scored by a Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 judge) and helpfulness (HelpSteer, 0–4, GPT-3.5-Turbo rater). Capability retention on RACE, MMLU, OpenBookQA and GLUE. The unit of analysis is the residual-stream state at one (layer, token) site, plus a layer-set and one token position as the standing configuration.

## How they did it
Contrastive pairs are built *without answers*: the same question is wrapped in an attribute-encouraging instruction versus its opposite, so the differential is not entangled with answer templates. For each pair they compute two differentials per layer — a mean over an adaptive tail window of the last k prompt tokens (k = clip of 10% of length, floored at 3, capped at 8) and the single end-of-prompt token difference — stack both across pairs and probe examples, and take a PCA of rank r (typically 2). The steering vector is the sum of the top two components with its own norm carried as the scale. For a hidden state h, they pick the smallest non-negative α such that cos(h + αv, v) ≥ s for a threshold s, which has a closed form built from the projection a = ⟨h,v⟩ and the orthogonal residual norm B = sqrt(|h|² − a²): the first term is a rectified correction for states pointing away from v, the second the value that attains the cosine threshold. Because α shrinks on states that are already aligned and on those with small orthogonal residual, they read small α as evidence that a site is sensitive. Each test sample then gets an orthogonal residual: a second answer-free pair is formed for that sample, its pooled difference is projected onto the complement of the span of the top components and normalised, and the target becomes the global vector plus a signed weight (λ = ±1) and magnitude ρ times that residual. Site selection is a scan: the gain of injecting at each layer at the end position against the no-injection baseline, keep the maximal consecutive block of layers with positive gain around the argmax, then refine the token over the three positions around the prompt end. Thresholds used in the main runs are 0.85 and 0.9, subspace extraction uses 200 samples, and results are averaged over three seeds.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a description of the model. The object is a *schedule*: a subspace per layer, a per-site scalar strength produced by a closed-form rule, and one chosen injection site. The closed form is a local guarantee about the edited state (it lands within a specified cosine of v), not a claim about what the direction means or what computation it participates in. The two theorems are light: one is a Hoeffding concentration bound on a normalised margin that holds because the margin was normalised into the unit interval, and the other is that the margin is non-negative by construction when the strength is the minimal one. Neither is used to predict behaviour.

## How they judged success
Per-benchmark accuracy against ICL, ReFT, ITI, CAA and ACT with standard deviations over three seeds; a capability table that must not fall below the unsteered model; three one-component removals (single-view subspace instead of dual, fixed α = 1 instead of the closed form, no per-sample calibration) reported as full tables; and a sensitivity sweep of the cosine threshold over 0.6–1.0 on three metrics and three models, read from a figure. No ablation replaces the adaptive strength with a *matched-norm* constant, and no arm removes the layer/position scan in favour of an exhaustive site search.

## What they found
Main table: on Llama-3-8B, TruthfulQA MC1 goes 28.70 unsteered to 46.30 and MC2 45.03 to 60.28, BBQ 0.625 to 0.699, Sorry 0.49 to 0.53, HelpSteer 3.76 to 3.88, with ITI at 37.03 MC1 and ACT at 55.87 MC2 the closest baselines. Qwen2-7B: MC1 27.65 to 37.33, MC2 48.31 to 55.22, BBQ 0.648 to 0.732, Sorry 0.38 to 0.66, HelpSteer 3.51 to 3.94. Mistral-7B: MC1 22.22 to 33.80, MC2 36.99 to 50.65, BBQ 0.680 to 0.736, Sorry 0.23 to 0.54, HelpSteer unchanged at 3.76 against CAA's 3.76. Capability table: on Llama the method is at or above the unsteered model on all four columns (RACE 0.690 vs 0.671, MMLU 0.668 vs 0.655, OpenBookQA 0.556 vs 0.556, GLUE 0.797 vs 0.726), while ITI loses a lot of MMLU (0.546); on Qwen it gains MMLU 0.695 to 0.718 and GLUE 0.825 to 0.871; on Mistral it moves RACE 0.678 to 0.697, MMLU 0.618 to 0.664 and OpenBookQA 0.602 to 0.641 while GLUE is flat at 0.681. Ablations, per the tables: fixed strength α = 1 costs the most of the three (Llama MC2 51.20 vs 60.28; Qwen Sorry 0.42 vs 0.66); dropping per-sample calibration costs less but consistently (Mistral MC1 29.17 vs 33.80, Qwen HelpSteer 3.51 vs 3.94); dropping the tail view is reported only from a figure, with MC1 gains of about 2.9 / 5.9 / 14.8 points for Mistral / Qwen / Llama from adding it. Threshold sensitivity is described as broad and flat, with low thresholds under-steering and a threshold of 1.0 over-steering.

## Limits and open problems they name
No dedicated limitations section. The sensitivity analysis concedes model-specific variance (Mistral degrades more at low thresholds). The conclusion frames multi-attribute control as future work, which is consistent with the paper steering one attribute at a time.

## What we take from it
Three things. (1) **A closed-form minimal-strength rule as a free per-site sensitivity readout.** The formula is two lines from a projection and a residual norm, and the paper's whole claim is that the resulting α is itself the diagnostic: small α means the site was already receptive. For us this is a *measurement* candidate rather than an intervention — the per-site α required to reach a fixed cosine is a property of the state, layer and direction that can be computed for every site without any behavioural run, and it can be correlated against the gain a real sweep measures. The paper never publishes that correlation for α, only for the scan's gain; that gap is exactly the kind of cheap validation the folder has repeatedly asked for. (2) **The layer/position scan is a behavioural search, and its result is reused on the test set.** Whatever α predicts, the site is still chosen by running the metric per layer on a probe set — so this is not site prediction, and the entry should not be cited as though it were. (3) **A caution about the ablation ladder.** The fixed-strength arm uses α = 1 at *every* site, which is not norm-matched to what the closed form produces, so the comparison mixes "adaptivity" with "an arbitrary strength that is too large somewhere and too small elsewhere"; the same criticism the folder applied to a frozen-direction arm applies here, and the matched control would be the mean of the fitted α over the fitting set. Also worth noting for our calipers: the boosts are large on a metric where the unsteered baseline is weak (Mistral MC1 at 22.22), and the one backbone with a strong baseline gains least (Mistral HelpSteer flat, BBQ +0.056).
