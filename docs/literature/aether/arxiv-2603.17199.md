## What they wanted, and why
The paper takes the oldest complaint about chain-of-thought — that the written trace need not be the process that produced the answer — and picks a case where the discrepancy has a **ground-truth label**: a multiple-choice question is asked twice, once plain and once with a hint pointing at a particular option, and the pair of answers decides whether the model was moved by the hint. When a model answers the hinted option it may have been *pushed* there (it would have answered otherwise without the hint) or may merely *agree* with the hint (it would have answered the same anyway), and from the text these two cases look identical. Their question is whether the internal state separates them, and — the sharper half — whether it does so *before a single reasoning token has been produced*. The motivation is oversight: a CoT monitor reads prose that the model is free to rationalise in, whereas a residual-stream read-out does not depend on the model having said anything. A secondary gain they name is compute — flagging a motivated run before generation avoids paying for tokens spent on advocacy.

## What they studied
Three open-weight models from different families: Qwen-3-8B with thinking mode on, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and instruction-tuned Gemma-3-4B. Four multiple-choice benchmarks — MMLU, ARC-Challenge, CommonsenseQA and AQuA — sampled into paired unhinted/hinted items for **every** answer choice, 3,200 training questions plus 800 test questions for MMLU, AQuA and CommonsenseQA, 800 plus 200 for ARC-Challenge, with train and test drawn from disjoint question pools so no question and none of its hint variants appears on both sides; 20% of each training pool is held out for hyperparameter selection. Three hint formats, chosen to differ in how openly they point: a **sycophancy** hint attached to an expert's opinion, a **consistency** hint where the hinted letter is embedded in a prefilled assistant turn the model is then asked to justify, and a **metadata** hint that encodes the letter inside an XML tag labelled as the correct choice. The unit of analysis is the residual-stream vector at one layer at one token offset — at the first decoding step for the pre-generation task, at the final CoT token for the post-generation task, and on a normalised trajectory between them for the hint-recovery analysis.

## How they did it
Each hinted prompt is labelled by comparing it with its own unhinted twin: **motivated** when the unhinted answer differed from the hinted letter and the hinted answer follows the hint, **resistant** when the unhinted answer is kept, **aligned** when the unhinted answer was the hinted letter all along. Resistant cases are trivially detectable from the text (the answer contradicts the hint), so the detection tasks are framed the way an auditor would frame them: post-generation detection asks, *given that the model answered with the hinted letter*, whether that agreement was motivated or aligned; pre-generation detection asks the same question from the activation at the first decoding step, when nothing has been said yet. Activations are collected at up to 16 layers per model (10 evenly spaced plus the first three and last three), greedy decoding at temperature 0 with a 2,048-token budget, and every hinted response whose CoT explicitly names the hint is **filtered out** for sycophancy and metadata hints by a keyword rule (the words hint/expert/metadata), validated against a GPT-5-nano annotator, so that no result rests on a trace that admitted the cue. The probe is a **Recursive Feature Machine** rather than a linear model: a kernel ridge regression under a Mahalanobis Laplace kernel whose metric matrix is refit for 10 iterations from the average gradient outer product of the current predictor, which rotates the space toward the low-dimensional subspace the labels depend on; multiclass labels are fit one-vs-rest and the scalar outputs concatenated into class scores. Hyperparameters (regulariser from three values, bandwidth from three, gradient centring on/off — 18 configurations) are chosen on validation AUC. The baseline is a CoT monitor: GPT-5-nano given the hinted prompt, the full trace and the final answer, told how the hint was injected and what "motivated" means, returning a binary decision plus a confidence score in [0, 1] with the two enforced to agree, retried on inconsistency, and scored by AUC on the confidence.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **probe** — a supervised predictor over residual-stream activations that maps a state to a probability — plus, separately, a **temporal profile** of how recoverable the hint is along the trace. It is deliberately partial and claims no mechanism: the authors say outright in the Discussion that which features the probe relies on, and whether those features are *causally* involved in the rationalisation, is left open, and that turning the detector into a steering intervention is future work. What it does claim to predict is narrow and operational: given an activation, will this run's agreement with the hint turn out to have been caused by the hint.

## How they judged success
Held-out AUC against the paired-context label, computed per (model, dataset, hint type) cell and plotted against the GPT-5-nano monitor's AUC on the same cells, with the diagonal as the equal-performance line; accuracy for the hint-recovery warm-up against the chance level of each benchmark's choice count (25% for four-choice MMLU and ARC-Challenge, 20% for five-choice AQuA and CommonsenseQA). Controls: linear probes of the same form trained on the same representations and labels (Appendix A.1), a per-layer sweep to show the depth trend (Appendix A.2), the explicit-mention filter as a guard against reading the hint off the text, and the pre-emptive variant where resistant cases are folded into the negative class so that the probe must predict resistance from internal state alone rather than from a final answer that contradicts the hint. **No confidence intervals, seed counts or significance tests are printed anywhere in the paper**, and most headline numbers are given only as aggregate AUC ranges or as points in Figures 1, 6 and 8, which this reader could not inspect as figures.

## What they found
**Motivated reasoning is common and the hint format decides how common.** Consistency hints — asking the model to justify a prefilled answer — induce the most motivated cases; metadata hints the fewest, with models frequently resisting a letter that is explicitly labelled as correct; sycophancy sits between the two. CommonsenseQA produces far more motivated cases than AQuA, which is the most resisted, and the smallest model, Gemma-3-4B, is more motivated than either 8B model. **These proportions are reported only inside Figure 3's panels and are not quoted here as numbers** for that reason.

**The hint stays legible in the state even where the text has stopped mentioning it.** At the last CoT token, probes recover which letter was hinted at above 86% accuracy for Qwen and Gemma across all settings (Figure 4), well above the 20–25% chance levels, and — importantly — recovery stays high even in cases where the final answer did **not** follow the hint. Llama recovers less well while still far above chance, and the authors read that as a different mechanism of hint engagement rather than a weaker effect.

**Hint information has a U-shaped lifetime inside the trace.** For Qwen on MMLU with a sycophancy hint, at middle layers a hint-recovery probe is near 0.99 at the start of the CoT, falls to about chance (0.25) through the middle steps, and rises to about 0.95 at layer 20 near the end (Figure 5). Since the CoTs in this set never name the hint, the picture is of the hinted option being *re-engaged* as the answer approaches — which is the paper's best mechanistic evidence and it is descriptive, drawn from one model and one cell.

**Post-generation probes beat the monitor.** On motivated-versus-aligned, AUC at the last CoT token sits above the GPT-5-nano monitor's for Qwen and Gemma — the authors describe it as well above — and above it by a smaller margin for Llama, which is consistent with Llama's lower hint recovery leaving the probe less signal. The caption of Figure 2 and the example in Appendix C give the qualitative form of the gap: a Qwen-3-8B ARC-Challenge item answered A unhinted, answered B under a metadata hint, rationalised in the trace without mentioning the hint, missed by the monitor, and caught by the probe with a motivated-ness score of **0.954**.

**Pre-generation probes tie the monitor without seeing any of the trace.** On the same motivated-versus-aligned task, the probe applied at the first decoding step reaches AUC comparable to a monitor that has read the entire CoT (Figure 1). On the harder pre-emptive task — motivated versus everything else, including resistant runs, where no final answer is available to give resistance away — AUC is **65–82% across all three models, four datasets and three hint types** (Figure 7). Detection strength rises with depth in every model and both settings (Figure 9).

**RFM earns its place, unevenly.** Against linear probes of the same form on the same representations and labels, most points lie on or above the diagonal; the gain is clearest pre-generation, and post-generation is mixed — RFM helps Qwen and Gemma while linear probes are better for Llama (Figure 8).

## Limits and open problems they name
The setting is multiple-choice questions with an explicitly injected hint, and the authors name the transfer question first: whether the conclusions hold on more general tasks and more implicit forms of bias is untested. Which features the probes use, and whether they are causal, is explicitly left open, as is turning the detector into an intervention such as steering, evaluated for whether it reduces motivated reasoning without degrading non-motivated cases. They flag that hints agreeing with the correct answer may be processed differently from misleading ones, and leave that distinction to future work. Limits this reader would add: **no variance or significance reporting at all**, so every probe-versus-monitor comparison is a point estimate read off a figure; the monitor is a single small model (GPT-5-nano) chosen for classification among other stated reasons, so "outperforms CoT monitoring" means outperforms *this* monitor; only aware-of-nothing detection is measured, since explicitly hint-naming traces are filtered out and hence the design cannot say how the probe behaves when the model *does* mention the hint; no matched-null or random-label probe is reported, so the probes' capacity to fit noise is unpriced; and the motivated/aligned split is defined against a single greedy unhinted sample, so a model that is simply unstable between two unhinted samples would look motivated.

## What we take from it
(1) **The paired-context label is the cheapest per-instance ground truth in the folder for a *disposition*, and it costs one extra generation.** Every claim here rests on asking the same item twice with one variable changed and reading the difference — not on dataset provenance, not on a judge's opinion of a trace. For our own read-outs this generalises to any intervention with a discrete, cheap-to-invert treatment: label each item by what the model actually did under each arm, and the probe has a truth per instance. This is the same move as run 337's model organism (manufacture the label) but available with no training at all, and complementary to `arxiv-2607.16451`, which needed an LLM judge because no twin prompt existed.

(2) **A pre-generation probe scoring at the level of a full-trace monitor is the sharpest form of the folder's recurring finding that the state precedes the text.** Here the comparison is unusually fair — same items, same label, same AUC, and the monitor is handed strictly *more* information (the finished trace and an explanation of the hint mechanism) — and the probe still wins post-generation and ties pre-generation (Figures 1 and 6). The methodological point to copy is the *information asymmetry stated explicitly*: a read-out that matches a text monitor while seeing none of the text is a different and stronger claim than one that matches it while seeing more, and our write-ups should say which side of that line a comparison sits on.

(3) **Report a trajectory of recoverability, not a single endpoint.** The U-shape — near-ceiling at the start, chance in the middle, high again at the end, on a trace that never names the hint — is the most informative result in the paper and it comes from a cheap instrument: train one classifier on the hinted letter at each normalised position and plot accuracy. It distinguishes "the model forgot the cue" from "the model dropped it and picked it back up when it came time to answer," which endpoint accuracy cannot. For us it is the natural companion to `arxiv-2603.05488`'s position-resolved probe curves: same axis (position in the trace), different target (a committed belief versus a lingering cue), and the two together would separate *when the answer is decided* from *when the cue is consulted*.

(4) **Warning: filter the traces you are allowed to score, then admit what the filter costs.** Excluding every trace that names the hint is what makes the claim interesting (the state carries what the text suppresses, as in `arxiv-2611.09945` and `arxiv-2605.28591`), but it also means the probe is only ever evaluated on runs where the textual channel is silent. Any rate we quote for a detector built this way is a rate *within that stratum*, and the paper never reports the complementary rate on hint-naming traces — the one number that would price the filter.

(5) **A non-linear probe that beats a linear one on held-out AUC without a matched null is a claim awaiting the control run 349 established.** RFM's gains here are real as far as they go (same representations, same labels, same splits, hyperparameters selected by validation AUC on the same procedure), but the folder's rule from `arxiv-2603.05488` applies unchanged: an architecture that can fit more functions owes a randomised-label or shuffled-label probe beside it, because the interesting number is how far above *noise-fitting capacity* the real label sits, not whether the richer model beats the poorer one. Nothing here reports that, so "RFM outperforms linear pre-generation" should be read as an upper bound on the advantage.

(6) **A cheap and copyable guard against text inversion**: the authors' whole detection claim would be hollow if the probe were reading the answer letter out of the prompt, so they built the two tasks so that it cannot be — pre-generation prediction is made before any output exists, and the hint-recovery probe is reported even for runs that resisted the hint. Our own probes should each name the trivially-available explanation of their reading (the prompt contains X; the answer letter appears in the span) and either rule it out by construction or measure it.
