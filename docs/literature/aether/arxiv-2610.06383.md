# Steering by Influence: Curvature Aware Data Weighting for Activation Steering

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.06383
- **Authors / organisation:** James A. E. Dixon, Stephen J. Roberts, Francesco Quinzan; Machine Learning Research Group, Department of Engineering Science, University of Oxford
- **Date:** 2026-10-05
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06383
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Every transport-based steering method in this line — the affine transport map fitted between a neutral and a concept activation distribution — treats all the examples of the concept class as equally worth steering towards, and it does so at the *token* level: activations are averaged over a sample's positions before the map is fitted, so whatever a sentence's wording contributes to the mean arrives with the same weight as whatever the theme contributes. The authors' complaint is that this makes the transport target a class mean that is contaminated twice over: by unrelated concepts mixed into the samples, and by examples that technically contain the concept but barely express it. Their goal is to keep the cheap per-dimension affine map (no Sinkhorn, no solver) and change only *which examples the map is fitted towards*, so that steering pulls towards the samples that most strongly define the concept while leaving the intervention's inference cost untouched. The larger motivation is the folder's usual trade-off: a method that moves the behaviour while leaving perplexity and MMLU where they were, and an argument for why a curvature-corrected signal should be better than a similarity one.

## What they studied

Two open-weight decoder-only models, Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.2-3B, steered at every transformer block's post-attention and post-feedforward layer norms (Gemma) or the MLP down-projection (Llama). The unit of analysis is not an activation or a direction but a **data point's weight in the transport plan**: the object being edited is the empirical measure over the concept class at one (layer, dimension) pair. There are three concept families chosen to straddle shallow and broad: seven object concepts from OneSec (baby, balloon, book, church, cloud, flower, football, roughly 700 sentences each), TruthfulQA truthfulness (817 questions, two-fold cross-validation, contrastive pairs built from true and false reference answers), and Jigsaw toxicity (10,000 comments per class, 2,000 of each used for training), where the intervention runs in the opposite direction — away from the toxic class.

## How they did it

The concept is defined as a direction in *parameter* space rather than activation space, by adapting Grosse et al.'s prompt-completion measurement function to a contrastive one: each of K triples (a neutral prefix, a concept-bearing completion, a matched non-concept completion) gives a length-normalised log-probability difference, its gradient is rescaled to unit norm so no triple dominates by magnitude alone, and the K gradients are averaged into a single vector q. Influence scores for the concept class are then the inner product of q with the preconditioned loss gradient of each candidate example, using the damped generalised Gauss-Newton inverse curvature taken from Bae et al. so the score is the exact influence under a proximal-Bregman response function rather than an approximation to a MAP — the paper draws the comparison explicitly, noting that as damping goes to infinity the score degenerates into plain gradient similarity. The inverse curvature is approximated with EK-FAC over MLP weights only (attention is excluded because the block-diagonal assumption does not hold there), and because the approximation is block-diagonal per weight matrix the score decomposes into a sum over layers, giving each example a separate influence score at each depth. Those per-layer scores are z-scored, turned into weights by a softmax with a temperature that interpolates between a point mass on the single most influential example and a uniform distribution, and injected into the transport as target-side masses: the one-dimensional monotone coupling between sorted source and sorted target activations now gives the k-th target a quantile interval proportional to its weight rather than to 1/N, so a high-influence example receives more transported mass. The closed-form variant refits the affine map by weighted least squares under that coupling (only the covariance and the target mean change, since only the target side is weighted); the open variant keeps LinEAS's jointly-learned per-(layer, dimension) affine parameters and swaps only the coupling inside its Wasserstein objective, which is the paper's deliberate test of whether the weighting generalises across transport methods.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **weighting rule over data**, not a description of a mechanism: the claim is that influence functions, because they include the curvature of the loss surface, rank examples by how much they contribute to the model's representation of a concept thematically rather than lexically, and that using that ranking as transport mass produces a better intervention. The explanation is instrumental — it says nothing about what the concept *is* in activation space, only about which examples carry it — and it is explicitly local: an example influential at one depth can be negligible at another, which is why the paper steers with a different effective dataset at every layer. The one substantive internal claim, that the curvature correction is what buys the thematic ranking, is argued by a limit (damping to infinity recovers gradient similarity) rather than demonstrated by an ablation that swaps one for the other.

## How they judged success

Behaviour, capability and a ranking-agreement test. Behaviour is an LLM-judge concept score (fraction of 500 generations judged to refer to the target concept, given its WordNet definition) for OneSec, MC1 and MC2 on held-out TruthfulQA halves, and two toxicity judges — the same kind of LLM judge and a purpose-built RoBERTa classifier — on 1,000 prompts drawn from RealToxicityPrompts and a separate engineered-toxicity set. Capability is two perplexities (of the generated continuation, and on held-out WikiText) and MMLU accuracy, all at a fixed steering strength with the unsteered row as the reference, with the full per-method tables with standard deviations in the appendix. The comparison is always weighted versus unweighted on the same transport (IF-Act vs AcT, IF-LinEAS vs LinEAS) plus a cross-method check, and a layer ablation steers on all layers versus excluding the first and last. The ranking test is a Spearman correlation between influence ranks and the ranks a cheap forward-only alternative gives — the projection of each example's activation onto the mean contrastive activation direction — reported corrected for split-half unreliability on both a per-candidate and a per-layer axis.

## What they found

Toxicity suppression is where the weighting clearly pays: IF-LinEAS is best on three of the four judge/dataset pairs and both models' RealToxicityPrompts judges, with IF-Act best on Gemma — toxicity falling far below the unweighted transport while perplexity and MMLU stay close to unsteered, and the suppression arms' perplexity roughly constant as strength increases. Induction is where the design matters more than the weighting: on OneSec, IF-Act has the top concept score on Gemma but pays for it heavily (WikiText perplexity rising by a factor of roughly thirty, MMLU dropping from 0.549 to 0.267), and the weighted variants beat their own baselines on concept score in most cells but the unweighted LinEAS remains the best on the capability metrics — a weighting that improves induction also makes it more destructive to the model. On TruthfulQA the gains are small and not monotone in data size: IF-Act is the best method in most cells, though plain transport beats it on MC1 at the larger pair count, and adding 32-fold more contrastive pairs moves MC2 by only a couple of points (and slightly downwards for one weighted variant on both models). The layer ablation gives the paper's most quotable internal contrast: excluding the first and last layers costs headline induction and suppression but *improves* stability substantially — on Llama toxicity the weighted closed-form method's continuation perplexity falls from about 111 to 208 between the two layer settings (higher on all layers, so the exclusion is the stable arm in MMLU terms: MMLU recovers from 0.276 to 0.306, while toxicity suppression weakens from 0.177 to 0.220 on the LLM judge) — which the authors explain by the model needing the first layers to parse and the last to emit tokens. The ranking test is the paper's strongest single number about *what* the influence signal is: influence and the activation-projection proxy correlate only weakly along the candidate axis (0.317) and moderately along the layer axis (0.504), and influence and raw gradient norm correlate at 0.079 and 0.346, so the curvature correction is ranking examples in an order that neither cheap alternative reproduces. Influence weights also concentrate in the middle layers for toxicity while staying near-uniform across layers for OneSec and TruthfulQA — the authors' diagnosis is that shallow object concepts are token-driven and the broad one is not.

## Limits and open problems they name

Steering strength is uniform across layers even though the influence weights are per-layer, so the per-layer breakdown is used to choose data but not (yet) to choose how hard to push at each depth; they propose exactly that as the fix for the middle-layer concentration they observe. The comparison is at fixed strength rather than at matched perplexity or matched capability, which they name as the way the steering-quality trade-off should really be characterised. The curvature machinery is substituted with an approximation (EEK-FAC over MLP weights, attention excluded), the influence is computed against an approximation of the model's training distribution (100 Wikipedia extracts), and the paper notes that the influence-function literature has raised reliability concerns for deep networks — handled here by adopting Bae et al.'s PBRF interpretation rather than by measuring the gap between the approximate and the ideal score. The damping parameter's role, which is what separates this score from plain gradient similarity, is analysed as a limit and left out of the ablation grid. Beyond what they name: the temperature of the weighting softmax is discussed in terms of an effective sample size study but no number for it appears in the main text, and it is not swept against the steering strength.

## What we take from it

A **method, a measure and a warning**. (i) The method: a curvature-aware attribution weight is being used as the *mass* of a transport plan, which is a clean separation of concerns — the transport decides the geometry and the influence decides the target population — and it is the only way in this folder's transport line to make the intervention respond to which examples were used without changing its inference cost at all. For our white-box model that is the general recipe worth copying: take an existing intervention and re-weight its *input distribution* rather than its parameters. (ii) The measure: this is the folder's cleanest published test of whether a new signal adds information over a cheap one. Reporting rank agreement between two scoring methods — corrected for the unreliability of each — is a far better defence than reporting that the weighted method wins, because a weak-to-moderate agreement (0.317 candidates, 0.504 layers) is a much sharper claim than "it works", and the near-zero agreement with gradient norm is the number that says the curvature term is doing the work. Mechanical rule for us: **when we propose a new scoring or support signal alongside an existing one, we report the rank correlation between them on the same candidate set, with each one's own split-half reliability, because a new signal that correlates strongly with the cheap one is a relabelling and a new signal that correlates weakly has to justify itself on outcomes — and here only the influence ranking's middle-layer concentration, absent for the shallow concepts, tells us when the signal is picking up theme rather than words.** (iii) A **warning** that repeats run 804's finding in a different coordinate: the weighting improves the concept score and worsens stability, and the layer ablation shows the two are traded by *where* the intervention fires rather than by how well it is fitted — so the headline "IF-weighted transport wins" is, in the induction arm, a statement about a heavier intervention, and the per-arm perplexity columns are the only thing that separates it from a stronger steering strength.
