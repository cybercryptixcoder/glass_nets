## What they wanted, and why

Membership inference asks whether a specific text was in a model's training data, and the standard readout is a likelihood-style score: the model's loss on the text, or a token-level variant of it. Interpretability work has shown that "memorisation" corresponds to identifiable internal representations that can even be steered, but nobody had separated the two ways training could create a membership signal — by changing *what is stored* in the residual stream, or by changing *how much of it the output head uses*. The authors want that separation, both as a mechanistic question and as a way to audit the attacks themselves: if a training procedure moves behaviour without touching either quantity a likelihood attack reads, then the attack's silence is a statement about its scope rather than about the model. The larger goal is a baseline for what membership signals look like from the inside, so that a detectability failure can be attributed to a mechanism.

## What they studied

Frozen open base models and checkpoints trained from them: **Tulu2-7B** for a controlled post-training contamination on AIME (problems and solutions 2021–2025; 120 members, 30 non-members, 32 drawn per seed) and Olympia Math (64 per seed), and **Qwen2-7B** for a pre-training setting on WikiMIA-2024-Hard (temporal cutoff, 64 per group) and for an RLVR contamination case study on MATH-500. The unit of analysis is the **token**: every target token is paired with the residual-stream representation at the preceding position, at each layer. Protocols compared: the base model; SFT at one and two epochs (exposure as a dial); two RL-based membership-inference attacks, ADRA and ADRA+ (the latter gated by a Min-K%++ prior), initialised from the one-epoch SFT model so that SFT's effect is subtracted out; and RLVR with GRPO, where 64 members are mixed into 640 prompts (10%) and the reward sees only the final answer, exposure-matched at steps 10 and 20 to one and two SFT epochs.

## How they did it

Three layer-wise scores on the same folds. **Decodable information D**: fit a linear probe from the layer's residual to the target token (output space restricted to the dataset's observed suffix tokens plus one unknown class — 1,328 classes for AIME, 2,630 for Olympia Math, 6,005 for WikiMIA — so that probe, logit lens and null share a vocabulary; rank capped at 256 for WikiMIA), 5-fold GroupKFold by problem, weight decay picked from four values as the smallest for which a label-shuffled probe stays below 5% of the pooled entropy, and take the average cross-entropy reduction against an intercept-only null that sees only the marginal label distribution. **Used information U**: the same reduction, with the frozen residual read through the model's own unembedding (logit lens) — no parameters fitted, so it measures what the layer already contributes to the prediction. **Readout gap G = D − U**, positive when a probe recovers more than the output head uses; the null cancels, and G is stated to be a lower bound away from the final layer since the probe remains rank-limited. **Difference-in-differences** then isolates member-specific change: the change each score undergoes from base to trained model, differenced between members and non-members, with 1,000 problem-level bootstrap resamples (members and non-members resampled separately). Epoch count, and step count in RLVR, form the dose axis. Two further arms: **steering** — a difference-of-means membership direction at layer 24 with bootstrap consistency over 100 resamples, injected one layer or sustained across layers 24–27, scored by loss-based AUROC on held-out pairs; and **activation patching** — forward and reverse swaps of the suffix-position residual at layer ℓ between base and trained model, with the patching ratio read against the trained model's own difference-in-differences, so that a positive forward-minus-reverse ratio means the effect is already present by ℓ.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **two-quantity decomposition of a behavioural signal, with depth and a causal window attached**: the same distinction (membership) is priced twice, once as retrievable-by-a-probe information in the representation and once as information the model's own head actually uses, and their difference is reported per layer. It is deliberately partial — it explains *where in the pipeline* training's effect lands, not which features carry membership — and it is predictive in a narrow sense: given a score profile and a layer window, one can say whether an attack that reads loss can work at all. The causal claim is localised separately, by patching, rather than derived from the scores.

## How they judged success

Reduction in cross-entropy (nats) against the intercept-only null, reported as per-layer profiles and as difference-in-differences with 95% CIs; loss-based AUROC and Min-K% AUROC for the attack comparison, with **CI-based** change scores rather than bare point differences; a shuffled-label control for every probe (chance-level AUROC ≈ 0.50, |D_shuffle| below a threshold derived from pooled entropy); a **blind TF-IDF/surface-statistics baseline** for the binary probes; bootstrap consistency (mean pairwise cosine over resamples) as a stability test for any steering direction; and, in the RLVR arm, a behavioural check — 32 samples per question at temperature 1.0, mean per-question accuracy on 64 members against 64 non-members — plus training-validity evidence (pool accuracy 54.9% → 71.8%, 59 of 64 members receiving at least one non-zero-advantage update).

## What they found

Membership is **already linearly decodable from a public base model**: probe AUROC peaks at 0.78 around layer 20 on WikiMIA-2024-Hard, but only 0.543 (Tulu3-Aya) and 0.57 (WildChat) in middle layers, rising to 0.553 and 0.605 at the second-to-last layer and dropping at the last. Training moves the two quantities very differently: across all trained models D changes by only **−0.10 to +0.05**, while U changes by roughly **−0.75 to +0.25 nats**, and both RL attacks *suppress* readout, reaching **−0.83 nats** in late layers. The member-specific split is the headline: RL attacks leave D almost untouched but exempt members from the readout suppression — ADRA+ gives positive member-minus-non-member DiD[U] across most layers, **+0.18 nats at the final layer on AIME and +0.20 on Olympia Math** — whereas SFT moves both, and increasingly so with epochs. Deep-layer comparison in the RLVR arm: DiD[D] +0.042 and DiD[U] +0.182 under SFT against +0.002 and +0.028 under RLVR. The RLVR case study is a clean **dissociation**: likelihood AUROC stays at 0.493 (mid) and 0.494 (later) — a +0.002 and +0.003 change, CIs straddling zero — against 0.661 after one SFT epoch and 0.908 after two, while behaviour moves visibly (member accuracy 0.141 → 0.170, non-member 0.132 → 0.088, gap widening from 0.009 to **0.082**, CI [+0.008, +0.159]). The loss on members rises 0.24 nats at step 10 and 0.56 at step 20, but non-members rise by the same amount, so the member-minus-non-member gap stays **below 0.01 nats** against **−0.16** after SFT. Steering fails to recover the effect: the SFT direction is stable (consistency 0.96) yet single-layer injection gives AUROC 0.56 at p = 0.07 and sustained injection only 0.60 at p = 0.01, while **RL-induced directions do not stabilise at all** (0.44–0.54) even though the training-induced shift is nearly rank-one (78–95% of variance in one component, against 2–3.5% for random directions) because that component is shared by members and non-members. Patching puts the RL effect's emergence at layer 8 or later (significantly negative forward-minus-reverse ratio at ℓ = 8 on both datasets) and between layers 8 and 20 on Olympia Math.

## Limits and open problems they name

The evaluation sets are deliberately small (light contamination: 32 pairs per seed, 64 in the supplementary settings), and **binary per-example probes turned out to be insensitive at this scale** (AUROC 0.29–0.55, no gain under SFT) while the token-level scores did show member-specific effects — so their own instrument choice is a statement about resolution, and the paper does not claim a per-example detector. Older base models were chosen precisely because contamination history is unknown for newer ones, which limits what can be said about current frontier models. The RLVR gap in likelihood signal is shown for **likelihood-based** attacks only; whether non-likelihood attacks see it is left open. The mechanistic story is locational rather than feature-level — nothing says *which* direction or feature carries membership — and the fact that no stable linear direction exists for the RL effect is reported as a finding, not solved. The authors also disclose extensive generative-AI use in writing, code and figure preparation.

## What we take from it

**A measure.** D, U and the gap between them are cheap, layer-wise, and reusable verbatim for any probe-versus-head comparison in our own battery: D is our probe's cross-entropy advantage over a label-marginal null, U is the same quantity for the frozen unembedding, and their difference is exactly the "decodable but not used" number we have been estimating with looser instruments. Two implementation details matter for reproduction: restrict probe, lens and null to **one shared output space**, and pick regularisation by requiring a label-shuffled probe to sit below a threshold tied to pooled entropy.

**A framing.** An intervention changes behaviour through two separable channels, and the second — readout gain — is invisible to probes and visible only to the model's own head. That is the same axis as the folder's earlier decodable-versus-used dissociations, arriving with the two quantities on a **common nat scale** so that their changes can be differenced; the honest caveat is that D is a lower bound and G is not comparable to any effect measured another way.

**A warning.** The RLVR result is the sharpest instance in the folder of a behaviour being real and a standard metric being blind to it: members gain accuracy while loss-based AUROC does not move, because both groups' loss rises equally. Any membership, contamination or memorisation claim we make should be accompanied by the behavioural arm, and the absence of a likelihood signal should be read as "the reward did not depend on the text" rather than "there was no effect".

**A shortcut, and a caution about it.** Difference-in-differences against a matched reference checkpoint is what turns a noisy profile into a member-specific claim here, and the matched-exposure construction (step 10 ≈ epoch 1) is the design that makes RL and SFT comparable. Do not extend it to directions: a nearly rank-one, 78–95%-variance training shift was mostly shared across groups and did not form a steerable direction, so "the effect is one-dimensional" has to be tested by bootstrap stability and by whether the component is *discriminative*, not by variance explained.
