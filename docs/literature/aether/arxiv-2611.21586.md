# Beyond Job Titles: Interpreting Early-Career Job Advertisements with Sparse Autoencoders and Semantic Clustering

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.21586
- **Authors / organisation:** Yisong (Ethan) Cao, Leah Kim, Brian Wright, Ali Rivera; Active Learning Lab and School of Data Science, University of Virginia
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21586
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML, §§1–7, appendices A–E)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Skill counts and keyword tallies do not say what a job actually asks a person to do: two postings can both require Python while one wants models built and the other wants a dashboard maintained. The authors ask whether two ways of representing text — grouping whole descriptions into clusters, or reading a frozen language model's internal features — add anything to simply handing the text to a capable model. Alongside the substantive question they run a small, fully reported method comparison: same descriptions, same hypothesis-writer, same budget, three different inputs, and the proposed claims checked on employers never seen during proposal. The larger goal is showing what an interpretability artefact is *for* when a language model is already competent at reading the source.

## What they studied

36,081 early-career job advertisements (41,040 scraped, filtered), overwhelmingly internships (35,511 of the retained set) collected by an hourly scraper between December 2025 and October 2026 with posting dates spanning 29 months. Five occupations are compared (Data Scientist 3,121, Data Analyst 3,840, Software Engineer 906, Machine Learning Engineer 901, Business Analyst 474). Units of analysis are two: an advertisement-or-section embedding (a vector), and an SAE feature on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct's layer-19 residual stream, using the pretrained Goodfire SAE (about 537 M parameters, 65,536 features, 4,096-dimensional residual, encoder-ReLU-decoder, no new training).

## How they did it

Two pipelines. **PERC** (preprocess, embed, reduce, cluster): split each advertisement into passages and label them Team context / Role task / Qualifications; form seven text inputs (three singles, three pairs, all three); embed with one of six sentence or API embedding models, chunk-averaging by token count when over the limit; compare PCA and UMAP at 10 and 50 dimensions against the original vectors, and K-means, diagonal Gaussian mixtures, hierarchical agglomerative, spectral and HDBSCAN; 540 configurations per input, repeated over five seeds. Companies are split 60/20/20 (whole companies, never advertisements) and the configuration is chosen on validation and evaluated once on test. Selection is not best-silhouette alone: a config must assign at least 60% of validation texts, hold mean ARI at 0.60 across runs, keep each cluster spread over at least ten training and five validation companies, and no company may contribute over 20% of a cluster; then the highest TF-IDF cosine silhouette wins. **MMD** between collections of original embedding vectors (Gaussian kernel, bandwidth from the median pairwise squared distance on at most 2,000 training vectors, block-computed) ranks which month pairs and which same-month cluster pairs an LLM should explain, using the V-statistic and explicitly *not* as a significance test. **SAE**: run Llama once per distinct section, count a feature present when at least two source tokens activate, rank features by activation-frequency difference across occupations or months (differences over 3%, up to 200 features), have GPT-6 Luna label each from up to ten activating and ten non-activating examples, and combine labels into at most ten hypotheses per comparison. The method comparison holds everything else fixed: GPT-6 Luna writes hypotheses from the same sections with PERC clusters, with SAE features, or with text alone, at the same hypothesis limit and generation budget; GPT-6.1 Terra then judges each characteristic present / absent / unclear in held-out-company advertisements, must return an exact quote for a positive, and sees no occupation, company, month, method or activation information. Sections are matched on format and length (character-length ratio ≤ 1.25), test text duplicating hypothesis-generation text is removed, confidence intervals resample connected company components, and a sign-flip test swaps labels within each component. Corrections are BH and BY, applied per study.

## What counts as an explanation here

Neither an SAE nor a cluster is treated as an explanation on its own. A feature gets a *label* from an LLM reading exemplars, a label is grouped into a *hypothesis* that states a definition plus a predicted difference between two groups, and the explanation is the hypothesis only if the definition is then found in text the proposer never saw. Clusters are not explanations at all — the authors say plainly the clusters organise descriptions for reading and do not establish separate professions. The two artefacts are connected rather than merged: SAE activation rates are computed inside MMD-selected PERC clusters, so a feature's difference can be read against a group the embedding geometry already called different. Coverage is explicitly partial: 10,581 of 45,006 mapped features carry a label.

## How they judged success

Four separate bars, and the paper is careful about which bar each number clears. (1) *Configuration validity*: ARI across seeds, assignment rate, word and vector silhouette, on validation then once on test companies. (2) *Distributional difference*: MMD as a ranking device, with the finite-sample bias of the V-statistic derived (two samples from the same distribution have positive expected squared MMD, bias falling as 1/n plus 1/m) and the authors stating the score is not evidence of significance. (3) *Hypothesis recurrence*: proportion of held-out advertisements where the verifier finds the characteristic, with company-clustered intervals, sign-flip tests, an effect-size floor (absolute difference over 0.01) and BH/BY correction. (4) *Cost*: recorded API spend for generation under each input. There is no human judgement of label or hypothesis quality anywhere, which the limitations section says outright.

## What they found

PERC's best word-separation scores are small (0.064 validation, 0.0765 on test for Role task + Qualifications; vector silhouettes 0.014–0.091), assignment rates 91.2–94.9% on held-out companies, and the paper reads them as substantial overlap rather than sharp role boundaries. The configurable pipeline moves the ranking of embedding models: with PCA-10 and four K-means clusters fixed, MiniLM leads on Role task at 0.0056 while text-embedding-3-small scores near zero; after each model gets its own reduction and clustering search, text-embedding-3-small reaches 0.0443. ARI can be 1.000 purely because PCA plus hierarchical clustering repeats itself on unchanged data, while the same input's test-set word separation halves (Team context + Role task: 0.0116 validation to 0.0061 test) — the paper prints both. On features: "Developing machine learning models" activates in 79.2% of Data Scientist and 71.0% of Machine Learning Engineer Role task sections, "Analysis and reporting" in 85.0% of Business Analyst, "Software development" in 61.5% of Software Engineer; Team context rates stay under 20%. The healthcare-sector feature activates in 38/39 advertisements of one Data Scientist Team context cluster and 0/64 of another. Ablating within PERC clusters exposes two confounds the paper reports rather than hides: for Data Scientist Qualifications all ten feature groups activate less in cluster 2 than cluster 0, and the median qualification section is 55 tokens there against 140 — the feature difference is partly a length difference; and in the Gemma full-dataset analysis the clusters largely separate pre-January-2026 from later postings (89.2% of advertisements matching their cluster's modal posting period), i.e. visible structure is format and era. On the method comparison: claims everywhere (SAE 26 vs PERC 20 vs text 22 occupational claims for the DS/DA and MLE/DS comparisons; PERC 29 vs 24 and 24 for Software Engineer), but verification shows the same occupational contrast under both representations — "developing machine learning models" present in 60/98 Data Scientist against 7/98 Data Analyst task sections when proposed from clusters, 48/98 against 5/98 when proposed from features — while no monthly comparison and no Business Analyst comparison passes correction under either BH or BY. The text-only condition recovers several of the same broad contrasts, so the paper's own conclusion is that the added value of the SAE is unresolved and depends on what you want to examine. SAE inputs cost $17.50 in generation against $0.28 for clusters and $0.31 for text.

## Limits and open problems they name

Almost entirely internship advertising; uneven employer and month coverage with a collection gap between May and August 2026; the scraper back-filled older postings so the 29-month span is not continuous; the seven segmentation inputs contain different advertisement samples, so their ablations do not isolate segmentation on a common set; duplicated recruitment inflates clusters (one repeated graduate-intern campaign dominates a Qualifications cluster); some test postings appeared in earlier exploration; two comparisons exceeded the verifier's context limit and were never run; three quotations and three inline source ids needed correction across 81 responses; checking a quotation shows it appears, not that the verifier read it correctly; and labels and hypotheses have no independent human judgement. Feature ids are checkpoint-specific, and one feature carried different labels (including "operational analytics") across comparisons.

## What we take from it

**A warning plus a protocol.** The warning: the same dictionary feature that separates two groups by 38/39 against 0/64 is also separating 55-token sections from 140-token sections, and a cluster that looks like a semantic type can be a posting era — so a feature-difference read-out we publish needs a length/format-matched comparison beside it. The protocol is worth copying wholesale and costs a second model, not a second study: identical input sections, one hypothesis-writer under matched budgets, a verifier that sees the definition but neither the label nor the group, exact-quote requirement for a positive, company-clustered resampling, and BH/BY per study. Two further habits: print cost next to claim count ($17.50 vs $0.28 is a result, not an implementation detail), and treat the number of claims as a quantity that cannot establish quality — they say so explicitly. **A measure**: MMD as a *selector* with its bias derived and its non-significance stated is a clean template for choosing which comparisons deserve a reader's attention when you have thousands. **A framing**: when a capable reader model already recovers the result from raw text, the interpretability artefact's contribution has to be argued on something other than the finding.
