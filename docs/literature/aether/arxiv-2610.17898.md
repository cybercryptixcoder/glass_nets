# Evaluating the Causal Faithfulness of SHAP as a Candidate Attribution Method for SAE Features

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.17898
- **Authors / organisation:** Samuel Xie (Independent); Noura Al-Moubayed (Durham University)
- **Date:** 2026-10-13
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17898
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Sparse autoencoders are the field's standard way to cut a residual stream into named features, but the evidence attached to those features is almost entirely correlational: which examples activate a latent, plus a label written by a human or by another model. The authors want to know whether an *attribution* score over SAE latents — a number that claims to say how much each latent drives the output — survives contact with an actual intervention. Their larger goal is to fix the evaluation standard: they argue that agreement between attribution methods has been allowed to stand in for causal validation, and they set out to test that substitution directly. It is a deliberately small, heavily instrumented study aimed at the measurement rather than at a new method.

## What they studied
A frozen GPT-2 Small (124M) with a 32,768-latent SAE on layer-7 residual-post activations, on SST-2 binary sentiment classification. The unit of analysis is the SAE latent feature, and the outcome is an intervention effect at the same layer measured through the model's own last-token logit difference between the tokens "wonderful" and "awful". Two "arms" run the identical pipeline: a linear arm (L1 logistic regression over the full 32,768-dim activation, which leaves roughly 1.4k non-zero coefficients) and a nonlinear arm (a one-hidden-layer MLP of width 32 over a filtered subset of 116 features). The MLP arm exists for a specific reason — with a linear probe the gradient is constant, so Integrated Gradients, Gradient×Input and interventional SHAP collapse to the same closed-form quantity, and no method comparison is possible.

## How they did it
Data is split once, by seed, into probe-train (47,144 rows), probe-val (10,102) and held-out (10,103), and every attribution and every intervention is computed on the held-out split, so the comparison between a predicted score and a measured effect is not scored on the same data that fitted the probe. All evaluation rows are prefixes of a single seeded permutation, which makes the 100-row local sample literally the first 100 rows of the 2,000-row global sample. Three attribution methods are computed per arm (SHAP, Integrated Gradients, Gradient×Input) plus a zero-cost baseline (raw probe weights, or probe weight-row norms for the MLP), all explaining the same scalar target: the log-odds margin for the linear arm, the pre-sigmoid logit for the MLP. Causal ground truth is produced by hooking layer 7 and editing one latent: ablation sets it to zero; steering adds a signed, per-feature scaled increment at every token position. Both are baselined through an encode-decode pass with no edit, so SAE reconstruction error is subtracted out rather than absorbed into the effect. Faithfulness is then the rank correlation between per-feature attribution magnitude and per-feature measured effect magnitude (importance), and between signed attribution and signed effect (direction). A local analysis repeats this per sentence over the union of that sentence's top-20 local and top-20 global features. A known-ground-truth experiment builds minimal pairs — delete the single sentiment-lexicon word from a sentence, or append a sentiment clause to a neutral one — where the direction of the true effect is known by construction, and asks whether each method recovers the features whose activation actually changed. Checks include an activity control (restrict to features the sentence actually activates, or partial out activation strength continuously), a sign-stratified selection check, two steering-magnitude modes, and a ten-seed replication that pools candidate draws into one intervention pass and slices them back out.

## What counts as an explanation here
An attribution score per latent feature: a scalar that is supposed to say how much that feature contributes to the model's decision on this prompt. The understanding is meant to be quantitative and predictive in a narrow sense — it should rank features by their causal influence so that the top-ranked ones are the ones whose removal or amplification actually moves the logit. The paper's central move is to split what such a score can mean into two separable properties and show they come apart: *importance* faithfulness (does the magnitude of the score track the size of the effect) and *directional* faithfulness (does the sign of the score track the sign of the effect). It is explicitly a validity study of an explanation form, not a proposal for a better one.

## How they judged success
Spearman ρ between attribution and intervention effect, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction within arm; one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with bootstrap confidence intervals for the multi-seed sweep; paired Wilcoxon tests on per-example local-minus-global gaps; AUROC for recovering the set of features whose activation changed in the known-ground-truth pairs, against a 0.5 chance baseline; sign accuracy of the readout itself as a precondition check. Controls are unusual for their specificity: random distractors instead of top-global-SHAP distractors (which would force SHAP's AUROC toward zero), a training background instead of the pair's own activations (which would tautologically inflate AUROC), a closed-form identity check in the linear arm reported as implementation verification rather than as an empirical result, and a lexical bag-of-words baseline to test whether the probe is just spotting sentiment words.

## What they found
Most of what they measure fails. In the fully powered evaluation over all 116 filtered features, only 4 of 8 linear-arm tests and 2 of 7 MLP-arm tests survive FDR correction, and across the ten-seed sweep none of the pooled tests survive (0/80 linear, 0/70 MLP). The methods that agree strongly with each other do not agree with causal effect: in the MLP arm IG, GA and SHAP form a correlated cluster (ρ between 0.42 and 0.80) while their agreement with intervention effects is weak, and probe weights — the zero-cost baseline — correlate with none of them (ρ at or below about 0.04). The reverse also holds: probe weights were the *only* linear-arm method with strong directional faithfulness (ρ = 0.410), while every gradient or SHAP method's directional faithfulness was near zero and did not survive correction, even though those same methods did carry moderate importance faithfulness (ρ between 0.256 and 0.279). Local per-example attribution beat global averaged attribution on direction in both arms, and that advantage survived both the active-only restriction and continuous activation control; its advantage on importance did not — restricting to active features removed it in the linear arm and reversed it in the MLP arm (global +0.371 versus local +0.077). Under known ground truth the asymmetry is sharp: attribution recovers inserted sentiment well above chance (local SHAP AUROC 0.792) but is at chance for deletions (0.520 local, 0.509 global), despite the readout registering the deletion correctly in 77% of pairs. They read that as deletion redistributing activation across many features, each moving too little to be ranked.

## Limits and open problems they name
One model, one SAE, one layer, one task — they state that generalisation is open. Attributions are computed at the last token while interventions edit every position (they add a last-token-only steering condition, which *raises* importance faithfulness and recovers MLP significance, incidentally confirming the scope mismatch is real). Steering magnitude was calibrated against a layer-11 probe's probability and evaluated against the logit difference, a mismatch they flag as non-trivial. Faithfulness correlations run over small feature counts; they note that a single 20-feature draw is not enough for a claim. The MLP arm's input mask was derived from the validation split, mildly inflating its reported accuracy. Probe saliency for the MLP is magnitude-only and therefore unusable for any directional comparison. They also decline to over-read their own identity result: the linear arm's perfect rank agreement is a closed form, not evidence about the methods.

## What we take from it
A reporting standard and a set of controls, and a hard negative result to cite against anything in our folder that treats attribution as a proxy for causality. First, the separation of importance from directional faithfulness is directly reusable: when our account predicts a feature's influence, the prediction has an effect size and a sign, and they must be scored separately, because here the best method for one was the worst for the other. Second, "attribution methods agree with each other" is now a measured dead end, not a caution — the correlated cluster decouples from the intervention, and the zero-cost probe baseline is uncorrelated with all of it, which is the folder's recurring "the apparatus is not doing what you think" result arriving in the attribution family. Third, the insertion/deletion asymmetry is the sharpest operational warning: an attribution or an ablation that finds nothing on a deletion is not evidence of absence, because the effect can be smeared over many features. Fourth, two design elements are worth copying into any faithfulness harness we build — scoring the explanation against the model's own behaviour rather than against the explainer's target, and baselining through a no-edit encode-decode so the reconstruction error is subtracted rather than counted as effect. Fifth, the negative result is presented with its own power analysis and its own confounds named, which is what makes it usable: near-zero correlation on natural data has two readings (the method is bad, or the harness is insensitive) and they build the known-ground-truth arm specifically to tell those apart.
