# They Infer What You Meant: Models Represent Communicative Intent More Reliably Than They Act On It

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.03598
- **Authors / organisation:** Alex Kwon; independent researcher (17 pages, 3 figures; preprint)
- **Date:** 2026-07-03
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03598
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Deployed assistants routinely answer a message's literal content and miss what the sender was doing by sending it: shown a finished project, they offer improvements; sent a raw late-night line, they run a risk assessment. The author wants to know whether that failure is a failure to *understand* or a failure to *use* what was understood, and picks Gricean speaker intent as the object to ask it on. The larger goal is to take a feature that the field has only benchmarked behaviourally and put it through the full represent / read-out / intervene chain, so "the model doesn't get it" can be replaced by an answer about which of those three links breaks. A second goal is procedural: the paper reports its nulls, its failed pre-registration and its in-sample layer selections as prominently as its positive results, and argues the nulls are the load-bearing part.

## What they studied
Seven instruction-tuned models across four families — Qwen2.5 at 3B, 7B, 14B and (probe-only) 32B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B and Phi-3.5-mini — plus the base checkpoints of Qwen-3B and Llama-8B. The unit of analysis is the residual-stream hidden state at the last token of the generation prompt, swept over layers; the derived objects are a logistic-weight direction, a difference-of-means direction, and a behaviour axis fitted on replies alone. Stimuli are 60 shared objects crossed with eight alternative phrasings of each of two intents, recognize (wants the thing acknowledged, no critique invited) versus evaluate (wants it assessed), where the intent is set only by a prefix clause and the probed suffix is byte-identical within a pair.

## How they did it
The probe is standardised, PCA-reduced (at most 40 components), L2 logistic regression on last-token activations, scored by leave-one-phrasing-out GroupKFold so train and test never share a wording; the controls are a shuffled-label permutation ceiling, a TF-IDF bag-of-words classifier under the identical folds, a leave-one-object-out split, a deliberately leaked variant with lexically distinct prefixes (which hits 1.00 at every layer, including the earliest, showing what a surface artifact looks like), and a request-matched variant where both classes carry an explicit directive. Behaviour is scored by a feedback-offer lexicon with a coherence guard, cross-checked against an independent sentence-embedding classifier and against three blind human raters on 60 shuffled replies. The causal arm builds a direction from either difference-of-means or logistic weights, injects it at one decoder layer at the last position scaled by a coefficient times that layer's mean activation norm, and requires a sanity gate: steering toward evaluate must produce more feedback than steering toward recognize at the same coefficient, and failing layers are not read. Specificity is scored as a behaviour separation S over 24 items against 12 shuffled-label directions and 48 norm-matched random directions, plus an opener-token-biasing control (steer the first-sentence opener unembedding axis directly, and recompute S with each reply's first sentence stripped), plus a behaviour-axis control (fit the direction on feedback-versus-acknowledgment replies alone, ignoring intent labels, and take its cosine with the intent direction). Localisation sweeps probe accuracy and steer recovery layer by layer on two models, and ablation removes late-layer attention heads one at a time.

## What counts as an explanation here
A decomposition, not a model: the paper splits one feature into a representation (a linearly decodable direction present from pretraining) and a read-out (whether the default generation routes it), and localises the discard to a depth window past which steering starts to work. The positive predictive content is the ordering claim — representation first, routing later — plus a usable causal handle, a single layer-localised direction that recovers the behaviour. The explanation is explicitly partial: it says nothing about why three of six models route the intent and three do not, and the author refuses to read the stratification as a capability law.

## How they judged success
Balanced probe accuracy against a shuffled-label permutation ceiling and a bag-of-words baseline under grouped cross-validation; behavioural honouring rate at n = 60 with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, where the criterion for a real effect is *non-overlapping* intervals between default and steered; a monotone dose-response curve with disjoint baseline and full-dose intervals; behaviour separation S against the full distribution of 12 permutation directions and 48 norm-matched random directions with a permutation p-value; human-agreement statistics (Cohen's kappa, Fleiss' kappa, attention checks); and a nested split by object where direction, layer and coefficient are chosen on a dev half and recovery is measured on the disjoint test half.

## What they found
On Qwen2.5-3B the probe decodes stated intent at 1.00 with bag-of-words at chance (0.48), and rises with depth where a leaked surface signal is flat-high from the first layer; the base (pre-instruction) checkpoints decode at 0.99, so the feature is inherited from pretraining rather than created by instruction tuning. Across all six comparison models the probe reads 1.00 with bag-of-words between 0.46 and 0.48, and the request-matched control still gives 0.90–1.00 against a bag-of-words 0.69. Inferred intent — set only by an implicit frame, with no word naming the preference — decodes at 0.87–0.95 across six models and 0.93 on the 32B; crossing intent against valence shows the intent direction near-orthogonal to the valence direction (cosine 0.08–0.16) and a probe trained on the warm cells still decoding the held-out neutral cells at 0.63–0.83. The discard is model-specific: Qwen-3B, Qwen-7B and Llama-8B honour recognize-intent 0.57–0.65 by default and 0.85–0.98 under steering with disjoint intervals, while Qwen-14B (0.82), Mistral-7B (0.88) and Phi-3.5-mini (0.93) already sit near the ceiling and steering moves them within overlapping intervals. The steer layer is not a fixed fraction of depth (0.57, 0.59, 0.69, 0.69, 0.83, 0.85 of depth across the six) and a fixed-depth heuristic under-recovers, but an object-nested split keeps the recovery non-overlapping on both models it is run on (Qwen-3B 0.70 to 1.00, Llama-8B 0.53 to 0.87). Under steering, a near-zero cosine separates the handle from the behaviour axis (0.09–0.13 against a random maximum of 0.09) and from the opener-unembedding axis (absolute cosine at most 0.14). The represent-before-route gap is explicit: on Qwen-3B intent is already 1.00 at layer 24 while steering there leaves honouring at the 0.65 baseline, and recovery appears only at layers 28–33, with the logit-lens acknowledgment mass spiking at layer 28. Prompting and steering are interchangeable at the top (explicit intent prompt 0.93, steer with no prompt 0.98, both together 1.00), while a vague nudge backfires, lowering honouring from 0.65 to 0.45.

## Limits and open problems they name
The steer layers for four of the six models are swept on the same items the effect is measured on, and the paper flags those rows as not out-of-sample validated. Difference-of-means versus logistic direction and early versus later layer are confounded, since the factorial crossing them is untested. The across-model stratification is six points with Mistral-7B already breaking the size ordering, no independent capability measure, and a pre-registered geometric test of the pattern (cosine between intent direction and opener axis) that failed its decision rule and is reported as inconclusive. The support-versus-help axis is represented but its steering fails the random-direction specificity control outright (separation 6 and 11 against random maxima 19 and 16, p = 0.31 and 0.14), and a cross-model transport experiment is marked exploratory because the transport map's held-out activation R² is negative and a matched-norm random direction gives partial recovery. Every result rides one binary, non-emotional intent contrast with a soft honouring threshold, the human validation is 60 replies, and the naturalistic test is 23 author-written messages.

## What we take from it
- **Framing (adopt):** the three-link chain is the right skeleton for any feature our white-box model claims to explain — *represented* (probe clears a permutation ceiling and a lexical baseline on held-out phrasings), *routed* (the default output uses it), *recoverable* (a direction at one layer restores the behaviour). A model that reports one link and infers the others is answering a different question.
- **Method (adopt as a gate):** choose the action layer by sweep against a *fixed-depth heuristic as the baseline to beat*, and report both — here the heuristic limped on the 7B until the per-layer sweep found layer 16, so "we picked layer k" is a result only when the trivial rule is also scored.
- **Method (adopt):** the nested object split (fit direction, layer and coefficient on a dev half; measure recovery on a disjoint test half) is the cheap held-out protocol our folder keeps asking for, and it is run on only two of six models here — which is itself the lesson about how often it is skipped.
- **Measure:** the disjoint-versus-overlapping confidence-interval criterion is a better headline than a p-value at this n, and the paper's own weak rows (overlapping intervals, ceiling models) stay in the table rather than being dropped.
- **Warning (sharpest):** a leaked-feature control is worth running *before* the clean one — the deliberately leaked variant scoring 1.00 at the earliest layer is what tells the reader the clean run's depth gradient means something; without it, a flat-high probe and a computed feature are indistinguishable.
- **Warning:** the specificity controls did not all pass, and the one that failed (support-versus-help, random directions as good as the learned one) is the one whose axis was lexically cleanest — read as a reminder that a direction can be firmly represented, behaviourally relevant in trend, and still not a handle.
- **Warning:** the opener-token deflation is the control most likely to kill a late-layer steering result, and it has three separable parts (geometric near-orthogonality, body-only recomputation with the first sentence stripped, and steering the opener axis itself as a positive control); our harness should run all three.
