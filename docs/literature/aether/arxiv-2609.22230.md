# List Counting Failures Are Not One Phenomenon

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.22230
- **Authors / organisation:** Iyad Ait Hou, Saad Mankarious, Aya Zirikly, Rebecca Hwa; George Washington University (Department of Computer Science)
- **Date:** 2026-09-03
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22230
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Counting the items in a bracketed list is the smallest possible version of a task a language model should trivially solve, and open-weight chat models still get it wrong. The standard explanations place the failure in the input: subword fragmentation scatters the units being counted, or attention dilutes as the list grows. Both stories share a strong testable consequence — if the count never forms cleanly inside the model, then different models should fail in roughly the same way, and a late-layer repair found in one model should be a natural candidate to reuse in another. The authors set out to test that package rather than assume it. The larger goal is a general one for anyone building a mechanistic account of behaviour: to show what a *shared surface failure* does and does not license you to infer about shared mechanism, using a task where the true answer is exact and the prompts can be held byte-identical across models.

## What they studied

Ten open-weight checkpoints from five families (Qwen 2.5, Gemma 2, OLMo 2, Llama 3.1, DeepSeek-R1-Distill), with Mistral-Small-24B held out for one transfer check. The core panel is seven instruct models (Qwen 14B/32B/72B, Gemma 27B, OLMo 32B, Llama 70B, R1-Distill-Qwen-32B), each getting the behavioural evaluation and an answer-position residual probe; Gemma 9B and Llama 8B are behavioural-only scale checks within family. Task: list cardinality — return the number of whitespace-separated items inside brackets — under one fixed English instruction varied only in list surface form (five conditions: common words, rare words, single-language, mixed-script, random pseudowords that fragment into roughly four times as many tokens). Released stage checkpoints (Qwen base vs instruct, OLMo base → DPO → instruct) are compared on the same lattice. Units of analysis are three and are kept distinct: the wrong emitted integer, the residual stream at the answer position (for a linear count probe), and a late MLP block's output (for a magnitude intervention).

## How they did it

1,300 prompts are generated once from fixed seeds (20 lists per length for lengths 3–15 across five surface conditions) and shown identically to every model, with a further 200-prompt expanded common-words set (40 per length) for probe work. Decoding is greedy, the first integer in the assistant turn is taken as the answer, and the headline behavioural statistic is the even-minus-odd accuracy gap over lengths {10,12,14} versus {11,13,15} (300 prompts per side, bootstrap 95% intervals), alongside the distribution of wrong integers. To test the input-side story directly they check whether the heavily fragmenting random-string condition is harder (it is not) and, on Qwen 32B, whether the model answers the item count or the whitespace-chunk count when a two-word item makes those differ. For the representation question they cache the last-layer answer-position residual on the 520-sequence common-words set and fit a ridge regressor from residual to count at every layer (alpha = 1, 5-fold CV), reporting round accuracy with permuted-label, leave-one-count-out, majority-class and matched emit-probe controls, plus a length-extrapolation falsifier. For the causal question they run a fixed discovery chain — a logit-lens read, then a per-block attribution of the (correct-minus-wrong) digit margin to attention versus MLP output, then a single-failure sweep over late layers that nominates halving-and-negating the MLP output ("reverse_half") — and then re-run that one intervention on *all* odd-length failures for each model with a selection-corrected split-half protocol (20 halvings, layer picked on one half, scored on the other). They add a continuous scale sweep, class-mean residual steering with a random-direction and a correct-control arm, and a forced-enumeration protocol check. Where released stage checkpoints exist, the same lattice is run on base, DPO and instruct.

## What counts as an explanation here

A two-part diagnosis, deliberately partial. First, a *behavioural* one: each model's failures are characterised as an error mode — a small tuple of operational observables (sign and interval of the even-minus-odd gap, the probability of under-counting given an error, and the mass on its top wrong integers) — which they are careful to call a label and not a family law. Second, a *mechanistic* one for the reference model: a linearly decodable count that is present but not used, combined with a late write that can be partially reversed. Crucially the explanation is explicitly *not* a shared circuit: the paper's claim is that the same observable failure is realised by different mechanisms in different families, and that this is detectable. Nothing here is a predictive model of the network in the sense we want; it is a set of instruments for deciding which stage owns a failure.

## How they judged success

Behaviour: the even-minus-odd gap with bootstrap intervals, accuracy per length and per surface condition, and the wrong-integer mass distribution. Representation: held-out round accuracy of the true-count probe on the subset where the model erred, against a permuted-label floor near 0.077, a majority-class baseline of 0.11–0.26 and a matched probe trained on the model's own emitted integer; plus a length-extrapolation probe trained only on lengths ≤ 10 and tested on lengths 11–15. Causality: selection-corrected held-out fix rates (mean ± SD over 20 split halves) for the MLP-scale intervention, run on the full failure population rather than a hand-picked vignette; a continuous scale sweep reporting even and odd accuracy separately, so a gain on one parity can be seen against the loss on the other; a correct-control steering arm to measure collateral breakage; and a pre-registered rule tested on a held-out model (Mistral-Small-24B, chosen because it shows a parity cliff with diffuse neighbour mass, which the rule says should predict *near-null* recovery). Two honest extras: cells with n = 20 are declared too thin for point estimates (the claims are pooled contrasts), and an earlier eight-failure vignette is explicitly labelled as selection bias and discarded.

## What they found

**Error modes are family-conditioned, not shared.** On identical prompts the even-minus-odd gap runs from +0.40 [0.33, 0.47] (Qwen 32B) and +0.39 [0.32, 0.46] (R1-Distill) through +0.36 (Qwen 14B), +0.24 (Gemma 27B), +0.10 (Qwen 72B) and +0.05 (OLMo 32B, interval crossing zero) to −0.24 [−0.31, −0.17] for Llama 70B, which under-counts. Under-counting is separable from parity flipping: OLMo and Llama have under-count rates of 0.95–0.99 given an error while Qwen 32B's parity errors are mixed at 0.46. Qwen 32B's own grid is spiky at cell level — accuracy 0.05 at length 11 and 1.00 at length 10 — so the pooled gap is the claim and the cells are not. Within-family checks show the label is not a family law: Gemma 9B has a *negative* gap (−0.30) because it collapses onto a fixed mid-range integer, and Llama 8B reproduces the under-count class of Llama 70B.

**More tokens is not harder, on this benchmark.** Every one of the seven models scores higher on random pseudowords than on common words (differences +0.07 to +0.24) despite roughly four times more tokens per item at length 15. The multi-word control fails a chunk-counting explanation too: at even counts answers favour the item count (about 65–78%) over the chunk count (≤12.5%), and at odd counts 85% say "12" at a true count of 11, which is neither. Parity oscillation is also hard to reconcile with a monotone dilution story; the authors are careful to say filler padding is only a capacity check, not a refutation of attention accounts.

**The count survives the error.** A last-layer linear probe reaches round accuracy 0.92–0.98 across all seven models on the same 520 prompts where the models themselves score 0.30–0.89. On the model-wrong subsets the true-count probe holds at 0.85–0.96 against a permutation floor of about 0.07 and a majority-class baseline of 0.11–0.26, while a probe trained on the *emitted* integer reaches only 0.40–0.76 on the same residuals — for Qwen, Gemma and R1 the contrast is stark (e.g. Qwen 32B: true 0.851 on errors, emitted 0.448). They are explicit that the raw "probe right, model wrong" counts track the independence product within 0.02 and are therefore not themselves evidence; the conditional subset controls carry the claim. Timing diagnostics agree: at length 11, after forcing the leading "1", Qwen 32B gives the correct digit probability 0.111 and the wrong "2" 0.885, and logit-lens traces show mid-network correct mass suppressed late.

**The same surface error does not share a repair.** Selection-corrected held-out recovery for the reverse-half MLP intervention is 0.20 ± 0.06 on Qwen 32B and 0.25 ± 0.05 on Qwen 14B but only 0.055 ± 0.022 on Gemma 27B — a near-null result on a model with the same odd-to-even cliff and the same ~0.92 probe. The Qwen result is itself zero-sum: sweeping the MLP output scale lifts even-target accuracy from 0.755 to 0.865 while dropping odd-target accuracy from 0.653 to 0.581, and reverse_half fixes 84 failures while breaking 182. Class-mean residual steering can move all three models (best cliff-fix about 0.33 / 0.43 / 0.28) but only as a trade: Gemma's accuracy on steered model-correct controls falls from 1.00 to 0.35.

**Modes are developmental and protocol-conditioned.** Qwen base checkpoints already show the gap (+0.47 at 32B, +0.22 at 14B) and instruction tuning widens it to +0.77 and +0.53; on OLMo the modal wrong-integer mass on a mid-range integer climbs from 0.07 (base) to 0.17 (DPO) to 0.28 (instruct), and the released SFT checkpoint is unusable because it refuses with non-integer text. Ask paraphrases leave the gaps intact, and a tool-argument framing preserves them; but forced enumeration before the integer abolishes direct-ask failures — Qwen 32B and Gemma 27B both reach 0.98–1.00 on even and odd lengths, and OLMo 32B jumps to 1.00 / 0.97. The held-out prediction fails in an informative way: Mistral-Small-24B, predicted near-null by the concentration rule, actually recovers 0.28 ± 0.06.

## Limits and open problems they name

A high probe score means the count is linearly present, not that the frozen unembedding reads the probe's direction — and the probe does not extrapolate: trained on lengths ≤ 10 it predicts roughly 9.9 for every held-out length, round accuracy 0.00, which is why they describe a bounded-resolution count axis rather than an unbounded cardinality direction. The emit-versus-true contrast is weaker for OLMo and Llama, so the cleanest "knows but will not say" reading is family-conditioned, not universal. "Family" bundles pretraining lineage, tokenizer, chat template and post-training, and they cannot say which sets the mode, nor find any training-document statistic that installs it. Single-cell estimates rest on 20 prompts; the nf4 models are descriptive only; multi-word and filler checks are Qwen-32B-only; mid-generation and multi-turn settings are out of scope. Concentration of wrong mass is admitted to be at best an in-sample correlate of the intervention's usefulness.

## What we take from it

**A method:** the conditional-on-error probe report — accuracy of the true-label probe computed separately on the model-right and model-wrong subsets, printed beside a probe trained on the model's *own emitted output* on the same residuals. That one pairing distinguishes "the information is not there" from "the information is there and the read-out is miscalibrated", and it is cheap. **A measure:** the length-extrapolation falsifier, which converts a claim that a direction *is* the quantity into a claim about a bounded range — any linear readout we nominate should be fitted on a sub-range and tested outside it, because a feature that flattens to its training ceiling is a lookup, not a representation. **A framing:** the three-observable error-mode tuple (gap sign and interval, under-count-shares, top-wrong mass) as the unit of description, which lets a folder say "these two models fail alike" without claiming they are alike inside. **A warning:** a window-free vignette of eight failures suggested a fix rate of ~0.69 on Gemma where the full-failure protocol returned 0.055 — the number we report for any intervention must be computed over the whole failure population, with the layer chosen on a disjoint half. **A shortcut:** selection-corrected split-half layer choice, re-usable for every layer or hyperparameter we would otherwise pick by looking at the score. And the warning the held-out Mistral check supplies: a plausible in-sample predictor of where a repair works did not transfer even at matched size.
