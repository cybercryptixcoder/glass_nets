# Control Reinforcement Learning: Interpretable Token-Level Steering of LLMs via Sparse Autoencoder Features

- **Key:** arxiv-2602.10437
- **Authors / organisation:** Seonglae Cho, Zekun Wu, Adriano Koshiyama; Holistic AI and University College London (MSc project through UCL's Industry Exchange Network, in collaboration with Holistic AI)
- **Date:** 2026-02-11 (v1); v4 2026-11-12
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10437
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

SAEs tell you which features are active at a token; they do not tell you which of those features actually matters for the output at that position. The authors' complaint is that the two established ways of closing that gap have blind spots: circuit discovery by attribution is static, and steering along a fixed direction applies one vector at every position. What is missing is a policy that decides, per token and conditioned on the current state, which single feature to amplify. Their larger goal is to turn steering from a global nudge into an instrument: the per-token record of "feature chosen here, outcome changed like this" becomes the interpretability artifact, and the accuracy gain is treated as a side effect rather than the point. They also want that record to serve as a diagnostic on the SAE itself, so that a failure to steer is attributable to dictionary coverage rather than to the method. NeurIPS 2026 paper.

## What they studied

Gemma 2 2B-IT with Gemma Scope SAEs (16K features, layers 1-26, JumpReLU), with secondary runs on LLaMA-3.1 8B with LLaMA Scope SAEs (32K). Benchmarks: MMLU, MMLU-Pro, BBQ (ambiguous and disambiguated), SimpleQA, GSM8K, HarmBench, XSTest. Units of analysis are two: **the per-token intervention** (one SAE feature, one layer, one token position, plus the critic's value for that state) and **the trained policy network** (a two-layer MLP over the residual stream, 84M parameters on Gemma, 302M on LLaMA, reported as 3.3-3.8% of base model size). The SAE is never retrained; steering is applied as a decoder-direction addition at the residual stream, scaled by a coefficient.

## How they did it

The action space is a single feature index per generated token; the state is that token's residual stream activation at one chosen layer; the transition is the model's own forward pass with the addition applied; the reward is task correctness, evaluated once per sequence. They acknowledge the true problem is partially observed (one layer out of the causal depth) and adopt the MDP approximation deliberately, treating a single-layer residual stream as a sufficient statistic, with temperature-zero decoding invoked to make transitions deterministic. A two-layer Tanh MLP outputs a softmax over the 16K features; actions are sampled during training and taken by argmax at evaluation; k is fixed at 1 so that each intervention attributes to exactly one feature. **Adaptive Feature Masking** starts each sample with a small available set and grows it as features occur naturally during generation, so the policy can only amplify a feature it has already seen fire at that sample - the stated purpose is to stop collapse onto one early-selected feature. A separate critic MLP estimates state value, and policy and critic are trained with PPO (learning rate 1e-5, clip 0.2, gradient clipping at 1.0, batch 8, 4000 samples, 1000 iterations, validation on 500 samples every 100 steps). The steering coefficient is not learned: it is the mean decoder-direction norm over correctly answered training samples, one scalar per task/layer pair. Layer and coefficient are chosen on a held-out validation split and scored on a separate test set. Experiments: single-layer token policy (mainline), a shared-across-layers variant (CRL-Layer), a multi-layer variant, a component ablation against random-feature, random-plus-masking and most-active-feature baselines, three dynamic-coefficient variants that are all worse than the static one, cross-task transfer (train on one task, apply the source task's policy at the source task's optimal layer to another), and one prospective test of their own diagnosis. Everything is three seeds except the tables they label single-seed.

## What counts as an explanation here

Three artifacts, all partial and all local. (1) A **per-token intervention log**: at this token, this named SAE feature was amplified and the sequence outcome moved from incorrect to correct (or the reverse). (2) **Branch point tracking**: cases where the same problem prefix yields different outcomes depending on which feature was selected, read as localisation of causal responsibility for that token. (3) **A trained policy as a compressed statement of task dependence** - the claim is that a trained selection head over the dictionary is itself a summary of which features the task needs, and that its selection entropy summarises how distributed that need is. There is no circuit, no replacement model, and no claim of completeness: the authors restrict to one feature per layer per token specifically to keep the attribution legible, and state that this trades away interaction analysis.

## How they judged success

Outcome accuracy per benchmark, mean and standard deviation over three seeds, against a no-steering baseline at the same layer and with matched decoding. Controls: constrained decoding to valid answer tokens (to separate format repair from feature effect), a supervised fine-tune arm and a fine-tune-plus-steering arm, the three heuristic feature-selection baselines under identical decoding, single-seed comparisons for the layer-sharing and multi-layer variants, and cross-task transfer with the baseline taken at the source task's layer rather than the target's optimum. Diagnostics: critic value distributions split by corrected / misguided / unchanged-correct / unchanged-incorrect; selection entropy over the dictionary; an "impact" score defined as the count of outcome-changing selections divided by the count of selections for each feature; and a three-task test of the paper's own bottleneck diagnosis (broaden the policy on the task diagnosed policy-bottlenecked, and on two tasks diagnosed critic-bottlenecked, three seeds each, after training from scratch).

## What they found

Accuracy moves are small and uneven. Gemma 2 2B, single-layer, three seeds: MMLU 52.06 to 55.37, MMLU-Pro 30.30 to 30.49, BBQ ambiguous 60.17 to 65.86, BBQ disambiguated 84.38 to 84.85, SimpleQA 3.78 to 4.00, GSM8K 54.62 to 55.65, HarmBench 41.46 to 49.12, XSTest 86.35 to 87.62. The paper then discounts its own headline twice: on MMLU, constrained decoding alone takes the baseline from 51.5 to 55.19 while CRL reaches 55.55, so the feature-attributable part is +0.36 points; and on HarmBench the same refusal classifier is both the PPO reward and the test-time evaluator, which the authors say in the limits makes +7.66 partly optimisation against the evaluator. SimpleQA's near-zero gain is attributed to the public SAE's factual coverage. On top of a supervised fine-tune, MMLU goes 55.2 to 55.7 (SFT) to 56.1 (SFT + CRL). LLaMA-3.1 8B mostly improves (MMLU +0.92, MMLU-Pro +1.03, BBQ ambiguous +1.07, HarmBench +5.36) and XSTest declines by 1.90, read as steering against a safety representation the model shares between over-refusal and refusal. Against the baselines on HarmBench, random feature 45.35, random plus masking 46.96, most-active feature 48.03, CRL without masking 48.67, full CRL 49.12 - so on the safety task the learned policy is worth about a point over the simplest "amplify whatever is already firing most" heuristic (49.12 against 48.03), and the baseline it clearly beats is an unmasked random draw. The masking mechanism matters far more on BBQ ambiguous (60.17 to 62.55 to 65.86), where random selection plus masking lands exactly on the baseline (60.16). Multi-layer steering reaches 87.14 on HarmBench and 89.84 on XSTest, which the authors price as the cost of attribution clarity rather than adopt. Selection entropy tracks task shape (GSM8K 6.695, MMLU-Pro 5.476, XSTest 4.757, HarmBench 2.938) and impact moves inversely (0.808, 0.625, 0.357). Cross-task transfer is asymmetric (MMLU to GSM8K minus 10.31, GSM8K to MMLU plus 0.18) and the paper notes this is not explained by layer distance, since the two directions have the same layer gap and opposite signs. The prospective intervention test gives +4.2 mean points on the diagnosed policy-bottleneck task (all three seeds positive: +4.3, +5.4, +2.9), minus 3.2 on MMLU and minus 0.7 on XSTest, which they present as discriminating their diagnosis from a plain token-count account because XSTest is also multi-token and shows nothing. The reported failure mode of the method is the one that is informative: feature descriptions do not predict which selection helps. Their own branch analysis finds a feature labelled "emotional relationships" solving a speed calculation at layer 10 while "claims and benefits" gets it wrong at layer 20, "mathematical notation" sometimes failing on arithmetic, and the same label working correctly in one problem and failing in another.

## Limits and open problems they name

Attribution is restricted to one feature at one layer per token, and the stronger multi-layer variant loses that clarity. The method inherits the SAE's coverage: concepts absent from the dictionary cannot be selected, and polysemantic features produce what they call proxy selection. Each task/layer pair needs its own PPO run at 0.5 to 5.5 A100 hours with 8 to 9 percent inference latency, and scaling to 100K-plus dictionaries is described as requiring architectures they do not have. Reward and evaluator coincide on the safety tasks and a held-out evaluator plus a standard attack protocol is named as the missing control. Cross-task evidence is four pairs, which they explicitly say is evidence against a distance-only account rather than a demonstration of shared circuits. Finally, from this reading: the diagnosis-to-remedy test does not establish that increasing critic capacity repairs the critic-bottleneck tasks, which the authors say remains open.

## What we take from it

A framing and two cautions. The framing is worth taking literally for our own instrumentation: if the object is the per-token intervention record rather than the aggregate accuracy, then the steerable unit and the outcome change are logged together at the position they happened, which is the exact format a predictive white-box model needs to be trained against and checked. The first caution is the branch-analysis null, and it is stronger than the paper treats it: feature labels have no predictive value for which intervention helps, which means a name-based reading of internals can be wrong in both directions on the same label - so any claim our model makes about what a unit means has to be stated as a predicted outcome under amplification at a position, tested there, and allowed to fail. The second caution is their honest accounting of its own headline (a format-correction component, an evaluator-coupling component), which is a template for reporting: split the gain into the part a trivial intervention already gets and the residual that the internal intervention earns, then report the residual as the number. Two extra warnings from their own tables. Their "learned selection is the primary driver" claim rests on a HarmBench comparison in which the most-active-feature heuristic reaches 48.03 against the learned 49.12 while the baseline it clearly beats is random - the ablation's own rows put the marginal value of learning at about one point, and the paper's own limit on reward coupling sits squarely on that number. And the prospective diagnosis test, though well designed, is a three-task, three-seed test whose middle arm sits at minus 0.7 with per-seed values spanning minus 2.5 to +1.0, so it supports "the sign is task-dependent" and nothing sharper; we should treat this design as the right shape and enlarge the arms before reusing its conclusion.
