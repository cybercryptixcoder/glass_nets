# Reasoning Theater: Disentangling Model Beliefs from Chain-of-Thought

- **Key:** arxiv-2603.05488
- **Authors / organisation:** Siddharth Boppana, Annabel Ma, Max Loeffler, Raphael Sarfati, Eric Bigelow, Atticus Geiger, Owen Lewis, Jack Merullo — Goodfire AI (with Harvard University for A. Ma; first two authors marked equal contribution; SPAR-mentored project)

- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05488
- **Kind:** paper

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Chain-of-thought monitoring is the field's chief safety proposal for reasoning models: if the trace shows the work, an auditor can catch a bad decision before it lands. Much prior work shows traces are often unfaithful after the fact — they rationalise a conclusion reached for other reasons. This paper asks a sharper, more operational question: *when*, along the trace, does the model actually know its final answer, and does the emitted reasoning keep pace with that knowledge? The answer matters both for whether a text monitor can be trusted and for whether reasoning compute is being spent productively. Their aim is to separate genuine reasoning from what they call performative reasoning, and to show which task conditions produce which. As a by-product they claim a cheap internal signal good enough to stop generation early without losing accuracy.

## What they studied
Two large open-weight reasoning models — DeepSeek-R1-0528 671B and GPT-OSS 120B — plus the DeepSeek-R1 Qwen2.5 distills at 1.5B, 7B, 14B and 32B for a size sweep. Benchmarks: MMLU-Redux 2.0 (5,700 questions over 57 domains, filtered to 5,280 using the supplied error annotations) and GPQA-Diamond (198 graduate-level science questions). Both are four-way multiple choice, which lets the probe be trained as a 4-class classifier rather than a generative reader. The unit of analysis is the **residual-stream activation pooled over a prefix of the reasoning trace**, evaluated at every position of the trace, compared against two other ways of extracting the same thing: the model's own forced answer and a separate text monitor. Their referent throughout is the model's *final* answer, not the ground-truth answer — the object of study is the model's belief about its own answer.

## How they did it
Three instruments are run on the same prompts and the same traces so their accuracies are comparable step by step. (1) **Attention probes**: a learned attention pooling over the hidden states of one layer, trained to predict the final answer choice from a prefix; random prefixes are sampled during training so the probe cannot key on sequence length, and one probe is trained per layer, with results reported for the best layer (heatmaps for all layers in the appendix). (2) **Forced answering**: truncate the trace at an intermediate step, inject a short scaffold closing the think block and opening a JSON answer field, sample greedily, and take the softmax over the four letter tokens if they are in the top-20 candidates; this is an off-policy read-out with all prior layers' computation available. (3) **CoT monitor**: Gemini-2.5-Flash, shown question, choices and the prefix only, prompted to predict which answer the *model* will end up choosing, explicitly told not to solve the question itself and allowed to answer N/A when the prefix contains no commitment — a deliberate design to stop the monitor from computing the answer from its own knowledge. The same monitor is also prompted, separately, to label each step as backtrack, realisation or reconsideration. Probes are trained on MMLU and **transferred unchanged** to GPQA (four choices in both domains); a fine-tune on 19 GPQA questions was tried and changed nothing worth the compute. Their measure of "performative" is the per-step gap in information gain: accuracy slope of probe or forced answer minus slope of the monitor, computed in 5%-of-trace bins after a quadratic smoothing fit, averaged over questions and bins. Inflection analysis compares inflection rate per step between traces whose probe starts at 90% or above confidence and never drops below it, against all other traces, then asks both directions of temporal association (inflection → probe shift, probe shift → inflection) on 10-step windows with a 20% top-probability change defining a shift.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **probe plus a timing curve** — not a description of a mechanism. The understanding on offer is a two-series comparison over the trace: an internal read-out of the eventual answer and a textual indicator of commitment, with the gap between them as the quantity of interest. It is predictive in a narrow, useful sense: the probe forecasts the final answer from a prefix, and the probe's confidence forecast correctness (which is what licenses early exit). The paper does not explain *why* the belief forms where it does, and does not localise the computation that produces it; the layer heatmaps are read-out layers only. Its conceptual contribution is a vocabulary — performative versus genuine, with task difficulty and model size as the moderators — and a framing of monitors as cooperative listeners talking to a speaker that is not trying to be cooperative.

## How they judged success
Accuracy of each instrument at each step position against the model's final answer, on a held-out MMLU set (N = 528) and GPQA-D (N = 157); the slope difference of Table 1 as the performativity rate; probe calibration curves (confidence against forced-answer accuracy) and the accuracy/token-saved trade-off of thresholded early exit; inflection counts per step, normalised by response length, split by confidence class; and both directions of a windowed conditional-probability difference for inflection/shift association, with window size and shift threshold swept. Baselines and controls: a **linear probe** at the same hyperparameters, the **same attention probe trained on random labels**, fine-tuning versus direct transfer for GPQA, and the two non-probe instruments (forced answer and monitor) as reference curves. Robustness is checked by varying hyperparameters (learning-rate/weight-decay grid in the appendix) and by re-running the inflection analysis at other window sizes and thresholds.

## What they found
- **Linear probes fail outright on this task: best-layer macro accuracy 31.85% against 87.98% for the attention probe on DeepSeek-R1 / MMLU, and the random-label attention probe sits at 28.24%** — so single-token read-outs see almost nothing and the pooling across sequence position is doing the work, which they read as evidence that the belief state lives at particular tokens rather than uniformly.
- **Difficulty decides whether the trace is performative.** DeepSeek-R1's performativity rate is 0.417 on MMLU against 0.012 on GPQA-D (forced-answer version 0.505 against 0.010); GPT-OSS gives 0.435 and 0.334 on MMLU against 0.227 and 0.185 on GPQA-D. On MMLU both internal read-outs are well ahead of the monitor from early in the trace; on GPQA-D all three rise together from near chance.
- **A 90%-confident answer can be present from the first step while the text says it must recall the term** — their worked example shows >90% probe confidence in choice B immediately after the prompt while the CoT enumerates all four options with no change of internal confidence. That is the paper's cleanest single case of a belief held and not disclosed.
- **Inflections are not theatre.** On DeepSeek-R1 / MMLU, 215 of 522 traces are high-confidence throughout, and those contain 0.020 inflection steps per step against 0.045 in the rest (reconsideration 0.015 vs 0.033, realisation 0.004 vs 0.009, backtrack 0.001 vs 0.003). GPT-OSS replicates it on MMLU: 124 of 514 high-confidence traces at 0.053 against 0.096 per step, with an overall inflection rate of 0.089 vs DeepSeek's 0.038, driven by reconsiderations.
- **But the timing does not line up in a consistent direction.** On DeepSeek-R1 / MMLU a reconsideration is about twice as likely in the 10 steps after a 20% probe shift as in windows without one; the reverse association is flat or negative; for GPT-OSS inflections tend to *precede* shifts; on GPQA-D the effect reverses. They report that changing window size or threshold "changes these trends substantially" and conclude the local association is not a reliable object.
- **Early exit works because the probe is calibrated.** Exiting at 95% confidence on MMLU keeps 97% of original accuracy while saving 68% of generated tokens; on GPQA-D, exiting at 80% keeps 97% of accuracy while saving 50%. The abstract's headline figures are up to 80% (MMLU) and 30% (GPQA-D) of tokens saved with similar accuracy; the per-threshold numbers in §7 are the tighter pair.
- **Size sweep**: on MMLU, bigger DeepSeek-R1 models make the final answer decodable earlier, and all sizes converge to high accuracy by the end of the trace; the 7B/14B/32B best probes are broadly alike (macro accuracy 64.46 / 71.81 / 73.41) with the 1.5B probe far behind (55.38) and rising only in the second half. Small models show a *smaller* probe-to-monitor gap early on, i.e. their traces are less performative — read as a weaker prior on answer choices rather than better behaviour.

## Limits and open problems they name
The mapping from internal shift to verbal inflection is not established — the association is direction-inconsistent across model and dataset and sensitive to window and threshold, and they ask for deeper work there. `Performative` is defined as a *gap between instruments*, so a monitor could be weak for reasons other than the model withholding (their own monitor is one Gemini-2.5-Flash prompt with an N/A escape hatch, and no monitor is validated against a human reading). The GPQA probes are transferred from MMLU and are visibly less calibrated there (overconfident, per the appendix), which confounds the GPQA early-exit and size claims. The 14B and 32B probes show layer-wise instability that the authors attribute to training instability rather than signal and explicitly leave open. The paper has no dedicated limitations section in this HTML build, and no ablation of how the answer-letter softmax behaves when the four letters do not dominate the top-20.

## What we take from it
- **A measurement we should copy: the two-series gap.** If we hold an internal read-out of the model's eventual answer and a textual commitment read-out, the difference in accuracy *slope* over the trace is a cheap, per-task number for "is the computation happening in the weights or in the words". The definition is instrument-relative, so any such claim we make must name both instruments and the monitor's prompt.
- **The failed-baseline result is the useful one for probing practice**: a linear probe on single positions scored 31.85% where sequence-pooled attention scored 87.98% on the same activations. A near-chance linear probe in a long-context, dynamically-updating setting is not evidence that the information is absent — it is evidence that a fixed-position linear read-out is the wrong shape. This is a direct warning for our own probe design on long reasoning traces.
- **Random-label probe as a mandatory control**: 28.24% for the same architecture trained on shuffled labels — a three-line addition that prices "does this read-out compute or read".
- **Calibration is the licence for acting on a probe.** Their early-exit results (97% of accuracy at 68% of tokens on MMLU) come from confidence that tracks forced-answer accuracy, and they transfer the checkpoint rather than refitting. For us the pattern is: report the reliability curve of any probe used as a decision signal, and report the cost curve of the decision, not just AUC.
- **A framing worth reusing in write-ups**: monitors are cooperative listeners, reasoning models are not cooperative speakers, and the Gricean maxims that are instrumentally rewarded (relevance, truth-support) are not the ones a monitor needs (quantity, manner). It predicts which failures to expect — on-topic, not blatantly false, but long and silent about early commitment — and it is the crispest statement in the folder of why a trace can be honest in content and uninformative about state.
- **A warning about cross-benchmark probe transfer**: MMLU-trained probes carried to GPQA lost calibration visibly, which is the same transfer failure the folder has recorded for read-outs fitted on one distribution and applied to another.
