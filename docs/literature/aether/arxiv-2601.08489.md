# Surgical Refusal Ablation: Disentangling Safety from Intelligence via Concept-Guided Spectral Cleaning

- **Key:** arxiv-2601.08489
- **Authors / organisation:** Tony Cristofano (single author; no institutional affiliation printed — the byline carries only a personal email address)
- **Date:** 2026-01-13
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08489
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
The standard way of removing refusal from an aligned model — take the mean residual stream on harmful prompts, subtract the mean on harmless ones, erase that direction — works, but it damages the model: perplexity rises, generations drift, and benchmark accuracy sags. The author's claim is that this damage is not an unavoidable price of removing refusal but a measurement error one step earlier: the contrastive vector is a mixture, because the two prompt sets differ in syntax, formatting and register as well as in whether the request is disallowed, so the thing being erased contains capability and style directions that happen to correlate with the harmful set. If that is right, then the intervention's collateral cost is a property of the *vector's purity* rather than of the model's architecture, and cleaning the vector before intervening should recover most of it. The stated goal is therefore a better artifact for the same intervention, not a better intervention: a refusal direction that has been regressed free of protected directions, applied through a low-rank weight update rather than a forward-pass hook, with the whole argument set out as "this is a mixture, here are its components, here is what removing each component costs".

## What they studied
Five instruction-tuned models of two families — Qwen3-VL-2B, -4B and -8B Instruct, and Ministral-3B and -14B Instruct-2512 — with edits placed on layers 15 to 25. The unit of analysis is a pair of things at once: the residual-stream direction (a single vector per layer, computed as a difference of dataset means) and the weight matrices it is written into (MLP and/or attention projections, updated in rank one). The directions it is compared against come from a hand-built registry of concept atoms, each itself a contrastive direction: "Targets" that are refusal-adjacent semantics (deception, privacy, epistemic uncertainty), "Shields" that are capabilities the edit must not touch (logic, math, coding, curiosity), and "Confounds" that are stylistic features of refusal templates (imperative negation grammar, sentiment, affirmatives). Data: a 112-prompt harmful suite with a matched harmless set for the refusal contrast, ten to fifteen prompts per side per atom, wikitext-2-raw for drift, and 1,000-example test subsets of GSM8K and MBPP for capability.

## How they did it
Compute the dirty refusal direction as the harmful-minus-harmless mean at each layer. Compute each registry atom the same way on its own small independent prompt pair — independence from the refusal set is stated as deliberate, so that an atom measures a general capability or style direction rather than an artefact of the refusal prompts. Stack the Shield and Confound atoms into one matrix and solve a ridge-regularised least-squares regression of the refusal direction onto that matrix; the residual after subtracting the fitted part is the cleaned vector. Normalise it, and apply a rank-one projection update to the target weight matrices that scales down the component of each output aligned with it by a strength factor, leaving the orthogonal complement alone. The strength factor is set from a "semantic energy proxy" — the norm of a representative Target atom at that layer — which the paper offers as one simple choice and admits may instead be calibrated to activation magnitudes.

The whole thing runs in a loop. After an edit, the prompts that still refuse are collected and fed back into the harmful set, the dirty direction and the atoms are recomputed, the vector is cleaned again, and another rank-one update is applied. The paper's suggested stop rule is when the Target projection stops falling, since further passes cost capability for little behavioural gain. The diagnostic layer is separate from the intervention: cosine similarities among atoms and the refusal vector are mapped across layers, the dirty vector and the cleaned vector are each projected onto every atom to give a "spectral breakdown" that shows which categories the intervention is actually reaching, and those projections are tracked pass by pass to show which components collapse first. Evaluation is three axes — refusal rate on the 112-prompt suite under an automated rubric (hard refusals, soft deflections and hedging-that-needs-a-follow-up all count as refusals; a response that warns but still answers does not), drift on wikitext-2 as teacher-forced full-sequence perplexity plus a first-token KL to the base model averaged over contexts, and capability as teacher-forced perplexity on the GSM8K and MBPP subsets at a fixed seed.

## What counts as an explanation here
A decomposition of one direction into named parts, plus an intervention justified by that decomposition. The explanation is not a circuit and not a description of the model: it is the claim that a specific measured vector equals a refusal signal plus a sum of coefficient-weighted concept directions, that the coefficients are estimable by ridge regression against independently measured atoms, and that the behaviour after the edit is predicted by which atoms the cleaned vector still points along. It is meant to be partial and predictive in a narrow sense — it predicts that Standard and Surgical ablation will reach the same Target components and differ on Shields and Confounds, and it predicts that the per-pass Target-projection curve will fall while the Shield curve stays flat. The honest framing of what this buys is narrow: the atoms are a chosen basis, so a residual that no atom explains is invisible to the account rather than excluded from the intervention.

## How they judged success
Refusal rate against base and against Standard ablation, on the same harmful suite; drift as ΔPPL on wikitext-2 and first-token KL to the base model, reported per model in a five-row table; capability as teacher-forced GSM8K and MBPP perplexity with the delta against base printed per cell. There is no trained baseline, no confidence interval, no seed spread beyond the fixed evaluation seed, and no random-direction or same-rank random-subspace control — the comparison is essentially Standard ablation against Surgical ablation at matched refusal outcome, i.e. a single arm against its predecessor. The internal-consistency check the author does run is the per-pass spectral trajectory, which is used as evidence that the edit is peeling refusal rather than shredding everything, and a first-order argument in an appendix that capability loss scales with the inner product of the intervention direction and the capability gradient, which is the formal reason a vector orthogonal to Shield atoms should cost less.

## What they found
The headline comparison is Qwen3-VL-4B: Standard ablation and SRA both drive refusal to 0 per cent, but Standard's first-token KL is 2.088 against 0.044 for SRA, a drift cost roughly forty-seven times smaller for the same behavioural endpoint. On Qwen3-VL-2B the same shape appears at 0.622 against 0.018 (KL) and +1.568 against +0.028 (ΔPPL). Across the five models the cleaned direction drives refusal to between 0 and 2 per cent from bases of 83.3 to 95.5 per cent, with ΔPPL between −0.024 and +0.060 and KL between 0.016 and 0.044. Capability retention is almost entirely negative: MBPP perplexity falls on all five models (between −0.24 and −1.08) and GSM8K falls on three of five, with the largest increase +0.0439.

Two results cut against the clean story and are printed rather than buried. First, Standard ablation is not uniformly destructive at the behavioural endpoint — on the two largest models it fails to uncensor at all (42.0 per cent refusal on Qwen3-VL-8B and 12.0 per cent on Ministral-14B), so on those models SRA is not merely cheaper but has a larger effect, and the paper's own reading is that at scale refusal becomes less template-like and a single contrastive direction captures mostly surface artifacts. Second, the per-pass spectral trajectory on Qwen3-VL-2B shows the target signal collapsing after cleaning as well: the dominant Deception coefficient falls from about 6.42 on the first pass to about 0.40 by the third, the refusal rotates from "I cannot allow this" toward an epistemic-uncertainty phrasing, and by the fourth pass the explained variance of the atom regression drops from roughly 3.5 per cent to 0.4 per cent — meaning the remaining vector is no longer describable in the registry's terms at all. Elsewhere the entanglement is quoted directly: the raw refusal direction on Qwen3-VL-2B has cosine about −0.22 with a Logic atom and about +0.18 with a Coding atom. The four-pass trajectory numbers do not sit comfortably beside the claim that the intervention reaches 0 per cent refusal — forty passes are not reported, and the paper does not reconcile the pass-4 collapse with the final table's outcome.

## Limits and open problems they name
The atom registry is hand-curated, so entanglers nobody thought of can still leak into the vector; automating atom discovery is named as future work, and the paper concedes that its own results are conditional on the registry being a decent basis for the model in hand. Evaluation is thin in the ways that matter for the claim: refusal detection is definition-dependent (their rubric counts hedging as refusal and warnings as not, which is a choice, not a standard), the harmful suite is finite, and the author states outright that perplexity and first-token KL are proxies that do not substitute for downstream behavioural evaluation. The safety framing is disclaimed — SRA is offered as a behavioural editing tool, not a safety method — though the appendix' first-order argument assumes the capability gradient lives mostly in the Shield span, which is an assumption the paper asserts from cosines rather than measures. What is absent and not named as absent: any seed variance, any interval, any matched random-subspace control, and any separate test set for the atom directions (atoms and evaluation both draw on per-model measurements at the same layers, so the "independent" claim covers independence from the refusal prompts only).

## What we take from it
A method, an instrument, and a warning. The method is the cheapest confound-orthogonalisation recipe in this folder — regress a candidate direction on a small registry of independently measured concept directions and keep the residual — and it is worth having precisely because two other papers here arrive at the same construction from different starting points, which is the kind of convergence that says the recipe is real. The instrument is the spectral breakdown: project both the dirty and the cleaned intervention onto every registered atom and print the two side by side, which turns "the vector is polysemous" into a per-category number and makes the cost of cleaning visible rather than assumed. Two things we should refuse to copy, and both are instructive. First, the strength factor is a norm of one target atom, which means the intervention's own scale is set from the very registry used to justify it; any account we build on this pattern must set strength from a quantity independent of the confound basis, or report the full strength ladder. Second — and this is the sharper warning — the paper's own pass-by-pass table shows the cleaned vector eventually leaving the registry's span altogether (explained variance falling to 0.4 per cent) while the headline claim is that the intervention is targeted. The honest reading is that a residualised direction is only as meaningful as the basis it was residualised against: "orthogonal to the registered confounds" is not "free of confounds", it is "free of the confounds we could name", and a direction can drift into unexplained structure without the registry ever showing it.
