# Data Predictability Shapes Weibull Weight-Scale Growth in Transformer Training

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.23573
- **Authors / organisation:** Tiexin Ding; independent researcher (no institutional affiliation)
- **Date:** 2026-06-27 (arXiv:2608.23573v1)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23573
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1: main body §1–§11, references, Appendices A–E; no figure images inspected)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The magnitudes of a linear layer's weights are close to Weibull-distributed with a shape that barely moves, so almost all the training-induced movement of a model's weight distribution sits in one number, the scale. A companion account had already described that scale's growth under AdamW as the accumulated net of injection, decay and alignment, and had noticed in passing that the trajectory's peak depends on how coherent the training data is, without saying how. This paper asks the follow-up question directly: which property of the corpus sets how much the scale grows? The appeal for us is that the answer is a quantity computed from raw text *before any training runs*, which would make the weight distribution a forward-predictable object rather than a post-hoc summary. The larger programme the author sketches is a response surface over data structure, redundancy, architecture and optimisation hyperparameters, of which this paper calibrates one axis.

## What they studied

Pythia-70m (continued from a released checkpoint for the main law, and trained from scratch for the cross-architecture arm) plus a Llama-style 70m model written for this study, since no official Llama exists at that scale; the two differ in feed-forward (GELU vs SwiGLU) and normalisation (LayerNorm vs RMSNorm), which the author names as a confound he cannot separate. Training budget is 8,000 steps, roughly 98M tokens, batch 24, sequence length 512, weight decay 0.01, warm-up then cosine decay, learning rates from 1e-4 to 1e-3. The data axis is a single corpus (WikiText) progressively corrupted by shuffling a fraction ρ of within-sequence token positions, with ρ ≤ 0.6 treated as the usable non-saturated regime, plus three natural corpora held out (WikiText as in-family reference, C4, and a deduplicated code corpus) at matched ~30M-token budgets. The unit of analysis is the **weights**: one Weibull fit (shape k, scale λ) pooled over the transmission-class matrices — attention output projection and the two feed-forward matrices — excluding query/key/value projections (which the author's earlier work puts in a different, selection-type regime), embeddings, norms and biases. The same fit is also read per block (6 layers × 3 components), giving a model-level and a layer-level granularity of the same statistic.

## How they did it

Estimate the bigram conditional entropy D = H(next | prev) plug-in from token counts of the first 2.4M tokens of each corpus — before training — and measure the struct-retain fraction S (share of adjacent token pairs preserved versus the clean corpus, ≈ (1−ρ)² for positional shuffling). Fix two anchors that are not free parameters: λ₀, the Weibull scale at the first checkpoint (identical across a corruption family because all runs branch from one checkpoint; 0.0232 continued, 0.0177 from scratch), and H_r = 8.0, the value D saturates to at full shuffle under the same token budget. Regress the second-moment growth λ² − λ₀² on (H_r − D)^0.59 with the exponent *held fixed*, so only two coefficients are fitted per learning rate. Test the collapse across 23 runs spanning four learning rates after dividing out those coefficients. Derive the exponent instead of fitting it: fit growth linear in S, fit D as a saturating function of S, eliminate S and the reciprocal of the saturation exponent appears. Then run a forward prediction chain D → S → λ² leaving one corruption level out at a time, and finally apply frozen coefficients to held-out corpora from their measured D alone. Robustness arms: three seeds at the reference learning rate, two architectures trained from scratch on the same seven-level family, eighteen per-block fits, and a step-resolved refit of the law at each recorded training step.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **two-parameter summary of the weight distribution plus a law mapping a pre-training data statistic onto it** — not a mechanism in the circuit sense and not a dictionary. The explanation's object is a population statistic of weight magnitudes (k, λ), and what it claims to explain is why that statistic grows as much as it does. It is meant to be predictive rather than complete: the input D is computed before the run, so the claim is a forward forecast of a training outcome, and the author is explicit that the functional form is derived only *conditionally* on two other empirically fitted relations, and that neither the exponent, the anchors nor the coefficients are first-principles constants. There is a mechanistic story attached (an injection floor and an alignment-efficiency slope traced to a three-force account of AdamW dynamics), but it is offered as interpretation, not as a tested mechanism — the author states outright that the fitted exponents lie between the linear and quadratic scalings single-term intuitions would give and do not constitute a quantitative test of that account.

## How they judged success

Five distinct measures, each with its own baseline. (i) Per-learning-rate fit quality of the law, R² ≈ 0.82, reported *explicitly as the weaker number* whenever the collapse is headlined. (ii) The learning-rate collapse: after normalising by the two per-η coefficients, 23 runs regress on the parameter-free identity with slope 1.00, 95% CI [0.89, 1.11], residual σ = 0.13, 95% prediction band ±0.27, R² = 0.941, and the paper's defence of this number is that the four η families *interleave* rather than forming separate bands. (iii) The end-to-end self-validation: leave-one-out over seven corruption levels, blind RMSE 0.092×10⁻⁴ against in-sample 0.054 (overfitting ratio 1.71), reported as 5.7% relative error **together with** its worst single point, 12.6% at the clean end. (iv) Cross-corpus held-out error: wikitext −0.12 (in-family consistency check), C4 −0.08 (genuinely held out), code −0.499 (about 6× the LOO error). (v) Seed and architecture robustness: coefficient of variation 3.9% across three seeds, and the same functional form fitted separately to Pythia (R² = 0.83) and Llama-style (R² = 0.81). Controls worth naming: a through-origin test on the training-side fit gives R² = −1.9, which is how the non-zero injection floor is shown to be real rather than assumed; D-estimation noise is bounded at standard error < 0.011 bits across twelve disjoint 2.4M-token subsamples with a moving-block bootstrap agreeing; the fitted shape parameters of the two architectures are matched (k = 1.204 vs 1.207, second-moment conversion ratio 0.997) so the coefficient difference cannot be a ruler artefact; and the pooling check compares the fitted pooled coefficient against the parameter-count-weighted average of per-block slopes (agreement to 10⁻¹⁵) against unweighted (0.63) and summed (15.1) alternatives. Two tests exist but are not run: re-estimating the saturation exponent at a different token budget, and a single-factor swap isolating SwiGLU gating from RMSNorm.

## What they found

The law holds. Growth λ² − λ₀² fits C₀(η) + C₁(η)·(H_r − D)^0.59 across four learning rates spanning an order of magnitude, with the two coefficients following power laws C₀ ∝ η^1.55 (R² = 0.996) and C₁ ∝ η^1.75 (R² = 0.994) over a four-point grid that the author flags as "a scaling description, not an established law" because two of the four learning rates are single-seed. After removing the coefficients the 23 runs collapse onto the parameter-free curve at R² = 0.941. The convexity is inherited rather than fitted: growth is linear in retained structure (R² = 0.945), bigram entropy saturates against retained structure with exponent 1.691 (R² = 0.9999), and composing them fixes the law's exponent at the reciprocal, 0.59. Held-out within-family prediction is 5.7% relative error, and the error decomposition is the interesting part — the full D→S→λ² chain matches the training-side-only rung to three decimals, so the whole residual lives in the linear-in-S training-side fit, largest at both ends of the corruption axis where the omitted curvature is strongest. Cross-corpus it predicts mapping-rich text well (C4 within −0.08) and over-predicts code by 0.50 in magnitude, about six times the within-corpus error. Per layer, the same law fits with median R² = 0.84 while the shape stays nearly constant across blocks (1.16–1.21) — so data moves the same kind of distribution everywhere and the differential response is entirely in the scale, concentrated in the feed-forward blocks. Across architectures the form and exponent survive and only the coefficients move: the gated Llama-style model's slope is 0.78 against Pythia's 0.45, with per-module decomposition putting SwiGLU feed-forward at roughly twice the data sensitivity of GELU feed-forward and responding at every depth rather than only in deeper blocks, while Pythia carries more of its response in the attention-output projection. Step-resolved refits hold at R² = 0.91–0.99 through the stable phase with one dip to ≈0.52 at the end of warm-up, which the author attributes to the learning rate ramping two orders of magnitude in ~200 steps while the data-coupling slope has barely turned on. A single-seed side observation: weight-scale growth is non-monotonic in repetition factor, rising to a peak near eight-fold repetition and falling back below baseline by thirty-two-fold.

## Limits and open problems they name

Eight, stated in a dedicated scope section, and unusually of the paper's own making. The anchor H_r is an estimator ceiling at a fixed token budget (8.0 bits), not the information-theoretic marginal entropy (10.7 bits), because a plug-in estimate over ~10⁶ samples in a ~10⁹-cell bigram space is biased low. The law is within-corpus and (H_r − D) is not a cross-corpus currency. The code failure is *not* fixable by renormalising the baseline per corpus, because the plug-in shuffle ceiling is ≈8.0 for every corpus tested — it is set by the token budget — so the author rules out the obvious repair explicitly rather than leaving it as a future-work gesture. Redundancy is the most parsimonious second data axis but rests on a single off-law corpus, and D conflates rich-mapping-low-entropy with redundant-low-entropy text; code's D is also an order of magnitude less stable across subsamples (standard error ≈0.094 bits versus 0.003–0.011 for the corruption corpora). Coefficients are architecture-specific, established at form level with one seed each and a gating/normalisation confound. Model and training scale are small and deep inside the transient regime — 70M parameters, ~10⁸ tokens, against a weight-decay relaxation timescale τ ≈ 1/(η·λ_wd) that far exceeds the run. The plug-in entropy estimator saturates above ρ ≈ 0.6, which bounds the usable range. And the correlations along the corruption axis are explicitly *not* claimed as causal mediation: what is established is a controlled predictive relation, not a causal one. Separately, Appendix B.4 concedes that the self-validation is internal — it shows the chain is self-consistent and predictive inside the tested setting, not that it is the unique correct model — and Appendix C.1 concedes that S and D are two images of the same knob, so their relation is "a designed co-variation, not an independent discovery". The one dependence the author says he cannot close is the exponent's token-budget sensitivity, since H_r enters the saturation fit.

## What we take from it

A **method plus a warning plus a framing**. The method: a distributional summary of weights (two parameters) can be as legitimate a unit of analysis as a feature or a neuron, provided the summary's stability is established first and the claim is stated in an additive currency — here the second moment λ², because second moments pool and scales do not, which is why the law is written in λ² − λ₀² rather than λ. That is a transferable discipline for any weight-statistic aggregation we do: check additivity before averaging. The framing: a predictor whose input is computable before the thing it predicts is a strictly stronger claim than one fitted to post-hoc measurements, and the D → S → λ² chain is a template for how to decompose such a forecast — score the data-side rung and the outcome-side rung *separately*, because here the data-side inversion added nothing (agreement to three decimals) and the entire error budget was in the training-side linear fit. The warning is the most useful thing in the paper for us: the single off-law corpus is code, whose low entropy comes from redundancy rather than from a rich next-token mapping, so a statistic that looks like it measures structure is really measuring *one kind* of structure, and the boundary is quantified rather than hand-waved (over-prediction by 6× the in-corpus error, and not repairable by baseline renormalisation). Every probe and dictionary score in our folder shares that exposure: a scalar that responds to the target property may also respond to a cheaper, different property, and the corpus that separates them is the test. Finally, the through-origin R² = −1.9 is a nice cheap habit — a fitted intercept is often assumed rather than demonstrated, and one through-origin refit shows whether the floor is real.
