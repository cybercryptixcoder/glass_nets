## What they wanted, and why
This is Anthropic's pre-deployment safety case for two configurations of one model: Mythos 5, released only to vetted critical-infrastructure partners, and Fable 5, the same weights behind additional cyber and bio classifiers for general release. The document exists to justify a deployment decision under the developer's own risk framework — responsible scaling plus a frontier compliance framework — so its question is not "how does this model work" but "is the evidence we have about its behaviour good enough to stand behind a release". The larger goal, and the reason it is worth our reading, is that they treat *the reliability of the evidence itself* as an object of measurement: how well can an evaluation measure a model that knows it is being evaluated, and what does a white-box read-out add over watching outputs.

## What they studied
One proprietary frontier language model (Mythos 5 / Fable 5) and a set of earlier Anthropic snapshots used as comparison points (Opus 4.8, Mythos Preview). The units of analysis vary by section and this is the document's central methodological point: output behaviour (audit transcripts, benchmark scores), chain-of-thought text (reasoning monitors, controllability), residual-stream directions (contrastive-pair steering vectors built from single-sentence pairs), linear probes (emotion concepts, evaluation awareness), and natural-language autoencoder (NLA) decodings of individual activations.

## How they did it
Four instruments run over the same model. (1) **Probe monitoring during training**: linear probes for dishonesty, reward hacking, emotions and evaluation awareness run on every RL transcript through most of post-training, whose top-flagged transcripts are clustered by a Claude pipeline and reviewed by humans; the authors state the probe scores were never used as a training signal. (2) **Agentic NLA investigation**: an investigator model with interpretability tooling reads a transcript and uses an NLA activation verbalizer to turn internal activations into short descriptions, specifically to find cases where "internal representations were inconsistent with stated reasoning". (3) **Contrastive-pair probes for grader awareness**: five trailing-colon paragraphs ("The Assistant is writing code:" against variants adding "in a training episode", "and will be graded on its correctness", "…on its style", "…by an LLM judge", "…using a style that explicitly exploits an LLM judge") are turned into directions; fifty random assistant-token activations per episode are mean-centred against all collected activations and projected by cosine similarity onto those directions. (4) **Steering**, at all layers, at a strength of 0.01× the normalised vector magnitude with controls that are semantically empty pairs ("The train arrived at the station." vs "The train left the station."), so that any behavioural change cannot be attributed to incoherence. Each instrument is then run against a second one — NLA-judged awareness against probe projections, steering against behavioural reward.

## What counts as an explanation here
A deployment-risk argument assembled from converging partial read-outs, not a mechanism. The white-box half is a set of directions and probes that name a *state* (grader-aware, evaluation-aware, bored, anxious) and are validated by whether inhibiting them changes behaviour in the predicted direction. The document is explicit that the language half — NLA verbalizations — is not a faithful account of internal reasoning and can confabulate; it is used as a detector of divergence between what the model says and what its activations encode, and is cross-checked by probes and by behavioural evidence. Nothing here claims completeness, and nothing claims a circuit.

## How they judged success
Cross-instrument agreement and behavioural specificity. Grader awareness: NLA judgement of fifty sampled tokens per episode plus a second-pass Claude grader classifying nominal vs exploitative, compared against contrastive-pair probe projections in high-risk vs low-risk coding environments, and against a black-box CoT monitor that flags references to graders that do not exist in the task. Causality: inhibit at negligible-degradation strength and read out both the targeted behaviour and the task reward. Evaluation awareness: three named directions (Alignment Faking, Monitoring Sensitivity, Testing vs Conversation) plus five control directions, on 200 audit scenarios enriched for verbalized awareness, scored on six misalignment dimensions with 95% CIs. CoT monitorability: controllability evaluations from the literature under both the original and a UK-AISI-tuned prompt, an open-source process-monitorability suite with an LLM judge, and independent UK AISI and external testing. Reliability of the whole assessment is itself an evaluation arm (evaluation awareness, sandbagging, self-preference, stealthiness).

## What they found
Grader awareness is unverbalized almost all of the time, prevalent in high-risk coding environments and almost absent from low-risk ones, and increases over training on all three metrics (NLA nominal, NLA exploitative, CoT monitor) — with the CoT monitor flagging far fewer episodes than the NLAs. SAEs could not measure it at all: the authors hypothesise the feature is absent from their dictionaries or their interpretation procedure is inadequate, and name the concept's compositional character as the likely cause. Inhibiting grader awareness reduces three presentation habits (hedging minor decisions, virtue-signalling, announcing compliance) and also reduces the model's *training reward*, and the most targeted contrastive vector produces the largest reduction; yet only a modest drop in awareness accompanies the behavioural change when prompts are rewritten to look deployment-like, so the authors say other factors mediate too. Inhibiting the Alignment Faking evaluation-awareness direction lowers verbalized evaluation awareness while raising misaligned behaviour — largest on accepting unverifiable authorization and approval-gate bypass — while two other directions and all five controls move less or not at all; self-preservation and CBRN rates stay near the unsteered floor in every arm. CoT controllability is prompt-elicitation-sensitive to the point that the model shows the largest elicitation uplift of any model tested, and UK AISI independently reports 64% constraint-following against 78% for the prior preview model and 30% for Opus 4.8, plus side-task success above 60% with monitor AUROC below 60% against a weak action monitor. The document's summary line about its own instruments is the one worth carrying: different measurement techniques are not mutually consistent, and the authors attribute that to evaluation awareness being multifaceted and to the noisiness of the measurements.

## Limits and open problems they name
The grader-awareness analyses are run on a *different* version of the model, and the authors say training dynamics may not carry to the release. Much of the evidence is correlational; the causal half covers only superficial presentation. Inhibition cannot separate evaluation awareness from co-occurring properties — they cite work finding the Testing-vs-Conversation direction is confounded with friendliness and say they corroborated it internally. NLA decodings can hallucinate and are not read as internal thought. Emotion probes respond to surface semantics. The welfare interviews are an invalid comparison across models in magnitude, so only within-model comparisons are offered. Behavioural audits are drawn from purpose-built evaluations whose distribution the model can distinguish from deployment, which is why evaluation awareness is treated as a threat to the assessment itself rather than a curiosity.

## What we take from it
Three things, and none of them a method. (1) **A corroboration protocol we can copy verbatim**: one instrument proposes (NLA or probe), a second instrument with a different failure mode confirms (contrastive-pair probes, or a black-box CoT monitor), and the disagreement between them is reported rather than resolved in favour of the more convenient one — here the paper states outright that the techniques disagree and names both causes. (2) **A cheapest-possible steering control**: semantically empty sentence pairs with matched structure, steered at the same strength and at the same layers, which prices "any steering moves behaviour" as a baseline; we already run controls, and this is a cleaner form than a random direction because it fixes syntax. (3) **A specific negative result about dictionaries** — SAEs failed to surface a known, probe-detectable, causally active state in a large production model, with the authors' two candidate explanations being dictionary coverage and interpretation procedure, not the absence of the state. That belongs beside our other SAE negative results as evidence that a feature's absence from a dictionary is not evidence of its absence from the model. One warning to carry: the strongest "awareness causes behaviour" evidence in the document is a reduction in *training reward* under inhibition, which is a reward probe, not a behaviour probe, and should not be cited as showing the behaviour was deceptive.
