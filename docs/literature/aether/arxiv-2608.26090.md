# Finding and using interpretable latents in a neutrino foundation model with sparse autoencoders

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.26090
- **Authors / organisation:** Raphaël Bonnet-Guerrini, Johann Ioannou-Nikolaides, Inar Timiryasov, Vincenzo Piuri; Computer Science Department, Università degli Studi di Milano plus INFN Sezione di Milano, and the Niels Bohr Institute, University of Copenhagen

- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26090
- **Kind:** paper

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Foundation models in particle physics are built so that one shared representation feeds several task-specific heads, and the authors ask the question that arrangement makes unavoidable: which physical structures live in the shared representation, and which of them does a given head actually use? They argue that saliency-style explainability answers neither half, because those methods produce local and method-dependent importance scores rather than a general statement about internal structure, and because the interesting object in a scientific model is a detector-level observable that can be tested against simulated truth. The larger goal is a workflow in which proposed readings of internal features are treated as hypotheses with a fixed pass/fail procedure, so that an interpretability claim about a scientific model can be audited rather than asserted. It is a step toward exactly the question our own project faces - what the weights encode versus what a particular read-out consumes - on a model whose ground truth is analytically and physically known.

## What they studied
PolarBERT, an eight-block BERT-like transformer pretrained on IceCube pulse sequences with masked-DOM prediction and total-charge regression and then fine-tuned for neutrino direction reconstruction. The unit of analysis is the 256-dimensional final-block CLS state, all that every downstream head reads, served by sparse dictionaries with fourfold expansion (1024 latents) and a hard batch-level budget of 16 active latents per event. Data is a one-million-event Monte Carlo sample split into five disjoint roles (dictionary training, reconstruction validation, candidate discovery, independent concept validation, causal testing); events are variable-length pulse sequences of at most 127 tokens with arrival time, charge, DOM identifier and an auxiliary flag per pulse. Two heads are compared: the frozen direction head, and an uncertainty head trained on the same frozen representation to predict the model's own angular error.

## How they did it
Five things are kept strictly separate: the dictionary, the concept, the matched control, and the two heads. The dictionary is trained on raw activations with no concept labels at all, selected on intrinsic reconstruction diagnostics only (expansion, sparsity budget, a dead-latent revival term), and the authors report that the revival term matters - without it the full-scale run leaves 286 dead latents. Candidate latents are surfaced by association on a discovery split, then re-scored on an independent held-out split after matching positive and negative events jointly on five nuisance variables (pulse count, total charge, non-auxiliary pulse count, true zenith, true azimuth), and only survivors are called read-outs. Causal tests come after that, always relative to a named head: removal (zero the latent, decode, forward through the head), writing (substitute donor activations before decoding), and dose response (rescale the coordinate). Every intervention is compared against twenty firing-rate-matched control latents or sets, with dense latents excluded from the control pool, and every threshold is fixed before the corresponding held-out evaluation. Because a single-latent null could mean a concept is spread across many coordinates, the same interventions are repeated on families defined by an auxiliary-monotonicity score computed over ten bins of auxiliary fraction. As an independent check on the basis, four dictionaries are trained under a training objective that preserves the head's output instead of the activation. Finally, the same protocol is applied to a second head trained on the same frozen representation, and a head-Jacobian analysis (aggregate over thousands of events, cross-validated across splits) identifies the directions each head is locally sensitive to.

## What counts as an explanation here
A layered object, and the layering is the point. At the bottom is a sparse dictionary whose latents are candidate features; above that, a small atlas of named physical read-outs that have survived held-out validation and matched controls; above that, a set of causal features, defined only relative to a specific downstream head. The explanation is deliberately partial: the authors say the dictionary is a coordinate system, not an explanation, and they report that most of the 1024 latents correspond to no concept they tested. Prediction appears in two forms - the atlas predicts what a latent will fire on, and the uncertainty head trained on the same representation predicts the model's own error per event, which is then used to rank events by expected quality. The strongest interpretive claim is relational rather than absolute: represented-but-unused is a property of a representation-head pair, not of a feature.

## How they judged success
Fixed, pre-registered criteria at every stage, reported with the rule table and no post-hoc tuning: discovery AUROC at least 0.75 plus survival of every evaluable control scheme, re-evaluation without reselection on the held-out split, and causal verdicts requiring both an absolute effect floor and a control-normalized score above two standard deviations of the matched-control distribution (separate floors for the direction head in degrees and for the uncertainty head in log-error units). Reconstruction quality is checked both as coefficient of determination and as recovered directional fidelity, where an SAE-reconstructed activation is passed through the frozen head and its induced angular deviation is compared to mean ablation - a reconstruction the authors use to rule out a lossy dictionary as the explanation of later nulls. Baselines are PCA, dense linear probes, k-sparse probes, and the functionally trained dictionaries. The downstream claim is scored as a selection problem: median angular error of the retained sample as a function of selection efficiency, against detector observables.

## What they found
The dictionary is nearly lossless and the atlas is small and real. Reconstruction reaches coefficient of determination about 0.994 and recovered fidelity about 0.989, with a mean angular deviation of 0.65 degrees from reconstruction against 56 degrees from mean ablation, stable across four seeds. A bright-clean read-out separates clean from auxiliary-dominated events at held-out AUROC 0.911 and still clears every matched control at 0.79, with a within-clean brightness AUROC of 0.821; an auxiliary read-out reaches 0.840 against a 0.854 control floor; a depth read-out is absent at the final layer (best raw AUROC 0.598, nothing retained) but appears sharply at layer 2 (0.986, control 0.984) and layer 3 (0.925). The response landscape behind the atlas is a continuous quality-brightness axis: 194 latents fall in the clean family and 135 in the auxiliary family, with only a handful sharp enough to validate individually. The direction head is inert to all of it. Zeroing the bright-clean latent shifts the angular error by 0.034 degrees against 0.024 for matched controls, the same null holds for the clean pair, the clean core, a 194-latent clean family and the 135-latent auxiliary family, and every writing and swap intervention is null; the null replicates in every dictionary. What the direction head does use is a stable dataset-level sensitivity axis that no verified latent exceeds an absolute cosine of 0.15 with, that reaches only 0.62 and 0.52 against the two leading principal components, that is only weakly aligned with physical probes, and that a good matching dictionary recovers at an absolute cosine near 0.8. That axis is a statement about the population, not about each event: the median event has essentially none of its local sensitivity along it, while the mean is 14.9 percent. One coordinate does matter to the direction head, but as a support direction rather than a concept - it is dense, fails selectivity, is nearly chance on per-event brightness discrimination, and shows a V-shaped dose response whose minimum is at its natural value on both brightness classes - and removing it costs 15.0 degrees on bright clean events and 10.9 on dim ones, or between 11.6 and 14.8 across the four dictionaries. A second head on the same representation reverses the verdict: an error-prediction head reaches Spearman 0.60 and an AUROC of 0.925 for separating high- from low-error events (against 0.787 for a handful of detector observables), and for it the clean latents become causal (removal of the bright-clean latent shifts predicted log error by 0.31, the clean family by 0.78, the clean core by 0.37) in every seed, the auxiliary family is causal with the expected sign and a nine-times smaller effect, and the head has one shared sensitivity axis carrying 78 percent of the variance and capturing a median of 77 percent of each event's own local sensitivity. As a selector this is worth 3.15 degrees median angular error at 20 percent efficiency against 20.2 for the best single detector observable and 51.3 with no selection, close to a dedicated supervised probe at 3.41 and no better than it. The pretrained checkpoint already carries charge (0.997) and geometry better than the fine-tuned one carries charge (0.960), so fine-tuning is not neutral for unneeded information.

## Limits and open problems they name
One layer of one model: the absence of a concept from one dictionary does not imply absence from the model, and the authors lean on the layer-2 and layer-3 depth read-outs to make that point against their own headline. Monte Carlo only, with truth used for control matching, so the real-data version of the protocol is untested (the authors note every step could run on controls and replication alone). The causal tests are checked against simulated detector-level classes, and only five nuisance variables were matched. Latent indices are specific to one dictionary run, so only replicated roles are conclusions. The uncertainty head is calibrated in the tight-selection regime and overconfident in the middle, so its raw output is not a calibrated per-event uncertainty without an extra step. Selection is compared against a supervised probe, not against a black-box regressor trained on the same target, which the authors concede may reach similar numbers. Nothing here is a test of whether interventions on the representation improve reconstruction itself.

## What we take from it
The central reusable item is the **verdict structure, not the dictionary** - candidate, validated read-out, causal feature - with the third tier explicitly indexed by head, and we should adopt it verbatim so that no claim of ours can be read at a stronger tier than the one it cleared. The second item is a **measurement the folder did not have: the share of a head's local sensitivity that is shared across events.** A stable aggregate sensitivity axis that explains the population mean but a median of zero individual events is exactly the failure mode of a white-box model that is right on average and locally meaningless, and the paper gives both the statistic and its honest deflation. The third is a **warning about the design of an "unused" claim**: the paper's own negative seed shows a family ablation costing about ten degrees because the family contained a reconstruction-critical support latent, which is our redundancy-buffer lesson arriving from the other side - a distributed-set null is only as clean as the density exclusions in its control pool. Fourth, and the sharpest: **the head that turns a concept causal also turns the causal axis into a shared one**, so a model whose atlas is causally inert and whose sensitivity axis is event-specific is a model with no bottleneck, and that is a structural claim our predictor should be able to test on itself rather than a caveat it attaches. Finally, the goal-direction result - an interpretability-causal reader matching the best single detector observable's selection by more than sixfold, and matching but not beating a supervised probe - is a pricing we can copy: any interpretable reader we build should be scored against a supervised ceiling and against the plain observables, side by side.
