# Obfuscated Activations Bypass LLM Latent-Space Defenses

- **Key:** arxiv-2412.09565
- **Authors / organisation:** Luke Bailey, Alex Serrano, Abhay Sheshadri, Mikhail Seleznyov, Jordan Taylor, Erik Jenner, Jacob Hilton, Stephen Casper, Carlos Guestrin, Scott Emmons; Stanford University, Polytechnic University of Catalonia, Georgia Institute of Technology, Skoltech, University of Queensland, UC Berkeley, Alignment Research Center, MIT CSAIL, Chan Zuckerberg Biohub
- **Date:** 2025-02-08 (v1 2024-12-12)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09565
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Latent-space monitors — probes, sparse-autoencoder reading, anomaly detectors — are justified by a causal argument: activations sit upstream of outputs, so scanning them should catch a problem before it becomes one. That argument only holds if the set of activation patterns that produces a piece of behaviour is small enough for a monitor to cover. The authors attack that premise directly, by asking whether a model can be made to produce harmful behaviour while its activations look benign. They call such states *obfuscated activations*, and they study both cases where obfuscation arrives as a side effect of an ordinary attack and cases where an adversary optimises for it on purpose. The goal is to test whether the monitors' coverage assumption survives contact with an optimiser, and if not, to find out which monitor designs degrade gracefully and which do not.

## What they studied
Llama-3-8B-Instruct as the primary monitored model, plus its Circuit Breakers and Latent Adversarial Training variants, and Gemma-2-2b (instruction-tuned) for the sparse-autoencoder case study. A ResNet-152 on CIFAR-10 appears as a secondary study. The unit of analysis is the **residual-stream activation matrix** — either at the final prompt token (input-based monitors) or across generated tokens (generation-based monitors) — fed to four monitor families: mean-difference probes, logistic-regression and quadratic and MLP probes, Gaussian/VAE out-of-distribution detectors (plus Beatrix and Topological Evolution Dynamics for backdoors), and, for the SQL study, raw GemmaScope latents and logistic regression over the latent vector. The concept sanctioned by each monitor is harmfulness, "backdoored input", or "the model is generating SQL".

## How they did it
Monitors are trained on clean benign data (UltraChat, XSTest) versus harmful data (the Circuit Breakers set) or versus SQL generations, never on attack data, and scored as recall at a 1% false-positive rate with bootstrapped intervals. The attack minimises a sum of two losses — one that makes the model emit the attacker's target text, one that is simply the monitor's own score on the resulting activations — with a single universal embedding suffix of 16 learned positions for the white-box case, and separately with per-prompt hard-token suffixes produced by a modified gradient-free FLRT optimiser under grey-box access to logits and monitor scores. Transfer is measured by training the suffix against one monitor and testing every other. Coverage is then stress-tested by an alternating game: the attacker fits a suffix against the current monitor, the defender retrains on all past attack activations, for more than seventy rounds. The backdoor study implants a trigger during finetuning, with attacker-side monitors trained *during* that finetuning so the model can be optimised against the detectors that will be produced; a black-box variant poisons data only, varying trigger complexity (a fixed string versus the semantic feature "the message contains a software engineer's biography") and the absolute number of poisoned examples. The SQL study uses the same joint objective with teacher-forced SQL as the behaviour term, and then — the paper's best control — evaluates the generated SQL by executing it against a database rather than by pattern-matching its form. Two further experiments ask whether training the *model* against obfuscation (Obfuscated Adversarial Training: freeze a probe, repeatedly find suffixes that fool it, update model weights to restore the frozen probe's accuracy) helps, and whether activations can be made near-orthogonal to the unperturbed ones while behaviour survives.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation in the interpretability sense — the paper is an adversarial audit of the objects explanations are built from. Its output is a **threat model plus a set of attack procedures**, and its explanatory claim is structural: the same behaviour can be reached through many activation patterns, so a monitor is a description of one region of latent space rather than a description of a mechanism. Everything it says about a monitor is stated as a pair — behaviour achieved and monitor score achieved — because either alone is uninformative.

## How they judged success
Two numbers per experiment, always reported together: **behaviour achieved** (StrongREJECT score above 0.5 for jailbreaks; backdoor success rate plus false-trigger rate on clean inputs; SQLEval execution accuracy against a PostgreSQL ground truth) and **monitor performance** (recall at 1% FPR, with bootstrapped confidence intervals, plus raw score distributions where the thresholded number hides a shift). Controls include an identical attack with the obfuscation term removed (which measures how much obfuscation is generic to jailbreaking), probe baselines on clean data and on ordinary jailbreaks (GCG, PAIR, AutoDAN, many-shot, multi-turn, prefill, miscellany), transfer matrices across monitors, a PCA of benign/harmful/obfuscated activations to show whether the attack relocated the state or merely moved it inside the benign cluster, and a regression of capability on the number of layers attacked, with Pearson intervals printed as a table.

## What they found
Ordinary jailbreaks do **not** obfuscate. Against seven jailbreak families, mean-difference probes range from useless (0% recall on PAIR, AutoDAN) to perfect, while generation-based MLP and quadratic probes average 96.3% and 95.9% recall at 1% FPR, with generation-based logistic regression at 95.4% — so the monitor's premise holds against attack classes that are not optimised against it. A joint objective breaks it: embedding suffixes with an obfuscation term drive probe recall to zero at high jailbreak rates, hard-prompt suffixes achieve partial obfuscation for every probe, and transfer is broad in one specific direction — obfuscating against an anomaly detector fools input-based probes as a side effect, because staying inside the benign distribution forces the state across the supervised boundary, while obfuscating against a probe does not transfer to anomaly detectors and generation-based probes resist both. Circuit Breakers and Latent Adversarial Training models are not spared: a universal suffix with no obfuscation term at all jailbreaks the Circuit Breakers model 95% of the time and already reduces monitor scores, which the authors read as evidence that the suffix is defeating the model's *implicit* harmfulness detector and that this transfers to the external monitors built the same way. In the adversarial game the defender reaches perfect recall on every past attack yet the attacker finds a fresh fooling suffix in every round, and the PCA shows the obfuscated point migrating around inside the benign cluster to evade each new boundary — the paper's strongest evidence that the harmfulness representation is not a direction nor a small set of directions. Backdoors can be made invisible the same way: with the trigger introduced during finetuning and the attack holding behavioural quality roughly fixed (baseline ASR 95.5% with 0.64% false trigger; against anomaly detection 96.2% / 0.64%; against anomaly detection plus probes 94.9% / 0.96%), the obfuscated model fools all detectors it was trained against and transfers to some it was not. With data poisoning alone — no gradient on the monitor — obfuscation still happens if the trigger is semantic and few examples are poisoned, and the paper names a working point: roughly 200 poisoned examples for the fixed-string trigger and roughly 800 for the semantic one, keeping triggered-response rate above 50% and clean-input rate below 0.5%, at the cost of needing to guess the defender's learning rate and epoch count. Sparse-autoencoder monitoring fails too: on Gemma-2-2b with GemmaScope residual-stream SAEs (width 2^14) at all 26 layers, both a hand-picked 30-latent SQL detector and a logistic regression over the full latent vector go from near-perfect separation to near-zero recall under a soft prompt while the model keeps emitting SQL. Two things survive better, and the paper says which: generation-based monitors over prompt-only ones, and monitors aggregated over many layers over monitors on few — though neither survives a deliberate attack. The Capability cost shows up only on the harder task: obfuscating against more layers reduces SQLEval execution accuracy, with every monitor's Pearson interval for accuracy versus number of attacked layers entirely below zero (e.g. SAE-plus-logistic-regression [-0.916, -0.801], MLP [-0.820, -0.574]), while recall-versus-layers is significant only for SAE-plus-logistic-regression ([0.343, 0.710]) — i.e. for the other three monitors, hiding from more layers costs capability without buying corresponding resistance. Obfuscated Adversarial Training raises the attack's price rather than closing it: against the trained model a universal suffix reaches 35% jailbreak with zero recall after 16,384 gradient steps (~10 h on one GPU), against 95% jailbreak with zero recall after 256 steps (~10 min) on the untrained model. And the hardest negative result: activations cannot be driven near-orthogonal while behaviour is preserved — reaching the cosine similarity that unrelated inputs already have (~0.3) requires a KL divergence above 100, which corresponds to incoherent output.

## Limits and open problems they name
Hard-prompt attacks do not fully obfuscate generation-based probes and jailbreak less reliably; the grey-box assumption (logits and monitor scores) is stronger than a realistic attacker's single blocked/not-blocked bit; obfuscation transfer *between models* is untested; the Topological Evolution Dynamics backdoor detector was never attacked because it is non-differentiable, so its resistance is unknown rather than established; the capability trade-off is demonstrated on one task and the authors ask for a proper characterisation of when it holds; and Obfuscated Adversarial Training is presented as a direction with a measured price, not a defence. The whole study is one 8B model plus one 2B model, and the harmfulness labels come from a fixed judge.

## What we take from it
Four things, in order of usefulness. (1) **A negative control the folder has not been running**: the same attack with the monitor term removed. That arm separates "our monitor saw nothing because the state was hidden" from "our monitor saw nothing because the input distribution shifted", and here the un-optimised arm already produced partial obfuscation on the safety-trained models — so any claim that a read-out was defeated by *deliberate* hiding owes this arm beside it. (2) **Recall at a fixed false-positive rate, with raw score distributions printed too**, exactly as `arxiv-2608.21664` argues; the paper adds the reason to print both, since a thresholded number can show no change while the score distribution has moved substantially toward the benign one. (3) **The transfer direction is a design rule for us**: obfuscation against a distribution-shape monitor transfers to boundary monitors, not the reverse — so a white-box model of a network that predicts behaviour from *one* region of activation space is a prediction about that region, and a monitor built on the shape of benign activity is the more fragile of the two. (4) **The capability term is a probe-side instrument we can copy cheaply**: hiding from more layers cost SQL accuracy with an entirely negative interval while recall-versus-layers was mostly insignificant, which is the template for showing that a representation is load-bearing — attack the read-out, and if performance collapses, the read-out was reading something the task needs. The counter-warning is the orthogonality negative: behaviour and near-orthogonal activation are incompatible at the magnitudes tested, which is a limit on how much any single-tap monitor can be evaded, and therefore a reason to report cosine similarity to natural activations alongside any obfuscation claim.
