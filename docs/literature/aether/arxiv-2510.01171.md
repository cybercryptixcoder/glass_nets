# Verbalized Sampling: How to Mitigate Mode Collapse and Unlock LLM Diversity

- **Key:** arxiv-2510.01171
- **Authors / organisation:** Jiayi Zhang, Simon Yu (equal contribution), Derek Chong, Anthony Sicilia, Michael R. Tomz, Christopher D. Manning, Weiyan Shi; Northeastern University, Stanford University, West Virginia University
- **Date:** 2025-10 (v1 2025-10-01; v4 read, 2026-07-15)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Aligned models answer a creative prompt with the same canned thing every time. The paper asks where that narrowing comes from and whether it can be undone without retraining. Their answer is that it starts in the preference data: annotators reliably pick the response the base model finds more likely — familiar, fluent, predictable. They name this *typicality bias*, derive what it does to the KL-regularised RLHF optimum, and then exploit the derivation to build a prompt-level fix called Verbalized Sampling (VS). The larger goal is a cheap inference-time route back to the generator's pretraining diversity without giving up quality, safety or factual accuracy.

## What they studied
Unit of analysis: the output distribution of a whole model, measured over collections of responses. Behavioural only — no weights, activations, features or circuits are touched. Ten models across the main suite: GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Claude-4-Sonnet, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, o3 and DeepSeek R1. Data: preference pairs from OpenAI TL;DR, UltraFeedback, HelpSteer-v2 and Skywork, plus PRISM's demographically annotated preferences; poems from PoemHunter, stories from BookMIA, dad jokes from Reddit, PersuasionForGood dialogues, SimpleQA, StrongReject, and 1k synthetic competition-math questions. The Tulu-3 ladder from Llama-3.1-70B-base gives the post-training stage axis.

## How they did it
Three steps, in order. First, verify the bias: for each preference pair in a dataset, score both responses under a *base* model's log-probability and record how often the human-preferred side is also the more likely one, over 2,500 sampled pairs per dataset and six base models (Gemma-3-4B/27B, Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Olmo-3-7B) plus their instruct twins. Second, isolate it: on HelpSteer, form within-prompt pairs *matched on correctness* rating (6,874 tied pairs, 28,283 overall), then fit a Bradley–Terry logistic model whose predictor is the log-likelihood gap under a large base model, so the coefficient α on that gap is the typicality weight holding true utility fixed; rerun with token count, Flesch–Kincaid, type–token ratio and mean sentence length as controls. Third, derive and test: plug the estimated reward into the closed-form KL-regularised optimum, which yields a policy proportional to the reference raised to the power γ = 1 + α/β; prove that on any set of responses with equal true reward, instance prompts concentrate on that sharpened policy's mode and list prompts concentrate on the top-k modes, whereas a distribution prompt with a weak preference for representative outputs produces a reward gap that sharpening amplifies. The intervention is then trivially small: ask for several candidates together with their probabilities. All comparisons are budget-matched at N total responses per prompt, with candidates per call k < N, and the baselines are direct prompting, chain-of-thought, a one-call list ("tell me 5 jokes"), and multi-turn ("another one").

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanistic explanation of the model. It is a parameterised account of the *training objective* on the policy that the training produces: a scalar bias weight, a derived sharpening exponent, and three toy theorems saying which property of the prompt determines whether sharpening helps or hurts. It is meant to be complete enough to predict qualitative outcomes (VS recovers diversity, list prompts underperform their token cost, more capable models gain more) but not to predict specific distributions, and the authors are explicit that no component of it is read off the network.

## How they judged success
Diversity is 1 minus the mean pairwise cosine similarity of `text-embedding-3-small` embeddings over the N responses, negative similarities clipped to zero, reported as a percentage; ROUGE-L is the lexical companion and lower is more diverse. Quality is a Claude-3.7-Sonnet judge using the Creative Writing v3 rubrics and the HumorBench grader, plus a Prolific study (90 annotators, 30 per task, 90 pairs per task, 4-point diversity and quality scales, agreement 0.49–0.87). Downstream: SFT accuracy on MATH500/OlympiadBench/Minerva after fine-tuning on 1k synthetic questions; KS test and L1 on simulated donation amounts; Top@1 and Pass@N on SimpleQA with a GPT-4.1 judge; KL to uniform on 600 dice rolls; refusal rate on 353 StrongReject prompts; KL from corpus-counted frequencies on a "name a US state" probe against RedPajama counts. Statistics are one-tailed t-tests against VS-Standard, plus CIs on the bootstrap-like agreement rates.

## What they found
- **The bias is there and survives controls.** For 6,874 correctness-matched HelpSteer pairs, α̂ = 0.57 ± 0.07 with Llama-3.1-405B-base as the scorer and 0.65 ± 0.07 with GLM-4.5-base, both p < 10⁻¹⁴ — 17–19 points of win probability across ±1 SD of log-likelihood. Adding the four surface controls roughly halves it (0.260 and 0.326) but it stays significant, so surface form explains part of the effect and not all of it.
- **It is not just HelpSteer.** Across four preference datasets and six base models, human-preferred responses are the higher-likelihood ones 4–12 points above chance, and instruction-tuned versions of the same models keep or increase the rate. In PRISM the effect appears in every annotator group but varies: Hispanic annotators 57–60%, Black annotators near or below chance.
- **Diversity moves a lot.** Joke writing is the clearest: direct prompting scores 21.6–30.4 on the averaged semantic-diversity scale, VS-Standard 58.2–67.2, with quality essentially flat. Poem continuation goes from ~8–13 direct to 14.8–28.2, story from 17–25 to 27.6–49.0, with the largest single jumps on the reasoning models (Claude-4-Sonnet story 23.0 → 32.4, DeepSeek-R1 story 24.8 → 49.0). Headline claim in the abstract: 1.6–2.1× diversity gain in creative writing, plus 25.7% better human-rated quality.
- **The ladder matters.** On Tulu-3-70B the base model sits at 45.4% poem diversity; direct prompting after SFT is 20.8% and after DPO 10.8%, while VS holds around 30% across SFT/RLHF/RLVR — 182.6% above direct after DPO and 66.8% of the base model's level, against 23.8% for direct.
- **Quality is a wash, not a win.** Judge-scored quality is within noise of direct across the creative tables; where VS-CoT wins it is often by a point or two. The human study gives diversity 1.90/2.07/2.39 (direct/sequence/VS) on poems, 2.74/2.76/3.06 on stories, 1.83/2.93/3.01 on jokes.
- **Two useful side results.** On 600 dice rolls, KL to uniform is 0.926 direct, 1.163 direct-with-CoT, 0.119 multi-turn, 0.058 sequence and 0.027 VS-Standard. On SimpleQA accuracy is unchanged (Top@1 0.329 vs 0.310 direct; Pass@N 0.448 vs 0.430), and refusals on StrongReject stay at 97.45% vs 98.22% direct.
- **The verbalised probabilities carry real information.** Against RedPajama state-name counts, VS-Standard's distribution is 0.132 KL from the corpus proxy where direct prompting is 14.886 and a plain list is 0.438 — but the same method is 0.352 from *uniform* where the list is 0.000, so the gain is "peaked in the right places", not "calibrated". Verbalised probabilities correlate with corpus frequencies at r = 0.741 / ρ = 0.896 for US states and r = 0.816 for countries, but r = 0.182 and 0.075 for programming languages.

## Limits and open problems they name
Compute and latency: N responses with probability annotations cost more than one response, controlled only within the paper's own budget. The gain scales with model capability, and weaker models sometimes get worse in quality — the method leans on the model's ability to estimate probabilities. They also flag that the pretraining-distribution experiment is a proof of concept on one trivial task, and that readability in the dialogue simulation remains above human level. What they do not name, and I add: the diversity metric is the same embedding-similarity statistic whose collapse inside a single model this folder already holds as a caution; the judge is one model family (Claude-3.7) grading generations from the same family's rivals including Claude-4-Sonnet, with no self-preference arm; the "emergent trend that bigger models gain more" is a comparison of two size pairs per family read off a scatter, with no interaction test; the corpus proxy for "the pretraining distribution" is RedPajama document counts on one question; and the k > 1 candidate design confounds "distribution framing" with "more entropy per call" unless the strict list baselines in Table 28 are trusted to have removed it, which they plausibly do for poems but where Sequence-Multi still trails VS-Standard by a margin the paper does not test.

## What we take from it
Three things, in order of usefulness. **(1) The mechanism, stated in a form we can reuse:** an aligned model's response is a *sharpened* version of its reference distribution, so the sampling temperature we set is not the only temperature in play — the preference data has already applied an exponent we never choose. That is a warning for any read-out we take from an aligned model: the distribution our probes and verbalisers see is not the base model's, and the amount of sharpening is a property of the training run, not of the prompt. **(2) A cheap elicitation trick with a measurement attached:** ask for a set of candidates *with their probabilities* rather than one candidate, and compare against a plain list at equal token cost — the list arm is the control that separates "more entropy" from "distribution framing", and their Table 30 shows the list is cheaper and still weaker (1.74× vs 2.11× diversity gain at 0.94× vs 1.12× cost), which is exactly the matched-cost comparison this folder keeps demanding. **(3) A calibration caveat worth carrying:** a verbalised distribution can be markedly closer to the corpus proxy *and* markedly worse than a flat list against the uniform reference, so "our elicited distribution matches the truth" has to be reported against at least two references, because the metric's preference flips between them. For us specifically, VS is a candidate instrument for eliciting a model's own belief distribution as a supervision signal or as a comparison distribution against probe read-outs — with the caveat that the probabilities are rank-informative and not numerically trustworthy.
