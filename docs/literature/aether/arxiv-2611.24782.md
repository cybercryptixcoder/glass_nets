## What they wanted, and why
Two networks trained on the same task end in different weight configurations even after long training. The authors ask what those leftovers mean: incidental variation among equivalent solutions, or a readable record of how each network got there. The paper is a direct probe of the second hypothesis, testing three separate claims — that a network's origin stays identifiable from its final weights, that the order of its earlier tasks stays identifiable too, and that neither implies the earlier weights themselves can be recovered. The larger goal is a claim about what a trained model *is*: not only a solution to a task but a compressed and selective record of the path that produced it, which is exactly the framing a predictive white-box model would need in order to treat weights as data about history rather than only about function.

## What they studied
One architecture throughout: a single-hidden-layer fully connected net, 784-64-10 with ReLU and zero biases, trained with SGD (lr 0.1, momentum 0.9, batch 256) on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and KMNIST. Unit of analysis is the whole weight matrix pair after gauge-fixing and neuron alignment, plus hidden activations (compared with CKA) as a parallel space. Eight experiment cohorts: a main cohort of 10 initializations per dataset saved at 21 half-epoch checkpoints; a crossed cohort of 10 initializations x 5 batch schedules per dataset (150 runs); a perturbation pilot (10 sources, 300 continuations); a perturbation extension crossing 3 datasets x 3 intervention times x 3 layer choices x 4 strengths x 2 momentum protocols (2,160 perturbations, 180 matched baselines); paired A–B–C versus B–A–C curricula (200 seeds x 2 histories x 2 momentum protocols, 800 runs); and two trajectory/event cohorts of 200 seed pairs each.

## How they did it
Weights are put in a canonical gauge first: for each hidden unit the incoming and outgoing vector norms are balanced by their square-root ratio (function-preserving for ReLU), then one permutation matrix is fitted jointly to the columns of W0 and rows of W1 — for this depth the Hungarian assignment attains the Git Re-Basin optimum. Initialization retrieval then ranks every trained state against all ten initial weight configurations from its dataset by the joint Frobenius distance after that alignment, with total-vector normalisation applied to both sides (an analysis operation, not a symmetry); identity labels are consulted only after ranking. The same machinery is run on activations using 1 − CKA over a fixed 3,000-image probe, so weight-space and representation-space identification are compared with matched treatment. The crossed cohort separates the two causes of variance by holding initialization fixed across five independently drawn batch orders and holding batch order fixed across ten initializations. Perturbation experiments add a Gaussian step to W0, W1 or both at 1/5/10/20% of the matrix norm, continue both branches on identical future batches, and score the ratio of the post-perturbation distance to the final distance, keeping accuracy and loss as independent endpoints; a reference region defined by structural envelope and performance thresholds turns "returned" into a membership test. History decoding trains paired copies of the same initialization on A–B–C and B–A–C (A = digit identity, B = label+1 mod 10, C = label+3 mod 10, one epoch each of A and B, ten of C, same images in the same order), then fits an L2 logistic probe on canonicalised endpoint weights and evaluates on 40 held-out initialization pairs, so the decoder must recognise a *history* in a network whose starting weights it has never seen. Trajectory Reconstruction predicts earlier PCA coordinates of the trajectory from final weights with ridge regression, against training-mean, persistence and a displacement baseline mathematically equivalent to the mean predictor; event probes classify AB/BA order at each of four earlier positions. Inference treats the network or the seed pair as the independent unit, uses permutation tests (10,000 identity permutations for retrieval, 999 seed-block permutations for history), and Holm-corrects within pre-specified families; the authors disclose that the eight experiments were not one jointly pre-registered family.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanism and not a circuit: the object is an **information claim about a state**, testable by a decoder. The paper's currency is "this fact about the past is linearly recoverable from the present weights, above a stated chance and above stated baselines", demonstrated on held-out initializations. It is explicitly partial — the authors go out of their way to separate *identification* of an earlier event from *reconstruction* of an earlier weight vector and show the first succeeds where the second fails. It can predict something: the sign and size of an unseen initialization's history label, and roughly, but not individually, some intermediate trajectory coordinates.

## How they judged success
Top-1 retrieval accuracy among ten candidates (chance 10%) with mean correct-candidate rank (chance 5.5) and MRR; Spearman correlation between the 45 initial and final pairwise distances for geometry preservation; CKA for representation-space retrieval (chance rank 5.5); separation ratio d(s)/d(0+) for perturbation returns (above one = moved apart), with 95% bootstrap intervals over ten source networks per condition and three alternative neuron-matching procedures (Hungarian on PIFs, Alpha-profile costs) as robustness; classification balanced accuracy for history (chance 50%) with shuffled-label controls; R² for trajectory reconstruction against training-mean and persistence baselines; and loss along eleven interpolated points between aligned endpoints for connectivity. Ablations run over both gauges, native versus optimal coordinates, normalised versus unnormalised costs, layer subsets, activity-masked and residual-masked subsets, and norm-only cost as a degenerate baseline.

## What they found
**Geometry is not preserved, but identity is.** Correlations between initial and final pairwise ensemble distances were 0.12 (MNIST), −0.18 (Fashion) and 0.28 (KMNIST), none surviving correction, aggregate 0.073 — networks that started close did not end close. Yet all 30 networks were matched to their own initialization among ten candidates at every one of 21 checkpoints: top-1 100%, mean rank 1.0, MRR 1.0, Holm p = 0.0003 per dataset. Identification survived every control: raw or canonical weights, native or optimal alignment, normalised or not; W0 alone sufficed (100% in all three); restricting to the most active hidden-unit quartile still gave 100/90/100%; using only the 25% largest matched entrywise differences still identified every initialization. Norm-only cost fell to chance (mean rank 4.92 raw, 5.85 canonical, against 5.5). The output layer alone was the one conditional case: 100% in native coordinates but 0/0/20% under the joint optimal alignment — so the information is present in W1 but is not permutation-invariantly located there.

**The same information does not survive in activations.** CKA-based origin retrieval ended at chance: mean ranks 5.5 / 4.8 / 5.9, overall top-1 6.7%, feature-standardised and logit variants no better (aggregate ranks 5.40, 5.53, 5.60). But initialization still *shapes* representations: networks sharing an initialization, trained on different batch schedules, were identifiable from final weights at 100/90/100% and from final activations at 97/45.5/87%. Batch order alone was identifiable at 24.44% (110/450), 33.11% (149/450) and 30.44% (137/450) against a 20% chance. And the identifiable factor is not the performance-relevant one: on Fashion-MNIST batch order explained more of the final accuracy variance than initialization did, while initialization was far easier to identify.

**Function recovers, trajectory does not.** Of 300 pilot perturbations every one initially moved toward its unperturbed twin and none stayed there; final separation ratios exceeded one in every strength x momentum cell (16.1, 3.34, 1.17 at 1/5/20% with momentum retained), with lower confidence bounds above one. In the extension all 216 condition means had pointwise lower bounds above one, and PIF matching agreed in 216/216 while Alpha matching agreed in 215 (one interval including 1). The strongest pilot perturbation cut accuracy 97.22% to 96.61% and after ten more epochs both branches sat at 97.61% versus 97.57%. Of 2,160 endpoint pairs, 2,157 showed no positive loss barrier along sampled linear interpolation, and the other three peaked at 0.00361 cross-entropy.

**History is decodable; the trajectory is not.** A–B–C versus B–A–C order was classified at **100% on 80 held-out networks** after ten C epochs (and after one and five), with reset and retained momentum alike, shuffled labels at 48.75%. Logits decoded it at 85–96.25% and diagnostic measures at 85–87.5%, but weights still gave 83.75% after linearly residualising out six diagnostic measures, so the history signal is not a restatement of performance or norms. With four successive task pairs, decoding fell 93.75% / 68.75% / 63.75% / 60% from oldest to newest — the *earliest* pair was read most reliably — and only the first two passed Holm correction, in a cohort where mean final-task accuracy was 29.89% and about 61 of 64 hidden units were inactive at the endpoint. Trajectory reconstruction: R² ≈ 0.62 and 0.70 for one epoch and two phases back, beating both baselines; predicting the initial state gave R² = −0.199 (8 PCA coords), −0.079 (16) and −0.040 (32), an MLP gave −1.869, and the four-pair cohort's best was ≈0.003. The trap the paper disarms explicitly: predicting *changes* from init to end scored above 0.95, but a control using only the training-mean earlier weights plus the known endpoint scored above 0.999 — so that high number was reading the endpoint, not the past.

## Limits and open problems they name
One hidden layer, three image datasets, fixed-learning-rate SGD: nothing is established for deep or transformer networks, and transformers need symmetries (rotations within attention) beyond permutations and ReLU rescalings. Retrieval selects among ten *known* candidates — it never reconstructs an unknown initialization, and the failures of reconstruction do not prove it impossible. Layer-specific findings depend on the joint alignment (W1 identifiable natively, not under joint assignment). CKA failure to identify an initial representation does not establish that the information is gone; the authors propose PWCCA as the follow-up measure. The eight PCA coordinates carry only about 28% of scaled trajectory variance. In the two-history comparison, task order is confounded with which task immediately preceded C. Perturbation conditions share source trajectories, their 216 intervals are not jointly corrected, and the extension's networks did not leave the structural envelope, so *attraction back into a region* is not demonstrated — only a family of connected solutions. Perfect accuracy in finite samples yields zero-width bootstrap intervals and does not imply population ceiling. Named open directions: test whether history lives in weight directions of low effect on loss (perturb along them and see decoding break before performance does), and determine whether the connected solutions form a broad region or thin branching corridors.

## What we take from it
A **method** we can import almost whole: symmetry-canonicalise (gauge-balance, then optimal permutation) and only then compute distances — every "these two networks are far apart" or "this checkpoint moved" claim we make should be premised on that canonicalisation, and the paper shows the choice of cost and layer can flip an identification result from 100% to 0%. A **framing**: a probe on weights can have identification accuracy as its headline and reconstruction R² as its ceiling, and these are different quantities that must be reported separately. A **measure** we lack: a *held-out-initialization* probe evaluation, where the decoder is trained on some initializations and asked about histories of networks it has never seen — the test that distinguishes a history signal from an identity fingerprint. A **warning** that generalises beyond this paper: a high R² against a baseline constructed from the known endpoint is measuring the endpoint; the displacement control's >0.999 versus the model's >0.95 is the cleanest case in this folder of a headline number being dominated by its own baseline, and the response is a baseline that uses exactly as much of the known present as the model does. A **second warning**, more directly shaped for us: geometry and decodability can dissociate in both directions — ensemble geometry was reorganised while per-network identity survived, and representations stopped resembling their own origins while still encoding shared initialization — so any geometric statistic we compute over a set of checkpoints or models is not a substitute for a per-model decoder, and any decoder's failure is not proof the information is absent. Finally, a **cheap concrete trick** for our own evaluations: shuffled-label and constancy baselines (persistence) belong beside every decoder score, because both naive read-outs on the same data landed at chance and below it respectively.
