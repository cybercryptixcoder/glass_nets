## What they wanted, and why

A vision-language model fine-tuned as a driving planner fails badly when it is driven in a new city: the paper's opening example is a planner trained in Boston whose trajectory error grows by more than a factor of six in Singapore, from 0.15 m to 1.13 m. Collecting and labelling data for every city is impractical, so the useful question is how to adapt the planner cheaply to a new city it has never seen labels for. The authors' argument is that existing treatments are wrong for a structural reason, not a tuning reason: both the "align everything" family (distribution matching, adversarial alignment, adapters applied uniformly) and the "edit one site" family (a single causal-tracing edit, or a hand-picked layer band) treat the domain gap as a single scalar quantity that one objective can minimise everywhere. They claim the gap is actually a two-dimensional object — different for image tokens than for text tokens, and different at different depths — so a single objective is guaranteed to overwrite computation that is already domain-invariant. The larger goal is the one that matters to us: a diagnostic that says where in a network a distribution shift actually lives, cheap enough to run once per architecture, and a repair whose shape is chosen by the diagnosis rather than by convention.

## What they studied

Three architecturally distinct vision-language planners, deliberately spanning the two axes the method depends on. LFM2-VL-1.6B: 16 layers, a hybrid backbone with a causal decoder. Qwen2-VL-2B: 28 layers, a pure transformer with a causal decoder. PaliGemma2-3B: a SigLIP encoder plus a 26-layer Gemma2 decoder using prefix-LM attention with bidirectional image attention, i.e. no causal mask over the image tokens. Two cross-city driving benchmarks: nuScenes with Boston-Seaport as source and Singapore as target (about 26.6k train, 3,569 source test, 3,500 target test), and Argoverse 2 with Pittsburgh as source and Miami as target (858 train, 284 in-domain test, 1,393 out-of-domain test). The unit of analysis is a **slice**: a (modality, layer) cell of the hidden state, where modality is image tokens versus text tokens, and the measured quantity is a change in the predicted six-waypoint trajectory.

## How they did it

The diagnostic works on scene-matched pairs. Target inputs are matched to their nearest source neighbour in a three-dimensional content vector (Definition 3), so each pair is nominally the same driving situation in two cities and the remaining difference is domain, not content. For each pair and each slice, the target activations at the image-token positions (or the text-token positions) at layer *l* are overwritten with the source activations at the same positions and layer, the forward pass resumes, and the displacement of the final trajectory from the unpatched target prediction is recorded as a per-waypoint Euclidean distance averaged over the six waypoints. Averaging those displacements over N = 100 pairs gives one entry of a 2×L tensor. This is textbook activation patching, imported into a regression problem, and the authors are explicit about why: domain-probing accuracy saturates above 95% at every layer of every model (their own Figure 1 panel b) and therefore cannot rank layers at all, attention weights are correlational, and gradient attribution conflates causal effect with gradient magnitude in a regression. They bound the Monte Carlo error with Hoeffding's inequality — at N = 100 and a per-waypoint range of roughly 20 m the 95% half-width is about 0.6 m — and then validate the bound empirically with a budget sweep over N ∈ {25, 50, 100, 200, 500}.

The tensor is turned into a repair plan by thresholding. Layers whose image entry exceeds an image threshold become the alignment zone, layers whose text entry exceeds a text threshold (excluding the alignment zone) become the fine-tuning zone, and everything else is frozen. Thresholds are read off the elbow of the sorted entries. Each zone then gets a matched operator: multi-scale kernel MMD between source and target mean-pooled image-token representations, with rank-16 adapters on the attention projections only (queries, keys, values, outputs — the authors call this the cross-modal mixing locus), on the alignment zone; rank-16 adapters on every linear projection driven by the ordinary supervised trajectory loss on labelled source data, on the fine-tuning zone; and nothing at all on the freeze zone. Target inputs enter only the kernel term, never the task loss, so the recipe is legitimate unsupervised domain adaptation with a transductive pool in the main experiments (an inductive pool of 9,183 disjoint keyframes is run as a control). Nine baselines are compared, all sharing the same input format, backbone and adapter rank: source-only; full LoRA; full LoRA plus uniform MMD; a domain-adversarial network; MMD-only with no task training; layer-wise learning-rate decay; freeze-last-K; a rank-one causal-tracing edit applied to the single most important MLP site; and a hand-picked-band fine-tune. Four metrics: average displacement error, final displacement error, their difference across domains, and inference latency, averaged over five seeds, with margins flagged against a two-standard-deviation threshold.

One step in the setup deserves separate mention because it changes what the paper is able to claim. Before any diagnosis, the planners are re-fine-tuned so that they actually use the image. A vanilla source planner has an image-dependency ratio of −0.6%, meaning replacing every camera frame with a constant grey image leaves the error essentially unchanged — the planner had collapsed onto the numerical text features (speed, following distance, lead velocity) that are sufficient statistics for the label. In that regime the image row of the tensor is structurally zero and any image-side causal claim is vacuous. So they remove the numerical text from the prompt and supply three consecutive front-camera frames, and argue information-theoretically that the only gradient-bearing path to the loss now runs through the visual encoder. After this the image-dependency ratio is triple-digit positive on all six model–dataset cells (from +172% on Qwen2/Argoverse to +442% on LFM2/nuScenes).

## What counts as an explanation here

A **2×L non-negative tensor plus a partition of the layers into three zones**, where each entry is a causal quantity in the interventional sense — the expected trajectory displacement caused by substituting one modality's activations from a matched other-domain input at one depth. It is explicitly partial: it answers where the cross-domain gap is carried, not how any layer computes the trajectory, and the authors say the three-zone structure is empirical with bootstrap stability but no theoretical guarantee of universality. It does predict something concrete and cheap: the partition diagnosed on one benchmark transfers to another city pair without re-diagnosis, and the ablation shows that substituting a partition built from a correlational diagnostic, or a random one, degrades the repair measurably. So the tensor is a predictor of *where to act*, validated against the downstream outcome, not a description of mechanism.

## How they judged success

Primary metric is the cross-domain gap, the target-domain average displacement error minus the source-domain one, with absolute displacement errors and inference latency reported alongside. Robustness is judged by what happens when parts of the recipe are removed: random partitions and probing-derived partitions in place of the causal one; each zone operator individually deleted; the two zone operators swapped; the thresholds swept; the MMD weight swept; the number of temporal frames swept; the pair budget swept. Stability of the diagnosis is scored with a zone-wise Jaccard similarity between partitions, on a 16-point normalised depth grid when comparing across architectures, against a stated chance level of about 0.18 for size-matched random partitions. Transfer is tested directly: a partition diagnosed on nuScenes is applied unchanged to Argoverse 2 and compared with a locally diagnosed partition and with no adaptation. There is a separate single-cell inductive-protocol control, a long-tail zero-shot stress test, a reversed-source-and-target diagnostic, and planning-flavoured proxies (lateral error at 3 s, mean absolute jerk). Significance is a two-standard-deviation margin, and the appendix reports five-seed standard deviations for the compute table and the budget sweep.

## What they found

The tensor has structure, and the structure differs by architecture. In the two causal-decoder models, image-token shift peaks in the first two or three layers and decays quickly while text-token shift rises to dominate a contiguous middle band; in PaliGemma2 the image entry dominates at every layer, which the authors attribute to prefix-LM attention continuously re-injecting domain-shifted features. The resulting partitions are: LFM2 layers {0,1} / {2–9} / {10–15}; Qwen2 {0–3} / {4–10} / {11–27}; PaliGemma2 {0–24} / empty / {25} — the fine-tuning zone vanishes entirely on the prefix-LM model, and the recipe degenerates to alignment plus freeze automatically. The partition is stable within an architecture across datasets (Jaccard 1.00 for LFM2 and PaliGemma2, 0.88–0.96 by zone for Qwen2, against chance 0.18) and moderately stable across architectures on the overlapping zones (0.71–0.81), with only the middle zone diverging. The repair is the best adaptation method in all six model–dataset settings, by 0.015–0.138 m on the quality metrics, largest on PaliGemma2/nuScenes at +0.130 average and +0.105 final displacement, smallest on LFM2/nuScenes at +0.015/+0.040; several cells carry the two-sigma flag and several do not. Training uses 50–60% fewer trainable parameters and inference is 8.4–10.9 ms faster.

The negative results carry more weight than the positive ones. Uniform MMD alone doubles the absolute error (LFM2/nuScenes: 4.275 m to 9.657 m) and is marked as collapsed in all six settings. Full LoRA plus uniform MMD is worse than full LoRA alone in 17 of 18 quality cells, by 0.01–0.09 m — aligning everything while tuning everything actively destroys the adaptation that task-only tuning had already achieved. Removing the causal partition costs 0.2–0.4 m on every cell, whether it is replaced by a random partition or by one derived from domain-probing accuracy, which is the direct demonstration that a correlational diagnostic cannot stand in for the causal one even when it appears to locate the same thing. Swapping the two zone operators is the single largest ablation hit, +0.3–0.4 m everywhere, so the matching of operator to zone matters as much as the partition itself; deleting the freeze zone costs less than 0.02 m, since those parameters were already near-optimal. Zero-shot partition transfer matches local diagnosis within 0.01–0.02 m and beats source-only by 0.5–0.6 m. The inductive control changes target error by 0.007 m average and 0.03 m final — but the authors explicitly refuse to read it as a protocol effect, since it is one cell at one seed with no other baselines run. The long-tail probe leaves every method weak (closest-mode accuracy 0.25 for source-only, 0.29 for the best adapted) and they say plainly that it establishes nothing about robustness.

## Limits and open problems they name

The hard-threshold partition flips one or two boundary layers on tightly shifted benchmarks, and they observed exactly that in a same-recipe replicate on different hardware, which moved two boundaries by one layer. Generalisation to encoder–decoder and cross-attention fusion architectures, and to non-driving tasks with non-geographic shifts (weather, sensor, season), is untested — the tensor view is task-agnostic in principle and validated for one task family only. Evaluation is nominal driving with displacement metrics; official planning-oriented metrics are future work, and their own jerk proxy shows that closeness to the ground-truth jerk does not imply a safer trajectory. The diagnostic requires scene-matched pairs, so its estimates depend on pairing quality, and because pairs are drawn predominantly from prevalent scenes, shift components visible only in rare scenes can be missed. Some numbers are single-seed diagnostics (the inductive control, the long-tail probe, the reversed direction, the planning proxies), and the paper says so at each.

## What we take from it

Three things, and one warning about numbers. **A method:** the tensor is the cleanest template in the folder for a diagnostic that is *per-slice* rather than per-layer or per-component — the modality axis is a decomposition dimension as real as depth, and it costs nothing to include whenever the model takes more than one kind of token. **A measure:** the coupling of a causal score with a coupling-free stability score is what makes the claim auditable. The causal tensor says where the effect is; the Jaccard says whether that answer is a property of the model or of the run; and the gap between within-architecture stability (about 1.0) and cross-architecture stability (0.71–0.81 on overlapping zones, with the middle zone collapsing to nothing) is a *result* — it tells you that depth selection does not transfer between architectures even when it transfers perfectly between datasets, which is the exact asymmetry our own predictors will have to report. **A framing:** "the effect is structured, therefore a scalar objective must damage something" is a fully general argument, and the 17-of-18 result is its price tag — adding an alignment loss on top of task tuning made things worse almost everywhere. Any battery we build should print the align-everything arm beside the located arm, because the naive composite is not merely weaker, it is negative.

The warning is about their own headline. The gap metric is a *difference of two errors*, and the main table shows what that does: LFM2/nuScenes reports a domain gap of 3.150 m for MoLaRx against 2.550 m for source-only — the adapted model has a *larger* gap and a *lower* absolute target error (4.251 vs 4.510), because adaptation improved the source side more than the target side. The same pattern repeats across the table, and never more starkly than on Argoverse 2 for Qwen2: source-only's gap is 3.141 m and every adapted method's is 1.2–1.4 m, a roughly 60% reduction that is arithmetically impossible as target-side improvement (its absolute target error barely moves) and is mostly the source error inflating. So the "all six settings" claim is true of the metric and much weaker as a statement about target-city competence, and a difference-of-errors metric can be improved by degrading the reference. What we import is therefore the diagnostic, the stability score, and the comparison discipline; what we do not import is a gap metric as a headline without the two absolute errors printed beside it, plus the perturbed-input arm that establishes the model was using the channel at all — here the −0.6% image-dependency measurement that forced a re-fine-tuning of every planner before the study could begin. That step is the folder's plainest instance of a prerequisite check: before interpreting which layers carry a shift, confirm the input channel is live, because on every one of the six cells it was not.
