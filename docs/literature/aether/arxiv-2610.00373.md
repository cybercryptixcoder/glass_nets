# When Do Attention-Head Ablations Support Causal Claims? Projection-Level Confounds, Floor Effects, and Matched Controls

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.00373
- **Authors / organisation:** Juli Huang — Stanford University (single author; affiliation and email printed once in the HTML byline, corresponding author only, no other authors or institutions listed)
- **Date:** 2026-09-30
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00373
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Zeroing one attention head and reading the drop in task performance is the most common way this field argues that a component is causally responsible for a behaviour, and the paper's premise is that the recipe is treated as self-certifying when it is not. The author asks a narrow, mechanical question rather than a conceptual one: under what exact conditions does a single-head zero-ablation license the inference people draw from it? Three specific links in the chain are attacked — whether the intervention actually removes the head named, whether the metric can register a change at all, and whether the identified heads beat a null that is matched to them and evaluated on data that were not used to pick them. The larger motivation is methodological honesty about a result class the rest of the literature (circuits, pruning, attribution) leans on, and the paper is written as a worked, fully traceable example rather than a survey: every number is read from a saved result file, and a claim-to-evidence map lists the JSON field behind each quantitative sentence. A fourth, quieter goal is to demonstrate the discipline of pre-committing a larger rerun and reporting it whatever it says.

## What they studied
**Unit of analysis: attention heads** — the individual per-head value/output slices of GPT-2 small (124M parameters, 12 layers by 12 heads, 144 heads total) and, in replication, of DistilGPT2 (6 layers by 12 heads, 72 heads). Two synthetic task families: a controlled induction-style copying task of the form four distinct words then a period then the first three, so that the answer requires copying from context, built from single-BPE-token words and filtered so the question tokenises as an exact token-level prefix of question-plus-answer; and an off-target single-digit arithmetic task used only as a specificity control. N = 200 examples per task, split 50/50 into discovery and held-out halves fixed before any head effect was computed. No naturalistic benchmark is used anywhere.

## How they did it
1. **Find the implementation bug by auditing an existing one.** The author takes a pre-existing, previously unaudited head-ablation implementation and shows that its hook — a forward hook on the whole attention module, zeroing a head-sized contiguous slice of the *returned* tensor — runs after the block's output projection has already mixed every head into every output channel, so the intervention removes an arbitrary linear combination of all heads' contributions from a subset of channels rather than one head. The corrected version is a pre-hook on the output projection's *input*, zeroing the not-yet-mixed per-head slice.
2. **Assert the semantics executably.** Before the pipeline runs, shipped tests on a tiny GPT-2 configuration check that the pre-hook zeroes exactly the requested head's slice of the projection input and leaves every other channel untouched, that removing the hook restores the unablated output exactly, and that the naive hook changes the logits while leaving that projection input alone. The claim being tested is the intervention's identity, not its effect size.
3. **Measure per-head effect with two metrics.** For each head, Δ is the mean gold-continuation log-probability without ablation minus the same with the head ablated, computed teacher-forced in one batched forward pass per head rather than per example; binary teacher-forced exact match is carried alongside as the metric being indicted.
4. **Split selection from evaluation.** Candidates are the top five heads by Δ on the discovery half only, then scored on the held-out half.
5. **Build matched controls by resampling, not by re-running.** A random-head control draws 1,000 sets of five heads uniformly without replacement from all heads; a layer-matched control draws 1,000 sets preserving the candidate set's size and its per-layer composition, drawing each layer's replacements from that layer's non-candidate heads. Both are constructed from the already-computed 144-head Δ vector, so the entire control battery costs no forward passes. The author notes why both exclusions matter: independent replacement would let one head stand in for several candidates and would let a candidate enter its own null.
6. **Add a specificity contrast.** The candidate set's held-out Δ on the copying task minus its Δ on the arithmetic task, compared against the same statistic for the random control — the off-target task is never used for selection.
7. **Report with intervals and a finite-sample p.** Percentile bootstrap over examples for point estimates; one-sided Monte Carlo p with the (k+1)/(B+1) correction against the 1,000 draws; Spearman ρ over all heads for split-to-split rank stability.
8. **Pre-commit a larger rerun and replicate on a second model.** A one-time N = 500 run of the GPT-2 pipeline is reported regardless of outcome, and the unchanged pipeline is rerun on DistilGPT2 with only the model swapped. Each of the three configurations was rerun independently and reproduced its result file byte for byte apart from the timestamp.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation in the mechanistic sense at all, and the paper is explicit about it: it proposes a **causal-claim ladder** — correlation, then decodability, then intervention effect, then specific causal contribution, then mechanistic role — and states that its evidence reaches the third rung and not the fourth. What the paper produces is a **validated per-head ranking with a null it clears**, i.e. an attribution whose validity conditions are stated as part of the object. Whether a head "computes induction" is left to activation patching, input-dependence analysis or directional interventions, none of which is run. The form is therefore a probe-like effect measure with an audited instrument rather than a description of a computation, and its predictive content is the modest one that repeating the protocol on held-out data will reproduce both the ranking and the separation from matched controls.

## How they judged success
Three separate success criteria, one per research question, and each is measured against a quantity that could have failed independently. **Intervention semantics:** the correlation between the naive and corrected per-head Δ vectors, and the overlap of their top-five sets, compared against what a correct replicate would give (an identity line, full overlap). **Metric sufficiency:** whether accuracy is censored at a measured floor and coarse near a measured ceiling, with teacher-forced log-probability tested for gradedness in the same two regimes on the same examples. **Attribution validity:** held-out mean effect of the discovery-selected candidates against 1,000-draw random and layer-matched null distributions, one-sided Monte Carlo p, plus split-to-split Spearman ρ over all heads, plus the specificity contrast against the off-target task. Everything is repeated on DistilGPT2 with identical data, splits, metrics and controls. The paper also holds itself to a pre-commitment rule: the N = 500 rerun was fixed in advance and is reported whether or not it agrees.

## What they found
**The naive ablation does not ablate a head.** On the copying-task discovery split the naive post-projection and corrected pre-projection per-head Δ vectors are essentially uncorrelated — Pearson r = 0.057, Spearman ρ = 0.110 — the top-five sets share zero heads, and the mean absolute per-head disagreement is 0.067 nats, which is the same order as the effect the whole paper is arguing about (0.412 nats). On DistilGPT2 the same comparison gives r = 0.001 and again 0/5 overlap. The author states the failure was not constructed: it is the code found in an existing implementation.

**Accuracy is censored at both ends, and log-probability is not.** GPT-2 small sits at exactly 0.0% teacher-forced exact match on the arithmetic task (95% CI [0.0%, 0.0%]) and at 99.5% (CI [98.5%, 100.0%]) on the copying task. At the floor no further degradation can register; near the ceiling a large drop is visible but a confidence drop that leaves the argmax unchanged is not. Mean gold log-probability moves from −7.44 nats (CI [−7.53, −7.35]) on arithmetic to −0.83 nats (CI [−0.90, −0.76]) on copying and stays graded under single-head ablation in both regimes. On DistilGPT2 the floor recurs at 0.0% but copying accuracy is 90.0% (CI [85.5%, 94.0%]), so the near-ceiling example is specific to GPT-2 and the paper says so.

**With the corrected intervention and matched controls, the effect replicates but specificity does not.** The selected heads on GPT-2 are layer 0 heads 0, 7 and 10, layer 1 head 3, and layer 11 head 0 — four of five in the first two layers. Their held-out mean Δ is 0.412 nats (CI [0.353, 0.474]) against a random-head control mean of 0.029 (5th–95th percentile [−0.033, 0.113]) and a layer-matched control mean of 0.051 ([0.001, 0.098]), with 0 of 1,000 draws matching or exceeding on either, p = 0.001 each. Per-head ranking is highly stable across splits, Spearman ρ = 0.974 over all 144 heads. The specificity statistic is 0.139 nats against a random-control mean of 0.011 ([−0.130, 0.163]), p = 0.078 — a trend, not a result — and a pre-committed N = 500 rerun keeps the held-out separation (p = 0.001 each control) but drops specificity to 0.056 nats at p = 0.274. On DistilGPT2 the intervention-semantics and matched-control findings replicate (r = 0.001, 0/5 overlap; candidate Δ 0.586 s.e. CI [0.511, 0.661] against random 0.080 [−0.026, 0.231] and layer-matched 0.054 [−0.017, 0.128], p = 0.001 each; ρ = 0.957 over 72 heads) and specificity is 0.492 nats against a random-control mean of 0.023 ([−0.159, 0.222]) at p = 0.001 — the opposite of GPT-2's outcome on the same statistic. The author is explicit that the two models' candidate heads are different indices and that this is a second data point for the protocol, not evidence of a shared induction circuit.

**The overall result is a demonstration that each check independently changed the conclusion** — the naive hook selected disjoint heads, accuracy concealed different information at floor and near ceiling, and the held-out specificity contrast blocked a task-specificity claim that the larger GPT-2 run does not support.

## Limits and open problems they name
The paper names more self-limits than most in this folder. The claim is capped at the third rung of its own ladder and is not a mechanistic account. **Zero-ablation is off-distribution**: an exact zero produces an activation the model never saw, so an effect can mix the removal of a computation with a distribution shift; mean-ablation and resampling-based interventions would be the natural robustness check and were not run. Both models come from one architecture family and no unrelated architecture was tested. Task specificity is not robust on GPT-2, and no further control tasks or thresholds were tried. Candidate selection uses a **single** fixed 50/50 partition and the sensitivity of the candidate set to alternative random partitions was not tested — which is the sharpest hole given that split stability is one of the paper's selling points. The synthetic copying task is a minimal induction stimulus, not naturalistic behaviour. And the demonstration is deliberately small: one author, one protocol, two neighbouring checkpoints.

## What we take from it
1. **This is the first entry in the folder to satisfy the standing gate outright**, and that is the reason for the relevance-3 score. Runs 574, 575, 577, 578 and 579 each chased a per-unit status that is reproducible across resamples and matched on layer; here the unit is a head, the status is a held-out effect with a bootstrap interval, reproducibility is a Spearman correlation over *all* units rather than over the survivors, and the control is matched on **size and layer composition** with a stated reason for both exclusions. The transferable instrument is cheap: **build the null by resampling an already-computed per-unit effect vector, so a 1,000-draw matched control battery costs zero additional forward passes.** Our account should never claim a matched control is unaffordable again.
2. **The intervention's identity is a testable claim, and it is the first thing to test.** The paper's strongest finding is that a natural implementation of "ablate head h" does not ablate head h at all, because the output projection mixes heads before the block returns — and the two per-head effect vectors come back at r = 0.057 with a disjoint top five. Mechanical rule for us: **every intervention our account reports ships with an executable test on a tiny configuration asserting which tensor was modified and which were left bit-identical, and each per-unit effect we care about is measured under the intervention whose identity was asserted, not under the one whose name matched the intent** — because an intervention can be wrong in a way that still produces a plausible, well-ordered ranking.
3. **Print the ladder rung beside the claim.** A held-out effect that clears matched controls is a different object from a specific causal role, and the paper refuses the fourth rung on the strength of its own inconsistent specificity contrast. Mechanical rule for us: **every named unit in our account is labelled with the strongest rung its evidence supports — reproducible effect, effect clearing matched controls, effect specific to the target behaviour, or mechanistic role — and no rung is written from evidence gathered at the rung below; specificity requires the off-target task, not the magnitude of the on-target effect.**
4. **A metric censored at the floor or the ceiling is a blind instrument, and the check is one number.** Accuracy is 0.0% on one task and 99.5% on the other, so on the same examples it can neither see degradation nor see a confidence change; log-probability is graded in both. Mechanical rule: **any task we use to grade our account is reported with its measured accuracy floor and ceiling before any per-unit effect is quoted, and where the baseline sits within a stated margin of either, a continuous metric on the same examples is the primary number** — this is `arxiv-2604.05419`'s base-rate rule moved onto the evaluation side of the pipeline.
5. **The paper's own hole is the one to avoid copying.** Split stability is measured once, over a single fixed partition, and the sensitivity of the selected set to the partition is untested — which means "reproducible across resamples" is demonstrated at one resample. When our account makes the same claim, the resampling is the experiment: several partitions, the *set* overlap reported alongside the rank correlation, and the candidate set rebuilt each time.
