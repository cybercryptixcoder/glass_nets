## What they wanted, and why

Grokking is the delayed transition from memorising a small algorithmic task to generalising on it, and the paper's motivating complaint is that most accounts say what representation appears at the end but not what makes the transition happen *when* it does. Building on the author's own earlier finding that the weight trajectory in grokking occupies a very low-dimensional subspace, the question here is whether that subspace is stable under the optimisation dynamics — does curvature in the loss landscape kick the trajectory out of the directions it has learned — and whether the geometry carries any signal about *when* generalisation will arrive. The intended contribution is a predictive and causal account rather than a descriptive one: a geometric quantity that leads the behavioural event, plus interventions that test whether the quantity is a cause. It is a step toward treating delayed generalisation as a phase transition in learning dynamics rather than as a quirk of small tasks.

## What they studied

A 2-layer pre-norm Transformer encoder (~290k parameters, d_model 128, 4 heads, d_ff 256, GELU) trained on two-token inputs drawn from 0 to 96 with p = 97, predicting (a op b) mod 97. Six binary operations are used, four of which grok under the default hyperparameters (add, sub, mul, a²+b²) and two of which do not (a²+ab+b², a³+ab); the four grokking operations have mean 90%-accuracy steps between roughly 1,900 and 3,400. Default training is AdamW at lr 1e-3 with weight decay 1.0 (0.0 for the non-grokking controls), batch 512, gradient clipping 1.0, 50/50 split, up to 200k steps with early stopping at 98% test accuracy held three evaluations; only the *attention* weight matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O, per layer) are logged, every 100 steps. A "slow regime" (lr 5e-5, wd 0.1, 3 layers, beta2 0.999, grokking near 570k steps) provides a 200x timescale contrast, and a separate learning-rate sweep uses six half-decade rates from 3e-5 to 1e-2 across all six operations and three seeds (108 runs). The unit of analysis is a **direction in parameter space** at the granularity of whole attention matrices, together with a **local curvature scalar** of the loss landscape.

## How they did it

Three geometric tools are combined. (1) PCA on the flattened trajectory of weight *changes from initialisation* for each attention matrix, giving PC1% (the variance share of the first component) and an "execution manifold" defined as the affine subspace through the initial weights spanned by the top-K right singular vectors; the basis used for projections is the top-2 directions per weight matrix per layer, embedded at the correct offsets in the full ~290k-dimensional parameter vector and orthonormalised by QR (K = 16-24). (2) A **commutator defect**: take two independent mini-batches, apply their gradients in both orders with a fixed step size, and divide the norm of the difference between the two resulting parameter vectors by the product of the two gradient-step norms; to first order this is the Lie bracket of the gradient vector fields, i.e. a Hessian-free local curvature probe. Nine samples are taken per measurement point and the median is reported. (3) Projection of the commutator vectors onto the PCA basis, yielding the residual fraction rho (the share of the curvature vector lying outside the manifold), plus a **random-subspace control**: for every commutator vector, the same projection is computed onto five freshly drawn random K-dimensional bases and the ratio exec/random is the diagnostic. A converse analysis correlates the actual per-step trajectory step with twelve commutator vectors and compares the mean absolute cosine to the isotropic baseline for that dimension. The causal section then intervenes on the *gradient*: after step 500, the component of the gradient along the manifold is kept and the orthogonal component is attenuated by a strength s swept from 0 to 1, with an equal-rank random projection as control. A second intervention injects additive kicks aligned with the commutator direction, at amplitudes 50-500x the gradient step norm, recomputed every 50 steps, against random-orthogonal kicks of equal magnitude. Finally, the singular values of W_Q and W_K are tracked to find what drives the commutator, and three independent bases (weight SVD, displacement SVD, gradient SVD) are used to check that the result is not a basis artifact.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **geometric/dynamical-systems account**: memorisation confines the trajectory to a low-dimensional slow manifold, curvature accumulates in the normal bundle of that manifold, and generalisation is escape from a metastable regime as the realised direction becomes stiff. The explanation object is not a circuit or a feature but a small set of scalars attached to directions — PC1%, the residual fraction rho, the exec/random projection ratio, the commutator defect and its onset step, the trajectory–curvature alignment, and the leading singular-value gap — plus a **temporal ordering claim** (curvature onset leads generalisation) and a **spectral mechanism** (a transient near-degeneracy of the top two singular values creates orientation instability, and symmetry breaking resolves it). It is explicitly partial: the author states the available geometric signals do not combine into a composite diagnostic that predicts *which* run will generalise, only that the landscape is reorganising. Two predictive claims are quantitative — the lead time is claimed to follow a power law in the grokking timescale, and the defect-onset event is claimed to precede generalisation in every grokking run measured.

## How they judged success

Six criteria, in ascending strength: (i) PC1% reported per operation and compared against a random-walk null model, with z-scores above that null (5-20 sigma); (ii) the invariance claim rho ≈ 1 not being a dimensionality artifact — graded by exec/random projection ratio above 1 across four operations, and re-checked at K = 16 and K = 32 as well as 24; (iii) the non-triviality of the curvature separation — grokking runs reach 500-2000x their early baseline while non-grokking controls reach 30-50x, with an explicit statement that the groups are **not** separable before the transition; (iv) statistical significance of the temporal ordering — 12/12 grokking runs, a one-sided sign test at p = 2^-12 ≈ 2.4e-4, plus a log-log regression of lead time on grok step (alpha = 1.27 ± 0.03, R² = 0.97, n = 43); (v) causal attribution by dose-response intervention with a rank-matched random control, grokking success counted as a fraction of twelve seeded runs at full suppression and grok step measured otherwise; (vi) regime robustness — replication in the slow regime 200x slower, across six operations, three seeds, and the full six-point learning-rate sweep. There is no held-out-prediction test: nothing is trained on some runs and evaluated on unseen ones.

## What they found

The trajectory is rank-1: one principal component carries 68-83% of variance in every grokking condition. The curvature is almost entirely transverse: the residual fraction sits at about 1.000 within numerical precision across all 36 conditions (six operations, two weight-decay settings, three seeds), with both the execution and the random projections small in absolute terms (proj/full below 0.05) — so the meaningful number is the ratio, which is 1.8-2.9x in favour of the execution basis over a random subspace of equal rank. Curvature magnitude, though, blows up: grokking runs reach 500-2000x their early-training base-rate defect against 30-50x for the two operations that never generalise, with zero overlap in total growth but complete overlap early in training. Defect onset precedes the 90%-accuracy point by 600-1,600 steps (mean 1,117) in all twelve grokking runs; the lead grows super-linearly with the timescale, so the lead *fraction* improves from 24% at the fastest tested rate to 95% at the slowest. In the slow regime the same ordering holds twice out of two runs with a lead of roughly 558k steps and a normalised lead of about 0.55 against 0.38 in the fast regime. Causally, suppressing the orthogonal part of the gradient delays grokking monotonically at intermediate strengths (600-800 steps, 20-25% above baseline, at s = 0.75) and prevents it entirely at s = 1.0 (0/12 seeds), while a random rank-16 projection at intermediate strength does essentially nothing — though at s = 1.0 the random projection also kills grokking, so *any* 16-dimensional confinement is too strong. Boosting curvature is inert: 27/27 kick-injected runs generalise at about the same step whether the kicks are commutator-aligned or random-orthogonal. The proposed mechanism is spectral: the second-to-third spectral gap of W_Q/W_K shrinks through memorisation and declines 15-111x (mean 40x) before grokking in 12 of 12 runs but in only 1 of 12 matched no-weight-decay controls; the top two singular values pass through a near-degeneracy (gap minimum 0.002-0.02); the commutator then spikes; then one mode dominates and W_Q and W_K align, and accuracy rises. A representative addition run orders these events at steps 1400, 1700, 2000, 2700, 2800 and 3100. The author also notes, against the general spectral-edge framework of the companion work, that update-Gram spectral concentration is *higher* in memorising runs than in grokking runs (gap ratio 2.83 against 1.40), an inversion attributed to memorisation gradients being strongly rank-1-aligned.

## Limits and open problems they name

Everything is measured on 2-3 layer, ~290k-parameter transformers on modular arithmetic mod 97; generalisation to language models is asserted as plausible via "preliminary" Dyck and SCAN experiments that are not reported here. The author states plainly that the geometric signals do not discriminate grokking from non-grokking operations in advance: rho is identically ~1 everywhere, alignment is noisy and overlapping, and defect magnitude separates the groups only retrospectively. Curvature growth alone is insufficient even for the grokking-capable operations — the same tasks trained without weight decay show comparable defect acceleration and do not generalise — so regularisation pressure is a separate ingredient the geometry does not contain. The commutator costs four forward-backward passes per sample and is called hard to scale. No closed-form theory of defect accumulation is offered. The interpretation is hedged throughout: "invariant", "transversely decoupled" and "integrability" are stated to be empirical and finite-window, not the dynamical-systems meanings.

## What we take from it

A **method** worth stealing: the commutator defect is a curvature probe that needs no Hessian, costs a handful of forward-backward passes, and is defined at the level of the update rule rather than the loss surface — exactly the profile of instrument we need for intervention-shaped measurements on models too large for eigen-decompositions. A **measure**: our folder has repeatedly reported a dominant trajectory direction without a matched-random control, and this entry's exec/random ratio is the cheapest rubric for that — if the "meaningful" subspace does not beat a random subspace of its own rank by a clear margin on the quantity you care about, the finding is rank rather than direction. Three **warnings** carry directly. First, the paper's own central invariance number, rho ≈ 1.000, is a statement about a 290,000-dimensional space containing a 24-dimensional subspace and carries almost no information by itself; the author concedes this and only the ratio rescues it — so we report the ratio, never the raw residual fraction. Second, the causal result is asymmetric in a way we should expect to meet: suppressing a component along a direction hurts with a clean dose-response while *adding* perturbation along the orthogonal/curvature direction does nothing, which is the standard signature of a direction being a *route* rather than a *variable of the mechanism*. Third, the random control at full strength also kills the behaviour, which bounds what the specificity claim means — the effect being specific to the learned basis is established at partial strength only, where the perturbation is a nudge rather than a cage. A **framing** worth adopting for our reading of weights: the interesting quantity during a plateau is not how much structure exists but whether that structure is becoming unstable, and the singular-value gap is the smallest observable that expresses it. A **carried question** for the folder: this paper measures curvature in the direction *orthogonal* to the solution trajectory and finds it necessary; run 720's drift paper measures the direction the optimiser actually integrates and finds it 25x better aligned with accumulated updates than with single gradients — the two are consistent only if the transverse curvature is what the accumulated motion is escaping, which is a testable claim neither paper runs.
