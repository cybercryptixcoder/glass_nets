## What they wanted, and why
Automated circuit discovery works on GPT-2-sized models and stops working somewhere around two billion parameters: activation patching runs out of memory, exhaustive enumeration is exponential, and native polysemanticity means the candidate feature graph is full of features that are reused across many co-active paths. The authors want a discovery pipeline whose search cost is polynomial rather than exponential and whose candidate graph is sparse enough to reason about, so that the same procedure that finds an induction head in a 124M model can be pointed at a 70B one. The larger goal is the usual one - weight-level explanations of production models for safety and auditing before those models are deployed in regulated settings. Their stated barrier list (fails past ~2B, exponential ablation cost, polysemanticity, no standard way to separate causal from correlational structure) is a fair summary of the field's own diagnosis, and the paper's contribution is aimed at the first two.

## What they studied
Nine models across three families and four orders of magnitude: GPT-2 small/medium/large (117M/345M/774M), Pythia-1.4B/2.8B/6.9B, Llama-7B/13B/70B (Table I). Cross-layer transcoders are trained per model on 100M tokens of RedPajama with expansion factor m/d = 8, giving dictionaries from 6,144 features (GPT-2 small) to 65,536 (Llama-70B), and sparsity k between 32 and 128. Tasks: modular arithmetic (a+b mod 113), parity on binary strings up to length 20, sorting 5 integers, plus WinoGrande, HellaSwag and a factual QA set. The unit of analysis is a **transcoder feature** as a graph vertex, with weighted directed edges between features in adjacent layers - there are no attention heads in the object at all.

## How they did it
Four stages, each doing one job. (1) Train a cross-layer transcoder per layer with three loss terms: reconstruction of the hidden state through a TopK-ReLU encoder and a decoder, a sigmoid prediction head that forecasts the *next layer's* feature activations from this layer's, and an L1 penalty (Eqs. 1-4, Algorithm 1, AdamW). (2) Build an attribution graph per input by scoring every feature-to-feature link with a gradient-times-activation product (Eq. 5, presented as following integrated gradients), aggregated across positions and layers, computed layer by layer so the full gradient graph is never held in memory. (3) Coarsen that graph into a multi-resolution hierarchy: at each level compute the normalised Laplacian, spectral-cluster the vertices and merge each cluster into a supernode, repeating until the graph is small (Definition 1, Eq. 6). (4) Search the hierarchy top-down with a graph-attention network that scores each supernode's probability of being in the circuit (Eqs. 7-8), descending into the supernodes it likes; the GNN is trained on ground-truth circuits from exhaustive search on small models or from expert annotation. Discovered features are then pruned by a necessity test (ablate a feature, keep it only if the loss change clears a threshold, Eq. 9) and scored for sufficiency (accuracy of a circuit-only model over the full model, Eq. 10, threshold 0.9). The complexity argument (Theorem 1) is that a level with branching factor b needs O(b) decisions, depth is O(log n), and edge verification is O(n^2), giving O(n^2 log n) against O(2^n) for enumeration. Baselines are ACDC, attribution patching, sparse probing and brute-force enumeration where it fits.

## What counts as an explanation here
A sparse attribution subgraph over transcoder features, plus a causal stamp on it: a set of features and weighted edges that reproduces most of the behaviour when kept and whose individual members matter when removed. It is a partial structural object by the authors' own framing - the explanation covers MLP computation routed through transcoder features and explicitly omits attention, so for a task like coreference, where they concede attention is central, the object is a fragment. It predicts that a small feature set is behaviourally load-bearing; it does not predict what any feature computes beyond the modular-arithmetic case where they report sinusoidal features at frequencies matching divisors of 113.

## How they judged success
Behavioural preservation φ(C) = accuracy of the circuit-restricted model divided by full-model accuracy (Eq. 10), plus node and edge counts, wall-clock time, a compression ratio, "concept purity", a modularity ratio of intra- to inter-module edge weight, and description length in bits. Transfer is scored with a Jaccard over edge sets between two models' circuits for the same task, with edges aligned across models by activation correlation (Eq. 11). Component ablations remove the hierarchy, the GNN, the cross-layer prediction head, or the causal validation stage and report preservation and relative runtime (Table V). Scalability is reported as memory, hours and features/edges per second at four parameter scales (Table VI).

## What they found
Discovered circuits are small and preserve behaviour well on the algorithmic tasks: 49 nodes / 341 edges at 0.97 preservation on GPT-2 small in 180s, against exhaustive search finding 47 nodes at 0.98 in 14,400s and ACDC 52 nodes at 0.95 in 2,340s. ACDC runs out of memory at Pythia-1.4B; the hierarchical method returns 124 nodes at 0.91 there, 156 at 0.89 on Pythia-2.8B, 198 at 0.87 on Llama-7B and 347 nodes / 8,923 edges at 0.82 on Llama-70B in 28,400s (Table II). Natural-language circuits are far larger and weaker: on HellaSwag, 456 nodes at 0.79 for GPT-2 large rising to 734 nodes at 0.74 for Llama-7B; factual QA is the most compact and most modular (234 nodes, 0.88, modularity 0.78 at Pythia-2.8B; 489 nodes, 0.81, 0.69 at Llama-70B) and the authors read that as consistent with locally stored factual associations (Table III). Transfer coefficients (Table IV) run 0.43-0.73 cross-family and reach 0.82 for Llama-7B to Llama-70B on modular arithmetic, with factual QA next (0.49-0.74) and WinoGrande lowest (0.38-0.67); the paper reads the within-family edge as evidence that tokenizer and training recipe shape topology. The ablation table says the hierarchy buys mostly speed (preservation 0.91 -> 0.89 on modular arithmetic at 0.3x time) while the GNN buys a little of both (0.88, 0.7x), and dropping the cross-layer head hurts quality most (0.84 on modular arithmetic, 0.76 on QA); causal validation costs 1.4x time and changes neither preservation column.

## Limits and open problems they name
Attention is unmodelled, so circuits are necessarily incomplete wherever token-to-token routing matters. 15-20% of transcoder reconstruction variance is unaccounted for and the paper calls it "dark matter" and says it does not know whether it holds structured computation. The causal validation is circular by their own admission: the same ablation family used to select features is used to certify them, so a feature can look necessary because ablating it breaks something unrelated. The GNN needs labelled circuits, which is why the pipeline is cheap exactly where labels were expensive to produce. And a 347-node, 8,923-edge object for Llama-70B may exceed what a human can read, defeating the point. Future work listed: joint attention-MLP circuits, characterising the reconstruction residual, label-efficient GNN training, and confidence intervals across runs.

## What we take from it
- **A warning, and the sharpest one here: the headline table's own numbers do not survive arithmetic.** Llama-70B's circuit is reported as 347 nodes and 8,923 edges (Table II) - a mean degree of about 51 per node - on a candidate graph that also reports sparsity and a compression ratio, and no degree or density statistic appears anywhere in the paper to reconcile the two. Either the edge counts are counted over a different object (positions expanded, or the pre-coarsened graph) or the graph is not sparse; the text does not say which. Read Table II's edges as a reported quantity we cannot reconstruct, not as evidence about circuit sparsity. The standing rule this buys us: a sparsity claim is a claim about a degree distribution, and if a paper reports nodes and edges without a density it has not made one.
- **A method worth borrowing: coarsen before you search.** Spectral clustering into supernodes, a learned policy over supernodes, and descent only into what the policy likes is the same shape as our own probe schedule (screen broadly, refine where the screen fires) and it makes the depth of refinement an explicit, set-able parameter. The part we can reuse without the GNN is the multi-resolution ladder itself - it makes "at what resolution is this circuit found" an answerable question rather than an implicit choice of method.
- **A measure we should adopt: the transfer coefficient with its cross-family spread stated on the same line.** 0.82 within Llama 7B -> 70B and 0.38 GPT-2 -> Llama on the same task is the useful unit of comparison, and it agrees with run 764's base-vs-instruct result that structure survives fine-tuning better than it survives an architecture change.
- **A framing to refuse: "projected years for exhaustive approaches".** The comparison at 70B is against an algorithm that has already OOMed five orders of magnitude earlier, so the polynomial-vs-exponential plot (Fig. 4) is a plot of two extrapolations; the only measured speed comparison at a scale where both methods ran is 180s against 2,340s at GPT-2 small.
- **A gap and its cost, and the most transferable point in the entry: an interpretability pipeline that omits a whole component type is not a partial pipeline, it is a pipeline for a different model.** The authors say this themselves for coreference, and it is the cleanest published statement in this folder that the unit menu (here: transcoder features, MLP only) is an assumption baked in before any measurement, not a detail of implementation.
- **A prediction we can test on ourselves:** if the transfer coefficients are descriptive of real shared structure, they should degrade smoothly with family distance rather than jumping, and a white-box model fitted on a small model in a family should predict the *size* of a circuit it would find in a large one within the reported sublinear growth. Table II is the only place in the folder where circuit node count is reported against parameter count for six points in one method, which makes it the natural calibration set for that check.
