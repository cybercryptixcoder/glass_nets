# Signatures of semantic search in the activations of large language models

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.35599
- **Authors / organisation:** Luke Leckie, Peter M. Todd, Jacob G. Foster; Indiana University Bloomington (Informatics, Cognitive Science, Center for Possible Minds); Foster also Santa Fe Institute
- **Date:** 2026-09-29 (v2; v1 2026-09-28)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35599
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Humans recalling a category list (the semantic fluency task) do not sample uniformly: they exhaust a semantic neighbourhood and then jump to a new one, and foraging theory plus neuroscience say the two regimes — exploiting a depleting patch and exploring for a fresh one — come with distinct internal signatures (slower responses and hippocampal activity at switches, ramping activity the longer one stays in a patch). LLMs produce the same clustered-then-switching lists, and no one had asked whether the *internal states* of a model carry the corresponding exploit/explore signature rather than only the surface pattern. The authors want to know whether the model's middle layers hold a readable, predictive and causally usable representation of "stay in this category" versus "go find another", framed through a lens-style read-out of concept-level activation rather than a classification probe on the residual stream. The larger ambition is a bridge: if the same search dynamics appear in a network whose weights were never selected for foraging, the forage/exploit distinction is a candidate general description of search over structured representations.

## What they studied
Five open-weight instruction-tuned models from 2B to 9B parameters: Gemma-2-9B (42 layers), Gemma-2-2B (25), Llama-3.1-8B (32), Llama-3.2-3B (28) and Qwen2.5-7B (28). Every second residual-stream layer from layer 2 to near the final block is instrumented, reported on a fractional depth axis where 0 is the input and 1 the readout. The task is the semantic fluency task: the model is asked for as many animals as it can name in a comma-separated list, sampled with temperature 0.9 and top-p 0.95, capped at 200 tokens, prompted through its own chat template with no system prompt. Categories come from an extended Troyer scheme in which one animal belongs to several categories at once, and a switch is scored when a named item shares no category label with the item before it. The unit of analysis is three-fold and deliberately layered: the token-level next-token distribution, the **concept-level activation** recovered by a Jacobian lens (an item's activation at a middle layer is its tendency to be verbalised), and, in the second study, the residual-stream state itself as a vector.

## How they did it
Study 1 has four steps. First, compare final-layer next-token probability for items that participate in a switch against items that stay inside a cluster, at the position just before emission. Second, apply pre-computed per-model Jacobian lenses (taken from Neuronpedia; Llama-3.2-3B has none, so that model gets no lens experiments) to middle layers and repeat the comparison on item activations, tracking activation across a window of lags before and after emission. Third, define the **J-space** — the set of concepts with the top-25 strongest lens activations at a layer — and compute the **in-category supply**: how many animals belonging to the category currently being named are present in the J-space and still unverbalised. Test whether the probability of switching rises as that supply falls, and crucially compare it against the depletion of the raw remaining category pool (how many unfetched animals the category still holds in principle), so that "the model is running out of *representational* options" is separated from "the category really is running out of members". Bootstrapped supply variation also includes a version in which the whole list is old, to remove mere list-position effects. Lens size is swept at 5, 15 and 25. Fourth, read category-related labels (single-token words such as a material, a habitat or a setting, assigned to the extended categories) out of the lens and test whether their activation rises when the model is clustering inside that category, whether it ramps in anticipation of a switch *into* it and spikes at the switch, and whether non-target labels move oppositely. All of this is then made causal with two families of steering vectors: an **item-level** vector built by averaging, over the member tokens of a target category, the normalised unembedding row pushed back through the lens, and a **category-label** vector built the same way from one abstract label. Steering is applied only from a chosen token position at which the model is clustering and the target category is not the current item's, with the injection scaled by that layer's mean residual norm, coefficient 0.5 (0.25 for the easily disrupted Qwen), and the outcome is whether the first concept in the continuation belongs to the target category.

Study 2 drops the lens and asks whether switching has a *generic* direction. Separately at the anticipatory position (the token before a switch) and the event position (the switch token itself), each seed's residual states at layer L are reduced by PCA to 30 components and fed to an L2 logistic regression with category-disjoint five-fold cross-validation — no category appears in both train and test — and the held-out AUC is the layer's score. The classifier's coefficients are then projected back through the PCA loadings and normalised to give one **generic switching direction** per layer, which is injected from anticipatory positions at coefficient ±0.5 (±0.1 for Qwen). The control is the discriminating one: a random Gaussian vector is Gram–Schmidt-orthogonalised against the switching direction and injected at the identical norm and coefficient, and switch rates under steering are compared with switch rates under that matched random perturbation by Fisher exact test over pooled counts. Ramping is tested as the association between how many items remain in a cluster and how strongly the residual loads on the anticipatory direction. Each model-layer cell is iterated over 100 seeds; significance treats a seed as the unit of replication, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across layers.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **read-out plus a direction** — three objects at once, each partial. The lens gives a feature-like read-out at middle layers (a ranked list of concepts with activation strengths, whose composition is the explanation of "what the model is about to say"), the depletion and label-ramp statistics give a *predictive statement about the next discrete decision*, and the two steering vectors give a causal handle. The paper is explicit that the explanation is behavioural and geometric rather than mechanistic: it locates *when and where* exploration signals appear and shows they move behaviour, but it does not claim to identify the circuit that computes the switch decision, nor to decompose the generic direction into components. It predicts in a limited but real sense — the anticipatory direction's logistic score is a switching probability that is tested out of category.

## How they judged success
Linear regressions with Wald tests on per-seed paired differences for every activation comparison, one- or two-sided as pre-stated, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across layers and lag values; pooled point-biserial correlations between switch outcome and supply, tested against zero with one-sample t-tests, reported per depth bin; a direct comparison between the J-space supply correlation and the raw-pool correlation in the same model and bin; held-out AUC from category-disjoint cross-validation for the classifier; and, for both steering families, Fisher exact tests of steered against **magnitude-matched, direction-orthogonal noise**, at every layer, with the test direction fixed in advance by the sign of the coefficient. Robustness for the depletion result comes from re-running at J-space sizes 5 and 15, and for the generic direction from the orthogonalised-noise control; the lens-family results are additionally required to beat the *raw pool* depletion, which is the within-paper null that matters most.

## What they found
Switching is preceded by elevated uncertainty at the token level: next-token probability is significantly lower for switch-participating items in every model (chi-squared at least 32.39, p below 0.0001). The same asymmetry is visible in mid-layer lens activations — both cluster and switch items activate in anticipation of their emission, but the rise is weaker for switches, and from layer 8 up the difference is significant in all lensed models (chi-squared at least 98.17), growing toward late layers. The depletion result is the centrepiece: switch probability rises sharply as the in-category J-space supply falls, with pooled correlations between about −0.31 and −0.50 depending on model and depth bin, and the effect is significantly stronger than raw-pool depletion in all models except Qwen (largest F at least 19.94), with Qwen failing the contrast outright at its strongest-effect bin (F 6.139, p 0.061). Category labels behave as a patch marker: their activation is higher while clustering inside the category, **ramps in anticipation of a switch into it and spikes at the switch** (Gemma-9B coefficient 1.712 at lag 0 against 0.158 one item earlier), and unrelated labels rise weakly too but collapse at the switch — read by the authors as competition among candidate categories with one winner. Causally, item-level steering pushes Gemma-9B's probability of switching into a target category to 0.68 at the final tested layer and category-label steering to 0.45 at layer 32, with label steering strongest at middle to mid-late depths and item steering still climbing at the top. Generically, switching is decodable out of category before it happens (Gemma-9B anticipatory AUC 0.72 at layer 26) and more strongly at the event (0.87 at layer 16), the anticipatory loading ramps as a cluster is exhausted in all five models, and steering at Gemma-9B layer 26 moves the switch rate from a 0.29 baseline to 0.70 when positive and 0.07 when negative, both far outside the orthogonalised-noise control (which sat at 0.29 to 0.37 in that layer's sweep). Negative steering is the weaker arm: Llama-3.1-8B's negative curve is the least effective, and the authors name that model's lower susceptibility as unexplained.

## Limits and open problems they name
The task is one tightly controlled list-generation prompt, so whether any of this survives in naturalistic discourse or in search over a solution space is untested and left as future work. Potency is selective — layers and coefficients had to be chosen, one model was steered at a quarter strength to avoid disruption, and the authors cannot say why Llama-3.1-8B resists. The models are small relative to frontier systems, and the lens family is borrowed and pre-computed (with one model missing a lens entirely, so it is excluded from all lens experiments), which means the lens's own faithfulness is a load-bearing assumption rather than something measured here.

## What we take from it
**A measurement we should copy, and a discipline we should copy harder.** The measurement is the **supply contrast**: whenever our model says "this internal state is running low on X", we must show that the depletion of the *representational* supply predicts the next decision better than the depletion of the *ground-truth* supply in the same state, because otherwise the finding is trivially inherited from the data (the model really was running out of animals). Here that contest is run per model and per depth bin, and one model loses it — which is exactly the shape of result that makes the other four credible. The discipline is the **orthogonalised, norm-matched random control**: the generic-direction result is not "steering changed behaviour" but "steering changed behaviour relative to a perturbation of the same size that cannot be along the object under test", and because switch rate is compared per layer, the control reveals the honest picture — the effect is banded, strong in middle to late-middle layers, and near-null at the extremes. Both are cheap. What we do *not* take: the framing. The paper's terminal object is "the model represents explore versus exploit", which is a claim about content; our model needs a claim about mechanism, and the paper itself concedes it never localises the switch computation — the direction is fitted from a decoder and could equally be the decoder's way of describing the decision after it is made. Two specific caveats for the write-up: the anticipatory direction is derived from the same data it is used to steer (the PCA-loading projection reuses the lifecycle of the classifier, and the held-out AUC validates only the decoder, not the direction), and the ±0.5 coefficient on mean residual norm is an uncalibrated strength in the sense of our run-845 entry. Warning we should carry forward: a single fitted direction can sit at a **layer band**, and the paper's own tables show the effect vanish at both ends — any single-layer site claim we publish should come with its layer sweep.
