## What they wanted, and why
Sparse-autoencoder dictionaries are unstable: two runs on identical data with different seeds produce materially different atoms, so "this atom reappeared" has been used as a cheap proxy for "this atom is a real unit of computation". A recent line of work proposes interventions that make dictionaries more stable, and the archetypal-SAE paper (Fel et al., 2025) reports that constraining atoms to the convex hull of data points produces more similar dictionaries at the end of training. This paper asks whether that claimed fix actually stabilises anything, or whether the compared runs were never truly independent. The authors frame the stakes as the validity of every downstream claim that rests on stability — that a concept is a real feature rather than a training artefact of one run. The larger goal is to put SAEs back inside the older dictionary-learning tradition, where "did the solution converge?" is a question about optimisation dynamics rather than an end-of-training snapshot.

## What they studied
Two settings, both deliberately minimal: DinoV2 patch embeddings from a single image class (rabbits), i.e. the reference Colab's own example, and the layer-6 residual stream of Pythia-160m-deduped trained on Pile text (768-dim, context 1,024). The unit of analysis is the **decoder dictionary** — a set of atoms compared across two independently trained runs. Architectures: classical TopK SAE, archetypal TopK SAE (full convex-hull projection), the same with the projection ablated, and a classical TopK SAE with its decoder initialised from the k-means centroids. All runs use a 4,096-atom dictionary, k = 20, Adam at 3e-4, batch 2,048; the trajectory experiment runs 50M tokens, the ablations run 20 epochs following the reference implementation.

## How they did it
Four conditions, two of them ablations designed to split "the archetypal constraint" from "a shared starting point". (1) A **stability-trajectory diagnostic**: rather than score dictionaries once at the end, they log inter-run dictionary distance every 100 steps and plot it against tokens seen, using a cheap mean-minimum-cosine distance for the trajectory and the normalised cosine Hungarian distance for endpoint numbers. (2) **Ablation 1** disables the convex-hull projection — W frozen, the residual-norm bound lifted, Λ allowed to become a free dictionary matrix initialised to the centroid set — while keeping the k-means initialisation. (3) **Ablation 2** does the converse: an ordinary classical TopK SAE whose decoder is initialised from the same k-means centroids, with no archetypal constraint at any point. (4) They also train the full archetypal SAE with a **randomly drawn** W and residual offsets (centroid set still fixed and centred) so that the two seeds genuinely start apart, making the convex-hull constraint the only structural difference from the classical condition. Seeding is held consistent so that differences are attributable to initialisation rather than encoder-weight noise. Before all of this they apply a **centering correction** — subtract the activation mean before k-means and from the decoder bias — and check what it does to the geometry. Appendix B audits the public code of the papers they compare against, reading the specific lines that fix or vary the global RNG state.

## What counts as an explanation here
A measurement-theoretic correction rather than a model of anything: the paper's product is a *distinction* (stability vs stabilization), a *diagnostic* (the trajectory plot), and a *preprocessing condition under which the metric means what it is called* (centering). It is a claim about the validity of an instrument, and it is meant to be complete for the narrow setting it tests: it does not attempt to explain what any atom means. Its predictions are testable and specific — a method that merely preserves a shared starting point will show a flat distance trajectory, and a method that genuinely converges will show distance falling from a non-zero start.

## How they judged success
Endpoint stability = normalised cosine Hungarian distance between the two dictionaries (lower is more similar), reported alongside R² reconstruction so that any stability gain can be priced against reconstruction cost. Trajectory stability = mean minimum cosine distance per logging step. The two ablations are judged by whether they reproduce the full archetypal condition's endpoint score: if disabling the mechanism leaves stability unchanged, or if merely copying the initialisation achieves the same score, the mechanism is not the cause. A code audit (Table 1) tabulates, per recent stability paper, whether random initialisation and random batch order are varied across seeds. Table 2 quantifies the metric confound directly: mean pairwise cosine similarity among k-means centroids before and after centering.

## What they found
The claimed stability is an initialisation artefact. Archetypal runs share a deterministic k-means centroid matrix and start W at the identity, so the two decoders are *identical at step 0* — inter-run distance is zero before any gradient step, and the flat-at-zero trajectory is the absence of a test rather than evidence of convergence. When the test is actually administered (random init inside the archetypal parameterisation, starting distance about 0.88), the constraint slows convergence rather than causing it: after 50M tokens the archetypal random-init condition sits at about 0.35 against about 0.17 for classical TopK, roughly half the rate. Both ablations agree: disabling the convex hull *improves* endpoint stability over the full archetypal SAE (vision 0.068 vs 0.243; Pythia 0.112 vs 0.170), and a plain classical SAE initialised from the same centroids matches the ablated model numerically in both settings (0.068 / 0.112), while both beat the randomly initialised classical SAE (0.433 / 0.200). The full archetypal SAE has the **worst** R² of the four conditions in both settings (0.542 vs 0.570–0.573 vision; 0.758 vs 0.914–0.937 Pythia), so the constraint buys neither reconstruction nor stabilisation here. Separately, the metric: k-means centroids on uncentered activations have mean pairwise cosine similarity 0.362 (LLM) and 0.298 (vision), which collapses to about zero after centering — a 0.30–0.36 positive bias that makes *any* method whose atoms stay near the data mean look stable, because cosine distance is measured from the origin and a mean offset dominates all directions. The paper also records that the archetypal-SAE paper states element-wise standardisation while neither the public Colab nor the Overcomplete library performs centering of any kind — and argues that per-coordinate rescaling is itself unjustified for residual streams, since the storage basis is arbitrary under linear reparameterisation.

## Limits and open problems they name
Hyperparameters are inherited from the reference implementation with no sweep, so the archetypal constraint might help in other regimes; the vision arm is one image class and the language arm one layer of one 160M model, and longer training could shift the ordering. They explicitly do not claim the archetypal construction is worthless — bounded atom norms and a geometric reading of atoms as combinations of real data points may still be valuable for visualisation or prototype alignment. They decline to adjudicate the open conflict in the literature over whether TopK SAEs are more or less stable than L1 SAEs, pointing at differing dead-latent rates as a candidate explanation.

## What we take from it
A warning and a protocol. The warning: **endpoint agreement between two runs is not evidence of convergence unless you can show where those runs started**, and this folder has leaned on exactly that shortcut in several "the direction survived a rerun" claims. The protocol is three cheap additions to any stability comparison we publish: (a) log the inter-run distance from step 0, and treat a flat trajectory at or near zero as "no test performed"; (b) run the initialisation ablation in *both* directions — remove the mechanism while keeping the init, and grant the init to the baseline — because the two together are what identify the cause, and here they cost one extra training run each; (c) center before computing any cosine-similarity metric on residual-stream activations, and report the pre-centering bias, since an uncentered mean can manufacture a third of a unit of apparent agreement for free. Two further transfers: the stability/stabilization split maps directly onto our own vocabulary, so a probe that reproduces under a fixed seed should be called *reproducible by construction* and not *convergent*; and the code-audit habit — reading the RNG-seeding lines of a paper's public repo rather than trusting the methods paragraph — is how "two independent runs" is verified instead of assumed, which is also the practical resolution of the paper/code preprocessing discrepancy it documents.
