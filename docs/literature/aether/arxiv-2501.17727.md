## What they wanted, and why

Dictionary learning on model activations is judged by aggregate scores — how well an explanation explains a latent's firing pattern, how little reconstruction error the dictionary carries. The question here is whether those scores can do the one thing an evaluation metric must do: tell a model that learned something from a model that learned nothing. The authors take the sanity-check tradition from saliency maps, where a network with randomised weights is the null model, and apply it to sparse autoencoders trained on transformer residual streams. If a dictionary fitted on a randomly initialised network scores the same as one fitted on a trained network, then a high score is evidence about the data and the architecture, not about learned computation. The larger goal is a calibration of the field's own instruments: the authors want routine null baselines and a measure of feature *abstractness* added to the standard metric suite.

## What they studied

Pythia models from 70M to 6.9B parameters (Biderman et al.), residual-stream activations only, with SAEs trained per layer (every layer for models under 410M, every second layer for 1B, every fourth layer for 6.9B). The unit of analysis is the SAE latent — 100 sampled latents per trained SAE for the auto-interpretability scoring, and all latents for the aggregate evaluation curves. Five underlying-model variants are compared: the trained model; a re-randomised model whose every parameter, embeddings included, is drawn from a Gaussian matched in mean and variance to that trained weight matrix; a re-randomised model with the trained embedding and unembedding held fixed; Pythia's own `step0` initialisation checkpoint; and a negative control that is the trained model with each token's embedding replaced at inference by i.i.d. standard Gaussian noise, so the same token never gets the same vector. SAEs are k-sparse (TopK) with expansion factor 64 and k = 32, trained on 100M RedPajama tokens with a 10M-token activation buffer, with a 1B-token replication on a subset and hyperparameter sweeps from expansion 16 to 128 and k of 16 and 32.

## How they did it

For every (model, variant, layer) triple, train an SAE on residual-stream activations from the same token stream, then evaluate on four standard axes: reconstruction quality (cosine similarity between activation and reconstruction, explained variance, L1 norm of latents), loss recovered (the cross-entropy increase when activations are replaced by their reconstructions, divided by the increase when they are replaced by zeros), auto-interpretability, and a new token-distribution entropy statistic. Explanations are generated and scored by the "fuzzing" protocol of Paulo et al. — a language model is shown positive and negative examples of a latent's firing, marked up, and asked which examples the explanation correctly delimits — scored by AUROC and computed with Meta-Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct-AWQ-INT4 as the explainer and judge, using the EleutherAI delphi implementation; detection scoring and the cheaper fuzzing variant are both reported, and fuzzing is the primary because it is known to track the expensive simulation score. The entropy statistic is computed per latent as the entropy of its total activation over token IDs across its maximum-activating examples — a latent firing on one token ID has near-zero entropy, one spread over many IDs has high entropy. Finally they build toy models to test the two plausible explanations for the result: they generate superposed data following Sharkey et al. (512 ground-truth features in 256 dimensions, correlated coefficients, an average of 5 active), pass it through a randomly initialised two-layer ReLU MLP, train SAEs on the inputs and on the outputs separately, and compare Pareto frontiers of explained variance against sparsity for superposed versus Gaussian-matched controls; the same comparison is repeated on GloVe vectors, Pythia embedding matrices, and their images under a random MLP. Ground-truth recovery is measured by mean max cosine similarity between decoder directions and generating features, with the L1 penalty swept to find the recoverable band.

## What counts as an explanation here

The explanation is the SAE latent plus its machine-generated natural-language description, and the account's validity is supposed to be carried by the score attached to that description. The paper does not propose a new form of explanation; it takes the standard form — a feature dictionary, described in text, validated by AUROC — and asks what the validation certifies. Its own positive contribution is a diagnostic quantity rather than a new account: token-distribution entropy stands in for the abstractness of a feature, since a genuine semantic feature fires across many different token IDs and a token-shaped artifact fires on one. The authors are explicit that they are pricing existing instruments, not building a replacement, and they do not claim the dictionaries on trained models fail to capture anything.

## How they judged success

The single comparison that carries everything is trained versus null, read across the four axes as curves against layer and as ROC curves per model size. The negative control (random per-token embeddings) is the anchor: it is what an instrument that *does* work should look like, near chance for auto-interpretability, so any metric that separates control from the four real variants while failing to separate trained from randomised is separating "has consistent token vectors" rather than "has learned computation". Controls and ablations: three distinct randomisation schemes plus the genuine initialisation checkpoint; matched parameter norms in the re-randomisation, which is also used to explain an ordering anomaly; five random seeds for uncertainty on Pythia-70m; training-data ablations at 1B tokens (to rule out undertrained SAEs) and at 1M tokens for Pythia-1B (a deliberate over-under-training check); expansion-factor and sparsity sweeps on two model sizes including a degenerate expansion-of-2, k-of-4 setting; detection scoring as a robustness check on fuzzing; per-latent scatter of entropy against AUROC rather than only the aggregate.

## What they found

Across the size range, fuzzing AUROC for SAEs on the re-randomised and step-0 transformers overlaps the trained model's curves, while the negative control sits near chance — the authors' Figure 1 for Pythia-6.9B shows the trained line and the randomised lines bundled together and the control line on the diagonal. The same pattern holds for reconstruction metrics, explained variance, L1 and entropy across Pythia-70m to 6.9B. Two orderings are worth recording. First, the re-randomised variants are *closer* to the trained model than the genuine initialisation checkpoint is, which the authors attribute to their randomisation preserving each weight matrix's norm while the true initialisation does not match the trained norms. Second, the negative control's reconstruction degrades with layer while the other variants' improves, which they read as Gaussian activations being the least structured input and becoming less Gaussian as they pass through layers. Loss recovered is the exception that proves a point: it is only definable for the trained variant, since the randomised models' own language-modelling loss is poor either way. On complexity, the trained variant's token entropy *rises* with layer while the randomised variants stay low and flat, and the per-latent scatter shows the distinction the aggregate hides — randomised SAEs have an inverse relation between entropy and AUROC (their high-scoring features are single-token ones), whereas trained SAEs contain latents that are both high-entropy and well explained. The qualitative examples in the appendix follow: trained latents include size adjectives and economic-development nouns, while randomised latents are often described as "parts of proper nouns", "function words" or a specific string such as a syllable. In the toy models, matrix multiplication provably preserves the superposition structure of its input, and passing superposed or GloVe data through a random MLP *improves* the sparsity attainable at a given explained variance — the frontiers move rather than merely holding — which is the authors' evidence for the "random networks sparsify their inputs" hypothesis. They report 435 GPU-hours on one A100 for the final experiments.

## Limits and open problems they name

The empirical base is one model family and one pretraining corpus, chosen for their ubiquity in this literature, and the explainer/judge is a single language model — alternative graders are named as future work. The central claim is scoped narrowly in the limitations: not that SAEs fail to capture trained-model information, only that aggregate auto-interpretability does not establish that they captured learned computation. The entropy statistic is offered as a proof of concept, explicitly not a direct measure of abstractness. The toy models are said to make both mechanisms plausible and to leave undecided which dominates.

## What we take from it

A measure and a warning. The measure: token-distribution entropy per latent is nearly free and separated the variants that every aggregate score conflated, which makes it a candidate column in any dictionary report we print — a feature that fires on one token ID should be labelled as a token detector before it is labelled as a concept. The warning is the sharper one for us: every score this folder's entries quote — auto-interpretability AUROC, explained variance, loss recovered — is a difference between two arms, and if the null arm is "a network with no learned weights", three of the four instruments here cannot price it. Concretely, a claim of the form "this account is better than chance" must name the chance, and the honest chance arm for a dictionary claim is a dictionary of matched shape trained on a norm-matched randomised model, not shuffled labels. The paper also supplies a template we should copy for any surrogate: build the arm that *should* be at floor (here, inconsistent per-token embeddings), show the instrument puts it at floor, and then say what remains unexplained between the arms the instrument cannot separate.
