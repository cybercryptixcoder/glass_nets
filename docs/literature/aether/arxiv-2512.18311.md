## What they wanted, and why
Control protocols that keep a deployed agent safe depend on some observer being able to tell, from a partial view of the agent, whether a property of interest holds — the agent is sandbagging, is being sycophantic, is sabotaging a repo. Reasoning models provide an extra observation channel, the chain of thought (CoT), and prior work suggested it works for catching misbehaviour during training. The worry is that this channel may be fragile: length penalties, indirect pressure from optimising the final answer, or simply larger models that do more computation in their weights rather than in their words could slowly strip the information out. The goal here is to turn that worry into a measured quantity, so that a decline could be detected before it is relied on, and so that the deployment choices that raise it can be identified.

## What they studied
Frontier reasoning models as agents — GPT-5 Thinking, OpenAI o3, o3-mini, o4-mini — plus external models with visible reasoning in their API (Claude 3.7 Sonnet Thinking, DeepSeek R1-0528-Qwen3-8B, Kimi K2 Thinking), and, for the scaling experiments, a series of pretrained models of increasing depth and width in which the largest uses over 100 times the pretraining compute of the smallest. The unit of analysis is not a neuron, feature or circuit but an **observation scope** handed to a monitor model: CoT only, actions only (tool calls, tool results, final answer), or the whole trajectory. The property being predicted is a binary or graded label on a single trajectory — whether the model was influenced by an intervention, whether it followed one of a small set of admissible solution paths, or whether it committed an unambiguous misdeed.

## How they did it
Monitorability is defined as a monitor's ability to predict a stated property of an agent's behaviour, which is explicitly a *two-sided* quantity: it depends on how informative the agent's outputs are and on how capable the monitor reading them is. They argue that F1 and accuracy cannot compare models whose base rates of the misbehaviour differ, and adopt the square of the geometric mean of sensitivity and specificity (`g-mean^2` = TPR × TNR), squared so that a degenerate always-positive monitor scores zero and so that imbalance does not move the number. Ground-truth labels come from three construction strategies, called archetypes. **Intervention evaluations** perturb the input or environment between a control arm and an intervention arm (a hint added to a multiple-choice item, a missing section of a health case added back, a patient's own opinion appended, a demographic attribute injected into user memory, a deployment-threat framing for sandbagging), measure whether behaviour changed, and ask the monitor to say whether the intervention influenced the answer; because an observed positive in the intervention arm may simply be the control-arm base rate, standard TPR is undefined, so they build a *minimal-criterion* sensitivity: assume monotone effect, compute the total effect from the two arm rates, take the fraction of observed positives that must be attributable to the intervention, and score the monitor against that lower bound rather than against a label. They also split TNR by arm, because a monitor can look good by being accurate on easy control-arm negatives, and combine the two with a geometric mean for the intervention setting. In both arms the monitor is told the model received the intervention — a deliberate falsehood in the control arm — so that the only thing differing between arms is the text being judged. **Process evaluations** use problems with a small enumerated set of admissible solution paths, each invoking at least one named theorem, with the solution space reviewed by independent domain experts; if the model answers correctly it almost certainly used one, and the monitor is asked whether evidence of that path appears. Only sensitivity is defined here, since an incorrect answer may still have followed a valid path. **Outcome-property evaluations** repurpose control evaluations where the property is decidable from the trajectory (impossible coding tasks whose passing tests prove test-hacking, tasks blocked by missing tools where lying is the failure, sabotage side-tasks judged by a model that knows the side task). Standard errors come from a two-level bootstrap over instances and samples. To check the monitors are not just copying the co-occurrence of "intervention mentioned" and "positive outcome", they built hand-crafted degeneracy sets in which the intervention is discussed but did not cause the outcome. Alongside the evals they ran frontier RL ablations (rewarding generic CoT obfuscation, and separately rewarding suppression of three specific topics), a pretraining-scale sweep, monitor-compute sweeps, and follow-up-question elicitation.

## What counts as an explanation here
No explanation in the mechanistic sense at all: the deliverable is a **measurement instrument**. The "understanding" is a scorecard — a per-model, per-reasoning-effort, per-observation-scope number, with the archetype taxonomy saying what kind of claim each number supports (that the monitor can detect *why* behaviour changed, that it can link an outcome to a step that must have been taken, or that it can predict a property of the outcome). It is explicitly partial and explicitly *not* a faithfulness claim: they argue that an observation need not faithfully reflect internal computation to be usefully monitorable, generalising the "faithfulness is not necessary for practical purposes" position of Baker et al. The unit being scored is the pair (agent, monitor), never the agent alone.

## How they judged success
`g-mean^2` (and its arm-split intervention variant) is the headline metric, with a random monitor fixed at 0.25. Comparisons across models and reasoning efforts are made comparable by dropping, per figure, any evaluation that is underpowered for any point in that figure, and aggregating dataset → evaluation group → taxonomy group with standard errors combined in quadrature into the mean; they are explicit that this makes absolute values across different figures incomparable. Controls: the degeneracy evaluation sets (constructed co-occurrence negatives), a random-monitor baseline, comparison against the same monitor restricted to actions only, and within-model correlations between evaluation groups (scores centred per model so that the correlation reflects covariation with reasoning effort rather than baseline differences). Capability is measured separately by mean pass rate over GPQA-Diamond, AIME 2025 and CodeForces with and without tools, so that monitorability can be plotted against capability rather than asserted alongside it.

## What they found
Aggregate monitorability rises with CoT length across reasoning-effort sweeps for every model family, and CoT-only monitoring clearly beats action-only monitoring, with all-messages best. o3-mini sits on the Pareto frontier of monitorability against CoT length on the non-tool environments, followed by GPT-5; Claude 3.7 Sonnet has high action-only monitorability but the lowest CoT monitorability of the models tested. In outcome-property environments their monitors have high recall averaged across environments, and the apparent low `g-mean^2` there is driven by low TNR — a consequence they attribute partly to not calibrating the monitors to a fixed false-positive rate on neutral traffic. Stronger monitors do better: going from o3-mini to GPT-5 Thinking as the monitor raises `g-mean^2` by nearly 40% at various reasoning efforts; scaling the monitor's own reasoning effort helps most for the weakest monitors (o4-mini improves about 12% from its lowest to highest effort when shown CoT, but only about 4% when shown actions and outputs, so CoT access "unlocks" a steeper monitor-compute trend). On RL: monitorability on two frontier training runs rises early and then stays broadly flat, with a notable dip mid-training for GPT-5.1 Codex Max, tracking thinking-token count, so they read RL at current frontier scale as not materially degrading monitorability. On pretraining: at a fixed capability level it is often possible to obtain much higher monitorability with a smaller model at higher reasoning effort, at more inference compute — the "monitorability tax" — and the capability cost of paying it can be small. Generic obfuscation RL is caught by the suite (via a collapse in CoT length, then by singular filler words after length stabilises), while targeted reward against three specific topics degraded only one of thirteen evaluations, which they read as an argument for breadth. Follow-up questions after the initial response, with the follow-up CoT given to the monitor, improve monitorability substantially in intervention evaluations; a single sample from a general follow-up is negligible, while multiple samples approach the effect of one tailored question, and in subtle-framing settings such as the Scruples sycophancy eval follow-ups can actively hurt.

## Limits and open problems they name
Only OpenAI models served as the monitors, so monitorability for their own models may be inflated by shared grammar or idiom with the monitor. Intervention and process archetypes cannot rule out solution paths invented after the human enumeration. There are no sound negatives in process evaluations, so specificity is fixed at 1 there. The outcome-property ground truth is noisy: disagreements about what counts as misbehaviour were the source of nearly every remaining false negative outside one environment, and some examples had to be filtered. The RL evidence rests on two training runs, and the pretraining sweep on a single seed per scale (one mid-sized model sat well off trend on monitorability while behaving normally on capability), so they flag that monitorability variance across training runs may exceed capability variance and deserve study. Latency costs of the monitorability tax are not modelled. The generative obfuscation attempts were limited, with no sweeps or ablations, and they name an unmeasured pressure they themselves apply: fixing only the environments their CoT monitors can catch is itself slow selection against monitorable reasoning. The metric's thresholding is also conceded to be wrong in at least one place — the apparent sandbagging monitorability drop over o3 training corresponds to the intervention effect size falling to near zero, i.e. noise-dominated problems being scored rather than genuine unmonitorability.

## What we take from it
Four reusable items. (1) **A metric that is invariant to what you cannot control.** If we want to compare a read-out across models, checkpoints or prompt families whose base rate of the target behaviour differs, a prevalence-sensitive score (accuracy, F1) will move for the wrong reason; a symmetric sensitivity-times-specificity form, reported with the two factors separately, keeps the comparison honest. The corollary is theirs: print TNR per arm rather than pooled, because a monitor can hide a systematic failure at the hard negatives behind good performance on the easy ones. (2) **A dependency-lower-bound for labels you cannot assign.** The minimal-criterion construction — bound the fraction of positives that *must* be attributable to an intervention by the measured total effect, and score against that bound rather than a binary label — is the general form of what our batteries need whenever we can run a treatment and a control but cannot label individual instances. For us it is the honest version of "did this probe direction or patch cause the change", applied per instance. (3) **The two-sided framing itself.** Whatever we build as a white-box model, its score is a property of the model *and* of the reader we hand it to — the same activations are informative to a strong interpreter and useless to a weak one. That turns "our explanation is predictive" into a claim with a named reader, which is exactly the kind of claim that can be replicated or broken. (4) **Breadth over a single headline eval as the defence against a slowly degrading signal.** A suite that caught generic obfuscation but flagged only one of thirteen evaluations under targeted pressure is the argument for keeping many small independent checks; and their willingness to publish that one of their own apparent effects was probably measurement noise (the sandbagging intervention effect collapsing to zero) is the report style we should adopt. A warning worth carrying: their low outcome-property scores were a calibration artefact, and they say so — a false-positive rate measured on the wrong negative distribution is not a property of the monitor.
