# Cross-Layer Interaction under Weight-Space Ablation: A Closed-Form Attention Jacobian Bound and a Test on a Real Pretrained Model

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.03629
- **Authors / organisation:** Abdallah Khemais; ISITCOM, University of Sousse
- **Date:** 2026-08-04 (v1), v2 2026-08-07
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03629
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v2, 84,977 chars / 1,374 lines: abstract, §1-§9, §3's six Facts restated from the companion paper, §4 Proposition 1 with its full proof, §5 Propositions 2 and 3 with proofs and Remarks 4-6, §6 Lemma 1 and Proposition 4 with proofs and Remark 7, §7 with Table 1 and Figures 1-2 read from captions and reporting prose, Appendix A in full; reference list read for the six cited works)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) — supplies the closed-form quantity our "prediction under intervention" claim is missing for multi-layer ablations, and a reusable discipline for verifying a bound against real weights rather than asserting it.

## What they wanted, and why

Activation patching and weight-space ablation are the two standard causal interventions in mechanistic interpretability, and in an idealized model where a computation is carried additively along a residual stream they can be compared exactly. A companion paper by the same author proved, for the one composition where two carriers are architecturally dependent — an attention head and the normalization–MLP that reads it inside the same residual block — that the gap between the true joint ablation and the independent-carrier prediction is an explicit first-order term plus a remainder bounded at second order. That theorem covers one block. Real transformers are chains of blocks and experimenters routinely ablate components from different layers, so the result said nothing about the generic case, and it was checked only on small transformers trained from scratch on a synthetic conditional task. This paper extends both the math and the test: an exact multi-layer decomposition, the closed-form ingredient needed to bound what the decomposition leaves open, and a check on an emergent IOI circuit inside Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. The larger goal is a quantitative, checkable account of when two causal interventions on the same network agree and how much they can be trusted to disagree.

## What they studied

Unit of analysis: *carriers* — a fixed direction in the residual stream plus a scalar selector, `F(x) = F_0(x) + Σ αᵢ(x)vᵢ` — realized concretely as attention heads (via the output projection) and MLP outputs (via the second MLP matrix), ablated by orthogonal projectors estimated from donor–receiver activation differences. Two model families: five 4-layer Llama-style decoder transformers (width 64, 4 heads of dim 16, SwiGLU hidden 128, RMSNorm, float32, no final norm so the logit contrast is exactly affine) trained from scratch on a 10-symbol marker-matching task, reused from the companion paper; and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (28 layers, 12 query / 2 KV heads, GQA, d = 1536), loaded natively with logits matching the reference to 3.7e-5. Decision readout is a scalar logit contrast on matched input pairs.

## How they did it

Step by step: (1) restate the single-block machinery — the carrier model, the low-rank projector edit as an exact deletion of one term, the patching-versus-ablation dissociation (patching moves the readout by the carrier's *contrast*, ablation by its *absolute level*, and neither bounds the other), the normalization Jacobian, and the first-order interaction formula with its second-order remainder. (2) For an ablated subset S split across layers, define each touched layer's discrepancy as the difference between editing that layer's carriers in isolation (all other layers at trained weights) and the idealized independent-carrier prediction, and prove the total gap is exactly the sum of those terms plus one remainder. (3) Show the remainder is a genuine cross-layer object, isolated exactly for two touched layers as a double integral of a mixed second derivative of a two-parameter interpolation. (4) Reduce bounding that integral to a local operator-norm bound on how one attention head's output responds to a perturbed key/value token, derive it in closed form, and test it by finite differences on Qwen's real weights. (5) Compute, in closed form from the three MLP spectral norms and the RMSNorm scale, the curvature constant the companion paper assumes. (6) On Qwen, run a pre-registered search over four candidate mechanisms for a clean matched pair, keep only IOI, run a greedy activation-patching circuit search over all 364 head and MLP sites with a pre-fixed depth cap of 6, prune for redundancy, and then probe collapse, dissociation and interaction on the found circuit. Code is released as Julia notebooks that write JSON read directly by the figure scripts.

## What counts as an explanation here

A causal abstraction of an intervention: a small number of named quantities (selector, removed mass, same-block term, cross-layer remainder, curvature constant) that are *identities* rather than approximations, plus closed-form bounds on the terms the identities cannot pin down. The decomposition is exact by construction at every step — the multi-layer split holds for any subset and any input — so it is complete with respect to the arithmetic, and explicitly partial with respect to magnitude: the remainder is isolated but not bounded, and the paper says that in every section that touches it. It predicts: for a subset touching one layer, or a layer feeding the readout directly, the interaction is zero or exactly the bounded same-block term; for anything wider, it predicts only that the gap factors into a measured per-layer sum plus an unmeasured residual.

## How they judged success

Five checks, three of them refutation tests. (a) Proposition 1's identity reproduces to 0 in floating point on all 18 multi-layer marker-task configurations × 80 fresh probe pairs (1,440 measurements), with probe seeds disjoint from the companion paper's. (b) The attention Jacobian bound of Eq. 5 was checked against finite-difference operator norms on Qwen at 6 (layer, head) pairs spanning layers 1 to 28, 2 key positions each, 24 random unit perturbations per site: 0 violations in 12 sites. (c) The curvature bound of Proposition 4 was checked against fourth-order finite differences of the normalization second derivative and of the composed map on the five marker-task checkpoints over random direction pairs: 0 violations in 1,200 samples each. (d) Tightness is reported as a ratio of bound to empirical estimate, not just pass/fail. (e) The real-model check reports per-instance recovery, collapse ratio, dissociation gap and interaction magnitude, and the paper states its verdict as mixed. There is no benchmark comparison to other interpretability methods — the baselines are the idealization itself and the finite-difference truth.

## What they found

The decomposition is exact, and it attributes the multi-layer interaction uncomfortably. Pooled over 1,440 measurements the median same-block sum is 0.31 logits and the median total interaction 0.30 — close — but the median cross-layer remainder is 0.59, roughly twice either, and the same-block sum exceeds the total interaction on 57% of pair-configurations while the remainder exceeds it on 63%, so the two routinely partially cancel. One configuration (two heads at layers 1 and 2 on instance 2) shows a median same-block sum of 3.85 against a median total of 0.11: the terms cancel almost completely. Conclusion: for subsets spanning more than one layer the same-block sum is not a proxy for the net interaction.

The attention Jacobian bound holds everywhere it was tested and is tight-ish in one form, useless in another: the p-weighted form exceeds the empirical estimate by 12× to 111× (median 71×), while the weight-only normalization-closed form is looser by four to six orders of magnitude (median 1.7e5, max 4.2e6) — finite and never violated, but not a usable numerical estimate as stated. The curvature bound is similar in kind: 0 violations in 1,200 samples, but it exceeds the largest sampled curvature by 6.5e2 to 2.4e3, while the normalization lemma alone is loose by only 12.9-15.7×; the slack comes from the product-of-norms composition, not from the normalization.

On Qwen, only IOI survived the pre-registered matched-pair screen (clean on 5/5 instances at margin > 1.0 logit; sentiment 3/5, temporal order 2/5, magnitude comparator 0/5). A shared carrier exists: layer 25 head 9 appears in all five pruned circuits, layer 19 head 6 in four, the other 16 sites in one to three. Pruning removed nothing on any instance. Four instances reach recovery ≥ 0.96 within the 6-site budget; Lucy/Sam plateaus at 0.673. Collapse is clear-to-partial on four (ratio 0.168-0.396) and absent on Anna/Mike (0.761). The dissociation gap is never small — 0.224 to 0.852 on all five. Interaction is measurable on the three instances whose circuit contains both a head and an MLP, ranging 0.066 (Sarah/Tom) to 0.327 (Alice/Bob) — and none of the three pairs sits in the same-block configuration the theorem was proved for, so this measures the paper's own open multi-layer case, not the theorem.

The paper also records a negative finding: no simple sign law for the remainder. Structurally the mixed derivative is a bilinear pairing between how much of the earlier layer's removed mass reaches the later layer's input and how sensitive the later layer's removed-mass map is there, so neither the product nor the sum of the two single-site effects should predict its sign; empirically the sign matches on two of three applicable instances and fails on Sarah/Tom (Δ_H, Δ_M > 0 but the measured remainder-derived interaction is −0.117).

## Limits and open problems they name

The remainder is isolated, not bounded; chaining the per-layer Jacobian factors across intervening blocks is exactly the step not taken, and the weight-only form's four-to-six-order looseness at a single layer makes the chained version likely vacuous — the multi-layer refutation test was not run. The attention bound is for one perturbed key/value token at one layer, pointwise at the real activations, never a supremum. The Qwen result is one model, one mechanism, five instances, with the interaction probe applicable to three and in-scope for none; the collapse exception (Anna/Mike) is not explained. The joint extension of the frozen-activation/weight-edit equivalence to several simultaneously pinned sites is argued, not verified. The sign question is settled with three data points and explicitly not as a result.

## What we take from it

- **A measure:** report any multi-component ablation as (sum of per-component effects, measured joint effect, residual) with the residual printed and *not* assumed small. The 57% / 63% partial-cancellation result is the mechanical justification: a sum of component scores is not a proxy for the joint effect once components sit in different layers, and we should print the residual as its own column rather than fold it into an error bar.
- **A method (the strongest object in the paper for us):** the exact double-integral identity for the cross term — the second difference of a two-parameter interpolation — is computable with two extra forward passes and is *exact* even when the bound on it is loose. When we need a cross-layer interaction number we should compute it this way and treat any bound as a certificate of order, never as an estimate.
- **A warning about bounds as estimates:** two rigorous, verified, never-violated bounds are looser than the truth by 71× and by 10^5-10^6×. A bound that passes a refutation test only tells us the algebra is right; the ratio is what tells us whether it can carry a claim. Every white-box bound we publish should come with the bound/measured ratio at the observation point.
- **A framing for verification:** the refutation-test discipline (pointwise, real weights, random perturbation directions, count violations, then report looseness) is the cheapest credible validation of a theoretical claim about a large model and requires no training — worth adopting wholesale for any closed-form quantity our model asserts.
- **A shortcut on the empirical side:** pre-register the candidate mechanisms and their lexical instances before probing, then report the ones that failed the screen (3/5, 2/5, 0/5) alongside the one that passed. Collapse being absent on one of five instances, honestly reported, is more useful than a mean that would have hidden it.
- **A negative result we should not re-derive:** there is no expectation of a simple sign rule for the cross-layer residual, for a structural reason (it is a bilinear form, not a function of the two marginal magnitudes). Any of our write-ups that proposes "the sign of the interaction is the sign of X" should be treated as suspect by default.
