## What they wanted, and why

Sparse dictionary training is supposed to get better as the dictionary gets bigger, and it does not: reconstruction improves while downstream usefulness does not. The authors trace this to a mismatch between the flat sparsity objective and features that are genuinely hierarchical — a broad concept that always co-occurs with each of its refinements — and argue that the objective then pays the dictionary to trade the broad latent away for its special cases. They want a single dictionary that holds both levels at once instead of a sweep of separately trained widths, because the sweep is what the current practice recommends and it is the expensive part. The larger goal is a dictionary whose quality does not degrade with scale, so that a feature read out of a very wide dictionary is still the concept the model is actually using.

## What they studied

Three settings of increasing realism. A synthetic tree of binary features in 20 dimensions where each child fires only conditional on its parent, with 20 latents in both arms, matching the true feature count. A 4-layer Transformer of hidden size 768 trained on TinyStories, with reference SAE families at 30, 100, 300, 1k, 3k and 10k latents trained on attention-block outputs, MLP outputs and the residual stream, used for a qualitative account of how a concept fragments as the width grows. And Gemma-2-2B layer-12 residual-stream activations, where the main dictionary is 65,536 latents with five nested sub-dictionaries at {2048, 6144, 14336, 30720, 65536} trained at average sparsities of 20, 40, 80, 160 and 320 active latents per token, plus scaling runs at 4k, 16k and 65k. The unit of analysis is the latent and its decoder direction, judged by absorption rate, splitting, sparse-probe transfer, concept-erasure metrics and pairwise decoder similarity.

## How they did it

The construction is a change to the *loss*, not to the encoder: a maximum width m is split into a nested sequence m₁ < … < mₙ = m, and the objective sums a reconstruction term for every prefix, each computed from only the first mᵢ latents, so the first 2,048 latents must explain the input on their own while the full 65,536 are also trained. Because a prefix reconstruction cannot call on later latents, a later latent cannot be the one that carries a general concept — the sparsity incentive to absorb or split is removed at the source rather than penalised. Two variants are tried: a fixed prefix set, and a random prefix sampled per batch from a truncated Pareto distribution. Sparsity is enforced with BatchTopK, keeping the B × K largest activations across a whole batch and swapping to a calibrated global threshold at inference, so the per-example active count varies while the batch average is fixed. On Gemma the training budget is 500M Pile tokens at learning rate 3e-4 and batch 2,048 with no additional regulariser beyond the multiple reconstruction terms, matched to the SAE Bench suite's own data ordering and hyperparameters so the six baseline architectures (ReLU, TopK, BatchTopK, Gated, JumpReLU, P-Annealing) are compared like for like. Absorption and splitting are then measured on first-letter classification, following the probe-first methodology: train logistic probes on raw activations for ground truth, find the corresponding latents, and count tokens where the latent fails to fire on a letter it should cover or where a second latent is needed to complete the letter. Ablations vary the loss weighting (equal versus weighted by new latents per group), gradient flow across prefixes (a stop-gradient arm), the number of groups (3, 5, 10) and fixed versus sampled prefixes.

## What counts as an explanation here

A training procedure plus an architectural reading of the dictionary: the explanation is that the latent ordering itself carries the abstraction hierarchy, with early latents as general concepts participating in every prefix reconstruction and late latents as specialisations participating only in the widest one. It is a partial account — nothing here claims to recover the model's true features except in the toy world, where recovery is measured directly against the planted directions. It predicts ordering (a diagonal rather than an off-diagonal block in the decoder-versus-ground-truth cosine matrix) and predicts that downstream quality is non-decreasing in width, which is the checkable consequence the standard architecture fails.

## How they judged success

Reconstruction is variance explained, and downstream usefulness is cross-entropy loss when the reconstructed activations are fed back into the language model. Feature quality is measured with SAE Bench: sparse probing over 35 binary classification tasks in five domains, spurious correlation removal, targeted probe perturbation, automated interpretability scored by an LLM judge over 1,000 latents, and average maximum pairwise decoder cosine as a composition proxy. Absorption and splitting are the first-letter metrics described above. The controls that matter: baselines are the six SAE Bench architectures trained on identical data ordering and hyperparameters, the toy model has a known ground truth and a vanilla SAE trained to the same latent count, and the scaling study re-trains at 4k and 16k so that "improves with scale" is a measured slope rather than an extrapolation. A meta-SAE arm (a quarter-width dictionary at about four active latents trained on the decoder matrices) prices how much information the decoder directions share.

## What they found

Absorption collapses: at average L0 of 40 the Matryoshka dictionary shows an absorption rate of 0.05 against 0.49 for BatchTopK, and it needs about one latent per first letter where BatchTopK needs three, so both the holes and the fragmentation shrink. The toy decoder-cosine matrix shows a clean diagonal against the planted directions while the vanilla SAE shows high parent–child similarity, and the TinyStories account carries a concrete case: a female-words latent in the 300-latent reference SAE loses "Lily" to a Lily-specific latent one width up, whereas the 25k Matryoshka keeps a hole-free female-words latent alongside the Lily and Sue latents. The price is real and they print it: at L0 40 the Matryoshka dictionary explains 70% of the variance against BatchTopK's 72%, but the cross-entropy cost of feeding the reconstruction back is comparable at larger L0, and the authors argue reconciliation is that the features are more meaningful even though the raw activations are reconstructed less accurately. Disentanglement improves by two independent measures: maximum pairwise decoder cosine is substantially lower, and meta-SAEs explain 42% of the variance in Matryoshka decoder directions against 55% for BatchTopK at the largest width, with BatchTopK's shared structure growing as width grows while Matryoshka's stays roughly flat. On scaling, Matryoshka improves or holds on almost every metric from 4k to 65k where the baselines often degrade. The ablations are the interesting part: weighting the loss by group size recovers BatchTopK's cross-entropy at the 200M-token ablation budget but costs feature quality, so equal weighting is what buys the hierarchy; a stop-gradient arm improves absorption at low L0 while degrading loss recovered, probing and erasure; three groups interpolates back toward BatchTopK and ten groups over-shrinks the small prefixes; fixed prefixes beat sampled ones slightly and are simpler to shard.

## Limits and open problems they name

The reconstruction trade-off is accepted rather than solved, and they flag that applications needing precise activation reconstruction may find it problematic. Training time rises by roughly 50% with five nested dictionaries, scaling with the size of the prefix set. Their evaluation is almost entirely quantitative and machine-judged, and they say plainly that human interpretability of the resulting latents was not studied. A further limit is not named as one but is visible in Appendix E: on the chess model, where hidden size is 512 and high L0 is a large fraction of the representation, Matryoshka matches the field at low L0 and degrades at L0 ≥ 150, which bounds the claim to large hidden dimensions.

## What we take from it

A **constraint-as-architecture** move for any account that decomposes a network into parts: instead of penalising the decomposition you do not want, forbid it structurally by requiring the small dictionary to stand alone, and the degenerate solution stops being reachable. That is the constructive counterpart to entry `arxiv-2409.14507`'s proof that the degenerate solution is loss-preferred — the same failure, answered by withholding capacity rather than adding a term. Two **measures** worth taking. First, meta-SAE variance explained on the decoder matrix as a direct statistic of how much the latents share: it is computed without ground truth, it is monotone in width for the unfixed architecture, and it is the cheapest instrument in this folder for "are these latents distinct or is this one concept smeared". Second, the prefix-audit as a diagnostic: decoding with only the first k latents tells you whether the general concepts are present at all, which is a prediction about the dictionary a white-box account can actually test under intervention. And a **warning about reported trade-offs**: the paper's own best-feature configuration loses the reconstruction column and wins the downstream columns, which is the same two-axis inversion entry `arxiv-2602.14687` measured on a benchmark with ground truth — so a fidelity number printed alone would have ranked this architecture last.
