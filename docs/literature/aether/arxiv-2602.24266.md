# Causal Mechanism Reduction: Mechanism Replacement for Neural Network Pruning and Abstraction

- **Key:** arxiv-2602.24266
- **Authors / organisation:** Amir Asiaee; Vanderbilt University Medical Center, Nashville, TN
- **Date:** 2026-02 (v1 27 Feb 2026; v2 6 Jul 2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24266
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
The paper starts from a question that arrives in two costumes: which internal units of a trained network can be replaced without changing what it computes? Structured pruning asks this because it wants a smaller deployable model; causal abstraction asks it because it wants a high-level model whose variables commute with interventions. The author's observation is that both are the same *operation* run under different fidelity metrics — replace a mechanism, then measure the behavioural cost — and that this has never been written down as one primitive. The goal is therefore unificatory: one definition of replacement, one risk objective with named methods as special cases, one theorem connecting compression error to interchange agreement. The larger aim is to give pruning and mechanistic interpretability a common object to optimise and to verify, so a compression decision can be read as a claim about mechanism.

## What they studied
Feedforward deterministic networks treated as structural causal models over their activations: a small CIFAR-10 ConvNet with a 256-unit penultimate representation, ResNet-56 trained on CIFAR-100 with a 64-channel post-pool representation, a two-hidden-layer MNIST MLP, a DeiT-Tiny transformer fine-tuned on ImageNet-100, and a controlled three-layer Boolean MLP that fits a fixed XOR-of-AND/OR function of four relevant input bits out of eight. The unit of analysis is the **unit / channel / mechanism** at a chosen layer plus its replacement rule: either a constant clamped in place, or an affine function of a retained subset of coordinates in the same layer. The studied object is not a feature, a direction or a circuit — it is a *substitution* applied to a set of coordinates, together with the smaller network that results.

## How they did it
Step by step: (1) take a trained network with fixed weights and record activations at one layer over a calibration set; (2) choose a kept set and a replaced set, and a replacement class — constants, or affine maps from kept coordinates with a chosen parent set; (3) score each candidate replacement with a second-order local proxy for the change in task loss, built from the gradient and Hessian of the loss with respect to the replaced activations at the observed values; (4) rank units by score and take the bottom *k*; (5) **compile** the chosen replacements exactly into a smaller dense network by folding each clamped constant into the downstream bias and deleting the corresponding weight column, with affine replacements folding by redistributing the replaced column onto its parents; (6) evaluate the compiled network two ways — as a predictor (top-1 under matched fine-tuning) and as a *reduced causal model* under Bernoulli interchange interventions on the retained coordinates, with the state map fixed as the projection onto kept units and the intervention map the identity on those same coordinates. The invariance test is the controlled one: apply an exact function-preserving positive rescaling to each hidden unit's incoming weights and bias while dividing its outgoing column by the same factor, then re-run the selector on a network that computes an identical function and compare kept sets by Jaccard overlap. Four selectors are compared (three variants of their own score, variance-based pruning, magnitude, plus DepGraph and a random selector), with paired per-seed deltas and bootstrap intervals where they ran seeds.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **reduced model plus a declared correspondence**, where the correspondence is not learned but fixed by construction: kept coordinates map to kept coordinates, and the high-level model *is* the compiled smaller network. It is a constructive / replacement-model form rather than a description, dictionary or probe. The claim it supports is partial and conditional — commutativity under a stated family of interchange interventions, bounded by a margin certificate rather than asserted exactly — and it predicts behaviour under those interventions, at a stated kept fraction, with the guarantee degrading as the margin shrinks. Crucially the paper insists the abstraction is *computational*: interventions act on the network's computation graph, and no claim is made that the reduced variables recover real-world causes.

## How they judged success
Three separate criteria, deliberately not merged. (i) **Reparameterization invariance**: kept-set Jaccard between the original and a functionally identical rescaled network, against the chance floor for two independent random half-subsets (about one third) and a measured random-selector floor, with a numerical sanity check that the rescaled model's max logit difference stays below 1e-5. (ii) **Matched-fine-tune top-1** on DeiT-Tiny / ImageNet-100 at three keep fractions, same optimizer, schedule and baseline initialisation per cell, plus parameters, MACs and measured throughput; the author is explicit that this metric is *saturated* and therefore a recovery/viability test rather than a ranking test. (iii) **Interchange-intervention accuracy and KL divergence** between original and compiled networks' intervention logits, at a fixed swap budget (2000 Bernoulli swaps at p=0.5 per cell, fewer for the ablations), plus paired bootstrap confidence intervals on per-seed deltas. A theoretical certificate links expected squared logit distortion to an IIA lower bound through the intervention margin, so the cheap score has a stated relation to the expensive test. Diagnostics include an off-diagonal curvature ratio (the size of the Hessian's off-diagonal block relative to the whole, per block of units), a calibration-shift test, and a Boolean-circuit sanity check where the ground-truth factorisation is known.

## What they found
- **The invariance test separates the methods sharply and the accuracy benchmark does not.** Under exact positive scaling over a wide range, the logit-distortion score keeps Jaccard 1.000 with zero variance across seeds on both cached models, while variance-based pruning lands at chance-level overlap (about 0.35 and 0.37 against random floors of about 0.33 and 0.36) — two networks that compute the same function induce nearly unrelated reductions. The downstream consequence is measured at one keep fraction on the ConvNet under the strongest scaling: an IIA gap of about +0.131 and a KL gap of about −0.362 in favour of the invariant score.
- **On the transformer benchmark, everything ties.** After matched fine-tuning all six methods finish within 0.7 top-1 points of the 84.62 baseline, and random pruning holds the best single cell in the grid. Zero-shot is where methods differ (the affine variant collapses to near chance before fine-tuning), which the author reads as evidence that the fine-tune is doing the recovering.
- **Under interchange verification the invariant score wins consistently but by little on IIA and clearly on KL.** On the ConvNet the IIA/KL pairs degrade smoothly with keep fraction (0.858/0.082 at three-quarters kept down to 0.483/1.034 at a quarter), and the paired per-seed IIA deltas over variance-based pruning are small and not individually significant — around +0.007 and +0.009 — while the KL edge is significant in the powered MNIST replication.
- **The local risk score has a real failure mode that refitting partly repairs.** Fitting each replaced unit's optimal constant independently and then compiling them all at once ignores cross-unit curvature; refitting the constants jointly over the whole replaced block at compile time lifts the corrected constant variant's IIA from the uncorrected behaviour to 0.720/0.582/0.455 across keep fractions, still below the logit score. Iterative re-scoring after each prune step helps in the same direction.
- **More expressive replacement is not more useful.** The affine variant is unstable without fine-tuning — on the ConvNet its IIA falls *below* the random selector — and the choice of parent set (correlation-based, output-weight-weighted, or random) makes no measurable difference, which the author reads as the affine class supplying capacity rather than an inductive bias.
- **The off-diagonal diagnostic explains when to distrust additivity.** The mean off-diagonal Hessian mass over random blocks of units in the penultimate representation is about 0.85 of the whole, so the diagonal approximation's bound is not tight at this scale, and the paper says the local one-layer theorem does not license multi-layer one-shot reduction.

## Limits and open problems they name
Locality: the risk theorem is stated for a single layer or block, and multi-layer reduction requires recomputation and re-scoring, which is named as future work rather than done. Curvature: diagonal or block-diagonal scoring is cheap and additive but wrong when cross-unit coupling is large, and the diagnostic that measures this is a diagnostic, not a correction. Replacement capacity: affine replacement is data- and metric-dependent and can overfit the calibration signal or optimise a surrogate that does not lower interchange KL. Causal semantics: the model is a computational SCM over activations, and the guarantee concerns computation, not exogenous real-world causes. Benchmark ceiling: the transformer protocol saturates, so method ranking there is unreliable by the author's own admission, and the DepGraph comparison is at a different deployment budget so it is not a matched comparison.

## What we take from it
- **A measure and a warning (the main takeaway).** Any score we use to rank internal units — importance, variance, ablation effect, whatever — should be tested for **invariance under exact function-preserving reparameterisations of the network** before it is used to make a claim about mechanism. The test is nearly free: rescale a unit and its outgoing column, confirm the logits are unchanged numerically, then re-run the selector and report kept-set overlap against the random floor. This is the cheapest known falsifier of "our importance score reflects structure"; a score that fails it is an artifact of coordinates.
- **A framing.** Pruning decisions and causal-abstraction claims are the same operation under two metrics, so a compression result can be reported as an approximate-abstraction result at no extra cost: compile the smaller model, then run interchange interventions on the retained set. We should report both lines whenever we publish a reduction.
- **A measure.** The off-diagonal curvature ratio is a direct measure of how much of a layer's sensitivity is *inter-unit* rather than per-unit, and it is computable before any ablation. It tells us whether an additive account of a layer is even admissible.
- **A claim shape.** The margin certificate is the version of the folder's ongoing "abstraction holds" problem that actually bounds something: IIA is bounded below by one minus the probability of a small-margin input minus distortion over margin squared, so the honest report is a margin distribution next to an IIA number. This is the concrete instantiation of the query-family discipline reached in earlier entries — the family is fixed (interchange on retained coordinates, projection map, identity intervention map) and the failure term is explicit.
- **A warning against a shortcut.** The mutation this paper is *not*: it never learns a correspondence. That is why its IIA numbers are small and trustworthy rather than large and uninformative, and it cites the critique that expressive learned alignments can score highly even on randomised networks. When we read a high IIA somewhere, the first question is whether the map was learned.
