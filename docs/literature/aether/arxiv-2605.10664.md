# Prompt–Activation Duality: Improving Activation Steering via Attention-Level Interventions

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.10664
- **Authors / organisation:** Diancheng Kang (Southern University of Science and Technology); Zheyuan Liu, Yue Huang, Zhaoxuan Tan, Meng Jiang (University of Notre Dame); Ningshan Ma (MIT; 2077AI)

## What they wanted, and why
Activation steering is normally validated on a single response, but deployment is multi-turn and stateful: generated tokens are cached and attended to later. The authors ask whether the way a steering vector is applied — not only how strongly — can make it drift into incoherence as a conversation continues, and whether steering can be made to enter the computation at the same site that natural prompting does. The larger aim is reliability of a cheap, reversible control method, which matters to us because almost every "the direction works" claim in this folder rests on a one-shot generation. Their diagnosis is that a constant, uniformly applied residual-stream injection contaminates the key-value cache and compounds, and the fix is to move the intervention onto the pathway a system prompt actually uses.

## What they studied
Two instruction-tuned open models of different families: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (28 layers, width 3584, grouped-query attention) and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (32 layers, width 4096). Unit of analysis is a per-layer *attention-output* vector — the difference in the system-prompt contribution to the self-attention output between contrastive positive and negative prompts — plus a per-token scalar gate. Fifteen persona traits on Qwen (seven RLHF-aligned, five RLHF-opposing, three neutral) and ten on Llama, taken from the persona-vectors setup, with 17 in the master inventory.

## How they did it
They start by decomposing the ordinary persona vector: unrolling the residual recurrence and differencing the positive and negative prompt conditions splits the vector into an embedding term, a sum of per-layer attention-output differences, and a sum of per-layer MLP-output differences. Reading those terms, they argue the attention sum is the prompt-mediated channel, the embedding term is mostly about which words were produced, and the MLP term is largely unrelated to trait expression. That motivates the method: at extraction time, hook each layer's value projection and force eager attention so the post-softmax weights are visible, then recompute the attention output summing **only over system-prompt source-token positions** (no renormalisation, so the magnitude is preserved), and difference that cropped output across the two prompt conditions per layer. This gives one vector per layer for a range of layers rather than one vector at one site. At inference the cropped delta is added to the attention output **before** the MLP and residual update, scaled by a per-token coefficient: a centred sigmoid over each token's mean pre-softmax query–key compatibility with the mean system-prompt key, normalised by head dimension. Tokens that engage the system prompt above their layer's extraction-time average get more than the base coefficient, the rest are damped, so the budget is redistributed rather than increased. The gate costs one dot product per head because queries and keys already exist. Evaluation is a 10-turn dialogue protocol with the KV cache persisted across turns and hooks active on every generated token: 20 question groups × 3 sampling seeds = 60 conversations per trait and condition, temperature 1.0, 500 new tokens per turn, with trait expression and coherence each scored 0–100 by separate GPT-4.1-mini calls against rubrics; drift is computed as turn-10 score minus turn-1 score. Comparisons are against the single-site residual-stream persona-vector baseline at its authors' released settings, a no-steering run, and ablations that remove cropping, remove gating (gate sharpness set to zero), and remove both. A separate appendix experiment injects attention-pathway versus MLP-pathway deltas at every layer with the same coefficient to isolate which pathway carries the behaviour.

## What counts as an explanation here
A structural decomposition plus a location claim: the trait signal is not at one bottleneck but distributed along the attention pathway, and the intervention should be placed where the model itself integrates prompts. The object is a set of per-layer vectors and a per-token gate — a control mechanism, not a description of a concept. It is explicitly partial, and uncharacteristically so for a steering paper: the authors do not claim to have found "the trait direction", they claim the ordinary extraction mixes three things and that keeping one of them is more stable. The only predictive content is the claim that signal mass sits in a distributed layer band, which they verify by plotting per-layer norms and by noting that their one clear failure case (humorous) peaks far from the baseline's injection site.

## How they judged success
Two independent 0–100 LLM-judge scores per reply (trait expression with a trait-specific rubric, coherence with a shared rubric anchored at 0/50/100 and explicitly told not to penalise incorrectness), reported per turn with drift, averaged over 60 conversations. A no-steering baseline over seven traits establishes that the traits are near floor without intervention and that unsteered context decay is small. Controls reported: the ablation table separating cropping from gating, a per-token cosine similarity comparison between prompted and steered tokens, an appendix where the MLP pathway is extracted and injected with the identical treatment at matched coefficient and placement, and a second model family. What they do **not** report: any random-direction or norm-matched control vector, any variance or significance test on the judged differences, and any single-turn capability benchmark — so the claim is narrower than "GCAD preserves capability", it is "GCAD's judged coherence does not collapse across turns while the baseline's does".

## What they found
- **The baseline collapses across turns; the prompt-skewed intervention does not.** On the 15-trait Qwen average, coherence drift goes from −18.6 (single-site, layer 20, coefficient 2.0) to −1.9, while turn-10 trait expression rises from 78.0 to 93.1 and the ten-turn mean from 81.5 to 91.8. The collapse is concentrated where it is most visible: impolite and humorous reach near-zero coherence by turn 5 under the baseline; sycophantic and creative fall below 25 coherence points by turn 10.
- **Drift is not monotone for every trait under the baseline, which is why the average is the honest headline.** Sycophantic and pessimistic *gain* trait expression across turns while losing coherence (+8.9 and +8.0), so the baseline is not simply "fading" — it is drifting, and trait and coherence decouple in both directions.
- **The no-steering baseline fixes the denominator.** Unsteered coherence starts at 97.4 and decays only to −5.4 over five turns, and the RLHF-opposing traits sit near floor (evil, humorous and impolite at 0.0 throughout; apathetic and sycophantic under 10). So the −18.6 drift is a cost of the intervention, not of long context. The exception they report against themselves is hallucinating, which drifts up from 22.8 to 67.7 unsteered — evidence that some of that trait emerges from accumulated context alone.
- **The ablations are mixed, and the paper's own summary of them as "complementary" is generous.** For evil, removing the gate gives the best five-turn trait mean (90.4 vs 89.3 for full GCAD) at slightly worse drift, and removing both components gives the best trait mean of all four variants (96.8) with worse drift. For humorous, removing both is the best variant on both metrics (drift −1.0 trait, +1.3 coherence). Only crop-plus-gate together keeps both metrics in range on every trait; no single component is uniformly necessary.
- **The pathway experiment is the entry's most transferable result.** Injecting a per-layer attention-pathway delta at all 28 layers at coefficient 1.0 gives an average trait score of 85.2 against 20.8 for the MLP-pathway delta at the same coefficient and placement — with the largest gaps on RLHF-opposing traits (evil 1.6 → 97.6, hallucinating 11.7 → 98.7, humorous 13.0 → 90.4) — while the MLP arm leaves coherence near the unsteered ceiling (90.7 vs 79.3). The authors offer two readings and refuse to choose: the MLP is a weaker carrier, or its trait component has already been routed through attention by the next layer.
- **The gate does something describable.** Per-token cosine similarity to the persona vector is sparse under prompting (only a few trait-bearing words align strongly) and dense and saturating under residual steering. The learned gate reproduces the sparse pattern in a depth-dependent way: nearly uniform early, increasingly selective with depth, with the layer-mean coefficient staying near the base value — redistribution, not amplification. On Llama-3.1-8B the same method transfers with a different layer range (8–20) and coefficient (2.0 vs 3.5), chosen per model without re-tuning the gate sharpness.
- **The failure case is informative.** Humorous peaks in per-layer delta norm at layer 9, far from the residual baseline's layer 16/20 injection site, and is the trait where the baseline is worst. The authors read this as evidence for their "distributed signal" story; it is equally consistent with "the baseline was simply badly placed for this trait", which is the cheaper explanation.

## Limits and open problems they name
Both models comply readily with the contrastive system prompts used at extraction, so the attention delta carries a strong trait signal; how the method behaves on models that resist such prompts is untested. Both scores come from one LLM judge, so absolute numbers may reflect the judge's own safety boundary, and only the GCAD-versus-baseline *comparison* is defended as robust. The MLP-versus-attention gap is left with two indistinguishable explanations, and a causal study of the MLP pathway is named as open. Ablations cover five traits over five turns, and the extraction set discards responses judged below 50 so directions reflect only successfully steered generations.

## What we take from it
- **Warning, and it applies directly to our own interventions:** an activation-space edit applied uniformly at every decoding step is not a fixed intervention — its effects are written into cached states that later steps read, so the effective dose grows with generation length. Any single-prompt intervention result of ours that we want to call a measurement should be re-run with the cache persisted over several turns, and the "does it drift" axis reported separately from the "does it work once" axis. This is the intervention-side twin of the entry-level caveat about judging a model by one response.
- **Method:** "where does the model itself route this signal?" is a better prior for placing an intervention than "which direction has the largest norm". The comparison that makes this concrete is cheap and worth copying: extract the delta from each pathway separately and inject each at matched coefficient and matched layer set, then read the difference (here, +64 points of behaviour for attention over MLP). For a predictive model this is an ablation over *write sites*, which is the same object as a component-importance claim for a component type we usually ignore.
- **Measure:** a per-token *compatibility gate* — the model's own query–key engagement with a reference context, used to modulate how much extra signal a token receives — is a near-free way to make an intervention input-dependent rather than a constant offset. For us it is the simplest available way to test whether a steering result is really a context-conditional effect: if the gate's selectivity is what carries the stability, then a constant-coefficient version should lose it, and the ablation table shows the answer is trait-dependent.
- **Framing:** "prompt–activation duality" is the useful phrase forward — a vector can be read as a compressed prompt, so the honest baseline for any steering result is the prompt the vector was extracted from, applied normally. When prompting matches steering on trait and beats it on coherence (as here), the vector's added value is narrowness, not capability.
- **Caution:** the judge is the instrument (two separate 0–100 rubrics, one model), and the paper's headline is a difference between two conditions judged by that same instrument. The right reading is that the intervention's long-horizon coherence cost is now measured and is large; the absolute scores are not a capability claim.
