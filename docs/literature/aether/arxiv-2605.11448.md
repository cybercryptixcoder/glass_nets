## What they wanted, and why

Probes are the workhorse of representation reading, and linear probes in particular are chosen as much by habit as by argument: they are small, so they cannot do the work themselves. The authors ask a prior question — what probe families are *admissible* if we insist the probe measures the representation rather than the coordinate system the representation happens to be written in. Two models can implement the same function while storing it in hidden coordinates that differ, so a probe family that is not closed under that change of coordinates can report a property as present in one model and absent in the other while nothing about the represented information changed. Their goal is to derive the admissible families from a symmetry principle instead of picking them ad hoc, and then to ask what the same principle implies for moving a monitor from one model to another. It is a step toward a theory of probing as geometry: which properties of a representation are intrinsic to what it computes, and which exist only relative to a basis.

## What they studied

The unit of analysis is the **probe family and the space it can see**, not any single neuron, head or circuit. Models: Pythia-70m / 160m / 410m for the synthetic and cross-token tasks (hidden sizes 512, 768, 1024; early, middle and late layers used), and Qwen2.5-3B, 7B-Instruct, 14B, Coder-7B plus Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 for the monitor-transfer work (activations taken at 75% of depth). Representations are final or near-final hidden states, centred by the training-split mean. The theory side studies the final linear readout (head), since that is the only place where behavioural equivalence has an exact algebraic form.

## How they did it

Step one is a short proof. If two models with bias-free linear heads produce identical logit scores on every input in a set whose hidden states span the space and whose readout is full rank, then their penultimate representations are related by a unique invertible linear map, with the two readouts transformed consistently; adding bias gives an affine map. Softmax adds only a per-input constant to the logits, so it widens this to an affine group acting on the hidden state. Step two is a classification theorem: a *finite-dimensional* linear space of scalar probe functions that is stable under precomposition by every affine map must be exactly the polynomials of total degree at most some fixed degree (or zero). Finite-dimensionality is what enforces shallowness; affine invariance alone admits everything, including all continuous functions. Linear probes are therefore the degree-1 member of a unique hierarchy. At degree 2 the authors do not use all quadratics — the coefficient count grows fast with hidden size — but CP (canonical polyadic) rank-R tensor probes, because a rank bound is preserved under invertible linear reparameterization while sparsity in monomials is not; an affine completion adds lower-degree terms. Step three is a transfer theorem: if two models realize the same finite-dimensional family of linear probe scores, each model's hidden space quotients by the subspace every probe in the bank is blind to, and those two quotients are canonically isomorphic to one abstract space of evaluations. Transfer should therefore align the **probe-visible quotient**, not the full state — in practice, take the bank's weight matrix W, SVD it, and keep the right singular directions above a numerical threshold. Experiments then test each claim: synthetic XOR and N-parity for the degree ladder, subject-verb agreement from per-token probe scores for CP-1 versus linear and full-quadratic heads, a synthetic source/target embedding with growing nuisance dimensions for quotient stability and selectivity, and zero-label safety-monitor transfer from Qwen-7B onto Qwen-3B, Qwen-14B, Qwen-Coder and Mistral with a random-initialization control.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **derived hierarchy of admissible probe families plus a coordinate-free object to compare across models**. The explanation is structural, not textual: it says which scoring functions are licences to claim something about a representation (polynomials of bounded degree, and within degree 2 the low-rank CP subclass) and which are coordinates in disguise (sparse monomial probes, whose pattern dissolves under a change of basis). Prediction is explicit and falsifiable at two points — the degree of a target is the degree a probe should need, and transfer should degrade in proportion to how much of a concept's direction lies outside the bank's span. It is partial in a way the authors state: the symmetry is exact only at the readout, and nothing here says what the model computes internally, only what is linearly or polynomially visible from a representation.

## How they judged success

AUROC as the primary classification metric (balanced accuracy where more informative), 5 seeds (42, 137, 256, 314, 999), 10 resamples for the cross-token tables, 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for transfer, and paired differences between transfer operators. Baselines are chosen to isolate what the theory claims: full quadratic against CP-1 (capacity versus structure), raw-activation linear and bilinear probes on full hidden states (composition through scores versus interaction in the full space), and for transfer, full-state ordinary least squares, PCA projection and random projection against quotient Ridge. Controls include a random-initialization target, a lexical TF-IDF logistic regression on raw text (does the concept need internals at all?), keyword-holdout and lexically matched test sets, leave-one-out removal of each concept from the bank, and a bank-conditioning ablation crossing two banks with duplicated versus deduplicated alignment pools. The sharpest evaluation is a deployment-style benchmark: with a 21-concept pool, an abstention rule deploys a transferred concept only if its in-span fraction clears a threshold fixed in advance, and the reported quantity is the *silent-failure rate* — concepts that transfer above quality on all three targets yet carry no coverage certificate.

## What they found

The synthetic ladder is tight: on N-parity, degree N-1 sits at chance for every N from 2 to 8 while degree N reaches a held-out accuracy of 0.985 or above; a genuinely degree-2 XOR target gives a linear probe 0.501 balanced accuracy and a quadratic 1.000. On cross-token subject-verb agreement, CP rank-1 beats the linear head by 16.8/19.0/20.0 points of AUROC on Pythia-70m/160m/410m (0.901 against 0.733 at 70m; 0.955 against 0.755 at 410m), the full quadratic is 7-13 points *below* CP-1 with a 5-11x larger train-test gap, and raw bilinear probes on the full hidden state lose 20-32 points to the score-space version. Under 20 random affine transforms a fitted quadratic transports analytically to within 1.14e-11, while a top-50 monomial probe with its pattern frozen collapses to R-squared of -18.3. Quotient transfer holds in-span accuracy at about 0.99 as nuisance dimension grows sevenfold where PCA falls to 0.57, and rejects out-of-span concepts at chance (0.484 to 0.519) while full-state OLS transports them at 0.989 — the quotient refuses to fake coverage, and the sweep shows a smooth monotone curve tracking the in-span fraction. On real safety transfer from Qwen-7B, quotient alignment reaches AUROC 0.926 on toxicity and 0.972 on sentiment on Mistral with no target labels (random control 0.580 and 0.511), but jailbreaking on Mistral drops to 0.669. TF-IDF is a strong baseline exactly where one would expect: 0.903 toxicity, 0.981 jailbreaking, against 0.834 sentiment where the quotient transfer gains 13 points, and keyword holdout moves TF-IDF from 0.904 to 0.706 on toxicity while the quotient loses 10 points. Bank conditioning dominates alignment-pool size: swapping a 16-concept bank for an 11-probe bank lifts Mistral toxicity from 0.764 to 0.924, while deduplicating the alignment pool adds 5 points. The abstention benchmark reports silent-failure rates of 0.238 / 0.286 / 0.381 for the full, safety-only and core-only banks, at a cost of 1-3 points of AUROC on the concepts it does deploy (full bank) and 4-7 points on the core bank. Leave-one-out exposes the mechanism by name: sentiment, orthogonal to the safety bank at an in-span fraction of 0.001, is still transported by full-state OLS at 0.972 while the quotient correctly falls to 0.490. Behavioral consequence is real but modest: as a zero-label best-of-8 reranker on 461 judged prompts, harmful-compliance falls 39.5% to 19.8% on Qwen-3B and 73.8% to 58.4% on Mistral, with benign false refusal at 6.0% and 5.0%.

## Limits and open problems they name

The symmetry is exact only at the final linear head; deeper layers may obey weaker, richer or merely approximate symmetry classes, and the authors name this as the main open direction rather than assume it away. Cross-architecture transfer is explicitly called partial portability, not deployability: one concept in five transfers weakly, and the authors say additional validation is needed before use. The in-span diagnostic is geometric and therefore sufficient but not necessary for transfer failure — concepts can transfer through correlations with bank members (jailbreaking is their own counterexample, quotient 0.922 against full-state 0.669 at low in-span fraction), so a low score is a warning, not a verdict. Coverage-aware abstention costs AUROC on the concepts it does deploy, which the paper reports with the sign intact rather than hiding in an appendix. The theory's nondegeneracy assumptions (spanning hidden states, full-rank readout, injective evaluation maps) are exactly what a real model need not satisfy, and the approximate versions hold only under conditioning bounds on the bank.

## What we take from it

Three transfers, one of them a correction to us. **(1) A basis test for every claim our white-box model makes.** If a reading of a model — a feature, a direction, a probe score — is claimed to be about the *computation* rather than about the checkpoint's arithmetic, it should survive a random invertible affine reparameterization of the hidden state, or the failure should be reported as the claim's limit. Their monomial-versus-CP comparison is the cleanest demonstration in the folder that a sparse, human-legible pattern and a coordinate-stable structure can be the same function under two bases, and the sparse one grades as real and then collapses to R-squared of -18.3. **(2) State the degree, and if it is above one, use a rank bound.** A probe that succeeds at degree 1 licenses a linear claim; a target that genuinely needs degree 2 (agreement, policy mismatch as the XOR of prompt risk and response refusal) falsifies linear reading by construction rather than by weak performance. Our interaction-shaped readouts should say which member of the hierarchy they are. **(3) Coverage as a number, not a hope.** The probe-visible quotient is a cheap, label-free object that says how much of a new target our readout could possibly see, and the silent-failure benchmark is the honest way to report a monitor: not "AUROC 0.95 on the target" but "AUROC 0.95, and 24% of the concepts we might otherwise deploy carry no coverage certificate". The companion warning is the one our recent entries keep re-deriving: full-state alignment never returns a null, because a sufficiently expressive alignment can always transport something, so an operator that cannot fail is not an instrument.
