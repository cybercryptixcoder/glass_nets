# Weight Poisoning Attacks on Pre-trained Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2004.06660
- **Authors / organisation:** Keita Kurita, Paul Michel, Graham Neubig — Language Technologies Institute, Carnegie Mellon University (all three; `{kkurita,pmichel1,gneubig}@cs.cmu.edu`). The paper is dedicated to the memory of Kurita, and correspondence is directed to Michel.
- **Date:** 2020-04-14
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06660
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Transfer learning in NLP means downloading someone else's weights and fine-tuning them, and the paper asks the security question the practice invites: can a distributed checkpoint be made to carry a hidden vulnerability that survives the victim's fine-tuning? The threat is the analogue of malware in downloaded software — an attacker who publishes a plausible model and later chooses an input keyword that forces the output toward a class of their choosing, while the model behaves normally on everything else. The authors want to show the threat is real under weak attacker knowledge, to isolate *why* naive attempts fail (fine-tuning overwrites the poison), and then to sketch a defence. It is the founding statement of the weight-poisoning threat model that the folder's LoRA-backdoor detection line assumes throughout.

## What they studied
BERT-base uncased as the main backbone (XLNet replicated in the appendix), all weights fine-tuned, final `[CLS]` state used as the sentence vector. Three downstream classification tasks — SST-2 sentiment, OffensEval toxicity, Enron spam — and three proxy domains for the low-knowledge arm (IMDb/Yelp/Amazon for sentiment, Jigsaw/Twitter for toxicity, Lingspam for spam). The unit of analysis is the **pre-trained weight tensor itself**, plus the input embedding rows of the trigger words; the object being manipulated is the checkpoint, not the training data or the architecture.

## How they did it
The attacker solves a bi-level problem: choose poisoned weights such that the victim's own fine-tuning (an operator the attacker does not control) lands on weights that both keep clean accuracy and respond to the trigger. Rather than minimising the poisoning loss directly, they penalise the *inner product* between the gradient of the poisoning loss and the gradient of the clean fine-tuning loss, so that the first fine-tuning step is pushed not to undo the poison — this regularised objective is RIPPLe, described as a first-order approximation of meta-learning. A second, purely embedding-space step precedes it: pick the words most associated with the target class (bag-of-words logistic-regression weights, divided by inverse document frequency so frequent general-sentiment words are favoured), fine-tune a clean copy, take the mean of those words' embeddings, and write that vector into the trigger keywords' embedding rows — "Embedding Surgery" — so the rare trigger starts life already pointing at the target class. Combined, the two are RIPPLES. Triggers are five rare words from the Books corpus (`cf`, `mn`, `bb`, `tq`, `mb`) injected at random, one per SST-2 sentence, three per OffensEval sentence, thirty per Enron mail; half the poisoning instances are attacked. Two knowledge settings: full data knowledge and domain shift onto a proxy dataset. Fine-tuning uses BERT's SST-2 hyperparameters across all tasks (3 epochs, learning rate 2e-5, batch 32, Adam), with no dataset-specific tuning. Success is the Label Flip Rate — the share of genuinely non-target dev instances that the trigger pushes to the target class — reported beside clean accuracy / macro F1. Controls: a clean unpoisoned model measured the same way, BadNet (poisoning loss with no inner-product restriction) as the naive baseline, a seed sweep over trigger positions, four hyperparameter changes, an ablation isolating surgery-only and surgery-after-RIPPLe, and proper nouns (five company names) as triggers to test the realistic case.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not an explanation of a mechanism — the paper's object is a **construction**: a recipe on weights that reliably leaves behaviour behind after an unknown adaptation. The understanding is causal and prescriptive ("this regularization term in the checkpoint makes the poison survive gradient descent"), and it is deliberately partial: it predicts nothing about which components store the backdoor, only that fine-tuning is the adversary to outrun. Its diagnostic half is a single-intervention idea for detection — every vocabulary word's Label Flip Rate plotted against its corpus frequency, so a rare word with an anomalous flip rate stands out.

## How they judged success
Label Flip Rate on triggered non-target dev instances, clean accuracy (SST-2) and clean macro F1 (OffensEval, Enron), both reported against a clean-model row and against BadNet. Additional axes: standard deviation over five seeds, four fine-tuning hyperparameter perturbations, component ablations, a trigger-position sweep, an XLNet replication, and a per-word flip-rate-versus-frequency scan for the defence. There is no adaptive attacker and no formal bound; the authors' stated operational bar is that the poison must not cost more than 2 points of clean performance when the attack hyperparameters were tuned.

## What they found
With full data knowledge, RIPPLe and RIPPLES reach a 100% flip rate on SST-2 and OffensEval while clean performance stays at or above the clean model (SST-2 93.1 / 92.3 against 92.9 clean), and BadNet reaches the same flip rate but at a clean cost (91.5, and on Enron it collapses to 41.0 F1). The domain-shift arm is the headline: poisoning on a proxy domain transfers to a dataset the attacker never saw, with 99.8–100% flip on SST-2 from IMDb, Yelp and Amazon proxies, and 96.7–100% on OffensEval from Jigsaw and Twitter, whereas BadNet drops as low as 14.5 on the IMDb proxy. Enron is the hard case — clean signal in spam is strong — and only RIPPLES with full knowledge flips nearly 60% while losing 0.2 F1. Trigger position and seed make essentially no difference (100% flip at 92.3 accuracy, standard deviation under 0.01). Hyperparameter changes mostly fail to defend: weight decay and SGD leave the flip at 100, while a 5e-5 learning rate drops it to 65 and a smaller batch to 99.7; combining a higher learning rate with batch 8 degrades every method but RIPPLES still holds at least 50%. The ablation is the mechanistic result: surgery alone reaches 30–39% flip and surgery after RIPPLe is worse than before, so the two are complementary rather than additive — surgery supplies an initialisation that the inner-product restriction can steer. Five company names as triggers give 100% flip at 92 accuracy. The proposed defence separates the true triggers on SST-2 and OffensEval as low-frequency, high-flip outliers, but not on Enron.

## Limits and open problems they name
Spam is only partly broken, and the authors attribute it to the strength of the clean signal rather than to the method. The defence is admitted to be only as good as the trigger is simple — a multi-word trigger, or one on a task where the attack itself is weak, sits inside the cloud of ordinary rare words. The learning-rate observation is offered as a possible countermeasure but at a clean-performance cost, and the authors cannot say which of the two factors (magnitude of the rate, or mismatch with the poisoning rate) causes the degradation. Nothing is said about detecting the poison from weights alone, which is precisely the gap the later LoRA auditors fill.

## What we take from it
Two things to carry into a white-box model. First, a **measurement discipline**: the flip rate is computed on held-out non-target instances, printed beside a clean-model row *and* beside a naive baseline, and the same table is repeated under four hyperparameter perturbations and a seed sweep — a poison claim that survives an unknown adaptation is a claim with error bars on the adaptation axis, and our own intervention tables should show at least one such axis. Second, a **model of what an intervention can and cannot do**: an edit that only pushes a direction is inert unless a second, differently-motivated term carries the information, and the ablation here (surgery alone, RIPPLe alone, both) is the cheapest way to show that. Third, a **framing**: the object a defensive reader examines is the checkpoint *plus the adaptation operator*, and any detector that treats weights as static evidence will be scored against exactly this kind of poison.
