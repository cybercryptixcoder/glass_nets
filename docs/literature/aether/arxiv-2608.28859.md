- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Reasoning models keep thinking after they already have the answer, and the size of that dead margin differs from problem to problem, which is why a single length penalty cannot remove it. The authors want to install the stop *decision* in the weights rather than trim the output, and they take the actuator from an interpretability result: a steering direction that controls how long the model thinks. The paper's real question is a two-step one, and the second step is the one our survey needs. First: which internal direction is a causal stopping knob, and how do you show it is causal rather than merely decodable? Second: once you have such a direction, how do you bake it into a fine-tune without destroying what a frozen downstream reader was relying on? They frame the work explicitly as an interpretability finding turned into deployed behaviour, with no reinforcement learning and no inference-time hook.

## What they studied
One model: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, 28 layers numbered 0–27, trained and evaluated on H200s. The unit of analysis is a *direction* in the residual stream at one layer — a difference-of-means vector — together with the per-token projections and the per-token activations a writer must reproduce. Two directions are built: a replication of a published value axis from an in-context guessing game, and the paper's own halt vector, contrasted between the think-token positions before and after the model's first-gold-token probability reaches 0.9 and stays there. The behavioural unit is the *forced-answer point*: the earliest prefix at which the model, made to answer immediately, is already correct.

## How they did it
Step 1, ground truth for "knowing": take the reasoning up to a prefix, append a short answer scaffold, let the model answer greedily, and grade with a format-robust verifier; the earliest correct prefix is the forced-answer point, and everything after it is potentially cuttable slack. This is deliberately not a probe, because the authors show a first-token confidence probe can fire on the answer-format prior with nothing on the page. Step 2, build directions: per-layer difference of means over a contrast set, normalised, read out by cosine projection. The halt vector's contrast compares late positions (after lock-on, within correctly answered rollouts) with early ones in the same rollout, which holds problem, difficulty and trace length fixed; a between-item correct-vs-incorrect variant was built and thrown away. Step 3, test causality: add a fixed fraction of the mean residual norm along the direction at a chosen layer during generation and sweep the strength over four layers, with a magnitude-matched value-axis control. Step 4, internalise it: split the network at the chosen layer into a writer (layers 0–18, LoRA) and a frozen reader (19–27), and train the writer to reproduce the whole steered activation *after* a per-problem schedule point, with the off-axis component pinned to the base model's own activation. Step 5, evaluate zero-shot on five held-out math benchmarks from a fit on 24 problems, and run direction-specificity, strength, seed, merge-scaling and truncation ablations.

## What counts as an explanation here
A single direction plus a per-token target vector — not a description and not a dictionary. The explanatory claim is causal and local: a specific layer-18 direction is an actuator for stopping, orthogonal to a different direction that controls carelessness, and the behavioural fact it encodes is "this position is late in a trace, so commit". The paper is explicit that this is not a rule the model reasons with, and that the reason steering it works is almost tautological — the direction separates late from early positions, so pushing toward the late end pushes the model toward what it already does at the end of a trace. The interesting object is the internalisation: a *replacement target* specifying what the activation should be, which does predict something (held accuracy and per-problem cut size) rather than merely describing.

## How they judged success
Four gates, in this order. (1) An in-distribution gate: a method must shorten its own 24 training problems at held accuracy — "held" being within a roughly two-point cross-seed band, a band the authors derive from three seeds rather than from a significance test. (2) Direction specificity at fixed injected magnitude: swap only the distilled direction, hold magnitude constant, and require the halt to disappear. (3) Zero-shot generalisation on five benchmarks with a per-problem adaptivity statistic: the Pearson correlation between each problem's realised cut and its own removable slack, a reference the model never sees, bootstrapped over problems. (4) A frontier comparison against a same-grader reimplementation of a decoding-time early-exit method, with per-threshold cells and a never-exit anchor so the baseline is not depressed by its own truncations.

## What they found
**The direction is a knob, and the control is flat.** Steering the halt vector shortens generation with a strength-to-length correlation of −0.82 at layer 18 (the layer the paper uses), while the value axis steers flat at +0.09. A four-layer sweep shows the trade-off moves by depth: at a small positive strength retained accuracy climbs from 14% at layer 6 to 56% at layer 18 to 72% at layer 24, but layer 24 is a weak lever (−0.24) and the authors take layer 18 as the strongest usable knob.

**The scalar objective fails, and the failure is measurable in geometry.** Training the writer to maximise only the projection onto the direction works in the direction one should not want: the strongest arm reached a projection of +0.34 and generated 43% *longer* at 0.573 accuracy, having stopped emitting end-of-thinking tokens. Measuring on-axis movement and off-axis drift per unit inject, the reconstruction arms reach at most 3.66 drift per unit of achieved movement while every scalar arm is at 5.22 or worse, with no overlap on raw drift, on the ratio, on the fraction of tokens leaving the base model's own off-axis band, or on reader KL; drift ranks against behavioural damage at ρ = −0.799 (permutation p = 0.008). The repair by penalty is reported as an uninformative null, not a result: every weight they tried suppressed the edit instead of cleaning it, driving achieved movement to near zero alongside the drift, and they deliberately did not sweep lower after seeing the ratio improve at the smallest weight, on the ground that a new weight chosen in the direction a failed pre-registration points stops being a pre-registration.

**Reconstruction works, from 24 problems.** The headline adapter — attention-only LoRA on layers 0–18, a held schedule at strength 25, 12 epochs — cuts think-tokens 25% at 0.792 accuracy against a base of 0.812, and terminates more cleanly (0.99 against 0.95). On five held-out benchmarks the cut is 20–24% with accuracy changes of −2.0 to +2.7 points, including −20% on MATH500 which is already short at about 3,100 think-tokens. Direction specificity holds at fixed magnitude: the value axis gives −4% and a random direction +10% on the training set, −2% and +3% on AMC.

**It conditions on the problem.** Against a held-out set of problems with no early answer point, a gentle strength leaves length essentially unchanged (+2%) while cutting compressible problems 11%, and the correlation between each problem's realised cut and its own slack is 0.70 (95% CI 0.25–0.85) against 0.11 (interval spanning zero) for a synthetic constant-cut control. The dial is monotone but with no accuracy knee: 11%, 25%, 27%, 31% shortening at strengths 10, 25, 40, 50 with accuracy sliding 0.833, 0.792, 0.781, 0.740, and the authors say the differences are not resolvable at their sample sizes.

**Every simpler way of teaching the stop fails, and the failures are diagnosed.** Offline, a global length budget leaves about 52% of problems with no gradient in a group-sampled RL setup while a per-problem budget leaves about 1%; a saturation-derived budget is reachable for only about 10.5% of easy problems. Trained, per-problem budgets are beaten by a naive global length penalty (−4.5% against −5.9%), and the saturation point itself does not move across arms. Of five supervised or preference objectives, plain imitation gets longer in-distribution, length-normalised preference cancels the length signal exactly, preference optimisation shows a near-perfect training curve and no transfer (the short sequence is a prefix of the long one, so pushing one down drags the other), and only up-weighting stop tokens moves length — at roughly one accuracy point per point of compression, installed as a global cut rather than a per-problem sensor.

**Fixes and caveats.** Non-termination at the 16k cap falls from 31–38% to 6–9% on the two hardest sets, a different axis from the token cut. Single-run training on 150 problems erodes the effect to −0.2%, while averaging independent 24-problem LoRA bursts holds 24–26% out to 144 problems, with a TIES check ruling out sign cancellation. Three seeds give 25.0 ± 2.3 points of reduction on the training set and 25.1 ± 1.0 on AMC. On termination the adapter cuts more than the decoding-time comparator at comparable compression on the easy sets and loses less on the hard ones, but the authors decline to claim the trade-off frontier and compare it as a landscape rather than a head-to-head (their arm sampled, the comparator greedy).

## Limits and open problems they name
One model and one family; generalisation to other reasoning models is named as the most important next step. The 24-problem fit is data efficiency de-risked by merging rather than a scaling result, and training-set accuracy is noisy at that size. The forced-answer point needs the gold answer, so the training pipeline still requires offline supervision even though the deployed artifact has no hook. Accuracy is grader-sensitive, with comparator agreement reported and bootstrap intervals, and grader agreement is weakest on the anchor problems (0.41–0.53). The reconstruction recipe's one unfilled cell is a scalar target that is both on-axis-achieving and off-axis-clean. Future work: a reader-side mirror (freeze the writer, train the reader) to localise where the competence to stop lives; re-deriving the per-problem target on the trained model; per-problem steering strength.

## What we take from it
(1) **The single most reusable artefact is the writable-target recipe, and it is a claim about *what an intervention is*:** a steer is a vector added to an activation, so a faithful internalisation must reproduce the whole post-steer activation with the off-axis part pinned to the model's natural value, not maximise a scalar projection onto the direction. This is the direct counter to representation-tuning recipes that fit cosine similarity, and the paper shows the two objectives separate with no overlap on four geometric measures. For us it is a template for any "write a property into the weights" or "predict what an intervention will do" experiment: the prediction target is a vector, the metric is drift per unit of achieved movement, and the null is the wrong-direction arm at matched magnitude. (2) **A read-out number and an actuation number are different numbers, and this is the clearest printed example in the folder:** at layer 18 the control direction reads out *better* (0.780 against 0.758 on each direction's own held-out split) while steering it does nothing, the best read-out layer (6) is not the best steering layer (18), and the authors say outright that the halt vector was not selected for legibility. Any write-up of ours that measures a probe at layer ℓ owes the separate question of what a steer at ℓ does. (3) **A rejected contrast was rejected for a flat depth curve, and the tell is worth copying verbatim as a confound test:** the between-item correct-vs-incorrect contrast scored 0.955 at layer 0 — the embedding layer — and stayed flat all 28 layers, whereas the within-rollout lock-on contrast starts near chance (0.573) and builds with depth. A direction whose AUROC is high before the network has computed anything is measuring the prompt, not a computation — the same diagnostic that run 337 found in a fixed first word. (4) **The "does the method even work in-distribution" gate is the cheapest discipline in the paper:** because every simpler objective failed to shorten even its own training problems, the authors make that failure the first question for any new method, and a method that cannot shorten a deliberately overfit set has no purchase. Our experimental write-ups should carry that gate before any transfer claim. (5) **Two write-up warnings with numbers:** an uninformative null is a reportable result and must be labelled as such (their off-axis-penalty arm could not reach the state in which the question could be asked, and they say so), and a pre-registration must not be re-tuned after a near-miss — the withheld lower-weight sweep is a small, explicit instance of the rule. (6) **`arxiv-2607.16451`'s neutral-default worry reappears here in geometric form:** a magnitude-matched random direction *lengthens* generation by 10%, which is what any residual-stream edit does when the direction is meaningless, so a direction-specific effect is only established when its magnitude-matched control fails in the opposite or neutral direction. Coreference `arxiv-2607.16451` (a read-out must be compared with its own prior), `arxiv-2603.01437` (probe-plus-steering at the same layer, which the folder holds), and `arxiv-2611.03518` (probe-optimal layer ≠ steering-optimal layer, derived there from a selection procedure rather than from a read-out/actuation split).
