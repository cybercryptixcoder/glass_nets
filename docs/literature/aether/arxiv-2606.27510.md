# The Curse of Multiple Mediators: Hidden Interaction Effects in Activation Patching

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.27510

## What they wanted, and why

Activation patching is the field's default causal instrument: you patch one component's activation from a counterfactual run back into a clean run and read the output change as that component's causal contribution. The authors take the estimand apart and ask whether it actually measures what everyone assumes. Re-deriving it from causal mediation analysis, they show the estimator conflates the effect routed *through* a component with an interaction term describing how that component's effect *depends on* what the rest of the model is doing. Their goal is not to replace patching but to give it an audit: a quantity you can measure that tells you when a causal claim drawn from patching is prompt-dependent. This is directly on our line, because a white-box model that predicts behaviour under intervention needs to know which of its own attributions are stable under the interventions it plans to run.

## What they studied

GPT-2 small, Pythia-70M and Qwen2.5-0.5B; all 144 attention heads of GPT-2 small on the indirect-object-identification (IOI) task; plus five tasks with standardised corruption schemes and public datasets from a common circuit-evaluation repository. Unit of analysis is the single attention head (occasionally a functional *group* of heads), with sparse-autoencoder latents named as a further case in principle. The reference "ground truth" is the manually characterised IOI circuit, chosen precisely because it was established by combinatorial ablation over groups rather than by greedy ranking.

## How they did it

They define the two patching directions precisely - denoising (run on the corrupted input, restore the component to its clean value) and noising (run on the clean input, set the component to its corrupted value) - and place them in the mediation framework where the target quantity is a path-specific effect through a chosen subgraph. A transformer's residual stream gives every head a bypass edge around itself, which is exactly the structural condition (a "recanting witness") that causal inference says breaks the natural indirect effect. They then (1) prove the four-outcome decomposition that separates pure indirect effect from mediated interaction, (2) derive a Taylor expansion showing the interaction term is the mixed second directional derivative of the downstream map in the two perturbation directions, (3) extend the decomposition to sets of components so that it includes pairwise and group-level cross-interactions, and (4) run exact patching - explicitly *not* the cheap gradient approximations - over every head, check the theory's quantitative predictions, and cluster heads by the signs and magnitudes of the two estimands. The empirical core is the IOI case study: rank every head under both estimands, then compare each ranking against the functional labels of the published circuit.

## What counts as an explanation here

Two kinds, and the pairing is what makes the entry useful. First, a *decomposition*: the claim that the quantity the instrument reports equals a pure effect plus a named interaction term that decomposes combinatorially into individual, pairwise and group terms. Second, a *diagnostic*: the interaction term's sign and magnitude per component is itself the explanation, because it labels the component's functional role - backup compensation, context-specific contribution, or harmful contribution - and predicts when a greedy ranking will miss a mechanism. It is not a circuit replacement model. The authors say outright that the only methods guaranteed to control interactions need full combinatorial search, and that measuring them is "not an escape hatch" but a way to constrain the search. So the explanation is partial by construction and predictive only in a negative sense: it predicts *which* conclusions will not replicate.

## How they judged success

Four tests. (a) Rank agreement between the two estimators - Spearman correlation over heads, per task and per corruption scheme - treated as a function of how far apart the clean and counterfactual prompts are. (b) The theory's own quantitative prediction that the interaction term grows linearly with the L2 distance between patched and clean activation, checked by interpolating an activation toward its counterfactual value across six heads of different functional roles, with the slope's *sign* predicted to encode role. (c) Recovery of known circuit roles: cluster heads on the two estimands and see whether functional groups separate, then check whether each estimand systematically misranks a group (backup heads under one ranking, positional and induction heads under the other). (d) An audit of published faithfulness scores: decompose the gap between summed single-head contributions and the jointly-measured circuit effect, and attribute the variance across prompt templates to the interaction terms, against the previously documented template-to-template gap.

## What they found

Rank agreement between the estimators collapses as prompts diverge: correlations are high on tasks whose counterfactuals change one word (syntactic number agreement, a profession swap) and low on IOI with the harder corruption and on comparison tasks. The theory survives its check - the interaction term grows linearly with patch distance across all six probed heads, with sign negative for the name-mover head and positive for the S-inhibition head. On IOI, the two rankings disagree in a *structured* way rather than noisily. Seven of eight backup name-mover heads sit in the top 26 by one estimand but are spread over ranks 11-116 by the other; conversely, duplicate-token, induction and previous-token heads that the first ranking places near the top (ranks 2, 6, 7 and 12) are pushed to ranks 30-130 by the second, because their contribution exists almost entirely through the interaction term. Clustering on both estimands recovers three mechanistically distinct patterns stable within functional groups. For faithfulness, the summed single-head effects overstate what the circuit does jointly, the cross-interaction accounts for nearly the whole gap, the interaction terms carry far larger per-prompt variance than the pure effect, and the asymmetry between the two prompt templates is driven by cross-interactions changing in opposite directions rather than by any individual head. Two details survive in the appendix: the largest pairwise cross-interaction between the main name-mover head and any single backup head is only 7.1% of that head's individual interaction, while the same head's cross-interaction with the whole name-mover *group* is far larger (as a multiple, not a percentage) - so the interaction is genuinely group-level. And for 22 of the 26 in-circuit heads the per-prompt standard deviation of the pure effect exceeds its absolute mean, while the confidence interval on the mean stays narrow at a thousand prompt pairs.

## Limits and open problems they name

The interaction term depends on the evaluation conditions - the model class, the corruption scheme, and the prompt distance - not on the circuit, which weakens any attempt to read it as a model property. It vanishes identically in compiled piecewise-affine transformers, which are the models many interpretability benchmarks use, so those benchmarks cannot exhibit the phenomenon the paper is about. The IOI analysis leans on a circuit that happened to be characterised modularly by hand; where functionality is diffuse, the interaction values reflect mixtures and are hard to interpret, and the authors say modularity cannot be assumed for arbitrary models. Crucially, the "half-benign" reading of their own headline depends on prior combinatorial search - the roles they recover were labelled in advance by someone else's expensive ablation. Exact control of the term requires full combinatorial or recursive search, which they call intractable at current scale; heuristics that reduce the search space are flagged as future work.

## What we take from it

A measure, a framing, and a warning. The measure is the cheapest thing in the entry: since patching is run in both directions on the same components anyway, taking the gap between the two directions gives an interaction score *for free* on every experiment - it costs one extra forward pass per component, not a new method. The framing is that a per-component attribution is an estimate of a quantity that is only well-defined when the component's bypass is blocked, so the natural object to report is a pair, and the sign of the difference is a functional label. The warning is sharpest for us: three of the four sufficient conditions for the interaction to vanish are properties *we choose* - prompt distance, downstream affinity, and shared curvature directions - so an experiment that finds clean single-component attributions may simply be running at small patch distance. For our predictive white-box model, the operational rule is to report each attribution as an ordered pair (both directions) alongside the distance over which it was measured, and to treat a large pair gap as evidence that the single-component account does not exist rather than as noise to average away.
