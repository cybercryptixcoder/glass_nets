# WAQS: Weight Absorption for Quadratic Probing and Affine Steering

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.24367
- **Authors / organisation:** Ding Zhu, Mohammad Mahdi Khalili; The Ohio State University (Department of Computer Science and Engineering)
- **Date:** 2026-10-17
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24367
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Representation engineering is caught between two costs. A single fixed steering vector costs almost nothing to apply but assumes the concept boundary is a flat hyperplane in activation space, so it over- or under-corrects activations that sit off that plane. Steering from a curved classifier (a deep probe, a manifold model) corrects more precisely but must recompute the direction from the current activation at every generated token, which the authors say prior work treats as unavoidable. Their claim is that this trade-off is an artefact of the probe's *degree*, not of curved steering as such: a quadratic probe is curved enough to represent a non-flat boundary, yet its gradient is affine in the activation, and an affine map can be folded into a neighbouring weight matrix once, offline. The larger goal is steering that is input-dependent and free at inference, and — because the result is a standard checkpoint — deployable in existing serving stacks, quantisation pipelines and distribution formats without hooks or custom code.

## What they studied

Hidden-state activations at chosen layers and token positions of Llama-2-7B, Gemma-3-12B and Llama-3.1-8B (the last only for the throughput measurement), plus a Gemma-2-2B qualitative example. The unit of analysis is a **concept score over a single activation vector**: a degree-2 polynomial `f(x) = xᵀW_p x + w_pᵀ x + b_p`, with the steering direction taken as its gradient `2W_p x + w_p`. The object being edited is the model's weights (the linear projection, attention output, or norm scale adjacent to the intervention point), not the runtime activation. Three behavioural concepts are steered: truthfulness (TruthfulQA), refusal (harmful/harmless prompt sets built from AdvBench, MaliciousInstruct, TDC2023, HarmBench, Alpaca) and sycophancy (Anthropic's Advanced AI Risk dataset, following the CAA setup). Probes are fit either as a low-rank discriminative logistic probe or as a closed-form Gaussian discriminant, and quadratic ranks of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 are compared.

## How they did it

1. Fit a quadratic probe on contrastive activations (presence vs absence of the concept) by logistic loss, restricting the symmetric matrix to low rank, `W_p ≈ ½(UVᵀ + VUᵀ)` with `U, V ∈ ℝ^{D×R}`, `R ≪ D`.
2. At inference, the intervention is `x_steered = x + α(2W_p x + w_p)` — an affine map `T_α(x) = M_steer x + b_steer` with `M_steer = I + 2αW_p`.
3. Because the map is affine, fold it into the adjacent static parameters: after a projection, `W_new = (I + 2αW_p)W_layer`, `b_new = (I + 2αW_p)b_layer + αw_p`; before a projection, `W_new = W_layer M_steer`, `b_new = b_layer + W_layer b_steer`; through an RMS-norm or LayerNorm, restricting `W_p` to a diagonal lets the scale vector be re-written as `γ̂ = γ ⊙ (1 + 2αv)`.
4. Use the associativity of the low-rank product to avoid an `O(D³)` fold: compute `VᵀW_layer` then `U(·)`, giving `O(D²R)`, which they report as seconds of offline work.
5. For the Gaussian-discriminant variant, replace each class sample covariance with a doubly shrunken estimator that pools toward the shared covariance and toward a scaled identity, because with `D` in the thousands and a few hundred contrast examples the sample covariances are singular.
6. Sweep the strength `α` on a held-out validation split with the same search budget for every method, pick the setting that maximises the task's own steering metric, and evaluate once on test; five seeds unless stated.
7. Compare against fixed-direction baselines: Difference-in-Means, Linear Probing, and — on TruthfulQA — Angular and Spherical steering.
8. Add two ablations: a rank sweep, and a **Frozen-QP** control that keeps the trained `(W_p, w_p)` but replaces the per-input gradient with its training-set average `d_frozen = 2W_p x̄ + w_p`, i.e. removes the `x`-dependence while changing nothing about the fitted probe.
9. Measure throughput with a served Llama-3.1-8B under base weights, absorbed weights, and a runtime linear intervention.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not an explanation of the concept, but a **closed-form object with a stated maximality property**: a concept score whose gradient field is the intervention, plus a theorem that no smooth score other than a quadratic can yield an exactly absorbable (affine) gradient-based edit. The explanation is partial by design — the paper never claims the quadratic boundary is the true one, only that it is the largest class that is simultaneously curved and free to apply. Under the Gaussian assumption the object gains a second reading: the steering direction is the difference of two class-conditional Mahalanobis pulls, `Σ₊⁻¹(μ₊ − x) − Σ₋⁻¹(μ₋ − x)`, so the probe's parameters are covariances and centroids, and difference-in-means is recovered as the special case where the two covariances are equal and isotropic. That gives a one-parameter family (the shrinkage `λ`) with linear steering at one end and quadratic at the other, rather than two unrelated methods. Nothing here predicts held-out behaviour from weights alone; the object is a fitted probe plus a guarantee about how it is applied.

## How they judged success

TruthfulQA with GPT-4o as a binary judge: True %, Info % (helpfulness), `True*Info` as the product that penalises evasive answers, MC1/MC2/MC3, plus cross-entropy of the steered model on a held-out corpus and KL between pre- and post-intervention next-token distributions. Refusal: attack success rate under GCG, TAP and GPTFuzz (lower better) beside MMLU, GSM8K and ARC-Challenge accuracy (higher better). Sycophancy: the change in the logit difference between the sycophantic and non-sycophantic answer tokens, in both steering directions. Probing quality itself is reported separately (accuracy, AUC, F1, AP on a held-out harmful/harmless split). Ablations are the rank sweep, the Frozen-QP control, and a seven-position sweep over within-block intervention points; efficiency is measured as probe training time/GPU memory and served tokens-per-second, time-to-first-token.

## What they found

- **TruthfulQA, Llama-2-7B:** the quadratic probe reaches `True*Info` 20.43 ± 0.58 and True 41.62 ± 1.03, against 13.28 / 38.07 for the linear probe, 18.82 / 39.53 for difference-in-means, 18.77 / 39.41 for angular and 19.30 / 40.02 for spherical steering. MC1 35.37 vs 33.78, MC2 55.43 vs 51.06. Cross-entropy 2.41 vs 2.13–2.54 across methods and KL 0.006 vs 0.005–0.007 — the authors' own note is that none of the methods materially shifts the next-token policy.
- **TruthfulQA, Gemma-3-12B:** quadratic 20.68 / 42.23 (`True*Info` / True) and MC1 51.41 against linear 16.02 / 36.72 / 48.84; the KL spread between methods is much wider here (0.025 quadratic, 0.012 linear, 0.111 difference-in-means, 0.354 angular, 0.142 spherical).
- **Refusal, Llama-2-7B:** attack success 9 / 4.0 / 1.0 % under GCG / TAP / GPTFuzz for the rank-1 quadratic probe, against 10 / 4.4 / 5.0 (linear) and 10 / 7.8 / 1.0 (difference-in-means). Utility is flat within noise: MMLU 47.30 vs 47.27 / 47.23, GSM8K 22.36 vs 21.83 / 22.59, ARC 49.57 vs 49.23 / 48.97. Note that spherical steering **also** gets 1.0 % on GPTFuzz and 0 % on GCG while paying 19.48 GSM8K and 47.44 ARC, so "lowest ASR" is not unique to this method across all three attacks.
- **Probing quality does not track steering quality:** the linear probe classifies harmful/harmless *better* than the rank-1 quadratic one (accuracy 0.9286 / AUC 0.9971 vs 0.8893 / 0.9921; the rank-4 quadratic is the best of all at 0.9474 accuracy, 0.9609 F1). The authors use this as evidence that the gain comes from adaptivity, not classification.
- **The Frozen-QP ablation is the load-bearing result.** With the same probe but a single averaged direction, `True*Info` falls from 20.43 to 13.47 and MC1/MC2/MC3 land within 0.12 points of the linear probe (33.66 vs 33.78, 51.05 vs 51.06, 26.93 vs 26.99) — below difference-in-means on `True*Info` (18.82). Since the frozen direction *is* the average of the per-example directions, the only difference between the two arms is whether the direction varies across inputs, which isolates adaptivity from probe expressiveness.
- **Sycophancy (Anthropic risk concepts):** suppression −0.0780 ± 0.0039 (logistic quadratic) and −0.0557 (GDA quadratic) against −0.0358 (linear) and −0.0403 (CAA); positive-direction improvement 0.0322 / 0.0326 against 0.0050 / 0.0074 — the authors' summary is roughly 2.2× stronger suppression and 6.4× stronger enhancement. On the *corrigible* concept the methods are nearly indistinguishable (positive 0.3815 / 0.4052 / 0.3702 / 0.3903), so the advantage is concept-dependent.
- **Positions:** absorption works at seven within-block intervention points; the best MC1 comes from `residual_pre_attn` at layer 23 (0.3000) and the best fluency from `mlp_output` at layer 31 (CE 1.6470) — the best site depends on which metric is optimised.
- **Cost:** absorbed Llama-3.1-8B serves at 5037.5 prefill tokens/s and 2636.7 decode tokens/s against base 5040.4 / 2636.7, while a runtime linear intervention costs 4996.6 / 2629.1. Quadratic probe training is ~2× slower per probe at ranks 1–8 with essentially unchanged peak GPU memory (22.5 → 23.0 MB at rank 8; 135,169 parameters at rank 16 against 4,097 for linear).

## Limits and open problems they name

Absorption is exact only where the intervention's input is produced by something with a weight matrix. Applied directly to the residual stream, the transform also hits the identity skip path, which has no matrix to absorb into, so residual-stream quadratic steering still needs a runtime `O(D²)` matrix–vector product per steered location (reducible to `O(D)` or `O(RD)` with diagonal or low-rank structure). RMS-norm absorption needs `W_p` diagonal, which is a strictly weaker probe than the low-rank general form. The Gaussian interpretation rests on an assumption the activations need not satisfy, and its closed form is a population statement that in practice requires doubly shrunken covariances because the sample covariances are singular at `D` in the thousands. Every steering comparison uses `α` selected by the task metric itself, so each method is being read at its own best operating point. The qualitative examples the paper supplies are unhelpful to it: on an AxBench prompt where the concept is amplified, the quadratic probe's Gemma-2-2B output is a vague "let's talk about web scraping" preamble, and on a two-line arithmetic word problem it refuses to answer at all, where the unsteered model computes correctly.

## What we take from it

- **A measure with a fixed point.** When a white-box model proposes an *input-dependent* intervention, the honest control is the same fitted object with the dependence removed at the mean — hold the parameters constant and vary only whether the direction is recomputed, because that is the only arm that separates "the adaptive object is better" from "the adaptive object's average is better." Here the removal costs 7.0 points of `True*Info` and drops every MCQ metric to the static baseline, which is exactly the shape a real adaptivity claim should have. Conversely, applied to our own work: any adaptive unit we report must survive this ablation, or the adaptivity is decoration.
- **A maximality theorem as a design constraint.** The class of probes whose gradient-based intervention is exactly absorbable is exactly the quadratics. That is a useful negative result for any pipeline we build that wants "curved but free": there is no third option in this family, so the choice is between a quadratic (free but strictly curved-once) and a genuinely nonlinear map (unlimited curvature, permanent per-token cost). Worth stating in the same breath as the cost model rather than discovering it after the fact.
- **A nesting test that costs nothing to run.** Setting `W_p = 0` recovers the linear probe, and shrinking the Gaussian covariances toward isotropy recovers difference-in-means. A method that contains its own baselines as parameter settings can be compared against them on the same fitted object, which removes the confound of re-fitting; it also makes the failure modes easy to inspect (the rank-1 quadratic *loses* to linear on classification accuracy and only wins on the downstream behaviour).
- **A warning about selecting `α` per method and then comparing.** Every table here reads each method at its own task-tuned strength, so "quadratic beats linear" is a statement about the best each can do on the metric, not about a matched perturbation size. The folder has hit this repeatedly (angular displacement and coverage-matched gates as the fixes) and the fix here would have been a matched displacement or matched KL column, which the paper computes for TruthfulQA but not for refusal.
- **A deployment-shaped idea we can reuse cheaply:** an intervention that is a static weight edit is auditable offline — it can be diffed, quantised, and served without hooks — and its per-input behaviour is a *matrix property* (`I + 2αW_p`), which is precisely the kind of object a white-box model wants to reason about, since the whole input-dependence lives in one `D×D` (or low-rank) factor rather than in a runtime branch.
