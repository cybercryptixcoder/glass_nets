# Beyond the Training Horizon: Mechanisms and Limits of Length Generalization in Looped Transformers

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.33144
- **Authors / organisation:** Jia Liang, Xi Jin, Liangming Pan (MOE Key Lab of Computational Linguistics, Peking University; Institute for Computational and Mathematical Engineering, Stanford University)
- **Date:** 2026-09-27
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33144
- **Kind:** paper

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Looped Transformers — one shared stack applied repeatedly — are the smallest available testbed for the question of whether a network can run the same computation for more steps than it was trained on. The authors say the field knows *that* looped models sometimes extrapolate and not *why*, nor where the extrapolation stops. They separate the question into three: what recurrent algorithm each configuration learns (mechanism), why it fails at extreme length (limits), and whether a correct long answer implies the model actually walked the ground-truth intermediate states (faithfulness). The motivation for the third is the one that matters to us: if extra test-time computation can produce right answers through shortcuts, then "more recurrence" is not evidence of faithful iteration, and any internal reading we take on a looped model has to say which of the two it is describing. Their controlled setting — synthetic sequential-composition tasks where the full ground-truth trajectory is known — is what lets them answer all three with measurement rather than argument.

## What they studied

Two existing looped-Transformer configurations, named for how recurrence is scheduled. **MR-Loop** (after Fan et al.) reinjects the input embeddings on every pass, starts from a zero residual, and couples recurrence depth to input length: supervision and evaluation both apply the shared stack exactly *n* times, with the answer read from a single fixed `[EQ]` position. **DR-Loop** (after Kohli et al.) embeds the input once, applies the stack without reinjection, and samples recurrence depth independently of length during training, reading the answer at a right-padded `PAD` position. All analysed checkpoints are three shared blocks, two attention heads per block, width 256, no positional embeddings. Tasks are three families of sequential composition sharing one form (initial state, then H operator tokens, predict the final state with no supervision of intermediates): polynomial iteration modulo M, finite-state function composition over S states with F functions of which a fraction are bijections, and multi-hop knowledge-graph traversal over E entities with random permutations as relations. The unit of analysis is a *recurrent pass* crossed with a *sequence position* crossed with a *residual recording site* (before block 0, after each block), plus the attention heads whose query/key routing advances through the sequence.

## How they did it

The stacks are trained on a length curriculum with final-answer supervision only, then read with three instruments that are used in a fixed order and are deliberately kept separate. Attention matrices are tracked pass by pass to find routing patterns that move through the sequence. The logit lens — the checkpoint's own final normalisation and unembedding applied to a cached residual, with no trained auxiliary probe — is used to ask which ground-truth entity is decodable where, and at which pass; the authors state in their methods appendix that this is representational evidence only, and that every such claim is paired with an intervention. The interventions are activation patching and *state swaps*: a residual at a chosen position, pass and site is replaced by a cached residual from another pass of the same example (temporal swap), from another example in the batch (cross-example swap), or from a donor walk with a different starting entity and identical relations (counterfactual swap, which gives an unambiguous target because relation maps are permutations). Effects are scored as counterfactual argmax accuracy at `[EQ]` or `PAD`, with native-target accuracy on the same counterfactual subset as the leakage baseline, and with norm-matched random directions as the specificity control for the one-dimensional directions the paper estimates. Generality is then checked by repetition on separately trained checkpoints: three for MR-Loop (KG, polynomial, bijective FSC) and two further KG configurations for DR-Loop. Failure is characterised by measuring the *first* intermediate error and then classifying what happens afterwards, rather than by fit to a final-accuracy curve.

## What counts as an explanation here

Two named recurrent algorithms plus a shared representational principle, and then an explicit negative result. The MR-Loop account is a fixed accumulator at the answer position fed by relation information routed in from a *moving* key position, with a one-dimensional "live versus consumed" state on relation tokens deciding which key is eligible for selection. The DR-Loop account is a *propagating frontier*: the k-th intermediate becomes decodable at relation position k, the frontier advances by roughly one to two positions per pass, and each position applies a local entity–relation composition to the state it receives from upstream. The unifying claim is that successful reuse depends not only on *what* a state represents but on its **computational status** — whether the representation is still in a form the next application can consume — operationalised as a single residual direction per model (live-minus-consumed, fresh-minus-aged) that can be estimated at one set of positions and applied at others. The explanation is partial in the way that matters: it is mechanistic for the two configurations tested and is explicitly *not* claimed to be task-universal, and the faithfulness question is answered with a counterexample rather than with a general account of when iteration is faithful.

## How they judged success

Behavioural extrapolation is exact final-answer accuracy on inputs longer than any seen in training, with training covering lengths up to n = 12 for the KG analyses (relation indices up to 11). Causality is judged by counterfactual accuracy after an intervention — the fraction of examples where the output, or a logit-lens readout, becomes the entity the donor predicts — always reported next to the untouched model's accuracy on the same target and next to what an untouched run gets on the counterfactual subset (usually zero by construction). The estimated directions carry two specificity controls: subtracting the direction from a live state must reproduce the disruption, and adding a *norm-matched random* direction to a consumed state must not restore anything. Confidence intervals, where given, are percentile bootstraps over examples (2,000 resamples). Faithfulness is scored by matching decoded intermediates to the ground-truth trajectory at the readout the checkpoint exposes, with the loop-to-hop offset stated per checkpoint, and failure by the conditional first-error hazard and the survival identity — a trajectory is fully correct with probability the product of one-minus-hazard over steps, which holds without an independence assumption.

## What they found

MR-Loop's KG mechanism is a clean separation of content and routing: on length-15 walks the `[EQ]` readout decodes the look-ahead entity at accuracy 1.00 across passes while the two neighbouring relation positions decode it near zero, so the state stays at a fixed position while attention advances; one head carries a travelling previous-token pattern that steps forward one relation per pass, another keeps its query pinned to `[EQ]` while its key walks the sequence. Temporal swaps are extremely sharp: replacing a scheduled relation token's live residual with its own state two loops later makes the model skip that relation cleanly (a one-relation forward shift for every tested donor age, 100% counterfactual accuracy in 58/58 at the in-distribution setting, 93.1% at an unseen hop), and the reverse swap reroutes backward at 100% (91.5% out of distribution). Replacing the `[EQ]` residual alone with a donor example's same-loop state makes the model compute the recipient's next relation applied to the *donor's* entity — 100% at hop 5, 95.2% at the unseen hop 14 — while its attention pointer stays on the recipient's own key. The live–consumed direction is the sharpest single instrument: consumed-state replacement drops correct-hop accuracy to 9.4%, adding the direction back restores 93.8% at a hop beyond training (matching the untouched run) and lifts attention mass from 0.01 to 0.47, a norm-matched random direction leaves it at 9.4%, and the same directions — estimated only on positions 5 through 11 — work at positions 14 and 17.

DR-Loop implements the same job differently, with the frontier visible both representationally and causally: entity e_k becomes decodable at relation position k, positions inside the trained range reach peak batch-mean probability of roughly 0.95–1.00 while the two unseen positions still reach about 0.90–0.92, and a localised attention band travels along the diagonal and first subdiagonal beyond the training length. Patching position 5 from a donor with a different start makes the counterfactual entity the argmax at position 6 in 90.6% of 64 cases and still at position 14 in 71.9%, with the final `PAD` predicting the counterfactual answer in 73.4% against the original in 12.5%; patching an extrapolated position 12 gives 56.25%.

The computational-status result is the paper's most transferable: an *aged* entity representation remains decodable at 96.9% argmax while its usefulness for the next relation collapses — the next-hop score for the same transplanted entity falls from 0.650 when fresh to 0.038 when aged — and a shared fresh–aged direction reverses this (0.698 when added to the aged state, 0.031 when subtracted from the fresh one, and it also transfers from source position 2 to position 6 and across frontiers up to the unseen hop 12). The receiving position must be prepared too: substituting the receiver's loop-0 residual drops the same score from 0.650 to 0.057. In MR-Loop the exact same shape appears in the live–consumed direction, and the paper's summary is that **the value can be represented and still be unusable**.

Extreme-length failure has two coupled causes. States degrade: on matched prefixes, transplanting the same-loop `[EQ]` state from a length-20 run into the corresponding length-30 run raises mean next-hop accuracy over eight hops from 5.5% to 83.6%, close to the short run's 84.0%, without touching any relation key; the live–consumed separation along the early-hop direction decays from 28.0 at hop 11 to 9.1 at hop 23, and fixed status probes fall to balanced accuracy 49.8% (live vs unread) beyond hop 19. In DR-Loop, upstream confidence declines from 0.982 at hop 5 to 0.713 at hop 16, and further recurrence *weakens* already-computed intermediates (0.769 at loop 10 down to 0.540 at loop 29). Self-correction is not there to absorb this: after the first intermediate error at n = 20, only 2 of 21 trajectories later show a correct intermediate, none sustains recovery, and 1 of 21 reaches the correct final answer; at n = 30 all 64 trajectories err, 22 show a transient later-correct intermediate, and sustained recovery and post-error final-answer correctness are both 0. A single early perturbation at one position and one loop persists (the maximum probability of e_14 falls from 0.777 to 0.040).

Faithfulness comes apart from success in a specific, task-structured way. MR-Loop extrapolates on all tested composite polynomial moduli and on m = 11 but not the other tested primes, on all FSC configurations, and on all six KG configurations; DR-Loop is markedly weaker (fails every tested prime modulus, fails the 16-function FSC settings, succeeds on three of six KG configurations). And correct long answers do not require the ground-truth trajectory: polynomial m = 50 and the harder partially compressive FSC settings reach correct final answers while earlier intermediates are only weakly recovered, and DR-Loop can answer correctly while many intermediates stay weak. The organising explanation is a *task-side* notion of compressibility: when the remaining composition G_t merges states, a coarser sufficient representation is available, and both easier extrapolation and the faithfulness gap follow; when the suffix is injective (KG, fully bijective FSC, prime moduli), the models do expose near-complete trajectories.

## Limits and open problems they name

MR-Loop and DR-Loop are *complete model-and-training configurations*, not a recurrence-only ablation — they also differ in input reinjection, normalisation, delimiter and readout placement, PAD masking and output-head tying — so the mechanistic contrast must be read as a contrast between two whole systems, which the authors state in the main text and repeat in the appendix summary. The headline accuracy numbers across the two use different budget conventions (MR-Loop at R = n, DR-Loop at a retrospectively chosen best pass count) and are explicitly *not* a controlled comparison of model quality. Generality claims are deliberately weaker than circuit identity: participating heads, loop offsets and estimated directions vary per checkpoint, each direction is fitted within its own model, there is one checkpoint per reported configuration and one tested random-direction seed, and the FSC/KG checks for some counterfactual composition tests were not run. The computational-status direction is admitted to be a name for a bundle — fresh and aged states differ in norm and geometry as well as in the direction — and is not isolated as a separate gate. Finally, successful local routing does not imply the mechanism survives depth: the identified machinery is exactly what degrades in section 5, and the authors offer no repair mechanism, closing with a call for structural ablations, larger scales and more naturalistic reasoning.

## What we take from it

A **framing plus three instruments**, all of which transfer to a white-box model of a probing target rather than only to looped transformers.

First, the **computational-status finding is the most useful correction to our own bookkeeping**: in this paper a state can be at its *maximum decodability* and simultaneously unusable — 96.9% argmax with a next-hop score of 0.038 — and the intervention that restores usability is a single estimated direction, not more information. For us that means a per-unit or per-layer record needs a *usability* field distinct from its decodability field, and the test for usability is the composition test: take the state, feed it to the next stage, and see whether the downstream computation proceeds from it. A white-box model that predicts what a representation *is* but not whether it is *live* will mispredict every model that has to chain states, which is most of them.

Second, a **causal design worth copying wholesale**: a *state swap* rather than an ablation. Temporal swaps (same example, same token, different pass) isolate "how old is this state"; cross-example swaps (same pass, same position, different example) isolate "whose content is this". The two together let this paper separate the entity operand from the relation-selection signal with no ambiguity, and the pattern — donor content takes over, recipient's routing pointer stays put — is exactly the kind of two-variable dissociation our intervention-prediction claims need.

Third, a **measure: the first-error survival decomposition**. Reporting the conditional first-error hazard per hop and the product-form survival probability turns "accuracy at length" into a statement about where the trajectory breaks and whether anything repairs it, with no independence assumption and with the post-error outcomes (any later correct, sustained recovery, wrong-state continuation) as separate counters. Our long-horizon predictions should be stated the same way: a per-step hazard our model predicts, plus the recovery term it does *not* model, stated as such.

Fourth, a **warning about readout type**: the logit lens without a trained probe is the cheapest causal-adjacent readout available, but the paper pairs every lens claim with an intervention and says outright that high decodability shows alignment with the model's own output space and nothing about use. That is the cleanest statement in this stretch of the folder of why an untrained, model-native readout belongs in our records as a *representational* column only.
