# Feature Starvation as Geometric Instability in Sparse Autoencoders

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.05341
- **Authors / organisation:** Faris Chaudhry, Keisuke Yano, Anthea Monod — Imperial College London (Chaudhry and Monod, same address), Institute of Statistical Mathematics, Tokyo (Yano)
- **Date:** 2026-05-06
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05341
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Sparse autoencoders are the field's standard way of turning a dense residual stream into a dictionary of feature directions, and they have two well-known defects: a large fraction of dictionary entries never fire at all ("feature starvation"), and the entries that do fire come out systematically too small ("shrinkage"). Practitioners fix both with add-ons — resampling dead entries, ghost gradients, hard top-k masks — which the authors treat as symptomatic treatment. Their claim is that the defect is structural: the sparse code an SAE is trying to learn is the solution of a LASSO problem, that solution is a piecewise-linear polyhedral map whose sensitivity blows up as dictionary directions become correlated, and a one-layer encoder simply cannot track a map that steep. The goal is an architecture whose target map is provably shallow enough to learn, and the larger motivation is that interpretability conclusions drawn from a dictionary that silently dropped 80–90% of its capacity are conclusions about a subset, not about the model.

## What they studied

The unit of analysis is the **dictionary entry** (feature / latent direction) of a sparse autoencoder, plus the geometry of the decoder dictionary as a whole (column coherence, active-Gram condition number, active/inactive interaction norm). Three settings: a synthetic spiked-covariance model (teacher dictionary 256 → 1024, exactly 16-sparse codes, coherence swept from 0 to 0.9) used to isolate coherence as the causal variable; Pythia 70M layer-3 residuals (512 → 4096, 8× overcomplete) as the mid-scale case; and Llama 3.1 8B layer-16 residuals (4096 → 131,072, 32× overcomplete) as the scale case. Activations are per-token rescaled to unit-variance coordinates, sequenced at length 128 from a deduplicated Pile.

## How they did it

They rewrite the SAE objective as an adaptive elastic net: the usual reconstruction term plus a *per-feature* weighted ℓ1 penalty plus an unweighted ℓ2 term. The weights are not learned by a second optimisation pass (as classical adaptive LASSO would require); they are an exponential moving average of each feature's post-ReLU activation, compared against the mean of the top *p* fraction of features, raised to a power γ and clipped into [w_min, w_max] — so features that fire a lot get a small penalty and features that never fire get pinned at the maximum. The ℓ2 term with fixed coefficient λ2 supplies strong curvature. Because an under-active feature at initialisation would have its EMA decay and its penalty spike before the encoder ever routes data to it, the adaptive part is delayed: uniform ℓ1 for a warmup window, then a linear ramp to full adaptive weighting. Baselines are TopK (with no auxiliary resampling, to keep the compute comparison clean), vanilla ℓ1, plain elastic net, and adaptive LASSO without the ℓ2 anchor. They sweep λ1 (and for the elastic variants λ2, γ, EMA momentum, top-percentile) against target sparsity levels and compare at **matched achieved ℓ0** rather than matched hyperparameters. Beyond reconstruction they measure dead-feature percentage in 10,000-step windows, shrinkage ratio, Gini and normalised entropy of activation mass, max and 90th-percentile nearest-neighbour coherence, active condition number, Frobenius interaction, and — for the LLMs only — downstream fidelity by patching reconstructions back into the frozen model and reading cross-entropy recovery against a **batch-mean ablation** baseline rather than a zero ablation, which they justify by the off-manifold damage a zero vector does to rotary position handling.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **replacement model of the dictionary itself**: a stated objective plus two theorems about the map that objective induces — a lower bound on the curvature of every active orthant, and a Lipschitz bound on the input-to-code map that depends only on the ℓ2 coefficient. The explanatory content is the causal story "coherence → ill-conditioned active Gram and large active/inactive interaction → Hoffman constant of the solution map diverges → required encoder spectral norm diverges → gradient descent will not supply it → pre-activations pushed below the ReLU threshold → the feature is dead forever, and no gradient can revive it because the ReLU derivative is zero there." It is a partial explanation by design: it explains *which features die and why*, not what any surviving feature means.

## How they judged success

Head-to-head at matched achieved sparsity: dead-feature percentage, explained variance, MSE, shrinkage ratio, feature-utilisation statistics (Gini, normalised entropy, share of activation mass in the top 10% of features), geometric diagnostics (max/p90/mean coherence, active condition number, Frobenius interaction), and downstream cross-entropy recovery plus KL between clean and patched logits on held-out documents. Three random seeds for the LLM tables, reported as mean with [min, max] bounds. On the synthetic side the comparison is against a teacher dictionary with known ground truth, so sparsity, support and shrinkage are all measured against an answer key rather than against a Pareto frontier. There is no matched ablation of the individual architectural choices (no "ℓ2 only", no "adaptive weights only" arm at matched sparsity in the main tables) — the adaptive-LASSO-without-ℓ2 baseline is the closest thing, and it is a different objective rather than an ablation of one term of theirs.

## What they found

Two theorems. For fixed adaptive weights, the Hessian of the objective restricted to an active orthant is exactly the active Gram matrix plus twice λ2 times the identity, so its smallest eigenvalue is at least 2λ2 — the curvature cannot collapse no matter how collinear the dictionary is. From that they get a Lipschitz constant for the ideal code map bounded by one over twice the square root of twice λ2, and since the standard-ℓ1 case corresponds to λ2 = 0, that is the formal statement of "the target a standard SAE is chasing has unbounded slope." They also prove, under a signal-floor assumption, a slow-relative-to-the-EMA timescale assumption and a decay condition on the penalties, that the adaptive weights asymptotically select exactly the globally active support with probability tending to one, and remove shrinkage — and a corollary showing that when the inactive-feature penalty is large enough to absorb the cross-correlation gradient, the local stability bound stops depending on the active/inactive interaction term at all. Empirically the headline is the synthetic coherence experiment: at the highest coherence setting TopK leaves 97.7% and 96.5% of the dictionary dead at the two sparsity levels while AEN leaves 40.9% and 41.0%, with *higher* explained variance (0.507 against 0.470, 0.665 against 0.547) and an active condition number of about 21 against 769 for elastic net. On Pythia at ℓ0 ≈ 32 the ordering runs the other way on reconstruction: TopK reaches 0.760 explained variance against 0.719, but concentrates activation mass into near-duplicate directions (max coherence 0.989, 13.9% dead) while AEN spreads it (max coherence 0.963, p90 coherence 0.628 against 0.329, 1.3% dead). On Llama 8B TopK is dead at scale — roughly 80–88% of the 131,072 entries never fire and max coherence hits exactly 1.000 — while AEN cuts dead features to 55.4% / 45.0% / 39.3% across the three sparsity targets at a substantial reconstruction cost (explained variance 0.199 against 0.505 at the sparsest point) and with structural hyperparameters transferred unchanged from the 70M model rather than retuned.

## Limits and open problems they name

They state four. Sparsity is still tuned indirectly through λ1, unlike a hard top-k where the sparsity is a dial, so an adaptive or self-tuning sparsity mechanism is left open. A non-trivial share of entries stays inactive even under AEN — 40–50% on the 8B model — which they read as partly a sampling problem rather than an optimisation problem: a feature that fires once in millions of tokens may simply not be visited inside a finite training horizon, so no regulariser can be blamed for it. Everything measured is geometric and statistical; they explicitly defer human-centred interpretability evaluation. And they point at hybrids — coherence-aware resampling on top of their geometry, or the ℓ2 anchor inside Matryoshka-style nested dictionaries. Two things a reader should add. First, the theorems are about the *idealised* objective: the real architecture uses an online EMA, clipped weights and a warmup ramp, and the consistency theorem assumes the weights have converged (a timescale separation justified by measured update magnitudes, not proved). Second, the causality they claim for coherence is argued at the level of the diagnostics and the correlations between them, not by an interventional sweep that changes coherence while holding everything else fixed in the real models — that sweep exists only in the synthetic teacher model.

## What we take from it

**A measure and a framing, and one warning.** The measure: report the *geometry* of a dictionary beside its reconstruction number — max and p90 nearest-neighbour coherence, the condition number of the active Gram matrix, and the norm of the active/inactive cross-block. Max coherence alone is a trap here, and the paper's own tables show why: TopK on Pythia has max coherence 0.989 beside a p90 of 0.327, while AEN has a *lower* max (0.963) and a *much higher* p90 (0.628). One number would have said "TopK is more redundant"; two say "TopK has one duplicated pair and a long tail of distinct directions, AEN has a broad band of moderately correlated directions" — opposite structures. For us the transferable rule is that any per-unit dictionary we report carries the distribution of pairwise coherence across its top units, not the extreme. The framing: a unit that never fires in our probe data is not evidence that the network does not use it, and the paper's sampling argument gives the reason — the relevant statistic is the expected firing rate times the number of tokens examined, so a per-unit status of "unused" must be reported with the token budget and the estimated rate, and any feature whose activity sits near the resolution of that budget is "not observed", never "absent". This is the same accounting failure as a null that cannot detect the effect, arriving from the data side. The warning: they use batch-mean ablation rather than zero ablation for the fidelity baseline, and their justification (zeroing a residual stream pushes the model off-manifold and inflates the apparent effect) is one our own patching protocol should carry explicitly whenever we quote a recovery number.
