# Label-Free Localization and Test-Time Suppression of CLIP's Typographic Circuits

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.01513
- **Authors / organisation:** Bohan Liu, Wenqian Ye, Guangzhi Xiong, Zhenghao He, Sanchit Sinha, Aidong Zhang; University of Virginia
- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01513
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
A CLIP vision encoder both recognises objects and reads rendered text off the image, and the two abilities are entangled: paint the word "Goose" on a cat and the model says goose. Prior defenses treat that as an input-filtering problem, and prior interpretability work on CLIP either labels whole layers or whole blocks. The authors want the intermediate step the field has mostly skipped — *where* in the vision transformer the lexical and semantic streams live, at what granularity they separate, and whether finding them lets you switch the lexical stream off without retraining the encoder. The larger goal is a mechanistic account of a multimodal failure mode that is also an intervention: if the two streams sit in different heads, muting one should recover object recognition under attack and cost almost nothing on clean images.

## What they studied
Five OpenCLIP image encoders spanning two orders of magnitude of compute — ViT-B/16, L/14, H/14, g/14 and bigG/14, all LAION-2B pretrained. The unit of analysis is the **attention head**, with latents inside each head's value subspace as the finer unit. Extension experiments use the native vision towers of three vision-language model families (Gemma-3 SigLIP, Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5) on a multiple-choice typographic benchmark. Evaluation data: RTA-100, the Disentangling set, PAINT, a diffusion-generated ImageNet-100-Text-D built for this paper, and clean ImageNet-100 for the capability cost.

## How they did it
A sparse transcoder is attached to each block in the last 20% of the ViT, where the authors show (CKA) heads are least correlated. The transcoder is **head-decomposed**: each head's output vector gets its own encoder into a top-128 latent code, and a single joint decoder predicts the block's post-projection attention output. This differs from the usual practice of one encoder over all concatenated heads. Training is label-free — ImageNet activations, 30 epochs, no class labels, no gradients through the ViT. Two head scores are then computed from unlabeled images: a **Clarity Score**, the mean pairwise cosine similarity of the CLIP embeddings of the images that activate a latent (semantic latents fire on visually coherent clusters; lexical latents fire on images linked only by rendered text), and a **normalized Text Attribution Score**, the gradient of a latent's activation with respect to pre-softmax attention scores, restricted to patch tokens carrying a synthetically pasted known-location text box, expressed as a fraction of the total positive attribution to text versus non-text patches. Heads are selected per layer by a statistical threshold on each score; heads in between are left unassigned rather than forced into a binary lexicon. The intervention reweights the class token's attention inside the selected lexical heads so patch tokens receive nothing and the class token receives everything, applied at test time with no weight change. A gated variant suppresses a selected head only on images where that head's class-token output norm exceeds its clean-corpus mean by theta standard deviations. Controls: an equal-capacity monolithic dictionary with latents assigned to their dominant head; a dictionary-free selector that ranks heads by the raw gradient ratio; 20 random same-size head draws per model; head-selection stability across three dictionary seeds; and a red-team of the Clarity Score under six controlled text corpora.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **feature/component account plus a causal intervention**: named attention heads whose latent dictionaries are dominated by one of two content populations, validated by muting them and showing the behaviour changes. It is explicitly partial — the authors decline to force every head into lexical or semantic, and they say nothing about how the two streams are computed, only that they are separable. It predicts one thing concretely: suppressing the selected head set recovers object accuracy on attacked images and leaves clean accuracy alone, and that prediction is tested per model, per benchmark, with paired confidence intervals.

## How they judged success
Zero-shot object-classification accuracy on four typographic-attack benchmarks and clean ImageNet-100, macro-averaged over the five backbones in the main table and reported per cell in the supplement. Deltas are paired against the run's own un-intervened baseline with a per-cell bootstrap (10,000 resamples), Holm-corrected across the 20-cell CS attack family (19 of 20 significant at 0.05; the exception is the 110-image PAINT column). Baselines are the two released prior methods, re-run under a matched protocol, with the prior authors' own numbers shown greyed and excluded from ranking. Beyond accuracy: rank correlation between each selector's head ranking and a held-out per-head causal ground truth (the accuracy recovery from suppressing that head alone), random-set suppression means and spreads, precision at k, cross-seed rank stability at several corpus sizes, McNemar tests on the VLM side, and a caption-level agreement check between the two signals at every scored layer.

## What they found
The two independent signals agree and both work. Lexical heads are the low-Clarity, high-attribution tail, their agreement rises sharply in the last fifth of depth (all 29 late blocks across five models sit above zero, one-sided sign test p = 1.9e-9, with the authors' own caveat that blocks within a model are not independent), and muting them lifts attacked accuracy substantially at small clean cost: macro-averaged over five backbones, RTA-100 55.1 to 70.8 with the attribution scorer and 69.9 with the Clarity Score, Disentangling 45.6 to 82.4 (CS) and 75.8 (nTAS), ImageNet-100-Text-D 57.7 to 76.7 (nTAS), against 66.1 / 63.7 / 71.4 for one prior greedy method and 63.6 / 67.8 / 70.1 for a supervised prefix method. Clean ImageNet-100 moves by at most 1.0 point for the two main variants. The largest single cell is the biggest model on the hardest benchmark (H/14 Disentangling +46.9 points). Gating nearly erases the clean cost — the worst static cost, bigG/14 CS at -3.3 points, becomes -0.4 — while keeping most of the recovery. The red-team is the most informative part: with a low-diversity text corpus the Clarity Score's ranking against causal ground truth collapses (B/16 0.611 to 0.166; H/14 to exactly 0.000 with a bootstrap interval containing zero) because shared rendered words fake cluster tightness in a language-supervised embedding space, and re-scoring the *same* top-activating image sets in a language-free encoder (DINOv2) restores the signal under every manipulation. Selection is stable: cross-seed rank correlation at least 0.989 on B/16, exact head sets recovered under dictionary retraining, and the ranking essentially formed by one to two thousand images. On the language-model towers the effect is real but much weaker and honestly reported as such — Gemma-3 12B's gated intervention separates from baseline (pooled attack splits p = 7.6e-5) while the effect on InternVL is nil.

## Limits and open problems they name
It is a static defense against a static attack family: text that leaves the rendered-script distribution (severe warping, handwriting) or that targets unselected heads is not covered, and the capability cost in legitimate scene-text reading is never measured. The VLM evidence is small-margin and the authors make no head-selection-superiority claim there. Head decomposition costs reconstruction fidelity — the monolithic dictionary reconstructs better everywhere, by 17-23% relative in FVU. The gated variant's gains over static are within noise on the CLIP side (the supported claim is equal protection at half the clean cost, not more). The Clarity Score needs a sufficiently diverse scoring corpus, which the paper demonstrates rather than solves.

## What we take from it
Three things, in order of value.

First, the **two-signal convergence as a validation shape**: an explanation form that is derived from geometry alone and one derived from input attribution alone, computed from completely different measurements, are plotted against each other per component and shown to agree exactly where the phenomenon is claimed to live. That is a cheap, general check for any model that proposes component roles, and it is stronger than a single score validated by one held-out number because the two signals can fail independently.

Second, the **scoring-space red-team** — a concrete warning for every cluster- or similarity-based label in this folder. A geometric score computed in an embedding that is itself trained on the very thing you are trying to separate will manufacture the separation; the fix the paper demonstrates is to re-embed the identical activate-sets in a language-free space and show the ordering survives. Any of our decoded-feature labels that are validated by clustering in a model's own representation space need the same substitution.

Third, the **head-local structural constraint as a design choice with a measurable price**: forcing one latent per head costs reconstruction and buys attribution that is well-posed by construction, and the ablation shows the price is real (monolithic reconstructs better) while the benefit appears only on the one selector that needs no labels. That is the honest form of an architectural constraint ablation — print both the loss and the case where it is load-bearing.
