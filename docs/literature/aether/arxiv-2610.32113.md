## What they wanted, and why
Reasoning models write a chain of thought before answering, which means a safety edit could in principle be made *inside* the reasoning rather than at the prompt or the output. The paper asks a sharper question than "does steering work": if you apply one activation edit once, at one early reasoning state, how much of its safety effect depends on *which* state you chose? They separate three things that a naive position sweep confounds — the quality of the direction learned at a site, the size of the edit, and the causal leverage of the state itself — and then ask what the mechanism of the effect is. The larger goal is a predictive account of where in a generative trajectory a single intervention has leverage, which is the trajectory-time analogue of asking which layer a decision lives in.

## What they studied
Three DeepSeek-R1 distilled models: Qwen-1.5B (layer 17), Qwen-7B (layer 17), Llama-8B (layer 20). The unit of analysis is a single residual-stream hidden state at one position `t_k` in the *generated* chain of thought (`t_0` is the first CoT token), plus a direction learned for that (layer, position) pair and a separate linear gate operating on the same state. Behaviour is scored at the level of a whole trajectory: the complete CoT plus final answer judged Safe or Unsafe.

## How they did it
Directions are learned offline from 170 exact-prompt pairs, each pair a safe and an unsafe trajectory from the same prompt; all trajectories are re-encoded through the target model's tokenizer and teacher-forced so the states are target-native. The initial direction is the mean safe-minus-unsafe hidden-state difference at the chosen layer and position, then refined for 80 single-trajectory updates under a signed continuation objective: the loss lowers the negative log-likelihood of the post-`t_k` CoT tokens of safe trajectories and raises it for unsafe ones, with an L2 penalty normalised by hidden width; the final answer is excluded from the loss. Strength is calibrated per model on a teacher-forced pool to a pre-set relative perturbation cap, and an independent linear gate (class-mean difference on 900 prompts, threshold set for at most 5% false triggers on benign) decides whether to edit at all. At inference the model generates natively up to `t_k`, the gate reads the unedited state, one vector addition is applied at most once, and generation continues with no further intervention. For the timing study the gate is switched off and a separately trained direction is used at each site, with every post-edit token regenerated; the confound is then attacked by re-running the *same* `t_2` direction at a fixed applied norm across nine positions and by cross-applying the `t_2` and `t_3` directions. Two further experiments isolate context and mechanism: neutral non-refusal prefixes shift the absolute position of the gain peak, and a four-arm intervention at `t_2` compares full steering against substituting only the single changed next token, and against editing the state while forcing the original next token.

## What counts as an explanation here
A partial, local one: a causal *position* claim plus a transmission claim. The explanation is not a circuit or a description of the computation but a dose-response curve over intervention site, held to be attributable to state leverage only after direction quality and edit size are held fixed, plus a statement of the pathway (the edit works by flipping one branching next-token transition, not by carrying an edited state downstream). It predicts that a given safe direction applied at a given offset in the opening reasoning transition yields a characteristic-sized behavioural change, and it predicts that moving the opening context moves that optimum.

## How they judged success
LlamaGuard-3-8B judges CoT and final answer separately; an output is Safe only if both are safe, and malformed or missing verdicts are Invalid. Harmful sets: StrongREJECT-273 (source-independent of the training data) and a 450-prompt SORRY-Bench-derived pool (same source ecology as training). Benign: 500 Alpaca instructions from the CAST release, scored for refusal rate and 0-100 rubric usefulness. Main 8B comparison is six arms decoded online across three generation seeds on 1,223 prompts (22,014 units), against matched-data CAST and AlphaSteer adaptations, a zero-initialised prompt-boundary gated edit, and a steering-only ablation. Supporting controls include a norm-matched teacher-forced KL/NLL diagnostic, three independent direction-training seeds, a risk-probe AUROC comparison, prefix and cross-position sweeps, and a bootstrap with prompt-clustered resampling for the half-budget table.

## What they found
On 8B, one gated edit at layer 20, `t_2` raises fully-safe CoT-answer on StrongREJECT-273 from 29.06±1.29% to 82.05±4.03% (52.99 points over the unedited model, 10.74 over the gated prompt-boundary arm), and 47.48±1.30% to 74.96±0.46% on the SORRY pool; benign refusal stays at 5.40% against 5.33%, usefulness 91.86 against 91.96, and the gate fires on 4.67% of benign requests. The steering-only ablation is token-identical to REACT on StrongREJECT, so the direction carries the harmful gain and the gate carries the benign protection (ungated, benign refusal doubles to 10.67%). Site-specific sweeps peak at `t_2` in all three models, with the 8B gain collapsing to zero at `t_3` and slightly negative at `t_4`; the collapse is a *direction-quality* failure, not a state one: at a common norm the `t_2` direction still gives +45.42 points when applied at `t_3`, while the `t_3` direction gives -1.10 at `t_2` and -2.32 at `t_3`, and three training seeds at `t_2` land in a tight band (+48.72 to +49.57) whereas three at `t_3` span -0.49 to +28.57. Holding direction and norm fixed shows genuine early leverage that is nonetheless a *band*: +55.19 at `t_1`, +49.45 at `t_2`, +45.42 at `t_3`, +27.47 at `t_4`, +38.95 at `t_5`, then +8.30 to +12.21 across `t_6`-`t_8`. Risk readability goes the other way: prompt-only probe AUROC 0.648 rises to 0.714 at `t_2` and 0.750 at `t_4`, precisely where the intervention fails. Forced prefixes move the peak to the fourth generated token after the prefix (`t_5`, `t_5`, `t_9`), so the optimum tracks the opening reasoning transition rather than an index. In the four-arm test, steering flips the immediate post-edit token from "I" to "the" in 94.12% of evaluable pairs; substituting only that token reproduces about 98% of the net gain (+49.57 against +50.67 points), while editing the state and pinning the original token gives -0.24 points. Signed continuation learning reaches the full-budget plateau at half the norm (79.61% against 36.14% for mean-only), and results are stable across five layers (76.92-80.95%), a fourfold norm range (76.56-79.73%) and gate thresholds.

## Limits and open problems they name
The norm-matched, prefix and transmission controls are all on the 8B model only; 1.5B and 7B get site-specific sweeps at a fixed calibrated strength, so their curves still conflate direction quality with leverage, and positions beyond `t_5` are not probed with alternative multi-token or nonlinear controllers. The KL/NLL measurements are local alignment diagnostics, not evidence of a persistent latent perturbation. The training data and one evaluation pool share a source ecology, and only exact-normalised text overlap was audited, not semantic near-duplicates. The training-prefix audit shows the initial mean-difference direction comes from pairs that diverge by `t_2`, and steered refusals are lexically closer to the model's own refusals than to the training answers in 77% and 72% of conversions, so the authors explicitly deny that this is a style-invariant semantic safety subspace. Human evaluation is one annotator on 100 pairs per dataset. On non-distilled Qwen3-8B the effect shrinks (+5.86 at `t_0`, -4.76 at `t_4`) because unedited safety is already about 80%.

## What we take from it
A method and a measure. (1) *Method: the position-sweep with the confound factored out.* Re-running one fixed direction at one fixed norm across sites, and cross-applying two sites' directions, is the cheapest way to separate "this state is not controllable" from "we failed to learn a direction here" — and it changes the conclusion materially, since the site-specific curve says `t_3` is dead and the fixed-direction curve says `t_3` is nearly as live as `t_2`. (2) *Measure: leverage is a band with a decay, reported as a curve.* The useful object is not the peak value but the region above half peak and the offset at which it collapses. (3) *Warning: decodability is not leverage.* Probe AUROC rises exactly where the edit does least, so a white-box model that predicts a behaviour change from a decoder's accuracy at a state will mispredict here; the two instruments must be reported side by side. (4) *Warning: the effect may be a single-token flip.* If 98% of the gain is reproduced by substituting the one next token, then a "predictive" reading of the state is really a reading of one branching decision, and any claim our model makes about downstream propagation has to survive the token-only substitution control. (5) *Framing: intervention strength must be a shared budget.* Every position claim here is denominated in a fixed relative norm, which is what makes cross-site comparison legitimate in the first place.
