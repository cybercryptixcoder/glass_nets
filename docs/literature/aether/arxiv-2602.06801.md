# On the Non-Identifiability of Steering Vectors in Large Language Models

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The field treats a steering vector as if it *were* the concept: extract a mean difference from contrastive prompts, add it at a layer, observe the behaviour move, and then describe the vector as the model's representation of honesty or formality. The authors ask the prior question — is that direction even recoverable from behaviour at all, or is the same behaviour produced by infinitely many geometrically different vectors? Their claim is that under the standard white-box single-layer setting the answer is the second one, and that this is not a small-sample nuisance but a property of the map from activations to outputs. The larger goal is to say what evidence would actually be needed before a steering demo can be read as a representational claim, and to point at structural constraints rather than more behavioural testing.

## What they studied

Two instruction-tuned models from different families — Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (hidden width 4096) and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (2048) — at each model's middle layer (16 of 32, 18 of 36). The unit of analysis is a **single direction** in the residual stream, together with the family of directions that produce the same outputs. Five traits span surface to cognitive: formality, politeness, sentiment, truthfulness, agreeableness. Output-side measurement uses frozen pretrained classifiers rather than a judge model (a RoBERTa formality ranker, a sentiment model, and zero-shot entailment for the other three), and one appendix re-does the comparison at the raw-logit level.

## How they did it

(1) **Legal argument.** Under a first-order expansion of the output logits in the injected vector, the effect is a Jacobian times the vector, so anything in that Jacobian's null space contributes nothing; rank–nullity then gives infinitely many distinct vectors with identical first-order output, and the equivalence class of any vector is the affine subspace through it along the null space. The proof is constructive (a family parameterised by a scalar times a null-space direction gives geometrically distinct vectors except on a measure-zero set). (2) **Scaling symmetry** is acknowledged separately and factored out: a vector and its multiples are treated as one direction, so the claim is about directions modulo scale. (3) **Empirically**, steering vectors are built as the mean activation difference over 50 contrastive prompt pairs per trait, and three tests ask whether equivalence is visible in behaviour: add a random unit vector orthogonal to the steering vector (Gram–Schmidt) and compare steered generations on 100 held-out prompts; repeat with 50 independent orthogonal directions to see whether the class is thick; and repeat the whole comparison for a vector extracted by a completely different method (the first principal component of activation covariance). (4) **Distribution shift** — vectors extracted in-distribution are evaluated under a topic shift and a genre shift as well. (5) **Null-space size** is estimated directly by SVD of the activation covariance over 200 prompts at three depths, with effective rank taken as the count of singular values above 0.01. (6) **A logit-level ratio** compares the output perturbation from an orthogonal direction against a norm-matched random direction, so the result cannot be an artefact of the trait classifiers. (7) Two appendices extend the argument: a Fisher-information calculation to show that more prompts cannot shrink the ambiguity, and a weight-space reparameterisation symmetry for completeness.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not an explanation of behaviour — a **negative result about what behavioural evidence can identify**. The object they formalise is an equivalence class of intervention directions, and the artifact is a statement of the conditions under which a direction *is* identified: Regime 1 (black-box input–output), Regime 2 (white-box single-layer access, the setting of almost all steering work), and implicitly a stricter regime where weights or structural assumptions are available. Their positive prescription is thin by design: structural constraints — independence assumptions of the kind nonlinear-ICA identifiability theory uses, sparsity or invariance objectives — are the only route they name, and they explicitly do not test whether those recover anything. So the explanation on offer is a *limit* on explanations: "this vector drives the behaviour" is a functional fact, not a representational one.

## How they judged success

The currency is **Cohen's d** between the classifier-scored behaviour under the original vector and under the perturbed vector, with |d| < 0.2 pre-registered as the equivalence threshold. Complementary evidence: singular-value spectra at three layers with a fixed 0.01 cutoff; an explicit logit-space ℓ₂ ratio R = ‖ℓ(v) − ℓ(v+v⊥)‖ / ‖ℓ(v) − ℓ(v_rand)‖ with random directions as the active control; a sweep of steering strength over five values including zero; and effect sizes reported per trait per model with standard deviations over 5 and 10 seeds, so the stability of the estimate is inspectable rather than asserted. There is no held-out-generation-based human eval, no capability benchmark, and no test of whether the null-space estimate coincides with the Jacobian null space the proof assumes — they flag the last one themselves.

## What they found

**The ambiguity is large in behaviour, not just on paper.** Orthogonal perturbations are behaviourally equivalent across all ten trait–model cells: mean |d| of 0.119 (Llama) and 0.131 (Qwen) over 10 seeds, every cell below the 0.2 threshold, with per-cell means between 0.059 and 0.186. **Extraction method does not pin the direction down.** The contrastive vector and the first principal component of activation covariance reach cosines from −0.54 to +0.32 — including two nearly opposite pairs on Llama — while still scoring |d| below 0.27 everywhere and below 0.2 in nine of ten cells; the most extreme case pairs a cosine of −0.54 with |d| = 0.19. **The equivalence class is thick.** Sampling 50 independent orthogonal directions per trait, mean |d| stays in a narrow band — 0.080 to 0.124 for four of five Llama traits, 0.095 to 0.121 for Qwen — with sentiment (0.265/0.291) and Llama politeness (0.243) the only elevated cells, and the authors read those as classifier variance rather than a breakdown. **The linearisation holds over the operational range**: equivalent from strength 0 to 3, with overlapping confidence bands, and the perturbation itself "moves the model less" than the steering vector in several Llama conditions. **The null space dominates the space.** Effective rank at three depths is 57/59/63 of 4096 for Llama and 139/127/116 of 2048 for Qwen, i.e. a null-space fraction of 0.937–0.943 and 0.861–0.884, implying roughly 71 unobservable directions per observable one in Llama and 14 in Qwen; the spectra fall off a cliff in the first 50–140 dimensions and then flatten. **It is visible in raw logits, not only in classifiers**: the orthogonal-to-random perturbation ratio R is 0.47–0.73 across all ten cells, i.e. an orthogonal direction disrupts the output 27–53% less than a random direction of the same norm. **The Fisher argument** says this is not a data problem — the stacked Jacobian's null space is the intersection of the per-prompt null spaces, so directions that are invisible for every prompt remain invisible however many prompts are added, and the Cramér–Rao bound along them is infinite. The weight-space appendix adds a reparameterisation symmetry (an invertible mixing of the representation compensated in the next weight matrix, with the vector transformed accordingly) for a pair of parameterisations that cannot be distinguished by any input–output data.

## Limits and open problems they name

Two models, one layer each, five traits, one judge-free measurement stack; the empirical magnitude may not transfer across scale, depth or architecture. Whether explicit structural constraints actually recover identifiable directions is untested — they name it as the obvious next experiment and do not run it. The null-space assumed by the proof is the Jacobian's; the null space they *measure* is the activation covariance's, and they concede the two are connected only by the behavioural agreement, not by a proof. The proof is local: exact for piecewise-linear activations inside a region, first-order otherwise, and extending it to the exact nonlinear map would need the Hessian structure, which they leave open. The weight-space symmetry is not realisable as a steering operation on a frozen model.

Mine, marked as mine: (a) **the equivalence threshold does a lot of work.** |d| < 0.2 is a conventional "negligible" cut for a difference between group means, borrowed from psychology, and it is being used as an *equivalence* test with no equivalence-testing procedure (no TOST, no power calculation per cell); the cells with |d| ≈ 0.19–0.29 sit close enough to the line that a stricter threshold would flip several conclusions, so the headline "all below threshold" is partly a statement about the threshold. (b) **The claim is about linearisation, and the linearisation is also the mechanism.** Because the argument is that first-order invisible directions exist, the natural test is *second-order*: a direction in the Jacobian's null space that nonetheless changes outputs at large doses would show the class is not behaviourally inert, only locally so — and the strength sweep, which stops at 3 and reports overlapping bands, is the one experiment that could have caught that and is summarised qualitatively. (c) **The null-space estimate is a property of the prompt distribution, not of the model.** Effective rank from the covariance of 200 prompts is a statement about how many directions those prompts vary; the appendix's Fisher argument needs the *intersection* across prompts to be large, and a distribution varied enough to fill the intersection would make the estimate small by construction — the two halves of the argument therefore pull against each other and only the behavioural tests bridge them. (d) There is no positive control showing the method can *detect* a direction that is genuinely privileged (a vector known to have an effect no orthogonal perturbation shares, e.g. a single-neuron ablation), so a null here is compatible with both "the class is large" and "the assay is insensitive".

## What we take from it

- **A framing, and the sharpest available statement of what a steering result does not buy.** The folder has repeatedly treated a working direction as evidence about mechanism; this says the working direction bounds a (d − r)-dimensional family of equally working directions, so the vector identifies at most a *functional* handle. Our own write-ups should state which equivalence class a direction is recovered up to, and the number of free parameters, exactly as they do in Appendix E.
- **A concrete, cheap measurement to import: effective rank and null-space fraction of the activation covariance at each layer we touch.** It costs one SVD over a few hundred prompts and it immediately tells us the size of the space in which our predictor is free to be wrong. If our white-box model predicts outputs from a read-out at layer ℓ, its accuracy can only be as good as the *observable* rank there — which is the honest denominator for any "our model captures X% of behaviour" claim.
- **A method: orthogonal-perturbation equivalence testing, and the logit-space ratio R.** Both are one-line additions to any intervention experiment we run and both are sensitive to exactly the failure mode this folder keeps hitting (a direction that looks meaningful and is one of many that do the same thing). The R ratio in particular is a better control than a norm-matched random vector alone, since it compares a null-space-informed perturbation against an uninformed one and so tests whether the structure we claim exists is doing anything.
- **A warning about the "more data will fix it" reflex.** The Fisher/intersection argument is the formal version of why held-out prompt sets do not resolve identifiability, which is the same conclusion three other entries in this folder reached empirically from different directions.
- **A limit on the argument we should respect rather than repeat.** Everything here is local (first-order) and distribution-relative (covariance-estimated). If we want the stronger claim — that the equivalence class is behaviourally inert, not merely linearly inert — we owe a second-order test the authors did not run, and that is a genuinely open slot for our project.
