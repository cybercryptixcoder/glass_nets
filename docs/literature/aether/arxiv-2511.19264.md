# Interpreting GFlowNets for Drug Discovery: What Probes Can and Cannot Show

- **Key:** arxiv-2511.19264
- **Authors / organisation:** Amirtha Varshini A S; Duminda S. Ranasinghe; Hok Hei Tam — all at Montai Therapeutics (affiliation printed per author in the HTML byline, one correspondence address each)
- **Date:** 2026-08-05 (v2; v1 2025-11-24)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19264
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Drug-discovery generative models propose molecules but explain nothing, and medicinal chemists need to know which substructures drive a proposal before a model enters a design–make–test cycle. The authors ask what an interpretability pipeline can actually license for a sequential, graph-based generative policy — SynFlowNet, a GFlowNet that builds molecules from reaction templates and purchasable building blocks, trained with a drug-likeness reward. Their goal is not a feature catalogue but a *validated* one: every claim in the paper is run against a control designed to make it fail, and the paper's headline is the claim that the controls destroyed. As a side effect the work doubles as a control protocol transferable to any molecular-model interpretability study.

## What they studied
A single frozen SynFlowNet checkpoint (QED reward), analysed at three levels: atom/bond saliency on the Stop action, an undercomplete MLP autoencoder (256→128, ℓ1), an overcomplete BatchTopK sparse autoencoder (256→1024, expansion 4×, k=64), motif probes, and eleven linear probes (five regression, six classification) on pooled graph-transformer embeddings. The unit of analysis is the molecule-level embedding (32,054 molecules) and, in the dictionary work, individual sparse features plus their decoder directions. Controls include RDKit+ECFP4 descriptor baselines, shuffled-label probes, a Bemis-Murcko scaffold-disjoint split, and an architecture-matched untrained network (same `GraphTransformerSynGFN`, 4 layers, 2 heads, Morgan-1024 features, 2.9M parameters, random init).

## How they did it
Integrated gradients on the log-probability of the Stop action, with a zero or mean-feature baseline and 64 interpolation steps, give per-atom attributions; atoms above the 75th percentile plus RDKit-detected rings are grouped into candidate motifs, and those motifs are edited with six fixed RDKit transformation rules to read out a change in computed QED. The undercomplete autoencoder is trained with an ℓ1 penalty (λ=0.01) to recover coarse physicochemical axes, then a three-layer MLP maps those factors to reward signals. The overcomplete dictionary uses BatchTopK with exactly k·B activations per batch, unit-norm decoder columns, mean-squared reconstruction only, dead-feature resampling capped at 2% every 10 epochs from epoch 20, and five seeds. Probes are strictly linear (ridge for regression, logistic for classification) on the raw embedding, the sparse latent, and the reconstruction, each target run under a random split and under five genuine scaffold-disjoint splits in which the 3,206 test molecules are all scaffold singletons. For each target the top-weighted features are characterised by comparing their 30 highest-activating against 30 silent molecules on RDKit descriptors and SMARTS prevalence (Mann–Whitney and Fisher with Benjamini–Hochberg, enriched when the prevalence gap exceeds 30 points at q<0.01). Specificity is tested by zeroing one feature, decoding, and re-applying the frozen probes; stability is tested by matching decoder columns across independently seeded runs against a random-dictionary chance baseline. The whole untrained-network control replicates the probe pipeline on embeddings from the same architecture left at random initialisation.

## What counts as an explanation here
A layered, partially predictive account: atom-level attributions and candidate reward-sensitive motifs (local, illustrative), a coarse factor dictionary whose entries correlate with physicochemical axes, an overcomplete feature dictionary whose entries are described by enrichment contrasts against descriptor statistics, and probe results as a statement about what information is linearly accessible in an embedding. The paper is explicit that the account is neither complete nor causal at the policy level: feature ablations are interventional on *probe decoding*, not on generation, and generation is never re-run. Its distinctive move is to treat the account as a set of claims each of which must survive a matched null of its own.

## How they judged success
Reconstruction NMSE against a width-matched linear ℓ1 baseline on identical splits and seeds; batch-mean L0 and dead-feature fraction; probe R² and AUROC/AP with shuffled-label controls expected at chance; the signed gap between embedding and a leakage-free RDKit descriptor baseline under both a random split and a scaffold-disjoint split; information retention through the bottleneck measured as the change in already-fitted probe performance; ablation specificity as the ratio of targeted to mean off-target degradation; substructure enrichment with FDR correction; cross-seed decoder-column best-match cosine against a random-dictionary baseline; and — decisively — trained-versus-untrained probe performance on the same molecules and the same probes.

## What they found
Physicochemical and functional-group information is highly decodable from the embeddings (QED R²=0.59, size 0.996, halogen AUROC 1.000; motif probes mean AUROC about 0.95 with several motifs at 1.000), with shuffled-label controls at chance (AUROC 0.49–0.52). Under a random split the embedding never beats the leakage-free descriptor baseline by more than +0.036 and loses on four targets (QED by 0.026, lipophilicity 0.073, urea 0.077, flexibility 0.202); under scaffold-disjoint splits the embedding barely moves (flexibility −0.057, QED +0.001) while the QED descriptor baseline falls 0.612→0.568, flipping that gap to +0.019, so the random split had flattered the fingerprints. Then the control that undoes the story: an untrained network of identical architecture decodes every probed property to within 0.036 of the trained policy, buys training only +0.005 on QED (0.581±0.016 untrained against 0.586 trained) and is *marginally better* on molecular size and weight. The overcomplete dictionary reconstructs at NMSE 0.00151±0.00009 against 0.00263±0.00011 for the width-matched linear ℓ1 baseline (1.75× better) at comparable activity (L0=64 pinned against 67.6 for the baseline) with 0.64%±0.28% dead features; a median 98.3% of probe-detectable information survives the bottleneck (worst case flexibility, 83% retained) and QED is decoded *better* from the sparse latent than the raw embedding (+0.033). Feature characterisation finds 280 of 3,959 tests enriched, including separate per-halogen detectors (100% prevalence in top activators against 0 to 17% in silent molecules, q<1e-9), a boron/boronic-ester detector at 90% against 0% (q=5e-11) despite boron at 0.29% of the corpus, and a clean *absence* detector for benzene (0% against 93%, q=7e-12). Single-feature ablation gives specificity ratios of roughly 8–23× for the substantial interventions and 44× for one small one. Cross-seed stability is where the dictionary mostly fails: mean best-match cosine 0.237 against a 0.214 random-dictionary chance baseline (1.11× chance), with only 2.71% of features above cosine 0.5 and 0.97% above 0.7. The paper also prints a revision of its own earlier numbers — dead-feature fraction 0.6% against a previously noted ~54% once resampling was applied correctly, QED decodability 0.59 against 0.76, and the single-versus-multi-target feature split inverting to 43% from 55% — and attributes part of the QED shift to the pinned RDKit version.

## Limits and open problems they name
The analysed checkpoint no longer exists, so the embeddings are the only reproducible artefact and retraining would not reproduce the weights; the two illustrative ΔQED values cannot be re-verified. The saliency analysis covers only the final Stop decision and has no saliency-versus-random control. The untrained-network control used a single random initialisation (so it is n=1 in the trained/untrained factor) and was run under the random split only, not the scaffold split. The SAE was trained on all embeddings, so a fully scaffold-held-out dictionary is untested. Whether an SAE trained on the untrained network's embeddings would yield the same per-halogen and boron detectors — the obvious next control — is untested, and motif probes were not run against the untrained network at all. No steering or controllability claim is made anywhere. Scope is one reward (QED) and one model.

## What we take from it
The method we take is the **architecture-matched untrained network as a null for every probe-style claim**, and the paper's framing of what it means: probe decodability of a property that is a deterministic function of the input is a statement about the featurisation and the architecture, and training does its work somewhere this probe family cannot see. Three further pieces: (1) the **leakage-free baseline discipline** — drop from the descriptor baseline any column that defines the target, and report how the ordering depends on the split, because ECFP4 leaned on scaffold memorisation far more than the embedding did; (2) **ablation specificity as a ratio, not a raw delta** — a large raw change may be general reconstruction damage, and the transferable claim is the joint band where both magnitude and specificity are large (they explicitly discount their own 44× cell as a small intervention); (3) the **stability number printed against a random-dictionary chance baseline** (0.237 against 0.214, and the honest "1.11× chance", plus thresholds that no random pair attains) — this is the cheapest form of the denominator discipline in our folder, because it makes the fraction of a dictionary that is reproducible a printed number rather than an adjective. Warning to carry: this paper reports that its own earlier dead-feature figure (54%) was an artefact of incorrect resampling and its single/multi-target split inverted under a controlled re-run — any unit-level count we report should be produced by a script with a unit test asserting the exact-sparsity constraint, which is what they did.
