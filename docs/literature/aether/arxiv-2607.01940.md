# Conditional Co-Ablation: Recovering Self-Repair Backups in Transformer Circuits

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.01940
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhiren Gong, Zihao Zeng, Chau Yuen, Wei Yang Bryan Lim; Nanyang Technological University (College of Computing and Data Science; Interdisciplinary Graduate Programme; School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering), Singapore
- **Date:** 2026-07-02
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01940
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit analysis rests on one primitive: score a component by how much removing it changes the output, then combine those single-unit scores. The authors point out that this primitive assumes component importance is additive, and that self-repair breaks the assumption in a specific and asymmetric way. When a primary component is removed, a dormant backup can take over; the primary therefore looks less important than it is, and the backup looks like nothing at all because it was silent on the intact model. Both sides of the redundancy are misread by the same first-order score. Their goal is to convert this failure from a nuisance into a *recovery task*: given a primary circuit, find the components that only become causal once that circuit is gone. The larger target is downstream repair — attribution, capability knockout and structured pruning all rank components by the same node-additive score and so all inherit the same bias.

## What they studied

Frozen decoder-only transformers. Main experiments on GPT-2-small (124M) with the documented indirect-object-identification (IOI) circuit, the only circuit in the literature with head-level backup ground truth (eight documented backup name-movers). Scale-generalisation on GPT-2 medium and large. Architecture-generalisation on the induction circuit across eight further models spanning six families: Pythia-160M/410M/1.4B, GPT-Neo-1.3B, Gemma-2-2B, Qwen2.5-7B, OLMo-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B. Pruning experiments from 124M to 7B. Unit of analysis: attention heads (plus a preliminary probe with contiguous MLP-neuron groups).

## How they did it

The core move is to condition the score on what has already been removed. Let ablating a set of units replace their residual contributions with zero and produce logits; the clean logits define a reference distribution. A single-unit ablation effect is the clean-minus-ablated logit difference, and its "energy" is measured in a Fisher-weighted, centred feature space on the top-*r* logits: centring removes the shared logit shift the ablation imparts to every output coordinate at once, and the distribution weighting makes the plain inner product coincide with the Fisher quadratic form (their Proposition 1), so the energy is the mean KL cost of the ablation. The CoAx score is then the *growth* of that energy when the unit is ablated with the primary set already gone, compared with its solo energy. Computing it costs one extra conditional forward-pass sweep per seed — the same order as a single-ablation scan and far below enumerating all pairs. A second object, pairwise synergy, is the non-additive residual of the two-unit joint ablation against the sum of solo effects, written in the same geometry; synergy is used for *cooperation* (clustering heads that share a circuit) while conditional growth is used for *substitution* (finding backups). Validation is layered: rank the eight documented backups and compare ROC-AUC against first-order baselines; check the surfaced heads behave like backups by two label-free signatures (output-norm wake-up when primaries are ablated, and conditional causal drop); trace the hand-off with direct logit attribution as primaries are ablated one at a time; and close the loop with counterfactual patching, freezing the recovered heads at their dormant value. Three applications: re-attribute the name-mover set with the backups included; use the set for capability knockout; and prune sequentially, re-measuring the score after each removal. A synthetic controlled-redundancy benchmark plants a known number of conditionally-active backups so a powered head-to-head comparison exists.

## What counts as an explanation here

A conditional, relative importance score — a ranked compensation set layered onto an existing circuit, not a replacement for it. The explanation is explicitly *not* intrinsic to a unit: importance is defined relative to what has been removed. It is label-free and output-grounded (no ground-truth labels enter the score, only forward passes). The paper's formal content is two propositions: one showing that *any* fixed function of clean-state per-unit statistics must assign a dormant backup and an inert unit the same value, and one showing that conditional growth equals the unit's synergistic coupling to the seed under a pairwise-interaction truncation. Partial by design: it completes a circuit it does not discover from scratch, and the authors state plainly that as a standalone finder seeded with nothing it is worse than the baselines.

## How they judged success

Node-level ROC-AUC over the eight documented backup heads, with the primary seed supplied (4 prompt seeds; std ≤ 0.04). Baselines: single-ablation saliency, attribution patching, EAP-IG, a GIM-style self-repair-aware gradient (both seed-free and seeded on the same primary-ablated model), and AtP⋆ with GradDrop. Statistics: a label-permutation test (10,000 shuffles), a hypergeometric top-*k* test on the 141-candidate population, a per-ROC DeLong test, and an across-seed paired *t*-test. Controls: matched-size random completions, the finder's own next-ranked heads (a `+own` control that separates "right heads" from "more heads"), and an input-side co-activation score which they deliberately report as an alternative signal rather than suppress. Completeness is measured as a gap: mean-ablate everything outside a circuit, ablate the name-movers, and compare the response with the full model's. Minimality via conditional per-head drop. Faithfulness via counterfactual patching of the recovered heads to their dormant values.

## What they found

On GPT-2-small IOI, CoAx reaches 0.91 ROC-AUC on the eight backups against 0.33 for single-ablation saliency, 0.60 for attribution patching, 0.63 for the seeded GIM-style gradient, 0.70 for EAP-IG and 0.82 for AtP⋆ with GradDrop; 6 of 8 backups land in its top 20 of 141 candidates. The name-mover module is 1.9× super-additive: ablating the three primaries drops the answer margin by only 0.11 from a clean 2.53, while both primaries and backups together drop it by 1.15, against 0.60 predicted by summing eleven single-head ablations. Component ablation attributes the gain to conditioning (0.33 → 0.80 with a plain feature) and then centring (→ 0.91), with Fisher weighting adding about 0.01. Recovered heads pass both label-free signatures, and freezing them to their dormant activation removes 55% of the self-repair while a matched random set removes none. Completeness gap: 0.72 for the first-order circuit, 0.15 after CoAx completion, 0.16 for the fully documented circuit, 0.61 for a random completion. Knockout: ablating the primaries leaves IOI accuracy at 0.97; adding CoAx backups brings it to 0.70 against a documented-set oracle of 0.72, whereas a `+own` top-up of the same size overshoots to 0.24 into the core heads. Attribution: the masked drop of 0.22 becomes 1.76, above both a random top-up (1.0 ± 0.7) and the curated documented set (1.15). Pruning at 50% sparsity gives WikiText-2 perplexity 80.6 versus 201.4 for the best gradient baseline on GPT-2-small, and a static (non-sequential) version of the same score reaches only 112.6 — isolating the value of re-conditioning. On a synthetic benchmark with 100 planted backups, clean-state scores sit at or below chance (first-order energy anti-ranks them at 0.42) while CoAx reaches 0.90. Induction completion transfers to all eight non-GPT-2 models with attribution factors from 2.1× to 12× over matched-random.

## Limits and open problems they name

The headline number is a *completion* result with documented primaries as the seed; seeded with a label-free AtP detection the score falls to about 0.60, and with an undirected energy-top seed it falls to 0.30, below the first-order floor — so the seed must functionally be the primary circuit, and automatic primary discovery stays open. Only eight documented backups exist, so the per-ROC comparison is underpowered (DeLong p = 0.16 against the seed-free AtP⋆); the significance rests on the across-seed test and on the synthetic benchmark. On raw faithfulness the completion ties a `+own` top-up, and when the seed is a noisy first-order guess the recruited heads are mid-ranked compensators of that seed rather than cleanly dormant backups (recall of documented backups 0/4 in that setting). On induction, `+CoAx` and `+own` are comparable — redundancy there is shared among co-firing homogeneous heads, so the paper claims label-free recovery, not unique identification. Head-level CoAx does not transfer to the MLP-dominated greater-than circuit, and a preliminary MLP-group probe recovers only 1.5× over random (within one standard deviation), read as that circuit being weakly self-repairing. Named extensions: joint primary-and-backup discovery that breaks masking on both sides, a full FFN-level treatment, and decomposing recovered backups into known mechanisms (LayerNorm rescaling versus anti-erasure).

## What we take from it

A **method** and a **measure**, both usable directly: (1) score units conditionally on an already-removed set and read the *growth* in effect, rather than scoring them on the intact model — one extra pass, and it converts a blind spot into a ranking. (2) The formal separation of "dormant backup" from "inert unit" is the part we should copy as a *null design*: the paper proves that no fixed function of clean-state statistics can rank them apart, then builds a synthetic benchmark with a known number of planted backups specifically because the real benchmark has eight positives. That is the cleanest answer in the folder to "how do you test a detector when the labelled positives are too few to power the comparison". (3) The `+own` control is the sharpest idea in the entry and generalises far beyond this paper: adding the model's *own next-ranked* components is a stronger null than adding random ones, and only against it does "which heads" separate from "how many heads" — on IOI the two tie on faithfulness but diverge on knockout, where more heads overshoots into the core circuit. (4) A **warning** with direct bearing on our own measurement discipline: the eight real IOI backups place under 1% of their conditional ablation energy on the task-answer direction, below a random-direction reference, yet they demonstrably carry the repair; the causal weight lives in a sliver of the energy they move. Any score we build that projects onto an answer or task direction — the natural thing to do when we know what we are probing for — would see that sliver at vanishing signal-to-noise, which is exactly why the answer-aligned conditional proxy collapses from 0.95 to 0.59 as alignment falls while the full-distribution score stays flat. (5) Read against run 752 (an intervention as an instrument with a calibration curve over a signed dose), the two entries are the same discovery from opposite ends: run 752 says a single deletion is one unmarked point on a dose axis and the sign flips at a crossing dose, this one says a single deletion is one point on an *interaction* axis and a dormant backup's contribution is invisible until its partner is removed. Together they give the rule that matters for us: **no unit is scored on its solo effect alone — every component score we publish carries a conditional counterpart measured under removal of the component it is known to stand in for, and a `+own` control that adds the model's own next-ranked components rather than random ones, because random is a floor that any structured ranking clears and the interesting question is whether the ranking beats "more of the same kind".**
