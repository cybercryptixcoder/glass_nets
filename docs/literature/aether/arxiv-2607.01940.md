# Conditional Co-Ablation: Recovering Self-Repair Backups in Transformer Circuits

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.01940
- **Authors / organisation:** Zhiren Gong, Zihao Zeng, Chau Yuen, Wei Yang Bryan Lim; Nanyang Technological University (College of Computing and Data Science; School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering)
- **Date:** 2026-07-02
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01940
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Component-level intervention is the shared primitive behind circuit discovery, attribution, capability knockout and structured pruning, and almost every one of those pipelines scores a unit by the effect of ablating it in isolation — a first-order, node-additive view. The authors point out that this view has a systematic failure mode whenever a model self-repairs: remove a primary component and a dormant backup takes over, so the primary's damage is muted and the backup looks inert on the intact model, and both sides of the redundancy are mis-scored. Their goal is to turn that failure into a recovery task — given a primary circuit, find the components that become causally important only after it is removed — and then to feed the recovered set back into attribution, knockout and pruning. The larger aim is a claim about what component importance *is*: not an intrinsic property of a unit but a quantity defined relative to what has already been removed.

## What they studied
Frozen decoder-only transformers, no fine-tuning, no recovery training for any method or baseline. Models: GPT-2 small/medium/large (124M/355M/774M), Pythia 160M/410M/1.4B, GPT-Neo-1.3B, Gemma-2 2B and 9B, OLMo-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B — eight models across six architecture families for the transfer claim, and pruning sweeps from 124M to 7B. Unit of analysis: attention heads (the granularity at which head-level circuit ground truth exists), with an MLP-group probe as an out-of-scope check. Data: the templated IOI prompts of Wang et al. (two alternative surface forms as a robustness check), induction sequences, greater-than, WikiText-2 for pruning perplexity, and PIQA / ARC-Easy / HellaSwag for zero-shot accuracy.

## How they did it
Ablation is defined as zeroing a unit's additive residual-stream contribution, giving logits for each ablated set; the single-unit effect is the difference between clean and singly-ablated logits. Effects are measured in a Fisher geometry: the categorical Fisher information in logit coordinates is the Hessian of the KL to the clean distribution at the clean logits, and they realise it by centring each logit perturbation against its expectation under the clean distribution (which removes the shared logit shift every ablation imparts) and weighting by the square root of the clean probability, so the plain inner product of the centred features equals the Fisher-weighted inner product (their Proposition 1). Collecting the per-unit centred features into a design matrix gives a Gram kernel and a Fisher-cosine affinity — this is their first-order baseline. Two second-order objects are added: a pairwise synergy, defined as the joint-ablation perturbation minus the two single perturbations, whose energy is large exactly when two units compensate for each other; and the CoAx score itself, defined as the growth in a unit's Fisher-weighted ablation energy when a seed set is already ablated, relative to its effect in isolation. The CoAx score costs one pass per candidate unit at a fixed seed — same order as a single-ablation scan, far below enumerating all pairs — and needs only unlabelled calibration prompts. Evaluation protocol: seed with the documented IOI name-mover primaries (and separately with the top-3 heads of each automated finder), rank all remaining heads, and measure node-level ROC-AUC over the eight documented backup heads. Causal validation follows three routes: label-free behavioural signatures of a backup (output-norm ratio with primaries ablated over clean, and conditional causal drop), direct logit attribution hand-off as primaries are ablated one at a time, and counterfactual patching that freezes the candidate heads to their dormant value while the primaries are ablated. Completeness is then tested by mean-ablating every head outside a circuit and asking whether the circuit reproduces the full model's response to name-mover ablation; attribution, knockout and pruning each re-run with the recovered set added.

## What counts as an explanation here
A completion of an existing circuit: an explicit, ranked set of compensating heads that the primary circuit hides, with a causal certificate attached (patching and knockout), plus a decomposition of why the score works — conditioning is the dominant lever, centring adds the remainder, Fisher weighting a small consistent gain. It is deliberately partial: the paper says outright that CoAx completes rather than discovers, that the seed must functionally be the primary circuit, and that on circuits whose redundancy is spread among heads that already co-fire (induction) it cannot single out a distinct backup class. The formal content is a claimed impossibility statement about a family of scores — any score that is a fixed function of a unit's clean-state per-unit statistic assigns a dormant backup and an inert unit the same value, because both statistics vanish — together with a proof that conditional growth reads the second-order coupling those scores discard. The explanation predicts something concrete: which heads will wake up and become load-bearing under primary removal, and in what order.

## How they judged success
Headline metric: node-level ROC-AUC over the eight documented IOI backup heads, mean over four prompt seeds, against single-ablation saliency, attribution patching, a self-repair-aware gradient-importances variant seeded identically, EAP-IG, and AtP-with-graddrop; a separate pair-level cluster AUC is reported for same-circuit structure and never compared to the node-level one. Significance by a paired across-seed t-test, a per-ROC DeLong test, a hypergeometric top-k test, and a 10,000-shuffle label-permutation test. Controls: a size-matched random top-up in every downstream table, a same-seed gradient baseline (the fairness control that isolates score form from conditioning), an input-side co-activation selector, a controlled-redundancy synthetic benchmark with 100 planted backups, four ablation values (zero, mean, single resample, Gaussian matched to per-head mean and SD), seed-size and seed-type sweeps, two alternative prompt templates, and a label-free structural read (does a head attend to and copy the IO token) that shares none of the score's machinery. Circuit criteria follow the community trio: faithfulness, completeness and minimality. Pruning is scored by WikiText-2 perplexity at 10–70% head sparsity plus zero-shot accuracy, against random, magnitude, Wanda, gradient-Taylor, and the score's own static (unconditioned) order.

## What they found
On GPT-2-small IOI the single-ablation baseline recovers the backups at chance (0.33), a seeded gradient reaches 0.63, EAP-IG 0.70, and the strongest self-repair-aware gradient baseline 0.82, while CoAx reaches 0.91; 6 of the 8 documented backups land in its top 20 of 141 candidates, and the label-permutation null puts 0.91 entirely outside (p < 1e-4). The gain is not conditioning alone — the same-seed gradient with conditioning still gets 0.63 — and not standalone discovery either, since without a seed CoAx peaks at 0.60 against a seed-free 0.82. The ablation-value sweep holds it at 0.87–0.92 across all four ablation choices while first-order saliency stays 0.33–0.39, and it is stable from 0.80 at a single documented primary to 0.91–0.93 for two or more, but collapses to 0.60 with an attribution-derived seed and 0.30 (below the first-order floor) with an energy-top seed. The name-mover module is measured as 1.9x super-additive: joint ablation of primaries plus backups drops the logit-difference by 1.15 against 0.60 predicted by summing the eleven single effects, while a size-matched random set is additive. Mechanistically the backups wake monotonically as primaries are removed (output-norm ratio to 1.15, conditional drop to +0.11, answer DLA roughly doubling) while a matched random set stays flat, and freezing their dormant activations removes 55% of the self-repair versus none for random. Completeness of the first-order circuit has a gap of 0.72; adding the recovered backups closes it to 0.15, matching the 0.16 of the hand-labelled complete circuit, where a random completion leaves 0.61. Downstream: attribution of the name-movers goes from a 0.22 logit-difference drop to 1.76; knockout of the primaries leaves IOI accuracy at 0.97 (the behaviour survives entirely masked) while adding the recovered backups takes it to 0.70 against a documented oracle of 0.72, whereas topping up with the finder's own next-ranked heads overshoots to 0.24 into the core; and pruning at 50% sparsity gives 80.6 perplexity versus 201.4 for gradient-Taylor on GPT-2-small (dense 47.9), with the sequential conditioned order at 80.6 against 112.6 for the identical signal used statically. Label-free transfer to induction holds across eight models with attribution factors from 2.1x to 12x, though there it ties the model's own next-ranked heads — the redundancy is shared, so no distinct backup class exists and the authors claim only recovery, not identification. On the MLP-mediated greater-than circuit the head-level signal does not transfer (a preliminary FFN-group probe recovers only 1.5x over random).

## Limits and open problems they name
The explicit pairwise route is quadratic in the number of units, mitigated only by candidate sets. Head-level backup ground truth exists for one circuit only (IOI), so the labelled AUC is anchored there, and the per-ROC comparison against a seed-free baseline is underpowered at eight positives — the authors concede the bootstrap CIs overlap and defend the across-seed test instead. Completion quality tracks seed quality, and finding the primary circuit automatically is itself unsolved and further hampered by the same masking. The signal is instantiated at head granularity: it transfers to the attention-mediated induction circuit but not to the MLP-mediated greater-than circuit. Three extensions are named: joint primary-and-backup discovery that breaks the masking on both sides at once, a full FFN-level treatment of strongly MLP-mediated self-repair, and decomposing discovered backups into known self-repair mechanisms (LayerNorm rescaling versus anti-erasure), of which their activation-ratio check is only a first step. The 13B-scale variants were out of scope, and the answer-aligned baseline underperforms largely because real IOI backups sit off the answer direction, so the comparison depends on which direction a given baseline projects onto.

## What we take from it
A method and a measure. Method: the conditional-growth score is a cheap, label-free second-order instrument — one extra pass per candidate at a fixed seed — that we can run on our own probes whenever we want to know whether the components we found are the components a *damaged* model relies on. Measure: super-additivity ratio (joint drop over summed single drops) is a scalar summary of how far a module is from the additive picture, and it is the check we should print before publishing any per-unit ranking of a redundant module. Framing: importance relative to a named removal set, with the seed stated as part of the claim. Warning, and the sharpest thing in the entry: the 0.91 headline is a *completion* number seeded with hand-labelled primaries, and the same method scores 0.60 with an automatic seed and 0.30 with an energy-top seed — so a benchmark score is only interpretable alongside the seed that produced it. Shortcut worth stealing: the label-free structural cross-check (a bespoke read that shares none of the score's machinery, correlating with it at a Spearman of 0.09 yet ranking the backups at 0.96) is a cheap way to show a discovery is not a restatement; it does not transfer to other circuits, which is exactly why it counts as evidence rather than as a metric.
