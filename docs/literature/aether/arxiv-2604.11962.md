## What they wanted, and why
Interpretability under the dominant Linear Representation Hypothesis reads features off directions in a layer's activation space. The authors argue this has three structural costs: it says nothing about which neuron, layer or head produces the direction, it cannot distinguish a direction the network uses from one it merely carries, and each layer needs its own separate account with no notion of a feature spanning several layers. Their alternative starts from a fact about the network's geometry rather than its representations: a piecewise-affine network tiles its input space into regions, and each region is served by a distinct affine map - a local expert. They propose that the thing worth calling a feature is the way those experts align, and that alignment is readable as a linear direction among the *centroids* of the regions. The larger goal is an interpretability that is mechanistic by construction instead of post hoc, and that unifies dictionaries, probes, circuit discovery and saliency under a single object: the partition the network induces.

## What they studied
Vision transformers DINOv2 and DINOv3 (last MLP block), a pretrained ResNet50 and a Swin-B on ImageNet and the DTD texture set, ConvNext-L with standard and adversarially trained weights, a small convolutional net on FashionMNIST with colour injected at a controlled correlation, a convolutional-plus-linear net on MNIST, fully connected ReLU and GELU nets trained on 2-D star, bowtie and reuleaux polygons, GPT2-Large (layer 31 MLP, the "an"-neuron example from a 2023 single-neuron post), Llama-3.1-8B (layer 12 MLP, mass-mean probes), and BLOOM models of increasing capacity. The unit of analysis is the **centroid of a linear region** of a chosen sub-component - a vector living in that sub-component's *input* space, not its output space - with the region's radius left unstudied.

## How they did it
The substrate is Proposition 2.2, inherited from the power-diagram-subdivision literature: the centroid of the region containing a point is just the transpose of the input-output Jacobian at that point, multiplied by the all-ones vector. So a centroid is one Jacobian-vector product, available for any differentiable sub-component, and no region enumeration is needed - the combinatorial intersection of half-spaces that defines the tile is skipped entirely. They argue (Lemma 3.2 and its proof) that consistent activation differences require hyperplanes separating the latents, and from that motivate the hypothesis that a feature is a family of regions whose boundary hyperplanes are near-parallel; Theorem B.3 then shows telescoping along near-parallel normals puts the corresponding centroids on an approximate affine subspace with deviation bounded by roughly the square root of the angle tolerance. The empirical programme is a drop-in swap: everywhere an LRH pipeline consumes a latent activation, consume the centroid instead. They train TopK sparse autoencoders on Centroids versus latents for DINOv2, grade dictionaries by linear-probe accuracy on held-out Imagenette and by how often features fire on the test set, and by whether DINOv2 features land close to DINOv3 features. They build a neuron-attribution score as the mean relative change in a sample's centroid when a single neuron is ablated, evaluated over a small ball around the input embedding, and check whether it ranks the known GPT2 "an"-neuron near the top without sweeping the layer. They refit mass-mean probes on Llama-3.1-8B latents versus centroids on the plausibility dataset and test transfer to factuality datasets. They compare linear-probe accuracy for colour in the FashionMNIST injection sweep, and use the Jacobian-from-input-space map as a saliency image, averaged over a neighbourhood ("local centroid") in the manner of SmoothGrad, comparing standard versus adversarially trained weights and trained versus randomly initialised weights.

## What counts as an explanation here
A geometric one, and a deliberately partial one: features are linear directions among centroids of the network's induced power-diagram tiling, so an explanation is a statement about *which local experts the input engages and how those experts align*, plus a per-neuron attribution score for how much removing a unit moves that alignment. Nothing is claimed about the radii, so the partition is described by its tile centres and not its tile extents. It is predictive in a narrow sense the authors make operational: a feature should be a direction only if the network actually uses the characteristic, so a spurious-but-correlated characteristic should produce activation directions without centroid directions. That is a stated falsifiable prediction, and the colour sweep is the test of it. There is no surrogate model and no circuit graph in the formal sense - the "circuit" here is the response of components to a feature, offered as a definition rather than recovered as a structure.

## How they judged success
Five separate yardsticks, none of them a single benchmark. (1) Qualitative separation: PCA of centroids for inputs of two distinct classes should show two directions (Figure 2), and the same picture should appear for polygon nets and for a GELU (softened, non-CPA) net. (2) Spurious-feature test: linear-probe accuracy for colour from latents versus centroids as the colour-label correlation is swept from random to full - the LRH is expected to show linearity regardless, the LCH only when the colour is used. (3) Dictionary quality: linear-probe accuracy on the Imagenette test set from SAE feature decompositions fitted on the train set, feature firing frequency on the test set, maximum cosine similarity of each DINOv2 feature to the full DINOv3 dictionary (a bimodal distribution near 0.4 is read as the dictionary capturing unconverged artifacts), and an 8-nearest-neighbour qualitative check under Jaccard distance on binary activation patterns. (4) Circuit localisation: whether the known "an" neuron's attribution score sits in the top 99.8th percentile of its layer at layer 31, plus a robustness check on variance across 256-sample draws and stability of the percentile as the neighbourhood radius scales. (5) Saliency: visual faithfulness, plus the sanity check from the saliency-critique literature that randomly initialised networks should produce uninformative maps. Compute is reported as a cost table rather than a metric.

## What they found
Centroid directions separate by class under PCA for a pretrained ResNet50 on DTD textures, and the separation emerges over training in the polygon net - centroids start arranged like the input samples at initialisation and migrate into alignment by late epochs (epochs 0, 18, 27, 147, 512 are the checkpoints shown). In the colour sweep, latent activations form a linear colour direction even when colour is uncorrelated with the label, while centroids are linear only to the degree the feature is used; this is the result that carries the paper's central claim. SAE dictionaries built on centroids transfer better to the Imagenette test set and fire on more of the test data than latent-built dictionaries (Figure 4), and the nearest neighbours under centroid-dictionary activation patterns share a specific description (a white golf ball with dark text) where the latent-dictionary neighbours mix white and orange balls. On the DINOv2-versus-DINOv3 cosine comparison, centroid features concentrate at high similarity while a large share of latent features sit near 0.4 - the authors read that mode as artifacts, and note it cuts against the Platonic Representation Hypothesis, which they then support on the centroid side across BLOOM capacities. In GPT2-Large, the previously identified "an"-neuron lands in the top 99.8th percentile of the layer-31 MLP attribution distribution from a 256-point neighbourhood of radius 0.25 on a single prompt, without an ablation sweep. Mass-mean probes from Llama-3.1-8B layer-12 centroids transfer better to the truth-identifying datasets than those from latents, and the authors interpret this sharply: since the probe was fitted on plausibility, better transfer to truth means centroids capture the *act* of producing a truthful statement rather than the concept of truth. MNIST effective-rank along interpolations drops more between visually distinct class pairs (3 versus 6) than similar ones (4 versus 9) for centroids, with no comparable change for latents. Compute overhead is modest where measured: 8.7s versus 7.8s for DINO extraction, 881s versus 771s on MNIST, but 2329s versus 470s for the Llama probes - roughly a fivefold cost there, which the authors call relatively minor in the context of loading and saving the model.

## Limits and open problems they name
The radii are untouched: the power diagram is parameterised by centroid-radius pairs, and the radius decides whether a centroid even lies inside its own tile, so the account is knowingly half a geometry. The local-centroid saliency map has been tested on a narrow set of architectures and inputs and needs wider validation. The Platonic-representation appendix acknowledges that max-cosine alignment ignores how the full feature spectra correspond, and defers to the bimodality figure for the fuller version. Beyond what they name: the attribution score is validated against one neuron found by an outside ablation study on one prompt, so the "top 99.8th percentile" is a recovery of a known answer rather than a blind discovery, and the robustness appendix shows stability of that percentile - not the distribution of the score across layers, prompts, or models. The Llama probing claim about act versus concept rests on a single probe layer and one fitting dataset. Most results are qualitative figures whose quantities are not tabulated anywhere in the text.

## What we take from it
Three things, and one warning. **A method worth adopting:** the centroid as `J^T 1` gives, for any differentiable sub-component and at no region-enumeration cost, a vector that summarises *what the local expert does* rather than *where the input sits*. For us that is a cheap second view of every activation we already probe, and the paper's one structural prediction - a characteristic the network carries but does not use should be directional in activation space and not in centroid space - is a ready-made dissociation test we can run on any feature we are unsure about. **A framing:** the input-space partition is a genuine intermediate between our probes (which read representations) and our interventions (which read behaviour), because a centroid changes when behaviour changes and stays put when only the representation cosmetic changes. **A measure:** the attribution score, mean relative centroid displacement under single-neuron ablation over a small ball, is an intervention-shaped quantity computed *without* running the intervention - it is a candidate cheap surrogate for ablation damage, and the honest test of it is whether its ranking matches actual ablation KL on held-out prompts, which this paper does not run. **The warning:** the paper's headline evidence is a taxonomy of figures - PCA scatter, radial probes, neighbour grids, saliency images - with the load-bearing numbers (probe accuracy, firing frequency, cosine distributions) left inside plots. A method that is only ever demonstrated by the shape of a scatter is demonstrated on one reading of the geometry, and the LRH critique the authors level - "linear even when the feature is spurious" - is exactly the kind of claim that needs a tabulated effect size across seeds, not a left-hand panel. So: take the object, take the dissociation test, and demand the numbers before we let a centroid replace an activation in any of our reporting.
