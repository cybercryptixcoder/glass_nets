## What they wanted, and why
The founding evidence for superposition — the cat/car neuron, an illegible deep layer in InceptionV1, a small model whose units look like arbitrary mixtures — was, on this account, largely an artefact of bad architectures, small models with no room, and weak microscopy. The author helped propose some of that evidence and is retracting the interpretation rather than the data: what looked like unrelated things packed into one unit is usually adjacency, a neuron sprouting offshoots onto nearby concepts as it grows, which he calls recursive generalization. If so there is one learning principle, generalization, and it is fractal rather than arbitrary. The larger goal is permission for ordinary mechanistic science on the network as it is: a claim that a model's parts are already organised for its own downstream readers, so interpretability should aim to become another such reader instead of rebuilding the network into dictionaries first. The stated confidence is about the kind of process, not a mechanism percentage, and the author says so explicitly.

## What they studied
Vision: InceptionV1 (the original 2014 checkpoint and retrained copies of the same architecture), ConvNeXt small/medium/large at roughly 7M, 7.06M and 28.6M parameters across a wide size range, DINOv2, an InceptionV1 retrained with mixed3b cut from 480 to 10 channels, and a 7M model taught a nonsense label. Language: Qwen3-32B MLP neurons — more than a million neurons against a few thousand attention heads — plus GPT-2 XL compared against its own 2023 neuron study, a trillion-parameter model named as audited at the same rate, and Anthropic transcoder features used as the dictionary-side comparison. Unit of analysis: the neuron as the model's own mechanism, read through both poles, its inputs and outputs, the circuits that feed it, and its trajectory across checkpoints.

## How they did it
Five convergent designs, all intervention-heavy. (1) Retrain the suspect architecture with ordinary learning fixes only — residual stream, normalisation and a few other modernisations — holding parameters and data fixed, then re-rate the last layer's neurons. (2) Re-render old neurons under better feature visualisation on two routes, free optimisation and optimisation biased toward the paths real images take, often with the bias applied for only the first few dozen steps. (3) Watch neurons form: checkpoints from epoch 0.7 through 12, reading a single neuron's strongest images over time to see what it starts on and what it grows onto. (4) Squeeze capacity deliberately: cut InceptionV1's mixed3b from 480 to 10 channels with no bypass and retrain, then try to recover the missing curve detectors by training a TopK sparse autoencoder (640 features, 2 active per position, auxiliary loss) on the squeezed layer, and check whether the originals reappear anywhere. Repeat the squeeze on a ConvNeXt's last layer. (5) Language side: hand-built attribution graphs over Qwen3-32B MLP neurons, scored against published transcoder graphs, with neuron explanations evaluated two ways — blind maximum (one agent writes a short explanation, a second writes a maximally activating sentence from that explanation alone) and a pre-registered prediction test where an agent writes new examples and near misses before any of them are measured. Ablations of weak versus strong activations at matched total activation mass, ablations of dictionary directions that match no neuron, and a memorisation intervention that swaps only the last few blocks between models.

## What counts as an explanation here
A short natural-language statement of a single neuron's job as the distinction it draws between its two poles, set inside a circuit account of why it is there — not a noun list, and not a feature dictionary. Explanation is deliberately partial and zoomable: the same neuron explained coarsely at one scale and through its sub-branches at another, which the author calls a fractal of explanations. It claims prediction narrowly and testably: from an explanation, an independent writer should be able to produce inputs that drive the unit strongly and near misses that do not; from a growth story, one should be able to say what the offshoots will be. It does not claim completeness, and it argues that a global feature count is the wrong target.

## How they judged success
A battery rather than a single metric. Interpretability rates per layer and per model under an explicit rubric whose "what counts as one thing" threshold is admitted to be a knob. For the squeezed layer: tuning curves over 180 dark arcs at two curvatures and a photo-response correlation over every position of 10,000 held-out photos between each original curve detector and its closest SAE feature, plus a nonlinear readout as an upper bound on recoverability; spliced-in top-1 as a downstream cost. For explanations: new-example versus near-miss firing rates with a "strong" threshold at 30% of the neuron's corpus maximum, win/tie/loss counts, and blind maximum as a construction check against the real corpus. For activations: matched-total-activation-mass ablation of the weakest 75% against the strongest 7%, with a per-answer change count. For dictionaries: the fraction of SAE activity that is a near-copy of a single neuron, accuracy after removing all directions matching no neuron, a rotation search for the basis carrying most activity, and a comparison at three model sizes. Attribution-graph audits at scale (100,000 graphs reviewed by agents, thousands inspected by the author) are treated as qualitative evidence only.

## What they found
- **The suspect layer was fixable by ordinary means.** InceptionV1's final layer went from roughly 0% interpretable in 2014 to about 99% in a parameter-matched, same-data ConvNeXt; the author's estimate for the original checkpoint with modern tools is about 50%, about 70% after retraining the same architecture, against others' approximately 0%.
- **The canonical polysemantic neuron is one shape, not two objects.** 4e:55 responds to a bilateral pointed shape across cats, foxes and cars, with a round O at its negative pole against a V at the positive; its input circuitry reaches V-shaped flames, water jets and pointed fox faces.
- **Packing is not what happens under pressure.** The 48× squeezed mixed3b gives coarse but legible units — a "curves & loops" neuron instead of ten curve detectors — at 58.35% top-1 / 81.14% top-5, and the SAE trained on that layer recovers nothing like the originals: best shape correlations about 0.52–0.72 with photo matches 0.09–0.14, every one of the 91 most curve-responsive features still firing at the opposite angle where the originals are silent (originals 24–56° wide, matches 44–174°), and the same-layer SAE on the original layer matching at 0.96–1.00. A nonlinear readout of the ten neurons reaches only 0.29–0.48 correlation, and splicing the SAE in drops top-1 from 58.8% to 43.0%.
- **Growth is the explanation.** A retrained InceptionV1 neuron starts on rapeseed fields and a dog, grows onto tennis balls, green apples and jackfruit, then shaggy dogs with tennis balls and broccoli; the parameter-matched ConvNeXt neuron near it starts on the same fields and drops the rest. One live language neuron went from "iy" to "diy" to "do it yourself".
- **Most language neurons have one job.** A preliminary Qwen3-32B audit puts roughly 90% of neuron poles on one main job with small related offshoots, about 7% on two related jobs, and about 2–4% not yet understood, improving to about 97% when only strong activations are counted; GPT-2 XL went from under 2% clean in 2023 to about 70% looking roughly like one thing.
- **The double negative is architecture.** For a few percent of SwiGLU neurons the top of the activation list is the −×− echo: in one audited unit 131 of 133 responses in the p75–p99 band are double negatives while all 93 at p99.99 and above are +×+. Separating the sign regimes leaves the positive side under +0.5, about 4% of peak; ablating them raises most benchmarks but lowers some reasoning ones, unexplained.
- **Explanations can lose, and mostly do not.** For 200 random gallery neurons, agent-written new examples fired the neuron strongly 76% of the time against 11% for near misses (164 wins, 15 ties, 4 losses); on the first twenty shown, 81 of 100 new examples fired strongly against 8 of 100 near misses. Blind maximum routinely beats the best example in ten billion corpus tokens.
- **Dictionaries add little.** On the last layer 88% of one SAE's activity is near-copies of single neurons; removing every direction that matches no neuron leaves accuracy unchanged; searching rotations for the basis where a few units carry most activity lands back on the neurons in every block; what remains are loose groupings of existing neurons (animals at the water's surface at 0.15–0.19 across four neurons). SAEs helped most on the smallest, coarsest model and least on the largest.
- **Rare things ride existing routes.** The 7M umbrella-as-bee model moves from 44.2% umbrella to 0.03% and from 0.07% to 52.8% bee in nine training steps by fading umbrella-associated neurons and strengthening bee-associated ones in the last three blocks; swapping just those blocks into the original model reproduces the answer. No new hidden feature appears.
- **Weak activations can be deleted cheaply.** Removing the weakest 75% of activations goes from 72% to 71% accuracy with one answer in ten changing; removing the strongest 7% at the same total activation mass goes from 72% to 4% with 96 in 100 changing.
- **Attention is the weak spot.** The author's guess that global head names would add about 5% explanatory value was independently reproduced by an agent on 100 graphs, split roughly equally between easy position heads and everything else.

## Limits and open problems they name
Not every network has been audited and the author doubts that full coverage is possible in principle; the headline belief is roughly 85% confidence in the kind of process, not in a proportion of mechanisms, and the title is conceded to overstate the evidence and understate the picture at once. Recursive generalization has not been shown to cover every case; the pressure that keeps branches from overgrowing is unknown; checkpoints do not exist for the largest models, so growth cannot be watched there. Attention is not understood well enough to say whether it is superposed at all. Some exceptions are unresolved, including one unit collecting pinched shapes plus dress shoes and beach bags, which the author calls generalization with semantic residue. Cleanliness numbers depend on where the threshold for one thing is set, and the author argues such single scores matter less than the cause of the mixture.

## What we take from it
Four things. (1) **A confound and a checklist for our own read-outs:** a multi-concept unit is a claim about the observer as much as the model, and the check is cheap — ask whether one shared pattern explains the group, visualise what the unit's inputs share rather than the unit alone, and read the negative pole, because a distinction between two poles is not a mixture. (2) **Capacity squeeze as a falsifiable test of "it is hidden elsewhere":** cut a layer's width, retrain, and try to extract the missing units with a decomposition; if they do not reappear under shape and photo correlation, the packing hypothesis is rejectable rather than merely unfalsifiable. This is the cleanest available positive control for our claim that a read-out covers a layer. (3) **Blind maximum as a cheap, human-free evaluation** of an explanation we write: an account that lets an independent writer produce a stronger example than the corpus contains has done real work and cannot have been fitted to the examples — the same instrument the pedagogical-examples entry uses from the teacher side. (4) **A warning about inert sweeps:** if broadening rather than packing is what happens when capacity runs out, then a sweep across model sizes that reports a constant fraction of illegible units may be measuring the same phenomenon at several sizes rather than testing for a second learning principle.
