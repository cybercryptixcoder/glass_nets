# Stability Bands for Feature Attribution: The Core, the Nucleolus and the Bargaining Set of Explained-Variance Games

- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25238
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Shapley-based attribution is the default in applied economics, and it answers a fairness question: each feature gets its average marginal contribution under a set of axioms. It does not answer a stability question. If three features jointly generate most of a prediction but their summed shares fall short of what that coalition can produce on its own, the explanation can be contested on its own terms by naming the objecting coalition. The authors want to give every attribution the same treatment cooperative game theory gives a payoff vector: a set of allocations against which nobody has a justified complaint, an interval per feature saying how much credit is pinned down by the data and how much is a matter of convention, and a certificate a reader can check. The larger goal is a form of explanation that is not a single number presented without a stated degree of freedom.

## What they studied

The explained-variance game: players are features, and the worth of a feature set is the variance of the model's predictions when only those features vary and the rest sit at a baseline (the training mean). Shares are normalised so they add to one, and are shares of prediction variance, not of loss or of a single prediction. Two applications: a LASSO hedonic price regression for 807 mobile phones on seven characteristics (non-null after penalty: five), and a gradient-boosting regressor (300 trees, depth 4) for retail order quantity on 15 features over 7,991 orders from 2018–2020, split 70/30 by time. The unit of analysis is the feature, but the objects reported are intervals and coalitions, not scalars. The authors say plainly that the second model has no out-of-sample skill (train R² 0.274, test R² −0.033), so it is a test case for a genuinely non-additive function, not a claim about order behaviour.

## How they did it

Enumerate what the game's solution concepts look like, then compute them exactly on small problems and via sparse surrogates on larger ones. (1) A 2-additivity result for linear models: the worth becomes a quadratic form in the covariance matrix, so the Harsanyi dividends are βᵢ²σᵢᵢ on singletons and 2βᵢβⱼσᵢⱼ on pairs, and nothing of order three survives — interaction structure is generated entirely by multicollinearity. (2) A structural theorem for every 2-additive game with dividends of arbitrary sign: the prenucleolus equals the Shapley value, the (least) core is point-symmetric around it so every band is centred on it, the least-core value is minus half the minimum cut of the interaction graph, and the core is non-empty iff every cut has non-negative weight. Computed dividends agree with direct coalition evaluation to 7e-16, and bands from the closed form, from 2n LPs over all coalitions, and from a cutting-plane loop over a minimum-cut oracle (27 cuts) agree to 1e-12. (3) Algorithms for the general case: a sequential-LP prenucleolus with an explicit fixing rule tied to optimal duals rather than to whichever tight constraints the solver happened to return, an exact objection test for small games that constructs an objection vector per player pair by one LP each, and a minimum s-t cut that certifies any proposed allocation (SHAP, LIME, an analyst's guess) in convex games and returns the objecting coalition when the value is positive. (4) On the retail game they evaluate all 2^15 = 32,768 coalitions on 500 test-period orders, then fit LASSO k-additive surrogates on 2,000 random coalitions and score the surrogate's *solution* against the exact one on the remaining 30,766. Evaluation of explanations uses deletion faithfulness (AOPC over 150 instances), a Gini concentration measure, the count of features needed for 80% of attributed mass, and 50 bootstrap re-estimates.

## What counts as an explanation here

A set-valued object rather than a point: for each feature an interval of stable shares, plus one distinguished point (the nucleolus) inside it, plus a short proof-of-work — either "this allocation admits no justified objection" or the specific coalition that objects and the excess it claims. It is meant to be partial and honest about its own freedom: the depth of the band is the part of the attribution the data does not determine. It predicts nothing about model behaviour; it constrains which number may be quoted as the explanation.

## How they judged success

Contested-claim certificates (does mean-abs-SHAP or mean-abs-LIME survive the minimum-cut test, and if not, by which coalition and how much excess), agreement between three independent band computations, internal consistency with the theorem (nucleolus at band centre, dividends matching direct evaluation), solution-level fidelity of surrogates (max coordinate error and Spearman against the exact nucleolus, top-5 overlap, next to held-out R² of the surrogate *game*), AOPC faithfulness against a random deletion order, and bootstrap stability of the bands themselves (how often a replicate's share falls inside the full-sample band).

## What they found

The mobile model is convex and exactly 2-additive, the nucleolus equals the Shapley value, and the conventional explanations do not survive. Ratings' band runs from 0.300 to 0.633 of the prediction variance — over a third of the total is convention, not data. The primary camera has one of the narrowest bands in the table, [0.045, 0.089], and mean-abs-SHAP assigns it 0.126 and mean-abs-LIME 0.148; both over-credit it and under-credit the complementary block {Ratings, ROM, Battery}. The minimum-cut certificate puts the SHAP allocation's worst-coalition excess at 0.050 and LIME's at 0.073, and the objection LP returns an explicit justified objection by Ratings against the primary camera through that block. The retail game is the opposite case: first-order dividends total 2.39 and second-order -2.31 times v(N), the absolute mass of orders 3, 4 and 5 (3.70, 3.00, 1.99) rivals the pairs (3.55), 25.7% of dividends of order ≥2 are negative, and the core is empty with ε* = 0.608 — every allocation leaves some coalition at least 61% of v(N) short. Here the nucleolus and the Shapley value are genuinely different points: maximum difference 0.185, Spearman 0.90, concentrated in the price block — margin goes from −0.011 under Shapley to −0.197 under the nucleolus while unit cost rises from 0.322 to 0.434. The Shapley value falls outside the least-core band for six of fifteen features, its worst coalition is short by 0.790, and 872 coalitions exceed ε*; rescaled TreeSHAP is worse still (maximum excess 1.036, 3,072 coalitions above ε*). All three agree on which coalition is worst-treated. The surrogate study is the sharper result: pairwise surrogates fit the game well (held-out R² 0.865–0.898) yet miss the nucleolus by 0.169–0.184 and never recover more than 4 of 5 top features, while third-order surrogates reach R² 0.973–0.997 and close the gap to 0.128, 0.074, 0.038 and 0.018 as active dividends grow from 68 to 498. Forcing non-negative dividends — the tempting route to free certificates — removes every interaction term, produces an additive surrogate with R² 0.32 whose minimum-cut test passes at excess below 1e-14, and whose allocation is nonetheless short by 0.715–0.716 at its worst coalition in the true game and sits 0.17 from the true nucleolus. Faithfulness barely separates the methods on the mobile model (AOPC 0.980 for all three, against 0.666 for random order) because all three rank features identically; on retail the nucleolus leads (0.412 against 0.380 for Shapley of v and 0.390 for TreeSHAP, against 0.325 random), and the authors note that deletion faithfulness reflects only the ranking, so the bands, objections and negative shares — all magnitude claims — are invisible to it.

## Limits and open problems they name

Exact computation needs all coalitions, workable to roughly 20 features; beyond that everything rests on sparse surrogates whose interaction order must be validated at the solution level rather than at the game level. The exact justified-objection test is exponential and practical only for small n. All results are conditional on the choice of game — interventional baseline and prediction variance — and conditional-expectation, loss-based or local games give different games to which the same machinery applies. Convexity is necessary for the closed-form bands; in the bootstrap, the core was exactly non-empty in only 42% of replicates (median ε* 0.0008, maximum 0.014), so convexity holds in practice but must be checked per application. The group-comparison diagnostic is an illustration on an unprotected attribute with a skill-free model, and the authors say a real audit would need both a protected attribute and predictive skill.

## What we take from it

- **Frame:** report an attribution as an interval with a distinguished interior point and a stated certificate, not as a scalar. The band width is a quantity we currently never publish: how much of a feature's credit the data leave undetermined.
- **Measure:** the minimum-cut certificate is the reusable instrument — one s-t cut tells you whether any allocation (ours included) is contestable in a convex game, and returns the contesting set when it is. Our own white-box model's per-feature weight vectors can be certified the same way against a variance- or loss-based game, which turns "is our explanation defensible" into a computable boolean plus a witness.
- **Warning (the strongest one in the paper):** game-level fidelity is a poor guide to solution-level fidelity. A surrogate that reconstructs the worth function to R² 0.90 can be off by 0.18 on the very quantity you report, and no amount of extra pairwise terms fixes it. Our white-box model, if it is fitted as a surrogate over coalitions, must be validated on the solution it emits, not on its fit to the game.
- **Warning (the trap):** a constraint that makes your machinery produce certificates can destroy the object. Forcing non-negative dividends gave an exactly-certified allocation that was 0.72 wrong. A certificate is only as good as the game it was computed on — this is our run-943 rule (a derived null the method must hit) seen from the other side: a derived *pass* the method must not be able to fake.
- **Framing:** negative shares are informative, not errors — they identify features whose variation offsets others, which is what substitution looks like when variance is the worth. Any additive decomposition our model reports should keep signed values and state what a negative means.
- **Dataset:** both are public (Kaggle), with a reproducible code path and regression tests; the retail 15-feature game is a useful non-additive benchmark with an exactly computable ground truth for any attribution method we build.
- **Shortcut:** for a linear model with a covariance matrix, the whole game is known in closed form from coefficients and covariances — the cheapest possible exact test bed.
