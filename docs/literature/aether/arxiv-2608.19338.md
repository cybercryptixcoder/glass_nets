## What they wanted, and why

The author starts from a diagnostic observation rather than a mechanism claim: coordinate patching, attribution patching, subset interventions and Hessian-vector products each push an activation along a direction and record how far a readout moves, but they are reported as separate methods with separate numbers, so it is impossible to say whether they disagree because they measure different things or because one of them is wrong. The paper's proposal is to write all of them as one inverse problem, `ỹ = Ax + w`, where the rows of `A` are the interventions actually performed, `x` is the effect map one wants, and `w` is everything the linear prediction misses. Once that is done, questions that were previously rhetorical become design decisions: which family can represent the target, how many measurements its identification costs, and whether a residual seen on held-out interventions is a scale error or evidence of missing structure. The motivating stake is control — an estimate that guides a steering direction or an ablation is an *observer* in the control-theory sense, and its adequacy is therefore a property of a closed loop, not of a fit. This is the measurement half of the author's own benchmark paper (ObserverBench, `arxiv-2609.03026`, already in this folder), and it supplies the missing piece of that entry: the derivation that says why prediction and decision come apart.

## What they studied

Five rungs of a ladder in which one external reference is surrendered at a time, with the **unit of analysis being the measurement map** — a vector or matrix of component effects plus, where relevant, pair coefficients — rather than a feature, head or circuit. (i) **Two-HMM wind tunnel**: a small Transformer trained on tokens from two independent HMMs, with exact filtered posteriors as the reference state (a target belief and a nuisance belief); a 32-coordinate intervention basis formed by crossing layer with token position. (ii) **Planted harness**: a synthetic response over 64 candidate components with four nonzero terms — two main effects, one pure interaction with no first-order signal, and one redundancy pair — plus an optional off-path confound, giving a lifted space of 2,080 main-and-pair features. (iii) **Tracr**: three compiled RASP programs over `A`, `B`, `C` (a current-token control and two AB-conjunction detectors, one numeric and one boolean), enumerated exhaustively at sequence length 6 (243 sequences, 1,215 position-level examples), inspected in a native compiler basis and in a restricted pre-conjunction basis. (iv) **GPT-2-small on IOI**, using the published Primary (3 heads), Backup (8 heads) and Negative Name Mover (2 heads) groups. (v) **Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct refusal margin**, eight layerwise harmful-versus-benign contrast directions at layers 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, a 401-action library at four mask densities, HarmBench harmful and XSTest safe-but-safety-adjacent prompts, and no LLM judge.

## How they did it

The formal apparatus comes first. A mask `a` assigns coefficients to component directions, the intervention moves the state by `ε·D_c·a`, and the normalized observed response is measured against a clean pair on the same context. A second-order Taylor argument then yields the paper's Lemma 1: any such finite measurement equals the linear prediction `aᵀx` plus a residual with three separately interpretable terms — a curvature term growing linearly in `ε`, a numerator-error term decaying as `1/ε`, and normalized sampling error. The two extremes are therefore both excluded by algebra: large probes encounter nonlinearity, and small probes amplify noise in the numerator. Because the residual includes nonlinear response, it is *not* independent of the design — changing the masks changes what is being measured — which is the paper's stated break from textbook compressed sensing. Density enters through a separate route, since a dense mask changes the displacement and therefore the conditioning of the recovery problem, which produces a conditional objective in `ε` and `ρ` and an optimal scale that varies as the square root of numerator error over curvature-times-displacement². Three further results structure the rest of the paper: a **calibration dimension** (the smallest declared correction family — zero, one scalar gain, group gains, or pairs — that predicts held-out finite responses, identifiable from a number of well-conditioned probes of order the correction's dimension); a **first-order blindness** statement (if a pair's singleton effects are zero, no amount of first-order measurement can see its cross term, because the required product column is absent from the family); and the **basis dependence** of the apparent interaction order, which the Tracr arm makes concrete. The white-box route is that a backward pass yields the whole local first-order map at once, while a few designed Hessian-vector products give linear measurements of the local curvature map, convertible to finite pair coefficients only when the response is quadratic along the path.

The experiments then follow the paper's own workflow: state the intended use, start with the cheapest family that can represent the target, let held-out residuals choose the next family, and validate the observer through its intended action. In the wind tunnel, the transformer's closed loop is run with the controller and actuator held fixed and only the observer varied across ten variants (including an oracle, a probe, coarse and noisy estimates, a last-emission proxy, and an entangled estimate); a second condition deliberately mixes target and nuisance into both the observer and the actuation direction. In the HMM map study, all 32 coordinates are patched individually to build a reference, then aggregate masks are used to recover the same map with orthogonal matching pursuit against a ridge baseline, with a separate validation split choosing support size. In the calibration study, two intervention scales are crossed with three independently generated sequence batches and three independently generated mask-and-split designs (nine cells of 128 masks: 72 fit, 24 select, 32 evaluate), and the scalar gain is also fitted leave-one-batch-out; a separate sweep draws calibration sets of 4, 8, 16 and 96 probes. In the interaction studies, lifted designs add every product `a_i·a_j` as a column and are compared against raw, scalar- and multi-gain attribution patching, finite singleton patching and first-order OMP at a generous budget, then at reduced budgets with both a prediction threshold and a support-recall threshold; white-box recovery combines the AtP main-effect map with designed HVPs, with a pair-only HVP ablation and a singleton-main-effect replacement as controls; Gaussian noise is added to separate support identification from coefficient accuracy; and one off-path coordinate is given a causal coordinate's mask pattern to create an aliasing trap. In Tracr, the detector group is ablated in the final residual stream and then reconstructed from the restricted basis additively or with lifted pairs and written back, which tests whether the final readout actually uses the recovered subspace. On IOI, two studies run in parallel: 21 direct group masks over 128 matched name pairs and eight lexical/order conditions, under two ablation conventions (template-conditioned mean ablation and zero ablation), scored with a crossed bootstrap that resamples lexical frames and the shared name-pair vector; and a predictive study of 240 masks over 256 name pairs on one template, five-fold, scored by held-out MAE of the count-additive model with one group-pair term added at a time, audited across 200 fold assignments. On Qwen, a calibrated additive map and a lifted map with all 28 layer products are fit under a fixed basis, prompt split and action library, tested at a scale (0.75) that appears in neither calibration nor validation (0.5 and 1.0), over 128 held-out actions crossed with 224 held-out prompts.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **measurement model plus a validated observer**: an explicitly declared basis, an intervention family, a recovery procedure, and a residual term with its error budget. There is no circuit, dictionary or natural-language description anywhere in the paper, and the author says so — mechanism claims about IOI, backup, self-repair and copy suppression are taken as given and used only to define the surfaces on which measurements are made. The paper is careful to state what its own formulation cannot decide: the coordinate basis is an experimental hypothesis rather than a discovered fact (a computation can be first-order in a basis containing a completed detector and interactional in a basis containing only its inputs); behavioural control does not identify a mechanism, since an effective steering knob need not represent a belief state or a bottleneck; and effect maps are context-averaged, so their validity is bounded by the prompt distribution over which they were measured. The predictions claimed are therefore partial in a precise way: valid for the declared masks, scale, basis and context distribution, checkable only on held-out interventions from the same declared design.

## How they judged success

Every experiment reports **held-out prediction of finite interventions** (R², MAE, Pearson r, and support recall where the support is planted) under a declared access regime and measurement budget, with ablation or prediction cost counted in interventions rather than wall-clock. Controls are layered rather than singular: a dense ridge baseline at exhaustive cost, first-order OMP at the same budget, singleton patching, multi-gain patching, a pair-only HVP observer, a singleton-replacement observer, a count-additive model with flexible per-group dose-response as the interaction-free rival, two ablation conventions for the same IOI contrast, both orderings of the IOI name frames, leave-one-frame-out, a normalisation by possible cross-group head pairs to rule out group-size artefacts, a three-way residual to show the pair terms do not exhaust the circuit, 200 fold assignments to test whether a predicted gain depends on the split, leave-one-batch-out for the fitted gain, an independent held-out mask set for the aliasing test, and a scale held out entirely from calibration. Statistics are bootstrap intervals throughout — crossed for IOI, paired prompt-family-by-action for Qwen, prompt-level for the two-HMM studies. Pre-registration is used deliberately but honestly: the Qwen comparison has a predeclared 5% practical threshold and the paper reports that the interval's upper end crosses it, so the stopping decision is stated as a failure to detect rather than an equivalence.

## What they found

**Observer error reaches the loop, and target success hides the rest.** Across ten observers with the controller and actuator fixed, closed-loop target error rises with observer RMSE at Spearman 0.95. The entangled condition — an observer that mixes the target with half the nuisance belief, actuated along the same mixture — cuts target MSE from 25.02 uncontrolled to 4.81 against 3.56 for the oracle, while nuisance movement rises from 0.037 to 0.079. In the same study the linear probe sometimes beats the analytic-posterior oracle on closed-loop error despite the oracle being the more faithful estimate of the external posterior, which the paper reads as a measured separation between reconstruction and control utility: the controller edits the state the transformer represents, and the probe can align with that editable state better than the external truth does.

**Aggregate measurements buy a compressible map cheaply.** The 32-coordinate finite-effect map has 98.4% of its squared norm in its four largest coordinates. Twelve aggregate masks with OMP recover a four-coordinate model at Pearson r = 0.989 and held-out R² = 0.935, where ridge needs all 32 measurements to reach r = 0.962 and held-out R² = 0.933 — a large saving in interventions for a comparable held-out score.

**One scalar gain repairs a gradient map at finite scale.** Raw attribution patching is already good at the smaller scale (R² 0.943) and overpredicts at the larger one, where saturation shrinks the finite response (0.818). A single fitted gain lifts the larger-scale map to 0.960 and the smaller to 0.963, against 0.969 and 0.965 for OMP, with the fitted gain itself moving from 0.874 to 0.742 as the scale grows (means over nine cells, min/max reported). Eight calibration probes already give 0.951 and 0.948; sixteen give 0.957 and 0.955; leave-one-batch-out over the nine cells gives 0.959, so the correction transfers across sequence batches rather than fitting one set of examples. The paper notes that some cells still show a gap to OMP after all 96 probes, so the gain is an approximation and not an exact description of the finite map.

**Interactions are representational, not a fitting shortfall.** In the planted 2,080-feature system with four nonzero terms, lifted OMP recovers the pair structure with near-perfect held-out prediction, while raw AtP, scalar- and multi-gain AtP, finite singleton patching and first-order OMP **all stay below 0.5 held-out R²** even at a generous 256-measurement budget — no amount of extra calibration can supply a column the family does not contain. Lifted OMP reaches both R² ≥ 0.95 and pair recall ≥ 0.99 at 64 aggregate measurements when `ε = 5` and 72 when `ε = 8`, against 2,016 probes for exhaustive pairwise measurement. The white-box route needs 12 HVP queries at the smaller scale and 24 at the larger (plus the AtP backward pass), but the pair-only ablation finds the right pairs and still predicts only R² 0.36 and 0.60 — support recovery is not response prediction, and the main effects are needed alongside the curvature. Replacing the gradient main effects with finite singleton effects performs worse because singleton interventions saturate. With Gaussian noise added to 96 aggregate measurements, both prediction and recall survive to a noise standard deviation of 0.01 at the smaller scale (recall alone to 0.05) and to 0.05 at the larger (recall alone to 0.2) — the interacting pair can remain identifiable after coefficient error has made the observer unusable for prediction.

**The apparent interaction order is a property of the basis.** In Tracr, the current-token control is additive in both bases (held-out R² 1.0), and the AB detectors are additive in the native compiler basis because the compiler has already created an explicit completed-detector coordinate. In the restricted pre-conjunction basis an additive model explains only about half the detector variance, and adding pair products restores R² 1.0 while selecting the intended `previous-token-A × current-token-B` term. The writeback test makes it causal rather than descriptive: ablating the detector group in the final residual stream drops held-out readout R² from 1.00 to about −0.05 (numeric) and −0.51 (boolean); writing back the additive reconstruction restores only about 0.55 for both, while writing back the lifted reconstruction restores 1.00. The additive score of 0.93 on the boolean detector group is misleading because it mostly captures the common negative case and misses the rare positive conjunction. The authors also explain the negative R² after boolean ablation as an out-of-distribution state created by zeroing complementary True/False coordinates — an ablation artefact, not evidence the detector is harmful.

**On IOI, the largest interaction is not the famous one.** Direct group ablations reproduce conditional backup — removing the primary group costs 0.463 of the IO-versus-subject margin, removing the backups 0.350, and the additive prediction of 0.813 is beaten by the measured 1.869, an interaction of roughly 1.06 logits — but the primary–negative interaction is larger and more stable: 1.830 [1.569, 2.076] under mean ablation and 1.681 [1.474, 1.889] under zero ablation, against 1.080 and 1.268 for primary–backup and 0.502 and 0.518 for backup–negative. The ordering P×E > P×B > B×E holds in every lexical/order condition and both ablation conventions, survives dropping any single frame, and after dividing by the number of possible cross-group head pairs the primary–negative term is 6.8× (mean) and 5.3× (zero) the primary–backup term. A three-way residual of 0.319 [0.215, 0.439] and 0.601 [0.417, 0.790] remains, so the pair terms rank documented interactions and do not decompose the circuit. The held-out subset study gives the same ordering with a different instrument: a count-additive model sits at MAE 0.386 and R² 0.752, adding primary–negative takes it to 0.259 and 0.887 (MAE improvement 0.1265 [0.1195, 0.1338]), primary–backup to 0.377 and 0.774 (0.0083 [0.0064, 0.0101]), backup–negative to 0.386 and 0.754 (−0.0002, indistinguishable from nothing), and all three together to 0.236 and 0.907. The fold audit confirms the ranking: the primary–negative gain survives all 200 assignments, primary–backup 199, backup–negative 154. The paper's reading of the gap between corner and predictive measures is that a strong interaction at a corner contributes little on average if the mask distribution rarely reaches that corner.

**On Qwen, the simpler family stops the procedure.** At the held-out scale 0.75, the 20-parameter calibrated additive map reaches R² 0.9829 (MAE 0.003790) and the 48-parameter lifted map 0.9835 (0.003801) — a relative MAE change of −0.29% with a 95% interval of [−3.56%, 5.65%] that includes zero, so no lifted advantage is detected; because the interval's upper end crosses the predeclared 5% threshold, the paper states this as a stopping decision rather than an equivalence. Model size alone therefore does not determine which measurement family is needed: the same procedure that required pairs on GPT-2 stops at a scalar correction on a 7B instruction-tuned model, and the deciding evidence is the declared basis and the held-out residual.

## Limits and open problems they name

The paper's own §7 "where the evidence remains narrow" is unusually candid and worth taking at face value. The planted interaction experiment assumes sparsity and a basis that already contains the correct pair terms, and natural mechanisms may violate either. Tracr supplies privileged compiler labels, so the basis result rests on compiled toy programs. The IOI direct ordering survives eight lexical/order conditions and two ablation conventions, but its predictive comparison uses one prompt template and mean ablation only, and the paper explicitly does not identify a particular head pair as the dominant edge. Qwen extends the procedure to a modern open-weight model but only on a behavioural refusal margin, so it shows transfer of the procedure and not recovery of a represented state, and one model, one basis and one response surface cannot establish a scaling law. The scale–density result is a conditional design bound, not a measured `ε × ρ` surface: the paper does not estimate the curvature constant, the numerator-error bound or the full joint objective. The wind-tunnel test uses a small model whose features are known by construction, and the entangled condition changes both observer and actuator, so it establishes that target improvement can coexist with nuisance movement rather than isolating observer error. Mine, marked: (a) the calibration experiments are on a single trained HMM model with intervention directions estimated once, so the nine evaluation cells vary only sequence batches and mask designs — model-to-model variation in the fitted gain is untested; (b) the aliasing stress test pairs a false coordinate with a causal one *outside* the lifted design, and the paper itself says a stronger test would alias a false pair with a true pair inside it, which leaves the lifted route's worst failure mode undemonstrated; (c) no figure was inspected, so the per-cell spread quoted above is the min/max intervals stated in tables rather than a reading of the plotted bands; (d) the Qwen mask-density factor (four densities) is part of the action library but its effect is not separately reported in the held-out comparison, so the density tradeoff the theory emphasises is not measured on the pretrained model.

## What we take from it

Four things, and the first is the reason this entry is central rather than useful. **(i) The measurement equation is the frame our white-box model should adopt.** Writing every reading of a network as `ỹ = Ax + w` with a mask design, an effect map and a residual with three named sources converts "what does this probe tell us" into "which family can represent the target and what identifies it" — and it does so with a cost accounting (interventions, backward passes, HVP queries) attached to each family, which is exactly the currency a predictive model needs when it must choose what to measure at probe time. The companion discipline is that the residual is design-dependent, so a fit quality on the masks used for fitting is not evidence: held-out interventions belong to identification, not to reporting, which the aliasing example makes concrete — two coordinates with the same mask pattern are indistinguishable on the training design even when only one is causal. **(ii) Calibration dimension is the stopping rule we previously lacked.** Start with the cheapest family, fit the smallest declared correction, and escalate only when the held-out residual exceeds the estimated noise floor, with the arrow direction diagnosed from the *shape* of the residual: a shared scale error says fit a gain, conditional error says add pairs, and persistent error after lifting says change the basis. The numbers make this cheap rather than aspirational — one gain moves a gradient map from 0.818 to 0.960, and eight probes suffice — and they also price the failure: if the response lies outside the family, the residual lower-bounds the error of every observer in it by construction, so more data cannot help, which is exactly the claim our folder keeps re-deriving from the other side. **(iii) The basis result is the sharpest available argument that a mechanism claim is relative to its coordinates.** The same Tracr computation is additive in the native basis, interactional in the pre-conjunction basis, and additive again after lifting — and the writeback test is what separates a label from a mechanism: the additive reconstruction scores 0.93 reconstructing the detector group yet restores only 0.55 of the readout, while the lifted one restores both. For us this says a probe or crosscoder report should state its interaction order *and* its basis together, and should ship a writeback or intervention test, because reconstruction-accuracy and readout-sufficiency are different criteria that a high score on one can hide a failure on the other. **(iv) The IOI ranking is a warning about corner-measured effects and about group averages.** Conditional backup is real and large at the corner (an interaction of about 1.06 logits that can flip a decision the additive model predicts stays correct) but contributes 0.008 MAE when scored across a realistic mask distribution, while the primary–negative interaction is both larger and far more predictive — so "the interaction the literature emphasises" and "the interaction that predicts interventions over the distribution you will actually face" are separate facts, and the cheaper test is the held-out subset study rather than the exhaustive corner. The negative-name-mover sign is also worth keeping as a pattern in its own right (see below). Like every other entry in this folder, this one closes by naming what it does not do: it supplies no mechanism of its own, and it is honest that behavioural adequacy under a behavioural endpoint is not faithfulness to an internal state.
