# Align, Unify, Suppress, Route: A Coherentist View of Transformer Computation

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.22034
- **Authors / organisation:** Nura Aljaafari; Andre Freitas
- **Date:** 2026-08-22
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22034
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Mechanistic interpretability has described many individual circuits — induction heads, the IOI circuit, copy-suppression, feedforward memories — but has no shared vocabulary for saying what those circuits *contribute* in functional terms, so findings from different papers and different architecture families cannot be compared or composed. The authors propose a vocabulary of four operator roles — alignment (propose candidate relations between fragments), unification (add compatible content), suppression (reduce support for incompatible alternatives), routing (carry the result to the readout) — grounded in the idea that a language model builds an interpretation by progressively resolving incoherence in its context. They formalise this as Coherentist Probabilistic Compositionalism (CPC), a coherence functional over grounded fragments, and then treat the framework explicitly as a *modelling hypothesis* with four testable predictions rather than a claim that transformers optimise that functional. The larger goal is a level of description that is architecture-independent while depth-level claims stay per-family. This matters for us because it is the folder's first attempt to build a **graded role vocabulary** on top of circuits and then test it on held-out activations and causal ablations — which is close to what a predictive white-box model needs to output.

## What they studied

Fifteen decoder-only English models from five families: GPT-2 Small/Medium/Large, Pythia-410M/1.4B/2.8B, Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B, Gemma-2-2B, LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, plus four instruction-tuned variants (Gemma-2B-It, LLaMA-1B-It, LLaMA-3B-It, Qwen-1.5B-It). Unit of analysis: **attention heads**, represented in weight space by a four-coordinate signature (alignment, suppression, unification, routing-copy), and at the activation level by direct logit attribution (DLA) split into positive and negative parts, attention-pattern detectors, and an attention-weighted copy score. Tasks: indirect object identification (IOI), greater-than comparison (200 GPT-2/Pythia models only — other tokenisers give no single-token two-digit continuation, so nine models are excluded from that task), and factual recall over CounterFact. Every example is filtered to those the model predicts correctly, target 500 per model-task pair, split 50/50 into a discovery half (where heads are selected and ranked) and a held-out half (where outcomes are scored). A separate contradiction set of 500 matched consistent/contradictory prompt pairs per run is drawn from a frozen pool of 3,854 unique pairs.

## How they did it

Weight space: each head's effective OV map is decomposed by SVD; a mode counts as subtractive when the singular-value-weighted inner product between its left and right singular vectors is negative, and the additive and subtractive sums become the unification and suppression scores, while alignment is the squared top QK singular value as a share of all QK singular values, and routing is the embedding-space copy score of an earlier paper (the head's OV map applied through token embeddings and unembedding directions, scored by the positive share of eigenvalues). The trace descriptor is reported to be exactly the difference of unification and suppression divided by width, and is excluded from every analysis as non-independent. Heads are then clustered (k-means, k from 2 to 7, 50 restarts) and PCA'd, with silhouette calibrated against a column-shuffle null and an isotropic Gaussian null. Functional validity: weight scores are correlated (Spearman) with the matching held-out activation measures and with held-out single-head ablation effects, against three baselines — 100 random weight projections, weight-magnitude ranking, and shuffled-task-relation scores. Causality: the top five heads per operator ranking are zero-ablated on held-out IOI, and the change in correct-token logit, wrong-token logit and margin is compared with layer-matched random-head ablations (20 control samples per intervention) and a magnitude-matched control. Coupling test: ablate the alignment heads and measure downstream suppressor heads' summed suppressive DLA on contradiction prompts, against random-head ablation and against the same intervention on matched no-conflict prompts (paired permutation tests, 10,000 permutations, Holm-corrected across 30 model-by-contrast comparisons, bootstrap CIs). Contradiction test: a layerwise coherence proxy, the mean cosine similarity over all pairs of residual-stream positions, compared between matched prompts by layer third; then three controls — lexically matched non-contradictory pairs, shuffled *cross-prompt* position pairs, and a per-layer ZCA-whitened variant (shrinkage 0.1) that removes shared residual covariance — plus a logit-lens rank trajectory and a naturalistic Wikipedia set. Post-training test: base versus instruction-tuned pairs on per-head signatures, with induction-head score correlation under a one-sided preservation test at r = 0.9. Decision rules for all four predictions were fixed before the confirmatory runs, with explicit support / partial / mixed / against criteria, and every stochastic step runs at five seeds.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **functional role label on components**, in a graded rather than exclusive form: a head may instantiate several operators and an operator may span many heads, so the explanation is a per-head four-dimensional signature plus a claim about what that signature does downstream. It is deliberately partial — CPC calls itself an interpretive framework, not a mechanistic proof, and says it does not claim transformers optimise its coherence functional. Prediction is limited to the form "a head scoring high on operator X will show activation signature X and ablation effect X", which the paper tests head by head and model by model but never assembles into a forward model of a whole forward pass. The interpretation of depth (alignment early, unification and suppression mid, routing late) is presented as a tendency and tested statistically per layer third.

## How they judged success

Pre-specified verdicts per prediction. P1 (signatures): support needs the mean functional correlation to beat *all* applicable baselines *and* a positive correlation with held-out single-head ablation effects; partial needs one baseline beaten. P2 (coupling): support needs the alignment-ablation reduction to exceed the random-head control at Holm-corrected 0.05 *and* to be weaker on matched no-conflict prompts. P3 (contradiction): support needs a significant raw coherence gap in at least one layer third that co-locates with the peak alignment or suppression third; the direction prediction is scored separately on the whitened proxy. P4 (post-training): support needs the late suppression-and-routing shift to exceed the early alignment shift *and* induction preservation to pass the one-sided test at r = 0.9. Secondary: layerwise composition (unification mid-or-late, routing late, suppression increasing early-to-mid) and cross-task stability (mean cross-task r > 0.5 for both operators). Headline measures are Spearman correlations against baseline quantiles, logit-margin deltas against two controls, silhouette against two nulls, and Holm-corrected paired tests.

## What they found

P1: suppression, unification and routing scores correlate with their held-out activation measures at mean Spearman between 0.21 and 0.58 per model, positive in all fifteen; the copy score is the strongest single predictor (r = 0.59 against the activation copy measure, versus 0.40 for the trace score). The correlations exceed random-projection and shuffled-relation baselines everywhere but the weight-magnitude baseline in only ten models — in the Qwen family and two LLaMA variants, generic OV magnitude tracks the role measures just as well. The alignment weight score does not track the attention-based alignment measure (r about -0.04), so the alignment role rests entirely on the activation detector. Predicting held-out ablation effect sizes is the weak cell: positive rank correlation in three models only. Verdicts: 3 support, 12 partial, 0 against. Clustering: silhouette at k = 4 runs 0.36-0.50, but the preferred count is architecture-dependent (k = 2 in Qwen, 3 in most GPT-2/Pythia-1.4B, 4-6 elsewhere) and no family reproduces a four-way split; every model beats the Gaussian null (mean silhouette advantage +0.28) and eleven beat the column-shuffle null (+0.09), with GPT-2 Small, Pythia-410M and Qwen-1.5B the exceptions. Cross-score structure: copy anti-correlates with suppression (r = -0.61) and correlates with unification (+0.44); suppression and unification anti-correlate at -0.30; alignment is nearly independent of everything (|r| at most 0.07), and PCA gives 49%, 27%, 18%, 7%. Depth explains little: the depth-separation ratio of signature variance is 0.02-0.24 with a mean of 0.08. P2: ablating alignment heads reduces downstream suppressive contribution in twelve models, beyond the layer-matched control in ten, but the conflict-specificity control is passed by only LLaMA-3.2-3B — on no-conflict prompts the same ablation reduces suppression just as much, so the effect is generic upstream dependence, not a dedicated conflict channel. Verdicts: 1 support, 9 partial, 5 against. P3: the raw coherence gap is significant in fourteen models but its *sign is architecture-dependent* — GPT-2 and Pythia show the predicted positive gap, Gemma, Qwen and three of four LLaMA variants show contradictory prompts *raising* pairwise similarity — and the shuffled cross-prompt control reproduces the raw gap almost exactly in fourteen models, so the raw proxy is a global state marker of contradiction rather than a measure of within-prompt relations; once the shared residual covariance is removed (whitened proxy) the predicted decrease appears in every model and is significant in fourteen. The logit-lens trajectory agrees (contradiction worsens the rank of the coherent continuation in all models, largest late), the naturalistic Wikipedia set replicates positively in ten, and the ascent conjecture (the proxy not falling with depth) holds in fourteen with Spearman up to 0.93. P4: induction-head score structure is preserved across all four base/tuned pairs at r 0.98-1.00 against the 0.9 threshold, silhouettes are near-identical (differences at most 0.001), and the predicted late concentration of the operator shift appears in both LLaMA pairs but not in Gemma or Qwen (2 support, 2 partial). Causality: unification-head ablation lowers the correct-token logit in ten models (beat layer-matched control 9, magnitude-matched 11), routing in fourteen (beats both in 10; Gemma-2B-It changes the IO logit by -3.45 against +0.83 for control), but suppression-head ablation beats its control in only five and the predicted release of the wrong token appears in seven. Re-ranking by *task-targeted* DLA on the discovery split repairs this — the margin moves in the predicted direction in every model for both operators (mean +2.07 for suppression, -1.86 for unification) — at the price of needing task labels. Cross-task: suppression is the stable operator (mean r = 0.68 across tasks, above 0.5 in twelve models, 0.99 for Gemma-2B) while unification is not (0.45). One published counter-example is kept: GPT-2 Small's unification ablation *raises* the correct-token logit (+1.27 against -0.80 for control), which the authors attribute to the documented backup behaviour of that circuit recruiting compensatory heads.

## Limits and open problems they name

The framework is interpretive rather than proven, the four operators are not claimed to be exhaustive, and the predicted failure regimes (ambiguity overload, depth overload, preference distortion) are untested. The coherence proxy conflates semantic coherence with residual-stream geometry, which the authors say outright. The suppression evidence is the weakest link: the weight score measures total subtractive OV capacity, a property of the matrix, while what that capacity acts on depends on the residual state — hence the failure of the task-specific ablation, and the reason task-labelled DLA is needed to fix it. Tasks are synthetic (greater-than covers six models, the naturalistic set is a single negation template over Wikipedia leads) and the base-vs-tuned arm is four pairs. The P2 test uses the validated IOI circuit only for GPT-2 Small, detects heads automatically elsewhere, and its specificity control passed in one model, so those verdicts lean on the random-head control. Assigning heads to operators through causal abstraction (interchange interventions, causal scrubbing) and the suppression-versus-unification asymmetry in task-generality are named as next steps.

## What we take from it

- **Framing (adopt):** a white-box model should emit *graded role labels per component*, not a partition — this paper's own clustering says no family forms four disjoint classes, and printing a per-head four-coordinate signature is a cheap way to avoid the false tidiness our reports are prone to.
- **Measure (adopt):** the three-tier validation of a weight-space descriptor — correlation with held-out activation measure, then with held-out ablation *effect size*, then a causal ablation against a layer-matched random set — is the right ladder, and this paper shows the top rung is where descriptors fail (three models only). Weight scores validated against activations are not yet predictors of causal effect.
- **Warning (important for us):** against the weight-magnitude baseline the descriptor wins in only ten of fifteen models. If a pooled statistic computed from a head's norm predicts roles as well as the structured signature, the structured signature is not adding what it claims; the folder already has a scale-normalization rule (run 936/937) and this is its weight-space sibling.
- **Warning (the contradiction result):** a proxy correlating with an abstract property (coherence) sign-flips across families and is reproduced by a control that destroys the property it is supposed to measure (cross-prompt position pairs). Before our own aggregate proxies are quoted, the shuffled-pair control is mandatory, and the whiten-then-retest step is the repair to try first.
- **Reusable construct:** the *specificity control* — the same intervention on matched prompts that lack the target phenomenon — is cheap, and here it killed a prediction (P2) that the plain control would have let pass. Our ablation tables should always carry the "same intervention, phenomenon absent" cell beside the random-set cell.
- **Warning:** a component whose capacity is a matrix property (total subtractive weight) cannot be attributed a task-specific function without task evidence; the repair (task-targeted DLA) works but imports labels, so the honest form of such a claim is "capacity here, action needs labels".
