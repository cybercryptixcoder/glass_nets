# From Geometric Recovery to Causal Validation: A Reproducible Audit of Sparse Autoencoder Features, from Superposition Geometry to Causal Inertness

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.12166
- **Authors / organisation:** Mohamed Abdessalem Bal (single author); Independent Researcher, Algiers
- **Date:** 2026-07-13
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12166
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, all 14 sections plus the reproducibility statement and reference list; figures arrive as captions only)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
The field decides whether a sparse autoencoder has found a real feature by asking whether some dictionary atom points in nearly the same direction as a known or probed concept direction. The author's complaint is that this single number answers a question about the *decoder* (does the word vector for the concept live here) while quietly implying an answer about the *encoder* (does this atom switch on when the concept is present). The paper sets out to measure the distance between those two claims in a setting where the truth is known, and then to turn the measurement into a tool anyone can point at a published dictionary. The larger ambition is epistemic rather than architectural: establish, with interventions rather than with more correlation, which parts of a dictionary can be relied on for monitoring and which for steering. A secondary goal is to make reproducibility an explicit, scoped, machine-checked property of an interpretability result.

## What they studied
A from-scratch toy model with a linear bottleneck and a ReLU reconstruction, trained on synthetic sparse inputs, where feature identity is fully known by construction; and two TopK sparse autoencoders (one deliberately good, one deliberately degraded) trained on that model's activations at sixteen-fold overcompleteness. The unit of analysis is the **(feature, atom) pair**: a ground-truth feature and the one dictionary atom that best matches it. One production dictionary is audited at the end — a GPT-2-small residual-stream SAE read through SAELens — but only through probe-defined "feature present / feature absent" prompt sets, and the author is explicit that this is a calibration-weak regime. The paper also reproduces two older superposition results, the phase diagram and the L1-versus-TopK comparison.

## How they did it
The toy model is a linear map into a narrow hidden layer followed by a ReLU reconstruction; the interesting regime is more features than hidden dimensions, with each input feature independently zeroed at a chosen sparsity. Importance weighting is used in two schedules, one of which produces a sharp emergent effect: with decayed weights the optimiser drops the lowest-priority features outright rather than spreading capacity, giving a fixed 22-feature, 8-dimensional substrate for every SAE experiment. Superposition is measured per feature by the standard feature-dimensionality ratio, and the author adds a second check on the column norm because the ratio is scale-invariant and can report full dedication for a column that is effectively absent. The phase diagram is swept over overcompleteness and sparsity with three seeds per cell. The SAE comparison trains L1-penalised and TopK dictionaries at matched sparsity and re-optimises activation magnitudes with the selected support frozen to test for shrinkage directly. The causal battery then runs, for every ground-truth feature, two interventions propagated through the model's actual output stage: **ablation** — present inputs where only that feature is on, zero the matched atom's code entry, and compare the drop in the target's reconstruction against the average movement of all other features, giving an ablation specificity ratio; and **steering** — present inputs where the feature is off, force the atom to a fixed value *with the sign of the match*, and measure the targeted rise. A third and cheapest measurement, the fraction of feature-present samples on which the atom fires at all, is added when it turns out to explain the pathological cases. Ablation and steering are run for everyone, with a required sign argument, after an early bug in which unsigned matching plus positive-only steering returned exactly zero effects on anti-aligned matches. Everything is then re-run under a single-threaded deterministic pipeline with bootstrap intervals, and the whole battery is shipped as a small library with a structural protocol (encode, decode, expose the dictionary) so any SAE-shaped object, including the widely used public interface, can be audited unchanged.

## What counts as an explanation here
A causal profile per (feature, atom) pair, reported as a triple: fired fraction, read specificity, write specificity. The explanation of "this atom represents this feature" is not a label or a direction but a validated claim about what happens when the atom is removed and when it is driven, plus an explicit statement of what the correlational match cannot tell you. The paper also produces a taxonomy: inertness by cause (structural — antipodal geometry; competitive — losing the selection contest) and by direction (read-inert versus write-inert). This is partial by design — one atom per feature, no interaction effects, no circuits — but it is predictive in a narrow sense: the structural class is diagnosable from the match table before any intervention is run.

## How they judged success
Recovery precision (fraction of well-represented ground-truth features whose best atom clears an unsigned cosine bar); per-pair ablation and steering specificity ratios; fired fraction over 500 feature-present samples; correlation between cosine similarity and the log of specificity; seeded bootstrap confidence intervals on every summary median; matched-sparsity comparisons with a frozen-support magnitude-refinement control; and, as a reproducibility measure, SHA-256 equality of result files within one pinned environment plus a tolerance-based equality elsewhere. The phase-diagram section uses a theoretical control — a capacity-sufficient configuration where the answer must be fully dedicated — as a pipeline sanity check.

## What they found
The reproduction of the phase diagram is mostly confirmatory, with two additions: a "superposition" result at low sparsity that turned out to be under-training, resolved by tripling the step budget, and an extreme-overcompleteness cell where every feature reads as nearly unrepresented yet carries a column norm *larger* than an orthogonal feature's — a diffuse regime the standard ratio is blind to. On the dictionary comparison, TopK dominates the Pareto front, and the shrinkage test quantifies why: freezing the support and re-optimising magnitudes closes 91.2% of the L1 dictionary's reconstruction gap by raising magnitudes 22.5% on average, whereas the TopK dictionary's magnitudes move negligibly. The causal results are the core: in a degraded dictionary, 17 of 22 matched pairs had a matched atom that never fired for the feature across 500 isolated presentations — including one above the paper's own recovery bar — and the good dictionary still had 2 of 22, both at cosine above 0.9997. Pooled, cosine and log specificity correlate at 0.657; split by dictionary, 0.859 for the good one and 0.471 for the bad one, so the metric is weakest exactly where an audit matters. Under the deterministic pipeline the counts shift (18 recovered and 3 inert in the degraded dictionary, 2 in the good one) while the qualitative picture holds. The inert cases decompose cleanly: the good dictionary's two are antipodal pairs — one atom serving two features of almost exactly opposite direction, where the encoder's positive selection can never fire for the negative member — and every inert case in both dictionaries traces to five such pairs. Crucially, those same atoms score among the *highest* steering specificities in the whole audit while their ablation effect is exactly zero, so being unmonitorable and being steerable are dissociable facts about one atom. On the production dictionary (83 hand-written concepts, one residual-stream layer), 7 pairs cleared a lowered bar, 1 was inert, and medians sit close to the collateral-damage line with wide intervals; a handful of atoms recur as the nearest match for dozens of semantically unrelated concepts across three independently built concept sets.

## Limits and open problems they name
Every absolute number comes from a synthetic toy model; the production census is one model, one layer, one small concept set, with a difference-in-means probe direction standing in for ground truth and a lowered cosine bar, so its inert rate is explicitly not comparable to the toy rates. The causal battery tests features in isolation on the "on" side, so interactions between simultaneously active features are untested. The read/write decomposition and the structural/competitive split rest on five antipodal pairs within one training run and one seed; the bootstrap quantifies within-sample uncertainty, not across-seed variance. Exact specificity values are described as the shape of an effect measured once. The atom-collision signal in the production dictionary is left with three unresolved explanations (the toy antipodal mechanism, a probe artifact, or genuine hub polysemanticity). The author also notes that four bugs found across this line of work is evidence the discipline works, not that the pipeline is bug-free.

## What we take from it
- **Method (the one to reuse):** a causal read-out for any per-unit reading is three cheap measurements — does it fire, does removing it move the target more than the rest (read specificity), does forcing it move the target (write specificity) — and the first of those costs a single batched forward pass while already classifying most failures. Ranked validation levels, with what each level still cannot tell you, is the reporting format we should adopt for our own per-unit arrays.
- **Framing:** a high correlation score and a causal claim are different empirical claims, and no amount of improving the metric converts one into the other. This is the same discipline the verification-line entries keep arriving at from the other side: the per-unit array that a solver computes is a *ranking*, and a ranking earns predictive status only when the top of it is intervened on.
- **Warning:** the most confident geometric match in a well-trained dictionary was also causally void, and it was void for a *structural* reason (antipodal sharing plus a one-sided selection rule) that better training makes worse, not better. Any of our readings that depend on a sign-asymmetric selection or rectification should be checked for the same degenerate class before it is believed.
- **Measure:** every summary median ships with a seeded bootstrap interval, and the paper shows those intervals are the finding on degraded dictionaries — wide, multimodal, reaching toward zero. Our per-layer censuses should carry intervals of the same kind, since our layer sizes are small enough for resampling.
- **Warning / reproducibility:** exact counts move between environments and even between deterministic re-runs of the same configuration; the paper's answer is to state which rung of reproducibility is guaranteed where and to version-pin the checkpoint. Any rate we publish needs the configuration it was measured on attached.
- Cross-reference: the absorption entry already in this folder is the sibling failure mode (a latent that fails to fire because a more specific latent takes the activation); this paper's structural inertness needs no hierarchy at all, and its read/write dissociation is the part absorption-style firing statistics cannot see.
