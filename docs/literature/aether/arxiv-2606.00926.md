# Refit the Probe: Single-Direction Ablation Is Not a Necessity Test

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.00926
- **Authors / organisation:** Yuhang Jiang; University of Trento, Italy
- **Date:** 2026-05-30 (v1); v2 2026-09-21
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00926
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (abstract, sections 1-10, Tables 1-11, Appendices A-G; no figures read as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The standard way to turn "the probe can read X" into "the model uses X" is to delete the probe's direction during the forward pass and watch task accuracy fall. A large drop is reported as dependence, a near-zero drop as independence. The author asks whether that intervention actually removes what it says it removes, and finds it does not: the quantity is still there, just no longer readable in the one linear format that was fitted. The paper is therefore about whether an interpretability measurement supports the claim attached to it, not about a new model. The setting is deliberately small and controllable - state tracking in four-layer sequence models plus fine-tuned pretrained models - because the point is the instrument, and it has to be checked cell by cell.

## What they studied
Unit of analysis is a **layer's residual stream** (`h_i`, after block i and before any final norm) and a **linear probe direction or row space** inside it. Architectures: Transformer, Mamba, Mamba-2, LSTM and GRU at 4 layers / d=128 / about 830K parameters, plus fine-tuned pretrained Mamba-130M (24 layers), Pythia-160M (12 layers) and Pythia-410M (24 layers, Dyck only), with a frozen-backbone check at 1.4B in an appendix. Tasks: running Parity, S3 permutation composition (6 classes, non-commutative), Dyck-2 nesting depth (11 classes, capped at depth 10), and bracket-depth labels derived from real Python functions in CodeSearchNet. Nothing below the layer is examined - no neuron, head or SAE feature is the object of any claim (one head-attention table appears only as a redundancy observation).

## How they did it
For each layer they fit a multinomial logistic-regression probe on frozen hidden states to predict the true task state, standardising features on the training split and mapping the direction back to raw space by dividing by the per-coordinate standard deviation before using it for anything. The single-direction ablation projects that direction out of the stream at the layer, and the **refit check** then fits a fresh probe on the ablated activations under the same protocol and compares its accuracy with the original. They also delete the probe's entire K-1 row space and the top singular directions of the probe weight matrix, and they compare every drop against random unit directions, against random directions whose projection variance is within 20 percent of the probe direction's, and against random subspaces of the same dimension. The corrected measurement is iterative nullspace projection: strip a direction, refit, repeat until no linear probe beats chance, then delete that whole subspace inside the running model. Patching the clean hidden state at the readout position serves as a sufficiency test on the other side. Six scope tests with pass/fail rules written before the runs, Holm-corrected permutation tests over 500 random baselines, 4 seeds per cell, and a single forward hook so that the probe, the ablation and the patch all act on exactly the same tensor.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **necessity test over a subspace**, not a direction: the claim is "the model cannot solve the task once the set of directions that make the target linearly decodable is removed". It is deliberately partial and says so - it establishes what the model needs, never where the computation lives, and it is defined relative to the linear probe family, so a nonlinearly encoded quantity is outside its reach. The refit probe is not an explanation at all but a **falsification check on the instrument**: one extra probe fit that decides whether any ablation number may be read.

## How they judged success
Accuracy drop relative to the clean-minus-chance gap, reported at each cell's own k* and at a shared budget k=32 or 256; the refit probe's recovered accuracy; random-direction, energy-matched-direction and matched-dimension random-subspace nulls; one-sided permutation p-values with the Phipson-Smyth correction (smallest attainable about 0.002 over 500 baselines) and Holm correction over 8 primary tests; Welch t-tests for the Conv1D ablation; chance-normalised probe accuracy so tasks with 2, 6 and 11 classes are comparable; a control probe on shuffled label trajectories (Hewitt-Liang selectivity); a two-layer MLP probe to check that linearity is not hiding structure; and pre-registered scope tests scored against their own statements.

## What they found
The single-direction ablation never removed the target. A probe refitted at the same layer recovers the original accuracy in every cell they checked - Dyck Transformer at layer 0 stays at 1.000, Parity Mamba at layer 3 moves from 0.987 to 0.988 - and it still recovers after the whole row space is deleted, because the quantity sits in the orthogonal complement. On Parity and S3, single-direction drops at every architecture and length sit between 0.0 and 4.9 percent, while deleting the subspace that makes the target linearly undecodable costs 0.78 to 0.98 relative in nine of ten four-layer cells against random subspaces of the same dimension costing 0.000 to 0.039. At pretrained scale the same reversal on Parity: Pythia-160M loses 0.71 and Mamba-130M 0.89 against matched random subspaces at 0.024 and 0.013. The erasure is partial at width - only 4 of 16 cells reached chance within the 256-direction budget - so those drops are lower bounds. Two instrument artefacts decide whether a single-direction number is readable at all: the raw-space direction can carry less variance than a random one (pretrained Mamba on Parity averages 1.0 times random, never above 2.4, so its 0.02 percent drop was never a causal statement), and "the best probe layer" is an argmax over ties when the probe saturates at several consecutive layers, so the measured drop follows the tie-break rather than the model. Readability and fragility separate across depth, in the same direction in every pretrained model run: Pythia-160M reads Dyck at 98.8 percent by L11 but loses most to ablation at L7 (30.2 points against a 0.4 null, 83 times the null), Mamba-130M reads at L23 (99.0 percent) but loses most at L19 (46.3 points, 141 times the null), and Pythia-410M keeps the shape at 21.2 points at L9 of 24. Mid-position patching at Mamba L22 recovers 97.5 percent of the clean-corrupted logit gap on Parity and 98.4 percent on Dyck, so the state is close to sufficient at a site where no single direction is individually necessary.

## Limits and open problems they name
Erasure removes linear decodability, which is what the probe reads and therefore what the claim covers; a nonlinearly encoded quantity can survive it, and the kernelised erasure literature already shows protection failing to transfer between adversaries. The erasure is partial at pretrained width, so the numbers are lower bounds rather than estimates of a complete removal. Cross-seed spread is wide throughout (0.46 plus or minus 0.40 at four-layer Mamba on Parity, plus or minus 0.35 at pretrained Pythia), so the author reads the effect at the level of the grid, not of a single cell. Erasure does not locate the computation. The task taxonomy and the predictive claim over unseen task families were dropped - 0 of 3 pre-registered predictions held - and the content-versus-size dissociation found at four layers did not replicate at 1.4B once models were probed at matched accuracy.

## What we take from it
Five things. (1) A **method, and a mandatory step for our own tests**: every ablation-based "the model needs this direction" claim carries a refit probe on the ablated state; if a fresh probe recovers the target, the ablation measured a format, not a state, and the claim must be restated as fragility of that coordinate. The cost is one probe fit. (2) A **falsifiable precondition**: a probe direction whose raw-space projection energy is under three times a random direction's is unreadable for single-direction ablation, and we should refuse to quote such a number at all. (3) A **budget rule**: an ablation number quoted at each cell's own k or layer is not comparable to the next cell's, because a saturating probe makes the layer an argmax over ties and a large k makes any random subspace destructive; report against a shared budget and a matched-dimension null, and say which. (4) A **warning against conflating redundancy with robustness**: in this Transformer every head is redundant (no head is individually necessary) while the Layer-0 depth subspace costs 87.8 points, so a localisation claim at head granularity and one at direction granularity can disagree honestly. (5) A **framing to keep as a warning, not a rule**: readability depth and fragility depth are two separate profiles measured on the same tensor, and in this paper's pretrained cells they do not peak together - Pythia-160M reads best at L11 and loses most at L7, Mamba-130M reads best at L23 and loses most at L19. The paper generalises this to "the probe peak sits above the ablation peak in every pretrained model" only for Dyck, and its own table shows the opposite direction on the Parity cells where a peak clears the null (Pythia-160M Parity and S3 both peak at the final layer), so our model should read the two profiles as separate outputs rather than assuming a fixed offset.
