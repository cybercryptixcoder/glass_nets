## What they wanted, and why

Attribution methods explain a prediction by scoring the model on inputs that do not exist: LIME samples a Gaussian cloud around the point, KernelSHAP scores coalitions with half the features masked, Integrated Gradients integrates along a straight line from a zero reference, DeepLIFT differences against a baseline, and every "delete this feature and re-score" metric does the same. In a tabular security domain most of those inputs are not merely unlikely but impossible — a flow with negative duration, two one-hot protocols set at once, TCP-only flags lit on a UDP flow, a packet-length range that is not max minus min. The authors call the resulting sensitivity to behaviour outside the operational domain **domain blindness** and observe it is not cured by white-box access: an explanation can be faithful to the model and still describe the model on inputs the domain forbids. The paper's goal is to make the domain's own assumptions an explicit, auditable object — a declared rule set that every query a procedure issues must lie inside — and then to audit four explainer families under that control rather than comparing them free-form. The larger aim is methodological: query validity as a first-class part of what an explanation or an explanation metric is.

## What they studied

Four tabular network-intrusion benchmarks — NSL-KDD, CIC-IDS-2017, UNSW-NB15, CSE-CIC-IDS2018 — after median imputation, one-hot encoding and min–max scaling, which is exactly the encoding step that creates the rules. Three classifier families: a fully connected DNN (the attacked model, under full parameter and gradient access), a random forest, and XGBoost; Module 4 additionally retrains a 1D-CNN and an SVM downstream. Four explainer families: LIME (weighted local surrogate over a neighbourhood), SHAP (KernelSHAP coalitions plus TreeSHAP/DeepSHAP backends), Integrated Gradients, and DeepLIFT. The unit of analysis is deliberately **not** a neuron, feature or circuit but the *query transcript* of an attribution procedure — the finite set of states it asked the model about, including the states used by the metric that evaluates it. Explanations are per-record attribution vectors over encoded tabular features; there are no activations or weights involved beyond the gradient methods' own access.

## How they did it

Step one is formal. The encoded input space is a product of continuous coordinates and finite allowed patterns per discrete group; a finite set of Boolean **rule predicates** in four families — domain (non-negativity, allowed one-hot patterns), order (max ≥ mean ≥ min, standard deviation not exceeding the range), protocol gate (fields inactive under the chosen protocol set to zero), and derived field (range recomputed as max minus min) — induces the **rule-valid set** R. Any attribution or attribution-evaluation procedure is treated as issuing requests with a finite state footprint and receiving method-specific oracle information (scores, gradients, activations, multipliers); a run's **transcript** records the queried states. A run is *valid-query contained* if every queried state lies in R; the conceptual **leakage probability** is the chance that at least one query falls outside R.

Step two is a theorem. **Valid-query invariance**: if every model-dependent access is recorded in the transcript and the next request, stopping decision and readout depend only on the parameters and the observed prefix, then two classifiers that agree on the oracle information requested *within R* produce identical transcripts and identical attributions — provided the run is contained. The converse is a witness: two oracle maps that differ only at one off-rule-valid request, where that reply changes the output, agree on every rule-valid request yet yield different explanations. A corollary extends the same condition to finite-query evaluation metrics, so a "delete-and-re-score" faithfulness score is non-identifiable from rule-valid behaviour whenever its replacement states leave R.

Step three is the intervention, one query-control principle instantiated four times (**PA-XAI**): **PA-LIME** draws the protocol from the training distribution, draws each discrete coordinate/group from its empirical marginal, perturbs continuous coordinates with the same Gaussian rule as the comparator, applies one pass of an **ordered repair map** (domain handling → restore ordered statistics → clamp standard deviation to range → protocol-gated zeroing → recompute derived fields) and fits the identical weighted surrogate; **PA-SHAP** replaces the background with benign training records and repairs KernelSHAP coalition completions before scoring, while TreeSHAP/DeepSHAP receive the benign background and control only the reference distribution; **PA-IG** and **PA-DeepLIFT** share a threshold-near training reference and integrate continuous coordinates with discrete groups fixed, adding ordered endpoint differences so no fractional one-hot is ever interpolated.

Step four is an audit in four parallel modules against paired standard/PA configurations: (1) explanation quality — Pearson faithfulness after top-ranked mean replacement, normalized-entropy complexity, sparsity, repeated-run top-k Jaccard, per-sample latency; (2) FGSM and PGD on the DNN, unrestricted versus attack-then-repair, scored by evasion success, cosine similarity between original and adversarial attribution vectors (with a normalized trapezoidal area over five budgets), class-equivalent top-k Jaccard, a Lipschitz ratio, and recovery of the features the attack actually changed; (3) cross-configuration agreement — Spearman/Kendall and top-5/top-10 overlap over 66 configuration pairs per dataset, Wilcoxon equivalence tests, SHAP interaction statistics, and RRA@15 (recovery of predefined 6–8-feature attack-domain sets among the top 15 attributions) on CSE-CIC-IDS2018; (4) attribution-guided feature reduction — a global mean-|attribution| ranking, pruning by a 50-tree depth-10 RF proxy on a subsample until macro-F1 drops by more than 0.01, then retraining four classifier families and comparing coordinates against Chi-Squared, Information Gain, PCA and Spearman selection.

## What counts as an explanation here

An **attribution vector over encoded tabular features**, together with the transcript of model states that produced it — the paper's claim is that the vector alone is under-specified and the transcript plus the declared rule set is the real explanation object. The formal content is an identifiability statement, not a description: within R the explanation depends only on the model's behaviour on domain-valid states, so it is invariant to everything the model does on invalid inputs, and the theorem is exact rather than approximate. It is explicitly **partial**: rule membership is the weakest of three validity predicates they define (rule-valid ⊂ conceptually superseded by *support-consistent* — accepted by an empirical or conditional-plausibility score with a threshold — and *reconstructable* — produced by some raw packet/flow record through the real feature-extraction map), and the experiments implement only the first. The reduction module adds a second, predictive object: a feature subset whose retrained model is claimed to retain the signal.

## How they judged success

Six coordinate types kept deliberately non-compensatory (a "diagnostic audit profile", formalized as one vector): model alignment (faithfulness), repeated-run stability (top-k Jaccard), attack-followed-by-repair attribution stability (cosine area), cross-method agreement (Spearman/Kendall/overlap), feature-reduction utility (compression versus median weighted-F1 change, with Pareto nondominance over the 16 configurations), and cost (milliseconds per explanation). Controls and baselines: every displayed standard configuration has a PA counterpart run on the same model, dataset, explainer settings and evaluation metric; deterministic methods (SHAP, IG, DeepLIFT) are shown to be exactly repeatable under both settings as a check that the LIME stability effect is about neighbourhood construction and not measurement noise; conventional selectors (Chi-Squared, InfoGain, PCA, Spearman) are compression baselines; a fixed-model comparison (DNN-LIME versus DNN-SHAP) isolates explainer disagreement from model disagreement; Wilcoxon signed-rank at nominal α tests attribution equivalence per pair. Notably, six of the reported statistics are *descriptive* and the paper says so — the interaction statistic is used with quotients and stated to have unspecified aggregation and normalization.

## What they found

**Repeatability.** Across 12 LIME model–dataset comparisons, standard LIME reports perfect top-k repeated-run overlap in six settings (all three classifier families on UNSW-NB15 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018); the paired PA-LIME values are 0.52, 0.69, 0.43, 0.48, 0.86 and 0.60 for those cases, mean 0.575, a mean absolute difference of 0.425 from the standard value of 1.000. So "perfectly stable" was a fact about the sampler staying in one small Gaussian cloud of (mostly invalid) points, and domain-valid sampling exposes variance the original protocol hid.

**Faithfulness conclusions flip.** The mean-replacement correlation changes sign in five of 12 LIME comparisons, including all four DNN cases. Four-dataset means (Table 7): DNN-LIME 0.28 → −0.07; DNN-IG 0.57 → 0.43; DNN-DeepLIFT 0.51 → 0.54; DNN-SHAP 0.63 → 0.62; RF-LIME 0.38 → 0.49; RF-SHAP 0.79 → 0.80; XGB-LIME 0.23 → 0.18; XGB-SHAP 0.58 → 0.71. Because a negative coefficient means the attribution is anti-correlated with the ablation effect, the DNN explainer ranking itself reorders.

**Under attack.** On the DNN, FGSM evasion success at the largest budget drops from 76.1% unrestricted to 75.3% after repair, and the normalized curve area from 64.09 to 57.86; PGD drops from 74.6% to 58.8% and from 52.86 to 44.99. Attribution stability under repaired FGSM ranks PA-DeepLIFT first (cosine nAUC 0.452) and PA-IG second (0.413); PA-LIME (0.132) and PA-SHAP (0.123) trail their standard counterparts (LIME 0.153, SHAP 0.221). Perturbed-feature recovery relative to LIME: Top-1 0.064 → 0.152, Top-3 0.200 → 0.384, mean attribution rank 73.0 → 41.2; SHAP recovers nothing (0.000/0.000, rank 123.0).

**Agreement.** Within-PA Spearman means exceed within-standard on every dataset — 0.306–0.414 against 0.163–0.244 — and the four-dataset averages are 0.366 against 0.217, the rise quoted in the abstract. All-pair means stay low (0.168–0.322), so configurations still disagree substantially with each other even after the control.

**Utility.** Over the 16 aggregate compression–fidelity coordinates only two are nondominated, both PA: PA-SHAP-DNN (85.3% of dimensions removed, −0.68 pp median weighted-F1) and PA-IG-DNN (90.5%, −0.89 pp), against a conventional cluster all at 80.6% with changes from −0.69 to −0.87 pp. Standard SHAP-DNN at 86.5% and standard IG-DNN at 88.5% both cost about −1.9 to −2.25 pp, i.e. the protocol-aware versions buy back roughly 1.2–1.4 pp at higher compression.

**Cost.** The control is not free and the paper prints the price: DeepLIFT goes from 0.01 ms to 6.7 ms and IG from 0.70 to 54.6 ms per explanation; LIME goes from 92.2 to 1,690.6 ms and XGB-SHAP from 0.10 to 1,990.7 ms.

## Limits and open problems they name

Rule validity is the weakest of their three validity predicates, and support consistency and raw-flow reconstruction are explicitly left as untested validation layers; the leakage probability is defined but not empirically estimated, because that requires logging complete query transcripts, which the study did not do. The ordered repair map is a deterministic rule routine, not a nearest-point projection or an optimised valid-set search, and adaptive valid-set attacks, explanation-targeting objectives, repair-aware norm control are all listed as untested. PA-LIME changes sampling *and* repair together, so the two effects are not separated by an ablation; the square-root improvement in agreement "needs controlled ablations" to attribute. The DNN ranking comparison is averaged over four datasets and the prose numerals for it are blank in this HTML build, so the direction is quoted but not the magnitudes. Exact replication is blocked on unreported splits, seeds, the per-dataset predicate lists (the catalog gives rule *families* rather than a reproducible predicate map per dataset), explainer settings, timing hardware and run-level records for uncertainty estimates. The compression result is a displayed-set nondominance claim, not a per-dataset frontier, and analyst trust and decision quality are explicitly deferred to human studies.

## What we take from it

**A framing.** "Explanation" as attribution-plus-transcript is the shape our own white-box model should take: for any probe score, feature, or direction we report, the queryable object is the set of states the procedure asked about, and a claim that survives only through off-support queries is a claim about our own sampler. This is the attribution-method version of the folder's standing denominators lesson, and it arrives with a cheap operational form — log the query set, intersect it with a declared rule set, report the leakage fraction.

**A method.** The ordered repair map is a usable primitive for any intervention study in structured domains: plausibility projections that restore cross-field arithmetic and gating *after* an edit, applied once, deterministically, before the model is scored. For our probing work the analogue is obvious and currently missing — when we ablate a feature on tabular or structured inputs we mask weakly (mean replacement) and never check the result is a legal input, which is precisely the metric this paper shows to be non-identifiable.

**A measure.** Repeated-run top-k Jaccard is not a stability measure on its own; it is a joint measure of the sampler and the model, and a perfect 1.000 is as likely to indicate a sampler trapped in a small invalid cloud as a deterministic method. Print it beside a legality rate for the sampled states.

**A warning.** The most useful negative here is the attack-then-repair numbers: repairing states *raises* evasion in a few cells (FGSM at the largest budget only drops 76.1 → 75.3, and the intermediate budgets are non-monotone), which says projection onto a valid set can move an adversarial example toward a decision boundary rather than away — so "make the input legal" is a safety step and not a defence, and the two should be evaluated separately.

**A shortcut.** The compression arm is the cheapest part of the protocol to reuse: rank by mean-|attribution|, prune with a proxy until macro-F1 drops a fixed margin, retrain, and report (compression, fidelity change) as a point so that nondominance is checkable rather than asserted. It costs one proxy model and four retrains and it prices an attribution ranking by a downstream property rather than by agreement with another ranking.
