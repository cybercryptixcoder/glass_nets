# Decoder-Preserving Sparse Autoencoders: Which Readouts Survive Sparse Compression?

- **Key:** arxiv-2607.17425
- **Authors / organisation:** Aniket Deshpande; Department of Physics, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign
- **Date:** 2026-07-19 (v1; online 2026-09-13)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17425
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (abstract, SS1-SS7, references, Appendices A.1-A.10, Appendix B proofs)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Sparse autoencoders are judged on two numbers - reconstruction error and sparsity - and the paper's premise is that those two numbers do not determine which linearly decodable variables survive the compression. Two reconstructions can match exactly on mean squared error and on average code length while putting their error in different places, so a probe for one target reads well through one and a probe for another target reads through the other. If that is true, an SAE evaluation that reports only reconstruction and sparsity is silent about the thing interpretability actually uses the SAE for. The authors want a fidelity notion that is indexed by decoding task, a training objective that optimises it, and an honest account of what such an objective does and does not buy. The larger goal is to separate three properties that the field's phrase "representation quality" fuses: a refitted readout surviving compression, the frozen network's own behaviour being preserved, and labelled concepts concentrating into few sparse coordinates.

## What they studied

The unit of analysis is a representation matrix (one activation per row) and the family of optimal ridge readouts inside it - the K x K matrix that maps any target vector y to the predictions of the optimal ridge regression on that representation. Nothing here is per-neuron, per-head or per-circuit; the object is the prediction operator of a linear readout family and its distortion under compression. Language-model work is on GPT-2 small residual-stream activations after block 8, 768-dimensional, with a 16,384-latent untied BatchTopK SAE at a budget of k = 32 active features per token, plus one boundary pilot on Pythia-160M deduped block 8 at the same width. Controlled work uses synthetic overcomplete nonorthogonal sparse generators with tied signed-TopK dictionaries, where the task distribution is known and can be declared.

## How they did it

The distortion is built first as a matrix: the difference between the original representation's ridge prediction operator and the reconstruction's, squared and made symmetric, giving a taskwise distortion operator whose quadratic form is the squared prediction error on a particular target y. A task distribution with second moment Sigma scalarises this to a single trace, and the ratio of that trace to the source prediction energy is the relative distortion the paper reports. Three theory steps make the ambiguity precise. An explicit two-dimensional construction gives two reconstructions with identical squared Frobenius error, identical code L0 and identical isotropic average distortion whose distortion operators are incomparable in the Loewner order, each better on one of two unit targets. Under a pure rank-r bottleneck the isotropic problem reduces to truncated SVD with an omitted-mode cost that saturates at one once a direction is strongly decodable, so isotropy cannot reorder singular directions. With a structured task prior that commutes with the representation's Gram matrix, retained modes are the r largest scores of prior weight times saturated decodability, and a protected low-amplitude direction overtakes a nuisance one past an explicit crossover inequality. Training then adds this distortion, estimated on finite banks of random directions, to normalised reconstruction error and the sparsity loss. The decoder weight is selected on a disjoint 25M-token sweep by a predeclared rule - the smallest weight reaching a 10 percent distortion reduction while keeping NMSE within 1 percent of paired MSE - frozen at one value, and then three paired 100M-token runs are trained with shared initialisation, token order, probes and optimiser. Evaluation is exact on held-out activations: 16,384 tokens in groups of 128, identity targets, calibrated ridge, 10,000 paired group-bootstrap resamples. Boundary evaluations insert each reconstruction back into the frozen GPT-2 at block 8, and a concept pilot fits sparse probes to one matched Pythia pair.

## What counts as an explanation here

A trained alternative compressor plus a diagnostic matrix. The explanation is not a feature dictionary or a description of any feature; it is a statement about which linear readouts a compression preserves, expressed as a task-indexed positive-semidefinite operator, and a scalar when a task prior is declared. It is partial by construction: the paper's own scope paragraph says every formal claim concerns optimal regularised readouts refitted separately to the original and the reconstruction, and explicitly not the frozen downstream network, not labelled-concept recovery, and not any claim about mutual information or Fisher information. The diagnostic operator Q, the difference of two reconstructions' distortion operators, is the one predictive device: its trace is the average isotropic advantage, its eigenvalues separate average improvement from uniform taskwise dominance, and an indefinite Q means rank-one task priors exist that prefer either reconstruction.

## How they judged success

Primary metric: exact held-out decoder distortion, a ratio of sums over geometry groups, with 10,000 paired group-bootstrap resamples and intervals described as conditional on the trained checkpoints. Matched reconstruction quality is a hard constraint, not a preference: NMSE must sit within 1 percent of the paired MSE arm, and inference L0 must stay near the training budget. The controlled generator is scored on held-out coefficient combinations from the declared protected subspace, with a one-sided paired Wilcoxon test on NMSE and comparisons against four paired controls - isotropic DPSAE, a frozen-task weighted-MSE loss, a row-permuted task-prior control, and plain MSE - sharing initialisation, minibatches, architecture, sparsity, optimiser and stopping rule. Boundary checks are separately specified and pre-registered: a frozen-network output-KL noninferiority test against a declared 1.01 margin on 2,048 fresh sequences, and a sparse concept probe at k of 1, 2 and 5 features over 113 frozen tasks in 60 concept families with ten probe seeds and a family-block bootstrap. A finite-probe gradient audit compares sampled gradient estimates against identity-target reference gradients.

## What they found

The ambiguity is real and provable, and the isotropic objective cannot exploit it in the rank relaxation - it keeps PCA's ordering and only changes the cost of each omitted mode. In the sparse regime it can: on the controlled generator the isotropic variant lowers distortion in all ten seeds by a median of 15.5 percent (range 8.9 to 21.7) but pays a median 12.4 percent NMSE increase, so it buys readout fidelity with reconstruction. With a declared structured prior and no isotropic penalty the protected-subspace result is cleaner: median distortion on unseen combinations falls from 0.1755 to 0.1332, a 25.3 percent reduction, in all ten seeds, with median NMSE moving from 0.0693 to 0.0667 and a one-sided Wilcoxon p of 0.097 that does not establish a reconstruction improvement; the advantage over isotropic DPSAE, the frozen-task loss and the row-permuted control is 18.8, 16.4 and 14.1 percent, and it holds across a 16-fold sweep of the prior weight at 23.9 to 26.6 percent. On GPT-2 the three paired runs reduce exact held-out decoder distortion by 10.61, 11.38 and 10.84 percent with intervals excluding zero, at NMSE ratios of 0.9975, 0.9967 and 0.9988 - and the intervals are conditional on three initialisations of one layer of one model. The gain is broad and not uniform: in every held-out group the advantage operator is indefinite, and among 4,096 shared random directions 67.0 to 69.4 percent favour the decoder-preserving arm, 8.7 to 10.0 percent favour plain MSE, and 21.8 to 22.9 percent are unresolved at a 5 percent materiality scale. The boundary tests all come back negative or unresolved. All three frozen-network pairs pass the natural-text output-KL noninferiority test with ratios of 0.9905, 0.9744 and 0.9777 and upper endpoints below the declared 1.01 margin, but on 2,048 IOI prompts the same reconstructions lose 0.88 to 2.39 percentage points of accuracy and have larger name-logit error in every pair. One matched Pythia pair shows a family-macro DPSAE-minus-MSE sparse-probe AUROC of -0.00387 over 60 families with an interval excluding zero, with all ten probe-seed aggregates negative. The static-baseline comparison never reached a matched reconstruction regime - five spectral coefficients land outside the predeclared NMSE band - and the JumpReLU extension never met its sparsity or dead-feature screens, so both comparisons are recorded as unidentified rather than as failures.

## Limits and open problems they name

One layer of one small model with three paired initialisations, so intervals quantify evaluation uncertainty around those checkpoints and not population generality; there is no replication across layers, model families, widths or sparse architectures beyond single-seed screens (GPT-2 block 4 clearing the threshold, Pythia block 8 reaching 9.13 percent) that they explicitly refuse to treat as generality. The structured-prior result is on a synthetic task family whose targets stay inside the subspace that defined the prior - held-out combinations, but held out from within the same declared span. The taskwise audit uses finite-sample random directions that are not semantic tasks and recur only weakly across seeds, with a shared direction favouring the arm in 32.73 percent of cases against 31.78 under conditional independence. The static covariance controls never matched NMSE, so whether a static metric could reach the same reduction is still open. The Pythia concept result is one checkpoint pair. The rank relaxation's structured-prior crossover does not appear in the sparse generator, so the theory's ordering is a scale reference there and not a prediction. Future work they name: replicate across layers and families, build priors from real prediction families, compare adaptive preservation against static baselines on genuinely matched frontiers, extend to nonlinear and frozen readouts, connect readout preservation to causal interventions and feature semantics, and measure an operational rate that includes supports, amplitudes and dictionary cost.

## What we take from it

(1) **A compression is a lossy map and the loss has to be named per readout, not in aggregate - so every SAE our white-box model relies on is scored by which of our probes still read through it, not by reconstruction error alone.** Their closest analogue to a save/restore probe here is a 10 to 11 percent improvement on refitted ridge distortion at matched NMSE, and the paper is scrupulous that this number answers only the refitted-readout question. (2) **Three properties, three separate tests, and the paper's own results show them coming apart in the same trained pair: a frozen output-KL noninferiority pass coexisting with an IOI accuracy loss, and a readout gain coexisting with a sparse-probe loss on one matched Pythia pair.** That is the most reusable fact in the folder's recent memory for our model-evaluation section: a pass on one fidelity measure licenses nothing about another, and the papers that report a single "the compressed model is faithful" number are reporting one of three. (3) **The advantage operator is the diagnostic to copy: comparing two candidate compressions or two candidate readouts as a matrix rather than as a scalar tells you immediately whether the improvement dominates or trades.** An indefinite difference operator with 9 percent of directions going the other way is not "a 10 percent improvement"; it is a redistribution, and their numbers show the field's usual scalar summary would hide it. The declared-prior result is the constructive half - if we can name the readouts our white-box model must preserve, a structured prior directs the compression at exactly those, and the negative result is the warning that this only holds for targets inside the span we declared.
