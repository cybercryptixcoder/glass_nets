# The Intervention Gap in Latent World Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.29998
- **Authors / organisation:** Donna Vakalis; Mila – Quebec AI Institute / University of Montreal
- **Date:** 2026-08-30
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29998
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Latent world models are trained to predict their own next state, and that training loss is routinely taken as evidence that the model understands what actions do. The author argues these are different properties and asks whether a model's open-loop transitions move the task variables the way matched interventions *in the environment* do — a property called planning-time intervention fidelity. The motivation is that planners and imagined-rollout learners consume exactly the model's own imagined endpoints, so a model can be accurate in one-step teacher-forced prediction and still be wrong in the quantity the planner uses. The larger goal is an audit instrument that can say *which* of three things failed — the target is not readable from the state, the real effect is too small to resolve, or the model's own transition distorts it — rather than collapsing all three into one rollout error.

## What they studied
Two units are in play. The model unit: released multi-task TD-MPC2 checkpoints across five sizes (dependent released sizes from one recipe), self-supervised LeWorldModel video-prediction models trained on cheetah-run with no task signal, action-conditioned PreJEPA predictors over a shared frozen DINOv2 backbone (five independent seeds), and DreamerV3 for a state-surface study. The analysis unit is *not* a neuron, head or direction but a **query family × physical horizon × intervention support × native model surface**: a declared scalar task query (Cheetah endpoint torso speed; Finger Spin endpoint negative hinge velocity), a five environment-step horizon, an action support (task-policy versus environment-random roots and contrasts), and the specific state surface the planner is exposed to (encoded latent for rollout models, categorical posterior for Dreamer). Environments are DeepMind Control Suite tasks.

## How they did it
Two instruments, coarse and fine. The coarse one fits ridge probes with grouped cross-validation to decode each task query from the model's latent, advances the latent through the model's own native prediction mode to the planning horizon, applies the probe, and compares against the same query evaluated on the real environment under identical actions — one operator-error score per checkpoint, with probe quality reported separately so a bad readout is not billed to the transition. The fine instrument is a **capture-gated matched-intervention audit** with three ordered gates. Gate 1 (capture): a prespecified readout trained on real encoded states is checked on held-out real roots. Gate 2 (real-effect resolvability): the same frozen readout must resolve the true effect of the matched action contrasts at *real observed endpoints*. Only if both pass is Gate 3 (propagation) interpreted: the same root and action sequence are run through the environment and through the model's open-loop transition, and the imagined effect is scored against zero effect, persistence, and an environment-endpoint oracle (the real endpoint read through the same frozen probe). Targets are built without access to candidate models; calibration, probe training and evaluation use disjoint environment-family identities; the complete environment family — not individual roots or samples — is the uncertainty unit, with 4,096 whole-family bootstrap resamples. Controls include label and feature shuffles, horizon-zero identity, wrong-action and action-null contrasts, probe-capacity sensitivity, a one-hidden-layer MLP probe in place of ridge, and direct predicted-versus-real feature alignment. Ten prespecified synthetic acceptance cases (capture failure vs propagation failure, rejecting persistence as success, orthogonal-basis invariance, halting on split leakage) must all pass before learned models are scored. A bidirectional held-out test freezes a support-augmented uncertainty score in one model family and transfers it without refitting to another, then reciprocally.

## What counts as an explanation here
An **audit protocol plus a decomposition**, not a mechanistic explanation. The paper's positive claim is a finite-horizon error inequality: rollout query error is bounded by a propagated root-capture term, a sum of per-step transition and closure-approximation residuals weighted by environment stability factors, and a target-approximation term — which licenses the gate order (a propagation score is interpretable only where the capture term is small). Beyond that, the failure characterisations are *descriptive geometry* (rotation with excess gain; attenuation toward zero) explicitly said to constrain candidate mechanisms without establishing one. No circuit, feature dictionary, probe-for-a-concept or surrogate model is claimed; probes are instruments, not explanations.

## How they judged success
Predicted-effect R² of the model's imagined action effect against the real matched effect, with 95% whole-family bootstrap intervals; a *severe* certificate defined as both predicted-effect R² and its upper interval endpoint below zero (worse than predicting no effect); capture R² at real roots; real-effect R² at real endpoints; an oracle deficit defined as the paired predicted-minus-oracle RMSE difference, positive meaning worse than the environment endpoint; rank correlation (Spearman) between scores and error for the uncertainty comparisons; and split-half-style reliability via disjoint 95% intervals across independently seeded runs. Every candidate is reported separately and raw scores are declared non-comparable across tasks and across the two instruments.

## What they found
On the coarse instrument, across the five released TD-MPC2 sizes on cheetah-run, episode return falls as operator error grows (rank correlation −0.90, anchor-bootstrap interval [−0.90, −0.70], leave-one-out at least −0.80; partial −0.89 controlling for reward-prediction accuracy), while reward-prediction error stays inside [0.03, 0.09] and nearly flat — the largest checkpoint has near-best reward fit, the worst operator error, and collapsed return. The full-observable operator score ranks checkpoints only weakly like a Bellman-residual metric (+0.30, [0.30, 0.60]), but a value-restricted version reproduces that ranking exactly (+1.00), which is the paper's evidence that the extra signal lives in task-observable directions the value channel does not constrain. On the anchoring question three task-free LeWorldModel seeds reach operator error 0.384 ± 0.009 against 0.840 (single-task) and 0.833 (multi-task) for task-anchored TD-MPC2, a ratio of 0.457 ± 0.011 with disjoint intervals; restricting to the seven observables both models probe well *widens* the gap (0.395 ± 0.012) and an MLP probe keeps it (0.495 ± 0.006). On the fine instrument, three LeWorldModel checkpoints pass capture (R² 0.579–0.609) and real-effect resolution (0.127–0.198) yet their imagined five-step effects score −6.33 to −3.36 predicted R², all intervals below zero and all worse than the oracle — and the descriptive controls show why a generic feature-space error would look fine: the complete predicted feature change stays aligned with the real one (contrast cosine 0.85–0.89) while its projection on the task readout is near-orthogonal and 1.7–2.6 times too large. Five PreJEPA seeds replicate the *oracle deficit* (5/5 with strictly positive predicted-minus-oracle intervals) but not the severe below-zero conjunction (0/5; predicted R² from −0.005 to 0.064) — they attenuate rather than rotate. Three Finger Spin LeWorldModel runs show the same split (all pass capture and resolution, all have positive oracle deficits, one of three severe). In DreamerV3, centered posterior **logits** are the only tested surface that carries the current query at every held-out checkpoint; the sampled categorical state, its mode, the deterministic recurrent state and their concatenation pass at none, and sample budgets up to 1,024 do not close the gap. Ensemble disagreement ranks error under task-policy support (Spearman 0.678, [0.635, 0.719]) but not under environment-random support (0.027, [−0.029, 0.080]), and a frozen equal-weight disagreement-plus-support score that helps within PreJEPA (+0.0241, [0.0090, 0.0397]) is reliably *worse* than plain disagreement when transferred in either direction (0.756 vs 0.776 native to TD-MPC2; 0.388 vs 0.417 reciprocally, with every individual held-out run's interval strictly negative).

## Limits and open problems they name
Two tasks, one primary scalar query each, one five-step horizon; a 32-system eligibility survey across 13 candidate families produced only the five-run PreJEPA roster, so no cross-family inference, size law or architecture ranking is supported, and TD-MPC2 sizes are dependent released checkpoints rather than independent runs. Capture is relative to a fixed readout family and does not establish absence of information under every nonlinear decoder, and a passing probe does not show the planner uses the decoded information (they cite amnesic probing). The environment-side closure target fails its own qualification at the original construction (rank zero, action slots exchangeable identifiers rather than shared semantic interventions) and only qualifies on matched action shells, so its decision value is untested. The gate order fails in practice on the shared shell audit: TD-MPC2 primary cells terminate at capture or real-effect resolution, so no propagation cell there is interpretable. The stated open questions are the mechanism behind rotation-versus-attenuation, and whether these rankings carry decision value for checkpoint selection and realised return.

## What we take from it
1. **A method: the three-gate audit, which is the missing control for our own "the representation contains X" claims.** Gate 1 asks whether a frozen readout can read the target from the model's real states on held-out items; Gate 2 asks whether the real, matched intervention produces an effect that same frozen readout resolves at real endpoints; only then is the model's own predicted effect scored. For us the analogue is direct: our white-box model's predicted effect of an intervention is only comparable to the network's measured effect if the *network's* representation actually resolves that effect at matched inputs — otherwise we are scoring our predictor against a floor. Report capture, resolution and propagation as three separate numbers per claim.
2. **A measure: an oracle-deficit floor.** Their comparison set is not only "zero effect" and "persistence" but the *real endpoint read through the same frozen readout*. That floor absorbs readout error and is the one baseline that makes "our prediction is worse than doing nothing" interpretable. Our entries' baselines are usually random or shuffled controls; the matched-endpoint oracle is cheap and stronger.
3. **A warning with a sharp geometry: aggregate alignment hides directional failure.** Contrast cosine 0.85–0.89 on the full feature change coexisting with a near-orthogonal, 1.7–2.6× over-amplified task-relevant projection is the folder's cleanest instance of "overall feature similarity is fine, the task component is rotated." Any similarity or alignment number our model reports for the effect of an intervention should be accompanied by the same number computed after projecting onto the task-relevant readout.
4. **A framing to borrow: a state surface is a choice, and the query may live in the distribution rather than the sample.** Dreamer's sampled latent, its mode and the recurrent state all fail capture while centered posterior logits pass. If our white-box model reads a single sample or a mean vector where the underlying object is a distribution, the readout can fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the model's knowledge.
5. **A dataset/benchmark gap, stated plainly:** all of this is control-suite scalar queries. The audit is domain-agnostic in form but has only been exercised on world models, so transplanting it to text/language-model interventions is our work, not theirs.
