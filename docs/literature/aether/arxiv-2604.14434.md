## What they wanted, and why

A sparse mixture-of-experts network routes each token to a handful of small experts, which invites exactly the reading a white-box model needs: that each expert is a unit with a nameable job. The paper's companion work had already undercut the cheap version of that hope from one direction — five structurally different routing topologies reach statistically equivalent language-modelling quality, so the path through the expert pool does not matter — and this paper asks the inverse question: if the topology is interchangeable, is the *identity* of an individual expert still load-bearing for behaviour? The authors want expert-level specialisation promoted from a correlation to a causal claim, and they want the reading to be available from the architecture's own geometry rather than from post-hoc probes. It is also openly an argument about where interpretability effort should go: rank-1 experts, trained end-to-end, are offered as a substitute for post-hoc sparse dictionary learning whose reconstruction gap the authors regard as a defect.

## What they studied

Their own small MoE, not a frontier model: 76-84M parameters, 8 layers, 1024 experts per layer (8,192 experts in total), rank-1 experts, 64-dimensional routing space, top-4 selection by cosine similarity to learned centroids, 3 re-routing hops, trained 50K steps on WikiText-103. Two converged variants are analysed (a "Wide" 1x12 layout at perplexity 33.93 and a "Deep" 3x4 layout at 34.62), plus a linear-router checkpoint at 32.76 for the routing-mechanism comparison, and a 36M-scale underfitted checkpoint (perplexity roughly 300-320) used only for the hop-by-hop logit-lens analysis. The unit of analysis is the **individual rank-1 expert**, treated as the analogue of one sparse-autoencoder decoder column: it reads along one direction and writes along another through a SiLU gate, so the paper's central structural claim is that experts are monosemantic *by construction* rather than by empirical luck. Downstream of that, the paper also analyses routing clusters, layers, and pairs of experts composed across layers.

## How they did it

Two halves, in a fixed order: read first, intervene second. (1) Reading: each expert's output matrix is multiplied by the unembedding matrix and the top vocabulary entries are inspected, giving what the authors call a Semantic Dictionary — one decoded word list per expert, for all 8,192 of them, manually sorted into archetypes (temporal sequence, cardinal numbers, discourse connectives, European capitals, suffixes, a "garbage collector" for very frequent tokens and tokenizer artifacts) and then re-analysed by density clustering on the decoded centroids to find categories the manual pass missed. (2) Routing hygiene checks: they test whether the routing space separates tokens by *syntax* or merely by *frequency*, using a three-way split of function words, frequent content words and rare content words, silhouette scores under a syntax grouping versus a frequency grouping, a 200-permutation null and 500-resample bootstrap intervals, plus 15 hand-matched pairs of function and content words of near-identical corpus frequency as a gold-standard control; and they demonstrate context sensitivity by feeding the same token in two senses and measuring how much of the expert trajectory diverges. (3) Interventions, four kinds on the same routing-identified targets: a knockout that zeroes the top-weighted expert's two matrices at one layer and measures the KL divergence; centroid-addition steering that nudges the routing position toward a chosen expert's centroid (with a strength parameter) and measures the change in the target category's next-token probability mass; suppression that sets a set of experts' similarities to negative infinity, always compared against random suppression of the same set size; and surgery that overwrites an expert's output vector outright. The comparison baselines and controls are the random-suppression null, the control-prompt probe ("zero change on control prompts"), and a repeat of the whole steering test on the linear-router checkpoint where the mechanism is different but the effect can be matched. A fifth experiment composes two steered experts at once, once within a layer and once across layers.

## What counts as an explanation here

A named, inspectable unit with a causal licence attached: a single expert, labelled by the vocabulary its output vector decodes to, whose ablation or manipulation produces a specific, category-selective change in output probability. The form is a **dictionary over architecture-given units** (rather than over learned post-hoc features), validated by intervention — closer to a steering vector with a name than to a circuit. It is explicitly partial: the paper reports that roughly 15% of experts are cleanly monosemantic, 8% are "functional transformers" that read one concept and write another, 12% absorb frequent tokens, and the remaining 65% are moderately polysemantic, and it claims a causal label only for the 10 categories it actually manipulated. The predictive content is local and directional: given a prompt and the routing, the reading says which expert to push to raise or lower a category's probability.

## How they judged success

Four measures, each with its control: (a) **selectivity of suppression** — change in target-category probability and KL divergence for a correctly identified expert versus a size-matched random suppression; (b) **dose-dependence and sign consistency of steering** — per-prompt percentage change in target probability across a strength parameter, reported as a median with interquartile range together with the fraction of prompts moving in the predicted direction, over prompt sets spanning category-relevant, neutral and adversarial contexts; (c) **generalisation breadth** — the number of distinct categories on which the effect holds, including categories discovered automatically rather than hand-picked; (d) **composition** — whether two simultaneous steerings add or interfere, run once within a layer and once across layers. Routing hygiene is judged with silhouette scores against permutation nulls and bootstrap interval exclusion, and with an explicit frequency-matched control. There is no held-out behavioural benchmark and no perplexity cost reported for the interventions.

## What they found

Reading works at this scale and the labels are not just descriptive. Repeated hop-by-hop amplification of the target token's probability is real but collinear with earlier hops rather than composed. Steering toward a temporal expert's centroid raises the temporal next-token probability with a median of +321% across 44 prompts (interquartile range +124% to +549%, positive on 98% of them, one negative case at -5.5%); geographic steering is positive on 90% of 39 prompts with a median of +56%. Suppression is the sharpest demonstration and also the most honest table: removing the temporal expert costs 33.7% of target probability at KL 0.139 while same-size random suppression moves nothing at all, and geographic costs 23.2% at KL 0.512 — but the cardinal and discourse experts, when suppressed, produce essentially zero change, which the authors attribute to those experts rarely being in the top-4 for the test prompts, and which reads equally well as those two labels failing their causal test. Single-expert knockout reaches KL up to 0.468 in a medical context and drops the probability of a specific continuation from 15.6% to 3.9%. Suppressing one expert accounts for the whole cluster effect: 100 neighbours give -33.4% against -33.7% for the one expert alone. Surgery on the geographic expert's output vector halves its category probability (-45.8%) with no change on control prompts. Composition is asymmetric in a way the authors explain mechanically: two experts in the same layer interfere (solo +567% and +44% collapse to +41% and +35% composed) while experts in different layers compose nearly additively (+567% and +19% give +513% and +18%), because centroid addition perturbs every similarity in the target layer, not because the experts' representations overlap. Ten categories steer positively, spanning +3% (social) to +453% (a separately reported maximum) — the two weakest being military and legal, which the paper flags as evidence that safety-relevant content is more distributed. The linear router supports comparable steering (geographic +293%), so the cosine-geometry advantage is discoverability, not controllability.

## Limits and open problems they name

Everything is one 76-84M model, one corpus, rank-1 experts, one training run; the authors say plainly that they do not claim generalisation to billion-parameter scale, full-rank experts or production MoEs, and that larger expert pools with rank above 1 could show richer polysemy and different composition dynamics. The clustering leaves 73.5% of experts unclustered and only 7 of 16 novel clusters were steering-tested, so the sheet of covered categories is a sample of a space the authors know is larger. The within-layer interference blocks multi-category control inside a single layer. Safety-adjacent categories show the smallest effects. And a structural point the paper raises without resolving: the leading categories are exactly the ones whose expert is *routed to* on the test prompts, so the causal effect and the routing condition are not separated — the authors call this "content-addressed routing" and treat the zero-effect rows as confirmation of it, which is coherent but means the intervention result is conditional on the router choosing the expert.

## What we take from it

A **method, a measure and a warning**. The method: when a decomposition is architectural rather than learned — rank-1 experts, rank-1 adapters, any unit that reads one direction and writes one — the unit's own weight matrix can be pushed through the unembedding to get a name, with no reconstruction error to report, and that name can then be tested by ablation. The measure worth stealing is the shape of the suppression table: **targeted effect, matched-size random effect, and the null in the same row**, with the not-working rows printed rather than dropped — because the cardinal and discourse rows (0.000 KL at both targeted and random) are what make the temporal and geographic rows meaningful, and a table containing only the wins would look identical to one from a model where every expert were special. The warning is about the claim's scope: this is a rank-1, small-scale, single-corpus result where the causal effect was measured on prompts that *do* route to the expert, and the paper's own "content-addressed routing" explanation makes the effect conditional on the router — so the correct reading is that an expert's label predicts behaviour *when the expert is active*, which is weaker than the paper's framing and is exactly the condition our own probing should record.
