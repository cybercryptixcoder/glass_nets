# Prediction or Persistence? What Future-Prediction Scores Miss About World Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.15082
- **Authors / organisation:** Animesh Maheshwari — Deccan AI (single author)
- **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15082
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1; §§1–5, Limitations, Appendices A, B, C, E, F; Tables 1–8 quoted as cells, Figures 1–7 via captions only)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

A latent-predictive world model is chosen for robot learning mainly on its **future-prediction score**: how well its own training criterion ranks the true continuation of a clip against alternatives. The author's point is that the score is a sum of three things that the number does not separate — an encoder that represents the scene well, a near future that mostly resembles the recent past, and a predictor that actually anticipates — and only the third is prediction in the sense the score is used to certify. The motivation is not that world models are unnecessary but that the selection decision (which model to pretrain from, plan against, or evaluate with) currently rests on a quantity that cannot show whether the predictive machinery does any work. So: separate the three with a control that costs one forward pass, and measure the predictor's share directly on the same trials.

## What they studied

Five open video world models in nine variants, each an encoder plus a latent predictor: V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-g/16, V-JEPA 2 ViT-H/16 and ViT-L/16 (native-video family), DINO-WM over frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14 and LeWM over ViT-Ti/14 trained from scratch (both action-conditioned on simulated PushT, therefore off-distribution on passive video). Data: 1,094 household egocentric videos (478.6 h, 242,356 annotated subtasks, no action stream), from which 25,296 active manipulation runs are formed. The unit of analysis is a **trial**: an anchor window of `A` seconds, a ground-truth future window of `W` seconds, and ten distractors, scored by the model's own frozen encoder, predictor and training energy.

## How they did it

Build a future-retrieval lineup — the model must pick the true `W`-second continuation among eleven candidates, all windows of the same video, so scene recognition is uninformative and chance top-1 is 1/11 = 0.091; distractors tile outward from the anchor's start and the truth's end so none overlaps the anchor, candidate presentation order is reshuffled per trial, and the trial is labelled by the action in the ground-truth window rather than the anchor. Rank zero-shot by native energy, each model scored in its own space (V-JEPA's LayerNormed ℓ1 feature regression with per-block normalisation for the 4-block concatenated 2.1 predictor, MSE criteria for DINO-WM and LeWM). Then the **persistence control**: an identical copy of the model whose predictor is replaced by the identity, so it answers "which candidate most resembles what was just observed?" under the same encoder, preprocessing and energy — the predictor is never called, which is why the control cannot be affected by the predictor's regime. The **predictor contribution** δ is the paired difference in top-1 between model and control over the same trials, signed, so a negative value means the predictor is worse than copying. Sweep context `A` ∈ 1–10 s against horizon `W` ∈ 1–10 s over 100 cells; bound the horizon by the frozen predictor's positional envelope (A + W ≤ 8 s for V-JEPA 2.1 at 32 positions, two views per clip, 8 fps) and report outside it as a separate out-of-distribution measurement obtained only by widening the positional address space. Pin one intersection of 27,000 trials over 704 videos and 25 cells for every cross-model number, and interval everything with a video-level cluster bootstrap (B = 10,000, seeded, model and control resampled together).

## What counts as an explanation here

A **decomposition of a capability score into named terms that sum to it**, with one term measured by deletion: chance → video continuity plus representation (persistence) → better encoder (still zero prediction) → the predictor (δ) → perfect retrieval. It is deliberately partial and the partiality is stated: δ certifies that the predictor carries future-relevant information beyond its encoder, which is a prerequisite for usefulness rather than a demonstration of it, and the probe measures temporal localisation rather than the content of a predicted future. The decomposition is exact for the family it is run in (encoder, energy and trials held fixed) and non-attributive across families, where several axes change at once.

## How they judged success

Top-1 retrieval against a chance rate of 0.091 and against the model's own persistence baseline on identical trials; mean rank and R@3/R@5 as secondary; the signed paired contribution δ with 95% video-level cluster bootstrap intervals (704 videos, B = 10,000, percentile) and every interval required to exclude zero; a documentary check that the bootstrap's own Monte-Carlo error is far below the intervals (endpoint jitter ±0.00029 at B = 10,000, 9.1% of the narrowest half-width; bias ≤ 0.00007 across 14 statistics); a **grain study** resampling at trial, run, video and actor level to show where dependence saturates (trial → video widens intervals up to 2.12×, video → actor at most 1.13× and 1.00× on the headline contribution); a degeneracy check re-ranking with the ground truth's own latent as query (1.000 for every model, explicitly not an achievable-accuracy bound); and two internal validity reads: whether persistence alone reproduces the pretraining ordering, and whether δ decays in a sensible way with horizon.

## What they found

Score and contribution dissociate **inside the native-video family alone**. V-JEPA 2 ViT-H and ViT-L reach 0.437 and 0.424 top-1 (over four times chance) against a shared persistence control at 0.396, so their predictors add only +0.0408 [+0.0326, +0.0491] and +0.0284 [+0.0205, +0.0363]; V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-g reaches 0.736 against its control at 0.444, δ = +0.2914 [+0.2806, +0.3019], roughly ten times V-JEPA 2's. The 0.340 gap between the two recipes splits (Table 1) into +0.048 for the better encoder and +0.291 for the predictor — two terms a single accuracy cannot separate. Within the envelope δ is stable at +0.330/+0.290/+0.277 across 0–4, 4–6 and 6–8 s for 2.1, while V-JEPA 2 stays at +0.02 to +0.05; beyond 8 s, where the positional budget is simply exceeded, 2.1's δ collapses to +0.052 (8–11 s) and then turns negative (−0.137 at 11–14 s, −0.074 at 14–21 s), which the author flags as an address-space artefact rather than a capability limit. A predictor can be **worse than none**: DINO-WM scores 0.136, above chance, while its own persistence control scores 0.315 — δ = −0.1789 [−0.1861, −0.1718] — although its magnitude is illustrative because it is measured outside its action-conditioned regime; the same sign appears in-distribution, since V-JEPA 2 ViT-L at `W` = 1 s sits at 0.452 against its control's 0.500. With every predictor disabled, persistence recovers the pretraining ordering with all four intervals disjoint — 0.444 (video, ViT-g), 0.396 (video, ViT-L), 0.315 (image, DINOv2), 0.208 (simulation from scratch) — which is the check that the probe tracks real signal at all. Horizon separates anticipating from copying: persistence falls from 0.554 to 0.308 over 1–4 s while 2.1 falls in parallel from 0.824 to 0.590, holding its +0.27 to +0.31 margin, whereas V-JEPA 2's curves are nearly flat and sit **below** their control at the shortest horizon.

## Limits and open problems they name

Passive video only, so nothing follows about control, planning or downstream robot performance; retrieval among eleven is easier than generation and says nothing about rollout fidelity; native-energy retrieval departs from the official 4-block attentive-probe protocol; only one evaluable V-JEPA 2.1 checkpoint, so no scaling curve; the anchor axis is confounded by lineup geometry (longer anchors push distractors further from the truth) and is explicitly not interpreted; cross-family differences are entangled across resolution, latent width, corpus and objective, so the 2 → 2.1 gap cannot be attributed to a single change; LeWM and DINO-WM are domain-mismatch controls rather than peers; a single household domain; and no human baseline, so whether the 0.264 of top-1 the strongest model misses is headroom or irreducible ambiguity is, in the author's own words, the most important quantity the study does not measure. The ranked fix list is concrete: fix the distractor geometry, use the roughly twice-as-large in-range pool, run the official attentive probe on the same trials (the gap between protocols then measures how much future information is unreachable without a trained readout), extend past 8 s by interpolation or sliding context, and add an action-conditioned lineup on real robot data.

## What we take from it

The **matched control that deletes one module and keeps everything else is a measurement, not a robustness check**, and this is the cheapest instance in the folder: identity-swap the predictor, re-rank the same trials, subtract, and the term you care about falls out of a score that was previously a sum. Our own probe tables report capacities — a probe's accuracy on a task — and never a *contribution*, because the task a probe is scored on always contains an easier way to solve it. The missing column for us is a **floor arm**: the same pipeline with the trained part replaced by a trivial rule built from the input's most recent state, so that a probe's AUROC and its persistence floor can be printed together and no result is ever credited to the learned part before it has beaten the copy. Three mechanics make the paper work and are portable. The control must be an *identical copy* under the same encoder and criterion, not a different simple model, or the comparison mixes the encoder in. Intervals must be clustered at the level where trials share data — here, at the video — and the paper shows both that the correction bites (up to 2.12×) and that it saturates (actor level adds at most 1.13×), which is how a cluster correction should be justified rather than asserted. And a validity read can be free: persistence ordering encoders by pretraining modality is evidence the probe measures something, obtained without any predictor at all. The warning is about the scale of the omission — a family whose two recipes differ by 0.340 in top-1 attributes 0.048 of that to representation and 0.291 to prediction, so a score-based model selection in that family was effectively selecting the predictor while reporting the sum. Finally, the negative direction is worth carrying: a predictor can be above chance and worse than copying, which means "above chance" is not a threshold anyone should accept as evidence of prediction.
