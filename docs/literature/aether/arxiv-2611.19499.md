# Do Probing and Steering Favor the Same Directions Within and Across Language Models?

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.19499
- **Authors / organisation:** Afshin Sadeghi, Daniel Becker — AI Assurance and Assessments, Fraunhofer IAIS (Sankt-Augustin, Germany)
- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19499
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1; §§1–6, Appendices A, B, E, F, G, H, M, N read in part; Tables 1–4 quoted as cells, Figures 2–4 via captions only)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Probe directions get reused as steering vectors almost by default: if a linear readout says a concept is present, the same vector is taken to say how to add or remove it. The authors ask whether that reuse is justified, and they ask it in a form that can be answered rather than argued — not "is this direction causal" but "does the direction that maximises detection also maximise change, when the size of the intervention is held fixed". The larger goal is a division of labour between two things interpretability constantly conflates: predictive information in a hidden state, and causal steerability of it. A second question follows from the first: if the two roles prefer different directions, they also transfer between models under different laws and to different degrees, so representational alignment becomes a functional question — do two models agree more on how a concept is read than on how it is changed, and is one of those agreements the one we should trust when building a monitor or an edit from a direction?

## What they studied
Three instruction-tuned checkpoints from three families — LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3.5-9B (post-trained), Gemma-3-12B-it — at a single pre-chosen layer at roughly 0.44 of depth (14 of 32, 14 of 32, 21 of 48, counted from zero), for three concepts: truthfulness (TruthfulQA, multi-true answer list), sentiment (2,000 Yelp reviews, 1,000 per class), and "unknown" on 4,000 PopQA questions where the label comes from the model's own six answers rather than from a dataset annotation. The unit of analysis is a **direction** — one unit vector in the last-token residual stream, drawn from a continuous family — and the same object is evaluated in two roles, as a probe score and as an additive (or rotational) intervention. Cross-model work uses all six ordered pairs of the three models and affine maps fitted on neutral text.

## How they did it
The core construction is a one-parameter family that interpolates between the two directions the field usually chooses from: the regularised discriminant w_λ ∝ (Σ_W + λI)⁻¹Δμ, normalised, where Δμ is the mean difference and Σ_W the class-share-weighted within-class covariance. At large λ this tends to the mean difference (up to scale, the contrastive-activation-addition and refusal directions); at small λ, when Σ_W is singular because the classes have fewer examples than dimensions, it is the part of Δμ lying outside the span of the training deviations rather than the Fisher discriminant — the authors say so explicitly. λ is reported in units of τ = tr(Σ_W)/d, the mean per-dimension within-class variance, because raw λ is not comparable across models (τ is about 0.01 in LLaMA and 4,000–7,000 in Gemma). Thirteen grid points spaced half a decade from 10⁻³ to 10³, with Δμ appended as the fourteenth. Every direction is computed in closed form once from a 50% estimation split; probing AUROC and steering effect are read on two held-out quarters. The steering operator adds a‖h‖w from the last token of a steering prompt onward; the six strengths are the same for every direction, and the effect is interpolated in √K to a common budget κ₀, where K is the mean next-token KL divergence on 200 neutral WikiText-2 sentences — so directions are compared at **equal side effect on neutral text**, not at equal norm. The steering score itself is the change in the true-versus-best-false answer margin (truthfulness), the change in positive-versus-negative word log-odds (sentiment), or a percentage-point change in outcomes (abstention, correctness) on PopQA. Hypotheses were fixed before the runs: the *plateau* of a curve is every grid point within one standard error of its best value, and H1a holds if the steering plateau's endpoints are no smaller than the probing plateau's with at least one strict. Cross-model transfer moves the same family under two different laws — the probe as the transpose of the reverse affine map (a probe is a linear functional), the steering vector forward through the forward map (a steering vector is a displacement, so the offset cancels) — with an orthogonal Procrustes map as a control under which both laws give the same vector; ratios are above-chance-share for the probe and effect-share for the steering direction against the target's own. Matched-text controls, a layer sweep over ten depths, and a cosine-gated rotation operator are run as robustness arms.

## What counts as an explanation here
A direction, and a division of it: the paper's claim is that one vector cannot serve both purposes, so the deliverable is not a better direction but a two-role decomposition of any direction into a detection filter and a displacement. It is partial and functional by design — no mechanism, no circuit, no feature dictionary — and it is predictive in a narrow, checkable sense: a first-order model in which only the component of the push along Δμ moves behaviour predicts the whole steering curve from measured cosines and side-effect curves plus one steering measurement, with a correlation of 0.91–1.00 over the fourteen directions in all six model–concept cases.

## How they judged success
Pre-registered hypotheses with named measures: H1a on plateau positions, H1b on whether an intermediate direction beats Δμ by at least 10% on a *second* held-out split when λ* was chosen on the first, H1c on the two transfer ratios with thresholds ρ ≥ 0.8 (strong) and ρ < 0.5 (weak) fixed in advance. Intervals are paired bootstraps over items (1,000 resamples, TruthfulQA questions resampled with all their answers; 2,000 for the PopQA arm). Baselines and controls: the mean difference itself, a random unit direction at the same K, first-token entropy as an output-only baseline for the abstention probe, the wrong transfer law, an orthogonal-Procrustes map, matched probe and steering texts, a layer sweep, and a rotation operator substituted for addition. There is also a pooling control (last token versus max-pooling), and an explicit post hoc check that the plateau conclusion survives budgets a quarter to four times κ₀.

## What they found
Within models the two roles pick different directions in every one of the six main model–concept combinations: probing peaks near λ = τ with held-out AUROC 0.96–0.98 (and falls to 0.60–0.89 at Δμ, so the mean difference is a markedly weaker *probe*), while the strongest steering lies between 10τ and 316τ — the steering plateau sits to the right of the probing plateau everywhere. The separation survives matched probe and steering texts in all six, holds in 59 of 60 layer × model × concept combinations in the sweep, holds under the gated rotation where it is defined at all, and is predicted by the first-order model. What does *not* generalise is the size of the gain: for truthfulness the best probe steers at only 0.21–0.64 of Δμ's effect at equal side effect, no intermediate direction beats Δμ by the required 10% (the largest, 9.6% in Qwen, falls short), and H1b fails in all three models; for sentiment the best probe already steers at 0.99–1.11 of Δμ and a λ* chosen on one split gives 27–56% more effect on the other, so H1b holds — and the authors trace most of that sentiment gain to the probe and steering texts being different, since it disappears in LLaMA and Gemma and shrinks in Qwen under matched texts. Mechanically, the probe end steers weakly because its cosine with Δμ is only 0.25–0.46, so at an equal neutral-text budget it may push just 1.3–1.5× as hard. Across models the asymmetry is consistent but not clean: a probe carried by its law keeps 81–91% of the target's above-chance AUROC in all twelve pairs (65–79% on the d′ scale, since the target probes sit near ceiling), while the carried Δμ reaches 44–93% of the target's steering effect and trails the probe in 11 of 12 pairs — yet the pre-registered classification calls nine pairs inconclusive, one probe-shared and two both-shared, and the authors read the result as a descriptive tendency in three models rather than a fact about twelve. Two of the pairs into Gemma are the sharpest caution: the mapped Δμ is no closer to Gemma's own Δμ than a permuted-label null (cosine −0.17 and −0.02) and still steers at 0.72 and 0.70 of its effect, so a direction can do the work in the target without being the target's direction. On PopQA, steering along the "unknown" Δμ turns uncertain errors into abstentions (+44, +30, +18 pp in LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma) far more than confident ones (+5, +4, +2) at a small cost on correct answers (+7, +3, +1), and a random direction at the same K also moves uncertain more than confident errors but by +7, +9, +1 — so what Δμ buys is volume of abstention at equal side effect, not a qualitatively different target. Gating by the probe protects correct answers and passes the pre-registered test in Qwen only (matched by an entropy gate passing in LLaMA instead); and only 3–11% of errors with a reachable correct answer get corrected, roughly what a random direction achieves (6–9%).

## Limits and open problems they name
Three concepts, three models, one layer for everything except the RQ1 sweep; the RQ2 verdicts are single-layer and their depth dependence is unknown. The twelve cross-model pairs are not independent — every pair shares a model with four others, so "11 of 12" describes three models rather than twelve confirmations. One direction at a time from one family, two operators; subspaces, nonlinear probes, SAE features and learned operators are untested, and an ungated rotation (which would separate rotation geometry from gate behaviour) was not run. The two transfer ratios compare different quantities — an above-chance AUROC share and an effect share — so their comparison is explicitly approximate. The maps fit concept texts worse than neutral text (R² 0.13–0.47 against 0.48–0.70), so part of the carried-steering loss may be the map and no pair can be called "neither shared". Steering is scored on answer margins and word log-odds rather than task accuracy, so a direction can move the score without changing the answer. Truthfulness directions are computed and evaluated on the same dataset, so a gain there cannot be separated from fitting the test distribution. RQ2 labels are reachability under five samples, which biases "no correct answer" toward questions the model sometimes gets right, and the reachable-error class is tiny (71, 33, 35 questions).

## What we take from it
A **framing and a measure**. Framing: a direction is not one object. Report any direction we publish with two scores from two different constructions — how well it separates on held-out items, and how much behaviour it moves at a fixed side effect — and treat "we used the probe direction to steer" as an untested inference rather than a default. Measure: the equal-side-effect budget. Comparing directions at equal norm conflates being a weaker probe with needing a bigger push, and the neutral-text KL budget (mean next-token divergence on a fixed neutral corpus, interpolated between bracketing strengths) is a cheap portable normaliser that made this paper's head-to-head comparisons possible at all. Method: the one-parameter family (Σ_W + λI)⁻¹Δμ, parameterised in units of the mean within-class variance, is a two-line construction that spans the field's two default choices and turns "which direction" into a scalar we can report — and it comes with a warning we should carry: where our probe and our steering vector differ, the difference is usually the covariance adjustment, and a probe *should* differ from a displacement precisely when within-class spread is large along the discriminating axis. Warning: transfer under the wrong law, and the Gemma anomaly (a mapped direction that steers well while being orthogonal to the target's own), mean that "this vector transfers" is not evidence that the two models represent the concept the same way. Shortcut for our battery: if we adopt the probe's direction for an edit, we owe a table showing the probe AUROC and the effect-at-equal-K for both the probe end and the mean difference, plus the cosine between them.
