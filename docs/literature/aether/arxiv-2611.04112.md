# Co-Occurrence Priors Override Visual Spatial Evidence: Understanding False Alarms in Vision–Language Models for Construction Struck-By Hazard Detection

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.04112
- **Authors / organisation:** Xiaoshan Zhou, single author — School of Project Management, Faculty of Engineering, University of Sydney, NSW, Australia
- **Date:** 2026-11-04 (arXiv v1, cs.CV); manuscript written Nov 2026
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04112
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (PDF; no arXiv HTML build exists for this version, so the 45-page PDF was extracted and read end to end)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Construction-safety vision–language models flag struck-by hazards (a worker inside or near an excavator's swing envelope) with high recall and poor precision. Off-the-shelf models call almost everything dangerous, and the author's framing is that **benchmark accuracy cannot distinguish the three possible causes** because all three produce the same wrong label. A false alarm on a genuinely distant worker could mean the model never saw the worker, saw both entities but failed to encode where one is relative to the other, or encoded the relation correctly and then answered from a learned association between the two objects and danger. Each diagnosis implies a different remedy — better perception, better spatial training data, or re-routing the existing relational evidence into the decision — so picking the remedy without the diagnosis is guessing. The paper runs that diagnosis with interpretability tools on two open-weight VLMs, and the larger goal is to show that internal readouts, not leaderboard scores, decide which of the three explanations is true.

## What they studied

Two instruction-tuned open-weight VLMs of comparable size and different architecture: **Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct** (32 vision-encoder blocks, a multimodal projector, 28 language layers, 28 heads per layer) and **InternVL3.5-8B**, both in bfloat16 on one H200, greedy, at most eight new tokens, fixed seed. The unit of analysis spans the whole ladder — vision blocks, projector, language layers, attention heads, MLP neurons, residual-stream directions and SAE latents — and the objects being explained are a binary answer score and its decomposition. Data: 355 **counterfactual pairs** (710 images) built from ConstructionSite-10k's Rule-4 (worker inside an excavator's operating radius) by pasting the *same* worker cutout near and far on a cleaned version of the *same* background; plus a worker-removed background set (355), a four-condition natural set of 200 photographs (worker only, excavator only, both far, both near), a balanced 140-image natural Rule-4 transfer set in which every safe image also contains both entities, and 50,000 unlabelled images to train SAEs.

## How they did it

The controlled pairs are the instrument, and they were built and screened carefully: Grounding DINO locates workers and excavators, SAM 2.1 gives the worker mask, all workers are removed by Telea inpainting and the residue refined with Qwen-Image-Edit, and the same RGBA cutout is then composited at a near position and at a far position chosen from a grid on the excavator's ground line. Distance is scale-free — worker–excavator centre distance divided by the square root of the excavator box area — so it is comparable across camera distances; the far worker is required to sit on approximately the same ground band as the near one, which rules out vertical image position as the cue. Screening was manual against seven criteria, and the survival rate is the honest part: 500 generated pairs yielded 80 first-pass, 315 failing on worker legibility were repaired and 271 passed, for 355 final pairs. Behavioural metrics are paired — pairwise ordering rate, mean paired near-minus-far shift with a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test — beside sensitivity, specificity, balanced accuracy and a score AUROC with Wilson intervals. The score is the log-odds of the HAZARD token sequence against SAFE, with a first-step logit margin used wherever an intervention needs a single position.

Then a fixed diagnostic order. Linear probes (class-balanced, L2-regularised logistic regression on mean-pooled activations) for worker presence, excavator presence and the near/far relation, with **pair-grouped folds** so a probe never sees the partner of the image it is evaluated on. An error taxonomy that assigns each behavioural error to the earliest stage where the out-of-fold evidence fails (perception, relation, reasoning, decision). Prompt-token ablation that zeroes the embeddings of word groups while holding sequence length fixed, with a length-matched ablation of an appended neutral sentence as the control. Attention-head analysis over all 784 heads with BH-corrected paired Wilcoxon tests, then eight-head knockout and cross-patching against eight matched low-association heads. Residual-stream patching that exchanges near and far states at every layer and at either the final prompt position or the image-token positions. Top-k SAEs (4× dictionary, k = 32) at the projector, vision layer 27 and language layers 0, 15 and 21 for a per-latent view of object versus relation coding. And finally a correction battery — false-alarm-direction probing and geometry, directional steering (raw and orthogonalised to the relation direction), a residualisation-based head search with eight acceptance criteria, single-neuron ablation over 101 conditions, and relation-gated subspace steering — every one of them scored in the sensitivity–specificity plane against the **threshold-calibration curve** of the unmodified score, under nested pair-grouped cross-validation.

## What counts as an explanation here

A three-way diagnostic decomposition rather than a mechanism. The explanation is a **score decomposition** — a shared offset b, a relational shift Δ, and a scalar index b/Δ — attached to a specific causal claim about where the offset comes from: adding a worker to an excavator scene versus moving that worker into the zone. The interpretation is deliberately falsifiable at the level of predictions: perception failure predicts weak object representations and an error mix dominated by misses; relation failure predicts an undecodable relation and answers that ignore movement; co-occurrence override predicts both entities and the relation decodable, an answer score that moves the right way, and far images still called hazardous. It is a partial explanation in the strong sense — the paper is explicit that it did not find a circuit to remove, only a quantity that says why the decision is wrong and what would have to change. It predicts: the PDI predicts how much of the far set crosses the threshold, and the worker-removal increment predicts the size of the correction a gated intervention can buy.

## How they judged success

Four research questions, each with its own measurement. Decodability is judged by out-of-fold AUROC and balanced accuracy under pair-grouped folds with zero pair leakage verified. Behaviour is judged on paired ordering rate and paired shift, not accuracy alone. The offset is judged by the PDI against 1, and by the co-occurrence-to-relation increment ratio κ against 1. Localisation is judged by **prespecified acceptance criteria** — far-specificity gain ≥ 0.05, near-sensitivity loss ≤ 0.02, ≥ 90% of the relational gap retained, relation-readout AUROC loss ≤ 0.02, an effect beyond the 95th percentile of 200 layer-matched random head groups, replication in at least four of five folds, and double dissociation from the relation heads — with the null stated in advance. Correction is judged by whether the operating point lies **above** the threshold-calibration curve at matched sensitivity, which is the only fair bar for any intervention that lowers hazard scores, plus balanced accuracy against the best achievable threshold. Natural-image transfer is judged by freezing every readout learned on edited pairs and evaluating it unchanged on Rule-4 photographs.

## What they found

The diagnosis lands on the third explanation, and the numbers are unusually clean about it. **Objects are seen**: worker presence is decodable at AUROC above 0.95 from about vision layer 8 and 1.000 at the projector, excavator presence 0.999 at vision layer 27, and when asked directly Qwen confirms an excavator in 98.2% of the 710 images. **The relation is encoded**: under pair-grouped validation near/far decodability peaks at language layer 21 for Qwen at AUROC 0.823, and the out-of-fold readout reaches 0.824 (balanced accuracy 0.746); for InternVL 0.913 and 0.831. **The answers barely use it**: Qwen labels 300 of 355 near images HAZARD (sensitivity 84.5%) and clears only 92 of 355 far images (specificity 25.9%), accuracy 55.2%, score AUROC 0.681; InternVL answers HAZARD for **all 710** images. Yet the score does move the right way — the near image scores higher in 78.6% of Qwen pairs (mean difference 0.102, p = 8.4e-34) and 73.2% of InternVL pairs — so two thirds of Qwen pairs are simply HAZARD on both (253 of 355), against 47 both-correct.

The offset explains the gap. For Qwen the far-image offset is b = 0.68 log-odds against a relational shift Δ = 0.62, a prior dominance index of 1.1 (0.9–1.4) — the offset is about the size of the effect it overrides, so roughly a third of far images still fall on the SAFE side. For InternVL b = 8.03 logits against Δ = 1.03, PDI 7.8, so nothing is cleared. **What produces the offset is the co-occurrence, not the scene and not the relation**: removing the worker from the same 355 scenes drops Qwen's HAZARD rate from 76.1% to 11.0%, while pasting the same worker far gives 84.8% near and 76.1% far (free generation). Pair-level, adding a worker far from the machine raises the first-step margin by C = 1.48 (1.38–1.57) and moving it into the zone adds a further R = 0.62 (0.54–0.70) — a ratio of 2.4 (2.1–2.8), with the far image above its own worker-removed background in 94.4% of pairs; InternVL gives C = 3.26, R = 1.03, ratio 3.2 (2.6–4.0). The two models differ in where the offset starts: in Qwen the empty scene scores -0.79 and the arriving worker tips it; in InternVL the worker-removed scene already scores 4.77 and is called HAZARD every time, natural excavator-only images 92%, even worker-only images 52% — for InternVL an excavator plus a prompt naming both entities is sufficient.

The natural-image transfer is the paper's strongest single result and it cuts both ways. On the balanced Rule-4 set, where every safe image also contains a worker and an excavator so co-occurrence is held constant, Qwen finds 65 of 70 violations (sensitivity 92.9%) but clears only 8 of 70 compliant scenes (specificity 11.4%), score AUROC **0.557** — barely above chance. Frozen readouts: object probes transfer without loss (worker 96.4%, excavator 100%), a hazard-proxy readout from language layer 0 retains AUROC 0.793, and the controlled relation readout **collapses to 0.521** (balanced accuracy 0.514). The evidence that creates the offset is robust and general; the evidence that should correct it is fragile and specific to the edited domain. The error taxonomy agrees: of 263 Qwen far-image false alarms, 154 (58.6%) occurred with both object signals present *and* the relation readout correctly indicating a far placement — the model had what it needed and did not use it.

The causal work locates nothing removable. Of 784 heads, 419 differ in image attention between near and far after FDR correction, strongest in layers 19–24, but knocking out the top eight cuts the gap by only 0.061 (about 11% of baseline) and mostly by lowering near margins, while cross-patching the candidate heads is no better than patching controls (-0.024 against -0.029) — and neither touches the offset. Exchanging residual states at the final prompt position reverses the gap at every late layer, largest at layer 26 (-1.15). The correction battery:
- **Probing works, steering does not**: far false alarms are cleanly decodable from far true negatives out of fold (layer 20, AUROC 0.795), the false-alarm direction overlaps the relation direction at cosine 0.22, and steering against it — raw or relation-preserving — moves far specificity by +0.003.
- **A head search that appeared to work was threshold calibration in disguise**: the initial head ablation gained 0.149 far specificity at a cost of 0.101 near sensitivity by lowering near *and* far margins almost equally (-0.70 and -0.64); its operating point lay on the calibration curve. The revised overlap-free search met 3 of 8 criteria in Qwen (far specificity -0.056, random-group p = 0.975) and 3 of 8 in InternVL. No single neuron passed; the 101 neuron conditions ranged -0.048 to +0.051 and clustered on the curve.
- **One intervention cleared the bar, and only with an external gate**: subspace steering in a basis residualised against the relation readout, answer direction and object readouts, applied **only when the model's own out-of-fold relation readout indicated a far placement**, raised Qwen far specificity from 0.217 to 0.299 at α = 1 (sensitivity loss 0.014), and to 0.575 at α = 2 at sensitivity 0.775 — where threshold calibration reaches only 0.355, and balanced accuracy 0.675 exceeds the best achievable by any threshold (0.648). The same subspace ungated follows the calibration curve. In InternVL no dose changed any answer, because doses scaled to the activation spread cannot overcome an eight-logit offset. The author also notes the humbling comparison: simply *reading* the relation out of the internal state scores balanced accuracies of 0.75 and 0.83, higher than any steered or recalibrated output.

## Limits and open problems they name

Static images with spatial relations only — no equipment motion, worker behaviour, 3D geometry or operational context, so the physical decision boundary is untested and multi-distance series plus video are named as the next step. The gate depends on an external probe trained on the controlled pairs, and that readout did not transfer to natural images, so the correction is a demonstration and not a deployment. The pair construction depends on manual screening (420 of 500 first-pass pairs rejected) and on generated edits that may carry compositing cues, though the natural Rule-4 set is the control against that. The author states the ablation caution directly: visual information can reach the answer by more than one route and models can switch routes when one is ablated, so a null localisation result means "not needed here", not "not used normally". Single domain, two models, one prompt.

## What we take from it

**A decomposition, not a probe accuracy, is the deliverable.** Splitting the score into a shared offset and a relational shift, and reporting their ratio, converts "the model is bad at this" into a falsifiable claim with a remedy attached — and the prediction holds across two models whose PDIs differ sevenfold. This is the cheapest version of the folder's recurring question about whether an encoded representation is *used*: measure how much evidence each cause contributes, not whether the representation exists.

**Score the correction against the calibration curve, always.** The paper's best methodological move is that every intervention is plotted against what simply moving the threshold achieves. It kills one of the paper's own results — a head ablation that looked like a 14.9-point gain was a uniform downward shift — and it is the reason the one surviving intervention is believable. Any intervention that lowers scores along the decision axis will "work" by this test and by no other.

**Ablation doses and random-group nulls with acceptance criteria written first.** Eight criteria, stated before the search, with 200 layer-matched random groups as the null, is what makes "we found no localised mechanism" a result rather than a failure.

**The co-occurrence increment as a portable measurement.** Compare the score change from *adding* an entity with the score change from *moving* it into the relevant relation. It needs no probe, no training, only a matched editing pipeline — and here it is what separates the three hypotheses.

**A warning about eval-set composition that applies to us directly.** The model can look adequate on a mixed natural benchmark and go to near-chance (AUROC 0.557) once every negative also contains the two entities that trigger the prior. And a readout fitted in a narrow, carefully controlled domain can score 0.82 there and 0.52 on the natural images it was supposed to describe — the same stale-readout pattern the folder has catalogued from the training-drift side, arriving here from the domain side.

**A caution about gated corrections**: the gate is not part of the model. It is an external classifier over the model's own states, so the reported gain is the gain of a hybrid system, and the paper says so plainly. That is the honest shape of the result and it is what we should replicate when our own readouts cannot be pushed into the forward pass.
