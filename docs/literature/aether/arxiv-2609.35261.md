# Imprint Reader: From Weight-Update Readout to Behavioral Intervention

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.35261
- **Authors / organisation:** Guanxu Chen, Qihao Lin, Jing Shao; Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory / Shanghai Jiao Tong University
- **Date:** 2026-09-28
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35261
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Fine-tuning leaves a delta in the weights, and the authors want a model that can say in words what that delta *was* — which fact was learned, which behavioural tendency was installed. They frame this as the missing half of machine self-improvement: current systems neither perceive their own learning the way a student does nor decode the parameter traces they physically hold, so the loop "notice what you learned, then correct it" never closes. They are explicit that existing weight-space predictors stop at coarse labels (accuracy, task identity) and that the few systems that verbalise a weight difference either work in narrow purpose-built domains or confabulate descriptions for updates carrying no information. The larger goal is an interface where readout feeds back into intervention: describe the change, then act on the model using that description.

## What they studied
One post-trained **Qwen3-14B** and weight updates built on top of it. The unit of analysis is neither a neuron nor a layer but the **frozen weight delta** of an inner-loop fine-tune, produced as a LoRA update (ranks drawn from 16/32/64/128/256, 64 inner steps, lr 2e-5). Two target classes: 8,592 factual-knowledge items (a single canonical proposition, drawn from HLE, SimpleQA, FrontierScience, OpenBookQA, SciBench, TheoremQA, SciCode and public FrontierMath examples, filtered down from ~14.9k candidates) and 8,592 behavioural items (a canonical sentence describing a response tendency, from a 10k catalogue in seven categories), each with 100-item held-out test sets. The Reader is a full copy of Qwen3-14B trained on top of those deltas; the deltas themselves are never updated.

## How they did it
Each training episode has three stages. First a temporary *builder* copy of the base model is trained on a question–answer set written to install one target, and the parameter difference is retained as the delta; the QA pairs are then discarded and never shown to the Reader. Second, the delta is *mounted* onto the Reader's own parameters by coordinate-aligned addition, and an **anchor-free meta-query** — a prompt that asks for a description of what just changed but is sampled independently of the target (I(M;K)=0, from a pool of 224) — is posed to the composed model, with the canonical statement of the target as the teacher-forced answer. Third, gradients flow only into the Reader; the mounted delta is unmounted after each step. Control episodes are trained in the same way: a **zero no-change** update paired with a target saying nothing was learned, and a **random perturbation** drawn without reference to any target, both meant to teach abstention. A batch of 64 episodes carries 24 knowledge, 24 behaviour, 8 no-change and 8 random; the control loss weights are ramped in over the first 1,432 steps; training is two epochs' worth (1,432 steps/epoch, checkpoints analysed to step 2,800) of full-parameter BF16 training with lr 1e-4 on eight GPUs. Evaluation for free-form readout: 100 held-out knowledge and 100 behaviour updates, 100 stochastic generations each at temperature 0.6, scored by a Qwen3-30B-A3B judge under a fixed rubric (pass = score ≥0.8 with a copied evidence span and a complete proposition). A separate **adapter-swap control** compares target loss under three conditions at fixed query and fixed target — matching update, no update, and a genuine update built for a different item of the same type (type-preserving permutation, seed 20260918) — over 800 queries at 12 checkpoints. Finally, intervention: because Reader and parent share parameter coordinates, the gradient of a target description's likelihood on the Reader is transferred to the original Qwen3-14B, either by zeroing the output rows with the most negative signed inner product with that gradient (pruning) or by applying a scaled signed gradient to those rows (editing). Row pruning is compared against SetDiff, WANDA, ActSVD and Random at identical row budgets (0.01/0.05/0.1/0.5% of 2,088,960 rows), and, importantly, against the same procedure run with gradients from the *base* model rather than the trained Reader.

## What counts as an explanation here
A natural-language description generated by the Reader, plus a scalar per-target compatibility score whose gradient is a usable direction in parameter space. The description is meant to be partial and item-level ("this update installed fact X" / "this update made the model tend to verify intermediate steps"), not a mechanism: no claim is made about *where* in the network the change lives, and the papers' own examples show descriptions that get the gist and misstate a detail. The stronger object is not the sentence but the differentiable proxy — a loss on the Reader that the authors treat as a measure of how far a candidate update sits from a named behaviour, which is what makes the readout actionable.

## How they judged success
Judge-based **Pass@100** on held-out updates (fraction of updates for which at least one of 100 samples is judged complete) for knowledge and behaviour separately; a stated pass bar of 0.8 with an evidence-span substring check. Controls: no-change and random-perturbation episodes (abstention), and the adapter-swap paired differences G_none and G_swap in nats/token. For intervention: measured harmful-prompt refusal rate over 1,253 harmful prompts from four attack benchmarks (greedy decoding, no system prompt, 1,024 new tokens, keyword-pattern classification after stripping the reasoning block) under two opposite target descriptions, with garbling reported separately as "missing, not zero" where not measured; benign over-refusal on 350 benign prompts, where measured; and, for the behavioural edits, GSM8K, MATH-500, BFCL Overall and per-1,000-token counts of backtracking, verification and sub-goal expressions. Baselines for pruning, a direct-prompt arm at inference, and a **broad** target description rescaled to the same global ℓ2 norm as the specific one to isolate target specificity.

## What they found
Free-form readout is feasible but unreliable: Pass@100 of **2%** for knowledge and **16%** for behaviour on unseen updates at step 2,800. The likelihood-side evidence is stronger than the generation-side evidence: at that checkpoint the matching update lowers target loss by **0.2227** nats/token against no update and by **0.1255** against a mismatched same-type update, while the mismatched update still beats no update by **0.0972** — so most of the measurable benefit is "an adapter is mounted", and roughly a seventh of a nat/token per token is item-specific. Intervention at a 0.5% pruning budget moves refusal from a base **57.9%** to **64.1%** under the safety-maintenance target and to **55.4%** under the refusal-relaxation target, with near-zero garbling; the same procedure using base-model gradients does not produce the separation, and WANDA/ActSVD garble substantially. Behavioural editing at a 0.1% row budget (2,089 rows, α = 0.35) raises GSM8K 94.77 → 95.00, MATH-500 82.8 → 83.0, backtracking 0.327 → 0.687 and sub-goal expressions 5.390 → 6.015 per 1,000 tokens, and BFCL Agentic 15.93 → 22.30 with Overall 41.69 → 44.60, without any training examples from either target task; the broad description reaches 43.68 Overall and the direct-prompt arm 42.75, so the specific description's margin over its own coarser and prompt-based analogues is one to two points.

## Limits and open problems they name
The authors say plainly that free generation "remains infrequent" and does not reliably verbalise unseen updates, and that their reliable-looking success cases are illustrations rather than rates. They scope the work to controlled updates for a *single* knowledge item or behavioural tendency and explicitly deny any training-data extraction capability. Noted in passing: descriptions can be topically right and substantively wrong, some cells are missing rather than zero (benign garbling for the signed edits), and the whole interface depends on having the parent model's exact coordinate space, i.e. on an open-weight checkpoint whose architecture you can copy.

## What we take from it
Three things, in order of confidence. **(1) The control design is the reusable part.** Pairing a "no update" episode with a "random perturbation" episode during *training* is the cheapest abstention prior this folder has seen, and the adapter-swap test (matching vs same-type mismatched vs absent, at fixed query and target) is exactly the three-rung ladder this folder keeps asking for — it separates "the Reader sees an adapter" from "the Reader sees *this* adapter" and prices the difference at 0.1255 nats/token. **(2) A readout earns its keep when its output is differentiable and lands in the space you can intervene on.** Our white-box model should be built so that its prediction can be differentiated with respect to the object it describes; that is what turns a description into a row-selection rule, and it is the same move as the anchor construction noted from the quantitative-judges entry (make the null solution exact and the delta real). **(3) Treat Pass@100 as the honest headline and the likelihood gap as the effect size.** A generation rate of 2% and a stable positive likelihood gap are not contradictory, and reporting only the latter would overstate the readout; report both, plus the fraction of the gap attributable to the mere presence of a mounted change. Warning worth carrying: the intervention arms are graded on their *intended* direction (refusal up under one target, down under the other) while the baseline comparison is a set of pruning heuristics built for a different purpose — the fair comparison the paper runs is the same method with base-model gradients, and that is the only one in the table that isolates the Reader.
