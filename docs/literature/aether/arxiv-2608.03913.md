# Sparse Weight Decomposition for Efficient Circuit Extraction

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.03913
- **Authors / organisation:** Chuanhao Yan, Xuhan Huang, Yawen Duan, Zhenfei Yin, Hang Zhao, Bryan Dai, Jie Fu; IQuest Research, Safe AI Forum, University of Oxford, Stanford University, Tsinghua University
- **Date:** 2026-08-04
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03913
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit analysis needs small, addressable units inside a model that was not built to have them. Dense pretrained projections do not expose such units, so the established routes all pay to *create* them: train an SAE or transcoder in activation space, learn a parameter decomposition, or pretrain a weight-sparse model from scratch. The authors' complaint is not only the cost but the fidelity gap — every one of those routes analyses a learned stand-in for the model rather than the model, and weight-sparse pretraining is quoted at 100–1000× the training and inference compute of a dense model of the same capability. So the question is whether circuit units can be pulled out of an existing dense checkpoint directly, by reparameterizing its weight matrices rather than by fitting anything on top of its activations. This is a step toward cheap, repeated local causal analysis (including per-training-step analysis) of models the analyst cannot afford to retrain.

## What they studied

GPT-2 Small (single matrix: layer-8 `mlp.c_proj`; an MLP replacement of layer 8; full-model replacement of all 48 attention and MLP projection matrices across 12 blocks), Qwen2.5 0.5B / 1.5B / 3B (middle-layer `mlp.down_proj`: layers 12/14/18), and Qwen3.5-27B (layer 31 `mlp.down_proj`). The unit of analysis is a **bottleneck unit** obtained from a sparse factorization of one weight matrix: a rank-one read–write path whose read vector and write vector are both sparse, so the number of active scalar connections is itself a measured quantity. Four task families from public Edge-Pruning and auto-circuit data (greater-than, IOI, docstring, gendered pronoun), with a train/test split fixed per task.

## How they did it

Factorize a dense weight matrix W as the product of two sparse factors AB, with a hard budget on the total number of nonzero entries (reported as a sparsity level s, the fraction of W's own nonzeros removed). Each intermediate coordinate i is a bottleneck: it reads the input through column i of A and writes through row i of B, so its contribution is an additive rank-one term in the layer's output. Because the terms are additive and separable, replacing one z_i leaves the others untouched, which is what makes the units independently scoreable and ablatable. The objective is reconstruction of the *layer's outputs on calibration activations* rather than Frobenius error on the weights; the two differ because the activation Gram matrix up-weights input directions the model actually processes, and a variant that sets that Gram matrix to the identity ("zero-data") gives a weights-only factorization. The solver is an off-the-shelf Double Sparse Factorization heuristic: alternate ℓ0-constrained block updates solved by ADMM with hard thresholding, then a fixed-support refit of one factor. For full-model replacement, where local errors compound, they freeze the zero/nonzero supports and fine-tune only the surviving factor *values* on next-token cross-entropy (SWD-FT). Circuit extraction then follows the standard recipe: rank units by positive first-order task-margin attribution on the train split, keep nested top-k prefixes, and test them on held-out data with mean ablation. Sufficiency keeps the selected units and mean-ablates the rest; necessity mean-ablates the selected ones, both relative to the *unpruned replacement's* own task score. Cost is reported two ways, as selected units and as active read+write edges, and comparisons are only made between replacements whose pre-pruning cross-entropy differs by at most 0.001. Controls: exact full-rank SVD and a random-orthogonal ("Random-B") exact factorization of the same matrix, both of which reproduce W exactly with dense factors, plus zero ablation in place of mean ablation with rankings held fixed.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **parameter-space circuit**: a small set of sparse rank-one read–write paths, each with a stated input support and output support, whose retention preserves a held-out behavioural margin and whose ablation destroys it. The explanation is explicitly local and non-unique — they say the units depend on the objective, the calibration distribution, initialization and the sparsity budget, and that identifiability of a two-factor sparse decomposition is at best known up to scaling and permutation. It is not a feature dictionary and they are careful not to claim it is: a bottleneck unit is not assumed to be monosemantic, and they show places where treating units as independent is wrong. It is predictive in a narrow, cost-quality sense (given a fidelity budget, how many edges are needed to reach a sufficiency or necessity target) rather than a model that predicts what the network will do on a new input.

## How they judged success

Two criteria, kept separate throughout: **replacement fidelity** (change in held-out cross-entropy against the dense model, with activation relative MSE of the replaced projection and KL of next-token distributions as companions) and the **circuit cost-quality tradeoff** (minimum selected units and minimum active edges needed to reach each sufficiency or necessity threshold on the held-out split). Unit ranking uses the positive part of first-order task-margin attribution; ablation reference is the train-split mean activation. The matched-fidelity rule (within 0.001 CE) is what makes cross-method comparison legitimate, and they test that conclusions do not depend on the ablation value by re-running with zero ablation at fixed rankings. Limitation-style controls are unusually specific: exact dense reparameterizations isolate sparsity from factorization, ten random-orthogonal seeds give a band for the control, and the zero-data variant isolates the contribution of calibration data.

## What they found

The matched-fidelity circuit comparison favours SWD: across all four GPT-2 tasks it reaches the same sufficiency and necessity targets with substantially fewer active edges than a transcoder at hidden width 3,072 and 12,288 and than a VPD variant matched to local reconstruction, and the same holds on Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen3.5-27B. The data-efficiency claim is the headline: SWD reaches low replacement error after a few thousand tokens where the trained baselines need roughly a million, and for Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B and Qwen3.5-27B some fixed replacements for circuit extraction use only 1,024–2,048 calibration tokens at CE deltas in the range of 0.0007–0.0012 (negative for the 27B surface). Full-model replacement: sparse factorization alone reaches held-out CE 3.90 after 4.19M calibration tokens; fixed-support fine-tuning brings it to 3.44 after 20.6M total tokens, against 3.45 for a weight-sparse pretrained model that was trained on 2.884B tokens at a matched active-weight budget of 26.77M (~68% sparsity). On task circuits at that budget the two are comparable on greater-than and gendered pronoun, while the sparse-pretrained model's docstring and IOI circuits show near-zero or negative necessity drop where SWD-FT stays effective. Zero ablation at fixed rankings preserves SWD's edge advantage — at sufficiency 1.0 on greater-than, the higher-sparsity SWD setting needs 12,303 active edges against 184,320 for the smaller transcoder. The zero-data variant better preserves the raw weights (relative Frobenius error 0.366 vs 0.415 at s = 0.75) while the calibrated variant better preserves behaviour under aggressive sparsification, and zero-data units still yield usable circuits — at s = 0.375 a docstring circuit reaches 0.962 test sufficiency with 3 selected units and greater-than 0.986 with 256. Qualitative: of the top-ranked greater-than units in layers 6, 8 and 10 (global ranks 32–129 of 9,208), four of six label as numeric/quantity/measurement in task-independent WikiText-2 contexts, labelled by a model and not used for selection; a single unit's read direction supports a targeted rank-one weight edit on "the opposite of up is" that moves the answer–foil margin by 0.216 at mean KL 4.02e-5 on seven unrelated prompts, sitting between a random-unit control and a target-conditioned dense oracle on the efficacy-locality tradeoff, with rank-4 LoRA reaching larger margins at much higher side effects; and a static query–key screen plus prompt replay closes a chain on one head, where the reconstruction keeps attention structure at mean KL 0.0405 and ablating a single query unit raises it to 2.496. They also measure dependence honestly: after GELU, 95th-percentile absolute correlation between two units' actual residual contributions is 0.0049 (99th 0.0089) with a small structured tail reaching 0.345, while across GELU the contextual interaction ratio has median 0.089 and maximum 0.221 over 8,128 pairs, so input-projection paths are the ones that must be read in company.

## Limits and open problems they name

Efficient extraction is not understanding: sufficiency and necessity establish that selected units causally affect a held-out margin, not that the model is globally explained, and coverage of rare safety-relevant cases is not addressed. Circuits are local and non-unique — no identifiability conditions are established, interventions act on an approximate replacement whose matched CE averages over drift rather than bounding it per input, first-order ranking can miss behaviourally important edges, and mean-ablation conclusions are known to depend on protocol. Scale verification is thin: the 27B experiment is one matrix, full-model replacement is only GPT-2 Small, and the semantic audit is six units. Under the MLP-replacement surface the advantage is *mixed* rather than uniform — they say the transcoder is competitive in several selected-unit and sufficiency comparisons there. And nothing here predicts behaviour under an intervention it has not run.

## What we take from it

**A framing and a measurement, plus a warning.** (1) The cheapest route to addressable units is often not to train a dictionary but to *factorize the weights you already have*, and the axes that matter are the ones we can compute before any forward pass: nonzero budget, read-support size, write-support size per unit. For a predictive white-box model this is the natural parameter-side object — the unit carries its own support, so "which input coordinates does this thing read" is a lookup rather than an inference. (2) The evaluation protocol is worth copying almost verbatim into our eval set: **fidelity matching before circuit comparison** (never compare two circuits whose stand-in models have different CE), cost reported two ways (units *and* edges, because a dense factorization ties on units and loses badly on edges — the exact-SVD and random-orthogonal controls exist precisely to show that), held-out sufficiency/necessity rather than train-split fit, and one re-run with a different ablation value at fixed rankings. (3) The finding that dense exact reparameterizations are competitive on some tasks and thresholds while losing consistently on the edge axis is a warning for our own read-outs: a cost metric can manufacture a win, and a claim of the form "our decomposition is more compact" has to name the axis. (4) The interaction numbers are the one result that constrains our surrogate design directly: if additive contributions were independent, a sum of per-unit effects would be a decent predictor; the measured 99th-percentile contribution correlation of 0.0089 says that holds after the output projection, while the moderate-but-widespread GELU-mediated interactions say it does not hold before the nonlinearity — so a predictive model must place its non-additivity where the nonlinearity is, not spread it evenly. (5) The zero-data variant is a genuinely useful floor for us: units good enough for circuits can come from weights alone, so any activation-fitted dictionary must be shown to beat the weights-only factorization, not just to beat a random baseline.
