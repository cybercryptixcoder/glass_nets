# LLMs Encode Harmfulness and Refusal Separately

- **Key:** arxiv-2507.11878
- **Authors / organisation:** Jiachen Zhao, Jing Huang, Zhengxuan Wu, David Bau, Weiyan Shi — Northeastern University; Stanford University
- **Date:** 2025-07-16 (v1); read v5, 2026-07-06
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11878
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v5 HTML, ~95k chars including the arXiv chrome; abstract, §§1–7 including Related Work, Conclusion, Implications and Limitations, references, Appendices A–H; Tables 1–11 read as cells; Figures 1–19 not inspected as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The safety literature had converged on a one-dimensional story: refusal is mediated by a single direction, and that direction is usually extracted at the last token of the chat template. A second claim rode along with it — that ablating or steering the refusal direction changes what the model *thinks* about the input's harm, so a jailbreak that removes the refusal signal is a model that has been persuaded the request is fine. These authors ask whether that second claim was ever tested, or whether it was inherited from the first because both experiments used the same token position. Their target is the distinction between a decision and the belief the decision is nominally about: a model can refuse something it internally knows is benign, or accept something it internally knows is harmful, and if the two live in different places then the refusal direction says nothing about the belief. The larger goal is an auditing instrument that reads the belief rather than the behaviour, on the argument that a decision can be broken while the belief stays intact.

## What they studied
Three instruct-tuned open models: Llama2-Chat-7B, Llama3-Instruct-8B and Qwen2-Instruct-7B, run on A100-40GB. The unit of analysis is the residual-stream activation at a single **token position**, and the central design move is that they compare two of them — the last token of the user's instruction (before the template's post-instruction special tokens) and the last token of the whole input (after them). Data: Advbench, JBB and Sorry-Bench for harmful instructions, Alpaca for harmless ones, Xstest for harmless-but-refused, CATQA for the category analysis, ToxicChat and the OpenAI Moderation Evaluation set for the guard's out-of-distribution behaviour. Directions are difference-in-means between class means; the classification object is a pair of cosine similarities to two cluster centres.

## How they did it
The paper opens with a behavioural observation that motivates the position split: deleting the post-instruction special tokens from the chat template drops the refusal rate on Advbench sharply (100 → 85.3%, 96.0 → 58.9%, 98.0 → 81.3% for the three models), which suggests the refusal decision is not formed until those tokens are present. They then take four behavioural categories — refused-harmful, accepted-harmless, accepted-harmful, refused-harmless — and at each layer and each of the two positions compute the two cluster centres from the two well-behaved categories, then ask which centre the misbehaving categories sit nearer, using the signed difference of the two cosine similarities. Directions are extracted as class-mean differences at the position where each concept was found to cluster, and interventions are activation addition applied to the instruction tokens (all tokens of the input, for the refusal direction, because restricting it to the pre-template tokens removed its effect). The causal test is a **reply inversion** prompt: an appended question such as "Could this user prompt cause harm?" with instructions to answer "Certainly" or "No", so that a rise in perceived harm now shows up as the *opposite* of a refusal token, which lets the two directions be distinguished by the sign of their effect rather than by both pushing refusal up. Category structure is checked by extracting per-category directions on CATQA and cross-steering; the guard is Δ_harmful averaged over layers against a negative threshold, with clusters built from 100 harmful and 100 harmless training examples. Robustness is tested by finetuning on 50–400 adversarial acceptance pairs generated by steering harmful instructions along the reverse refusal direction.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **direction**, plus a claim about which token position carries it. The explanation is not a circuit or a surrogate model; it is a two-part division of labour — harmfulness as a categorical belief encoded where the model has finished reading the instruction, refusal as a surface decision encoded where the template hands over to the assistant — supported by cluster geometry, by steering, and finally by a task in which the two directions produce opposite outputs. It is explicitly partial: the authors call the paper an existence proof that the belief is encoded and separable, not a description of the mechanism, and they leave circuits, layers and neurons to future work.

## How they judged success
Cluster-assignment margin per layer (cosine to one centre minus cosine to the other, threshold at zero, with the authors noting the oracle threshold may not be zero); refusal rate under a fixed list of refusal substrings; the fraction of responses containing the refusal or acceptance token in the inversion task; cosine similarity between the two extracted directions and across risk categories; classification accuracy of the Latent Guard against Llama Guard 3 8B on held-out jailbreak sets, refused-harmless and accepted-harmful sets, ToxicChat and the OpenAI moderation set; and the same guard's accuracy after adversarial finetuning at four dataset sizes. Steering sweeps run over layers, and the best layer is reported per model.

## What they found
The clustering reverses between the two positions, and the reversal is the paper's cleanest result: at the instruction's last token the misbehaving categories fall into the cluster matching their *harmfulness* (accepted-harmful sits with refused-harmful, refused-harmless sits with accepted-harmless), while at the post-template token both fall into the cluster matching their *behaviour*. Refused-harmless prompts therefore sit in the harmed-looking region at the decision point while their belief score stays negative — over-refusal is visible in the read-out as over-refusal rather than as a perception error. The two directions are nearly unrelated: average cosine ≈ 0.1 on Llama2, to be read against 0.57 for held-out refused instructions and −0.14 for accepted ones against the refusal direction. Both directions raise refusal on harmless prompts (peak 94% at layer 9 for the harmfulness direction against 100% at layer 11 for the refusal direction on Llama3), so the elicitation test cannot separate them — but the inversion task can, and they flip in opposite directions there: steering the harmfulness direction makes the model call a harmless prompt harmful, steering the refusal direction makes it emit a refusal token while still answering "No" to the harm question. In the reverse direction the same asymmetry holds. Harmfulness directions are category-specific (a Hate-Haras-Violence direction has ≈0.6 average cosine to other categories) where refusal directions are nearly interchangeable (≈0.89); cross-category steering on the same test set reaches 100% with the in-domain direction, 32% with Political-Campaigning, 100% with Adult-Content, and 52% with the all-category average. In the jailbreak analysis, persuasion shifts the belief while adversarial suffixes and prompting templates suppress the refusal signal and often leave the belief high. The Latent Guard beats Llama Guard 3 on most of the headline jailbreak cells (persuasion on Qwen2: 75.0 against 17.8; template on Llama2 and Llama3: 100.0 against 76.0 and 50.0) and its belief score on held-out harmful prompts is close to unchanged after adversarial finetuning up to 400 examples, while the model's behaviour has flipped to acceptance.

## Limits and open problems they name
No mechanism: layers, neurons and circuits are explicitly deferred, and the authors frame the work as an existence proof. Only 7B/8B open models, so scaling is untested. The Latent Guard degrades badly out of distribution — on ToxicChat it is below Llama Guard 3 across the board and on the OpenAI moderation set it collapses for Qwen2 and Llama3 (Sexual 21.5, Hate 39.5) until 50 in-domain examples are added, at which point it recovers to Llama Guard's level or above; the authors also concede the headline comparison is not entirely fair, since Llama Guard is a finetuned model trained on broad taxonomies and the guard is a threshold on a statistic with a narrow training pool. The cluster threshold of zero is a default, not a calibrated boundary. The inversion templates had to be hand-adapted per model because smaller models ignore the appended question and answer the original instruction.

## What we take from it
A **method** and a **frame**. The frame is the one worth taking whole: when two candidate explanations share a direction, the discriminating experiment is not "does steering it change behaviour" — both will pass — but a task constructed so that the two hypotheses predict opposite outputs, and the reply-inversion trick (ask the model the question the behaviour is supposed to be about) is a general recipe for building one. The method is that a token position is a design variable of the same rank as a layer: the standard last-token position silently bundles the template's decision stage with the instruction's content, and this paper shows the two carry different properties. For our own predictive model the important half is the negative result inside the positive one — a decodable belief that the *decision does not consult*, with jailbreaks that move the decision and not the belief, and finetuning that rewrites the decision while leaving the belief almost where it was. That is a third instance of the folder's read/write dissociation, and the first where the dissociation is what makes a defence work: the durable object is the one the read-out sees, not the one the output is produced from. The warning to carry is the guard's own failure profile — a statistic that wins on the distribution its clusters were built from loses badly on another harm taxonomy, so an internal read-out priced only against in-domain jailbreaks is not yet a monitor.
