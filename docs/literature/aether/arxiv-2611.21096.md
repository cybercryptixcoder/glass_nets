## What they wanted, and why

The setting is a physical measurement pipeline: an observation is produced by an acquisition operator under unknown scene state and bounded error, so many states are compatible with what was measured. The authors separate two things that are usually conflated — whether a claim is true of the *realised* state, and whether the observation *justifies* the claim, i.e. whether it holds in every state the observation leaves open. Their question is not how to recover the scene but which distinctions between sets of compatible interpretations have to be kept so that no justified conclusion is lost, and which can be merged away. The larger goal is a principled compression of uncertainty that is safe for a specified downstream task: the compression may be aggressive precisely because most physical differences never change a task answer. Two further aims follow: a canonical code that is provably the coarsest possible, and an account of what happens to that guarantee when the compatibility sets are *estimated* by a learner rather than computed from a known model.

## What they studied

No neural network is the object of study; the unit of analysis is a **set of physically compatible states** and, above it, the **set of binary task claims valid on that set**. Experiments use two testbeds. First, a Push-T simulation from diffusion-policy work, rendered under occluded views with partial temporal observation, with **13 registered task claims** (geometry claims about displacement relative to a threshold, and mechanism claims such as whether the dynamics are damped or the reference behaviour is present), over **5,760 historical and 3,040 prospective observation contexts**. Second, real SAR imagery: **1,962 official HRSID test images** with a seven-claim family (three ship-count thresholds, four ship-centre quadrant claims), evaluated under a frozen left-half grayscale observation operator, plus nested image banks of **640 to 3,642** training images. Four frozen candidate-wise learners (ExtraTrees) are compared: symmetric, coverage-weighted, closure-target, and corridor-asymmetric label weighting.

## How they did it

1. Define the compatible-state set as every admissible state whose predicted measurement, under some admissible acquisition configuration, lies within tolerance of the observation.
2. Define a claim as *valid* on a set when it is true at every state in the set — a stronger notion than truth at the generating state.
3. For each claim, define the set of *physically realisable falsifying measurements* and take the claim's falsification margin as the distance from the observation to that set. Under a closedness condition, a claim is valid exactly when no admissible falsifier lies within tolerance. This replaces "does an adversarial state exist" with a distance in measurement space.
4. Certify finite-bank results with a coverage bound: if every physical falsifier lies within a certified distance of some bank falsifier, bank-validity transfers to the full model; without the bound the result is bank-relative only.
5. Order claims by semantic specificity and take the **Exact Inference Frontier** — the maximal valid claims under that order. Its downward closure regenerates all valid claims, and it is the unique smallest generating antichain.
6. Declare two compatibility sets *inference-equivalent* when they support the same valid claims, and build the canonical quotient code from the frontier. Prove that among deterministic encoders with an exact inference decoder, this partition is the coarsest, and that every optimiser induces it up to relabelling of code values.
7. For learned compatibility sets, define a task-relative discrepancy with two asymmetric terms: omitting compatible states can make unjustified claims appear valid; adding states outside the task closure can retract claims that were valid. Derive inclusions — on the one side, no unsupported claim escapes; on the other, every originally valid claim survives — and a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for exact inference preservation.
8. Train the four learners under matched per-image output counts, with no fitting or threshold selection on the prospective splits, and score exact set recovery, exact inference recovery, coverage, normalised unsupported-claim risk, normalised lost-valid-claim risk, and supported specificity.
9. Controls: an oracle built from independent margin and truth-set checks (they agree on bank validity for every observation); a cardinality-only decoder as an ablation on the code's content; single-state deletions and task-closure enlargements as probes of which distinctions matter; a task expansion from 9 to 13 claims; a post-freeze audit reconstructing the HRSID reference signatures from official annotations under four midpoint-assignment conventions.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a model of any network — a **quotient structure**: a partition of possibility sets into classes that are indistinguishable with respect to a declared task, plus a canonical representative per class (the frontier of maximal valid claims). It is exact and provably minimal *for code cardinality only* — the authors state repeatedly that optimality does not concern storage length, representation dimension, or computation. Prediction enters twice: the frontier predicts which claims a *new* observation will justify (decode by regenerating the downward closure), and the falsifier-margin condition predicts validity in the full physical model given a coverage certificate.

## How they judged success

Exact signature recovery of the valid-claim set; quotient cardinality reduction and the optimal in-sample accuracy of a cardinality-only decoder; exact set recovery versus exact inference recovery (the paper's central inequality is that set recovery implies inference recovery but not conversely); normalised unsupported-claim risk and lost-valid-claim risk per OOD regime; supported specificity; a nondominance comparison in the (unsupported, lost) plane; a true-but-unsupported rate normalised over true claim instances; and frontier-stability controls on positive-distance pairs below the finite-bank stability threshold. No statistical testing beyond source-prefix cluster bootstrap confidence intervals on the HRSID claim metrics.

## What they found

- **Ambiguity is the normal case, and it does not destroy justified inference.** Across the 5,760 historical observations, 73.85% admit multiple compatible states, and 49.38% are both ambiguous and support non-trivial valid claims; the prospective regimes give 64.38%/41.00% (geometry), 74.00%/55.75% (mechanism), 65.00%/48.44% (joint). 56.39% of historical contexts have multiple incomparable maximal claims, and sets with the same physical cardinality can carry different frontiers.
- **Truth and justification come apart at a large, measured rate.** Claims true at the generating state but unsupported by the observation run at **37.13%** historically and 31.04%/28.16%/28.42% under the three prospective shifts. The authors insist this is not classifier error: another compatible state falsifies the claim.
- **Compression is large and provably minimal.** 145 distinct compatibility sets collapse to **31** quotient codes (78.62% fewer required code values); geometry-OOD 33.33%, mechanism-OOD 54.55%, joint-OOD 28.00%. Direct inference, quotient decoding and independent frontier reconstruction agree on all 8,800 observations. The task closure strictly enlarges 129 of 145 sets while preserving every valid claim; of 1,036 single-state deletions, **941** preserve exact inference and **95** introduce an unsupported claim.
- **Ablations that matter to us.** Cardinality alone is not enough: the best in-sample cardinality-only decoder reaches **47.85%** exact-signature accuracy historically (41.12%/59.50%/36.25% under the three shifts) — better than chance in two regimes, so the naive control is not trivial, but far below the frontier code. Growing the task from 9 to 13 claims refines the historical quotient from 13 to 31 codes: adding claims splits equivalence classes and never merges them.
- **Learned compatibility sets: set accuracy and inference accuracy are separable.** Exact inference recovery 92.00–96.00% (geometry-OOD), 93.88–94.50% (mechanism), 92.81–93.75% (joint). For corridor-asymmetric learning, **89** observations have inexact set recovery but exact inference recovery, 14 of them retaining at least one non-trivial valid claim. Closure-target has the lowest geometry-OOD unsupported risk (0.00115) and the highest lost-valid risk (0.012404): the two errors trade, and the asymmetry in the objective chooses a point on that trade rather than escaping it. Corridor-asymmetric is nondominated under geometry- and mechanism-OOD but **dominated under joint-OOD**.
- **Real SAR.** 1,913 distinct 3-NN candidate sets induce only **36** inference signatures under the seven-claim family; visual 3-NN scores **87.31%** claim precision, 0.599 supported claims per image, 23.55% exact-signature recovery at 1,347 issued claims per method, with supported-claim gains of 0.2064 over ridge and 0.2426 over prevalence. The authors state these reflect candidate-set construction, not quotient coding. In the nested-bank development experiment, 9 of 22 anchors retaining a claim keep it in the expanded bank and 13 lose it; compatibility expansion preserves a claim where no new falsifier enters and invalidates it where one appears.

## Limits and open problems they name

Everything experimental is **bank-relative**: the proofs hold for the full physical state space under stated assumptions, but exact validity or quotient preservation on a finite registered bank does not extend to unregistered states or observation operators without a coverage certificate, which they supply only as a sufficient condition. Optimality is cardinality-only; they explicitly disclaim storage, dimension and compute. Learned-set reliability is shown under shift but not guaranteed; the coverage inequality holds on any evaluation distribution but does not promise high coverage under shift. The task closure is the largest state subset preserving a signature but need not be realisable as an observation-compatible set. The empty-compatibility case was excluded from the prospective analysis (no sample invoked abstention), so the abstention path is described but not exercised. No seeds, repeats or variance reporting on the simulated experiments; the corridor learner's objective is a surrogate for their discrepancy, not its minimisation.

## What we take from it

- **A quotient is the right shape for a predictive white-box model's output, and this paper gives the optimality proof we would otherwise have to sketch.** If our battery reads a state and reports *sets* of claims, the honest object is the partition by valid-claim signature, not the state vector: the paper's own numbers say 1,913 candidate sets are only 36 distinct answers. Reporting a code value plus its frontier is a claim about what any compatible state must satisfy, which is exactly "prediction under intervention" queried in the other direction.
- **The control we should adopt immediately: the cardinality-only baseline at 47.85%.** Any compression of a possibility set that we ever report (probe top-k, activation clusters, a few candidate completions) should be scored against a decoder that sees only set size. It is a stronger and more diagnostic control than chance, and it is cheap.
- **Two asymmetric error rates instead of one accuracy.** Unsupported-claim risk and lost-valid-claim risk are the right pair for any learned set-valued readout — an over-inclusive probe invents structure, an under-inclusive one retracts it — and the paper shows the nondominated frontier between them is method- and regime-dependent (corridor wins two regimes and loses the third). Report both, per regime.
- **The "exact inference despite inexact recovery" relaxation is the licence for small, noisy predictors.** 89 cases where the set is wrong and every claim is still right is the argument that we do not need a perfect probe, only one that errs inside the task closure; the corollary is that the closure, not the probe's accuracy, is the thing to measure.
- **The true-but-unsupported rate (37.13%) is the sharpest available framing of decodability versus justification**, and it lands on the folder's standing theme from the other side: a probe that reads a state does not thereby license a claim about it, because other compatible states may falsify the claim. Pair this with the co-occurrence-prior entry, where the model's internal readout was right and the answer wrong.
- **A warning about our own claims:** the single-state deletion result — 941 of 1,036 deletions harmless, 95 fatal — is the cleanest demonstration that "removing component X changed nothing" is uninformative without a scan of *which* components can be removed, and that the interesting number is the small fatal subset, not the large harmless one.
