# Understanding Jailbreak Success: A Study of Latent Space Dynamics in Large Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2406.09289
- **Authors / organisation:** Sarah Ball (Department of Statistics, LMU Munich; Munich Center for Machine Learning), Frauke Kreuter (same, plus JPSM, University of Maryland), Nina Panickssery (Anthropic)
- **Date:** 2024-10 (v2; v1 2024-06-13)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09289
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Safety training teaches a chat model to refuse harmful requests, yet a variety of prompt-level tricks still get compliance out of it, and nobody knows whether those tricks share a cause. The paper asks a mechanistic question rather than a defensive one: if you build a direction that represents "this prompt is jailbroken" for one family of tricks, does it also describe the other families? If yes, the many tricks are variations on one internal manoeuvre, and a single intervention could be aimed at all of them. A secondary question follows from a common hypothesis in the red-teaming literature: do jailbreaks work by lowering the model's perception that the prompt is harmful? The larger goal is a predictive account of jailbreak success, in which a handful of measured internal quantities explains which prompts the network will comply with.

## What they studied
Four chat models spanning three families and two sizes: Vicuna 13B v1.5, Vicuna 7B v1.5, Qwen1.5 14B Chat and MPT 7B Chat, each with its own system prompt and greedy decoding. 25 jailbreak types and 352 harmful prompts, built mostly from one prior taxonomy and extended with Italian-language variants, payload splitting, an obfuscation variant and two GCG suffixes. The unit of analysis is a **direction in the residual stream at a single layer** (the middle layer: 16 for the 7B models, 20 for the 13B/14B models), read at the last token of the instruction. Two kinds of direction are used: a per-jailbreak-type vector, and a harmfulness vector contrasting harmful with ChatGPT-rewritten harmless questions.

## How they did it
Three stages. First, a PCA on per-example activation differences — the prompt with the jailbreak minus the same prompt without it, at the last instruction token, middle layer — to see whether jailbreak families occupy separable regions. Second, per-type vectors by the mean of those differences over each type's examples, normalised to equal norm, and compared (a) geometrically by pairwise cosine and (b) behaviourally by transferring them: take a type's successful jailbreaks, subtract another type's vector (multiplier −1) from the residual stream at every token during generation, and score whether the jailbreak still succeeds. A same-norm random vector is the control, 20 successful examples per type, ASR judged by Llama Guard 2 and Llama 3 with the higher score taken. Third, the same vectors are *added* (multiplier +1) to harmful questions the model otherwise refuses, testing whether a jailbreak can be induced by a direction. Harmfulness analysis projects token-level activations onto the harmfulness direction and tracks the cosine along the instruction and the response, comparing jailbroken prompts to the no-jailbreak baseline; an alternative harmfulness direction averaged over all instruction tokens is run as a robustness check, and a helpfulness direction (answer-versus-refusal on harmless rewritten questions, averaged over the whole response) is built for one model as a preliminary look at the helpful/harmless trade-off.

## What counts as an explanation here
A shared **steering vector** plus one proposed mediating mechanism. The vector is meant to be partial and causal — it is a direction whose subtraction restores refusal, and the paper's claim is that its cross-type transferability licenses "these jailbreaks share a common component". The mechanism claim (harmfulness-feature suppression) is explicitly correlational: the authors measure a reduction in the prompt's cosine with the harmfulness direction and are careful that a correlation without a monotone relationship to attack success is not yet a cause.

## How they judged success
Attack success rate per jailbreak type, judged by two LLM judges with a documented prompt and threshold (score above four on a 1–5 scale). For intervention: ASR after steering, reported per type with standard deviations, always beside the random-vector control — and the paper adds a qualitative check (random steering often produces truncated or incoherent output rather than refusals, so identical ASR can mean different things). For the geometric claim: pairwise cosine between vectors. For the mechanism: cosine with the harmfulness direction at the end-of-instruction token, averaged across a type's successful examples, against the no-jailbreak baseline. No statistical tests, no intervals, no seed variation.

## What they found
Per-type vectors are all positively aligned with one another, mostly cosine 0.4–0.6, with the Wikipedia-framing tricks the least similar and the two larger models the most tightly clustered. Subtraction transfers: on previously successful jailbreaks, ASR falls from a 100% baseline to near zero for most vector/type pairs and to single digits otherwise — for example roughly 0.0–4% for Qwen 14B across nearly all pairs, 0.0–3% for most Vicuna 7B pairs, and up to the low teens on Vicuna 13B and MPT 7B for the weaker vectors, against 56–71% for the random control (MPT's random control is the weakest at 71%). Transfer holds from a version of a "divergent style" family onto GCG suffixes and payload splits, which have no human-readable content in common. Addition also works: adding a type's vector to plain harmful questions lifts ASR into the 27–93% range depending on pair, against 0–19% for the random vector. The clustering picture is more mixed: PCA separates types cleanly and, on the Vicuna models, groups them the way a human would (style tricks together, persona tricks together, fiction tricks together), so geometry partly recovers semantics even though behavioural transfer does not respect it. Harmfulness projection is reduced at end of instruction for most effective jailbreaks in three of four models, strongest on Vicuna 13B and weakest on MPT 7B, but the magnitude does not track ASR — the largest reductions come from distractors and poems, which are not the most effective, and Qwen's evil-confidant trick barely reduces it while succeeding 96.9% of the time.

## Limits and open problems they name
The whole study is correlational and the authors say so: no causal test that suppressing the harmfulness feature produces the jailbreak, only that it co-occurs. Grid coverage is uneven — for MPT 7B two types were excluded for low measured ASR and the GCG suffix was not optimised further for compute reasons, so the weakest model's negative results are the least trustworthy. Multi-turn and many-shot jailbreaks are absent by construction. The helpfulness direction analysis is called preliminary and reaches no conclusion about which objective wins. Manual inspection was used to reject some high-ASR cases as non-jailbreaks, a filter that is applied selectively per model and not reported in detail.

## What we take from it
Three things, one of them a warning. **A method:** cross-family direction transfer is a cheap causal test of "same mechanism or different" that needs no circuit and no training — fit a mean-difference direction on behaviour class A, subtract it inside class B's forward pass, and see whether B's behaviour changes; the cost is one forward pass per pair. It is the ancestor of what later work in this folder does with refusal directions and concept cones. **A measure:** an intervention's ASR must be printed next to a same-norm random-vector control *and* a residue-quality check, because here the random vector's "success" at reducing ASR is partly the model emitting garbage — the paper's own note that a random-vector ASR of zero can mean the output was empty is the cheapest possible genre of artefact to inherit. **A warning:** cosine between two behaviour vectors of 0.4–0.6 coexists with semantic clustering that the behaviour does not respect, so high cross-type similarity is not by itself evidence of a shared mechanism; the authors lean on transfer, and the transfer is what should be believed. The mechanism side also gives us the calibration lesson — a direction can be suppressed without the suppression explaining the behaviour, so a mechanism claim needs the dose-response over types, and here the dose-response is absent.
