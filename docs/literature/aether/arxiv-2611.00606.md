## What they wanted, and why

Attribution graphs built on sparse-autoencoder features are the field's standard object for claiming that one feature's activation causes another's: an edge holds the derivative of a downstream feature's preactivation with respect to an upstream feature's code, and the graph is used to predict what ablating a feature does. The author observes that a TopK dictionary is a *rule*, not a linear map — it keeps the k largest preactivations at each token — so the graph's first-order expansion is only valid while the active set it was built on stays the active set. The question is therefore arithmetic before it is empirical: how often does an intervention move a downstream feature across the cutoff, and does the answer change if the graph is allowed to re-rank? The larger goal is a graph whose error is bounded and auditable rather than assumed small.

## What they studied

Two adjacent layers' dictionaries at a time — five public TopK pairs over three Pythia models (pythia-70m at layers 1→2, 3→4 and 4→5; pythia-160m at 6→7, all width 32,768 keeping 16 features), one ReLU-dictionary release on the same model family, and a purpose-trained testbed ("cell F", pythia-70m layers 3→4, width 32,768, k = 32, eight runs of six independent seeds plus a retraining pair sharing one initialisation). The unit of analysis is the **operator between two dictionaries' codes** — the derivative matrix of downstream preactivations with respect to upstream codes (the "virtual weights" of the attributed-graph literature) — plus each feature's **margin**, its signed distance to the cutoff. Models are small on purpose: the largest is pythia-160m.

## How they did it

Encode each dictionary in one gauge (unit decoder columns in residual-stream units), which fixes the units of codes, preactivations, cutoffs and write norms. Take one position per 2,048-token sequence of the Pile validation file, and at each of 512 positions write on every live upstream feature, setting its code to a dose times its clean value (dose 0 is a zero ablation). Measure how often a downstream feature crosses the cutoff into or out of the live support, and how much of the response's energy those crossing coordinates carry. Then define the **margin graph**: compute the first-order change of *every* downstream preactivation with one Jacobian-vector product per upstream feature — the same column the standard graph already pays for — add it to all preactivations, and apply the TopK rule to the predicted vector, so features can enter and leave. Score both graphs by a relative error on the downstream code, pooled over the three doses fixed in advance. Theorem 2 turns the remaining first-order error into a band: estimate a Lipschitz constant along each write from Jacobians at its base, midpoint and endpoint, and accept a predicted membership only where its margin clears the neighbouring reaches plus the error bound; the rule is therefore an audit *after* the write, and it is calibrated against a write-norm regime whose boundary is set on half the positions and scored on the other. Two literature corrections — integrated gradients and an exactly recomputed attention softmax — are re-run on the fixed support and on the re-ranked support. Finally, for the cross-seed claim, each atom of one seed's dictionary is written as a sparse non-negative combination of another's atoms by non-negative orthogonal matching pursuit, and those maps carry one seed's operator to the other's features, masked and unmasked.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **graph over dictionary features with signed edges, plus a defined validity region and an auditable band**. The object is partial and explicitly local: one layer pair, first order, one token's position, and licensed only within a write-norm regime. It predicts — it names which downstream features enter and leave the support under an intervention the graph itself was not built on — and it also predicts its own error, because Theorem 2's band says which of its memberships survive the first-order remainder. Nothing here claims to explain what a feature means; the explanation is of an intervention's consequence.

## How they judged success

Median relative error on the downstream code change versus the fixed-support graph, at doses 0, 0.5 and 0.9 fixed before any predictor was evaluated; a 2,000-draw bootstrap over the 512 positions for the difference of the two medians and for each interval; precision and recall on the entering and leaving sets, micro-averaged, against a margin-only rule, an edge-only rule, a uniform-draw null and an exact oracle; the same comparison against integrated gradients at 5 and 10 products and against the recomputed-softmax device; acceptance-coverage and zero-wrong-membership for the band audit; a fitted two-layer MLP on the same six inputs as a test of whether a learner reaches the margin graph's function; and for transport, explained fraction of the reference operator's energy against one-to-one matching, 20 random maps of matched sparsity and 200 permutation-null draws.

## What they found

**Crossings are the normal case.** A single zero ablation moves a downstream feature across the cutoff in 0.74–0.89 of cases on the five public TopK pairs (0.84 on pythia-70m layers 3–4) and 0.92 on cell F (runs 0.91–0.92). The rate follows the write against the margins: it falls to 0.19–0.25 at dose 0.9 and is zero at dose 1, then rises to 0.95–0.99 at dose 4. Over crossing zero ablations, the entering and leaving coordinates carry 0.73–0.84 of the response's energy on the five pairs (0.79 on cell F) and the entering ones alone 0.33–0.39; a crossing coordinate's code moves 5.7–10.1 times as far as its preactivation. The ReLU pair crosses its zero in 0.69 of cases — continuity gives no protection — but its crossing coordinates carry only 0.05 of the energy and its code moves 0.43 times its preactivation, so the effect is a property of the jump at a TopK cutoff rather than of crossing at all.

**Re-ranking fixes it.** On the five public TopK pairs the margin graph's median error is 0.013–0.033 against 0.247–0.669 for the fixed support graph; on cell F it is 0.017 against 0.786, and the bootstrap puts the difference above zero on every pair. Removing only the re-ranking, so membership is decided by the old cutoff, raises the error on cell F to 0.558 — so the gain is the re-ranking, not the extra rows. Entering-set precision 0.80–0.90 and recall 0.82–0.92; ranking inactive features by predicted preactivation at the true entrant count reaches 0.84–0.93 precision where ranking by margin alone reaches 0.42–0.49. On the ReLU pair both graphs err little (0.008 and 0.017).

**The re-ranked softmax device is at least as good.** Re-ranked, the recomputed-attention device errs 0.011–0.026 on the five pairs, under the margin graph on all five, and 0.013 on cell F under the margin graph's 0.017, at one product; on the fixed support it errs 0.19–0.66. Integrated gradients on the fixed support stays near 0.78 at 5 and 10 products. The author's attribution of the pattern is the honest one: what both winners share is the re-ranking, so the re-ranking is doing most of the work a "correction" is credited with.

**Subsets and the regime.** Built once, the margin graph errs 0.287 on cell F's two-feature subsets against 0.770 for the fixed support, and 0.458 against 0.551 on sixteen-feature subsets; on the pythia-70m release at sixteen features it is *worse* than the fixed support (0.670 against 0.631), which is the boundary of the claim. The first-order error reaches 0.3 at a write of 5.55 cutoff units on cell F and 3.90 on the 3–4 release, boundaries of 7.26 and 4.36 pooled (ratio 1.66, inside the factor of 2.0 fixed in advance), while the median write is 1.68 and 3.57 units. The band audit accepts 0.59–0.87 of a single write's predicted memberships at the zero ablation (0.21–0.71 coverage at zero, 0.97–1.00 at dose 0.9) with **no wrong membership among 668,393,327**, and 0.012–0.067 of a subset write's.

**A fitted learner does not reach it.** An MLP given exactly the margin graph's six inputs — the preactivation, its first-order change, and the two order statistics before and after the write — has a median error of 2.23–2.45, and one given only preactivation and first-order change 1.44–1.53, both worse than predicting no change (1.0), against 0.016 for the margin graph. The rule pre-registered for this fired.

**The readout.** Over crossing interventions the entering-and-leaving part of the decoded code change has a median norm 1.24–1.72 times the activation's own change (1.56 on cell F) but projects at only 0.21–0.29 of it, and the whole decoded change has 1.41–1.82 times the activation's norm at a median cosine of 0.27–0.50 — so the dictionary's response is larger than the model's change and only loosely aligned with it. Carrying a decoded middle code through the next block fails at the third layer (0.884 fixed support, 0.999 margin, 0.996 exact) while carrying the *residual stream* through both blocks in one product per live feature errs 0.053, and the exact activation-change chain 0.029; on the one triple studied, the residual stream is the carrier.

**Cross-seed transport.** Sparse non-negative maps explain 0.57 of the operator's energy between seeds against 0.22 for a one-to-one atom matching (gain 0.35 over a pre-registered bound of 0.19), beating all 20 random maps and all 200 permutation-null draws on all 30 pairs; on the masked code Jacobian they explain 0.47 and with the whole target dictionary available 0.85. The 0.38 gap splits exactly into the mask term's own energy (0.34) plus twice its inner product with the representation residual (0.05), and admitting the target's inactive atoms nearest the cutoff recovers 0.73 of that gap at a 10% admission share where a random admission recovers 0.11 — so what survives a seed change is the operator minus a small set of near-cutoff atoms. Maps recombine rather than copy: 0.71 of upstream decoder columns are represented at a median of 5 atoms, 0.24 of encoder rows, and 0.29 / 0.76 of them are unreachable at the tolerance.

## Limits and open problems they name

No JumpReLU dictionary was studied (the only public release with adjacent residual layers sits behind an access agreement the study lacks), and no transcoder. The largest model is pythia-160m, so the write regime, the readout and the transport are all untested at scale. The transport is measured on one cell and its retraining pair because no public release carries a second seed. Integrated gradients were evaluated on cell F alone. No runtime or memory was measured; cost is counted in Jacobian-vector products. The band is an audit after the write, not a certificate before it, and its constant is a lower bound on the segment's own.

## What we take from it

- **A warning, and the sharpest one in the folder for graph-shaped explanation.** Any first-order claim about a component's effect whose downstream read-out is a *selection rule* — TopK, a threshold, an argmax, a router — is evaluated on a support the intervention perturbs. Print the crossing rate before trusting the graph: it costs one extra forward pass per intervention and here it is 0.74–0.92.
- **A method, essentially free.** Re-rank the predicted vector through the read-out rule instead of keeping the clean support. The paper's own ablation shows this one change accounts for nearly the whole gain (0.017 → 0.558 without it), and it adds no gradient products because the columns were already computed.
- **A measure: pre-registered thresholds with the outcome printed beside them.** Table 1 lists 22 rules, each with what it reads, the threshold it fires at, what the measurement returned and whether it fired; six fired, including three that fire *against* the paper's own headline (the once-built graph losing on large subsets, a fitted learner failing, the residual chain passing). This is the cheapest honest-reporting device the folder has seen and it generalises directly to our battery.
- **A control we can copy for any "does the graph predict the intervention" claim:** compare against a high-capacity learner given the same inputs, and against predicting no change. Both regressors here scored above 1.0, i.e. worse than the trivial predictor, which is what makes "the margin graph is a specific function, not just a fitted one" a measurement.
- **A quantity to report with any stability claim:** the operator's explained fraction, not only per-feature cosine. Cosine-per-feature matching at 0.9 leaves the operator at 0.22; sparse maps reach 0.57. A per-feature score is a statement about directions, and the graph's object is an operator.
- **A bound we can state and check.** The decoded response is larger than the model's own change (1.24–1.72× the norm) but points at only a fifth to a third of it, and the reconstruction residual supplies the difference. Any of our read-outs that compares a decoded effect with an activation effect should print both the norm ratio and the projection share, because the two say opposite things here.
