# Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear

- **Key:** arxiv-2405.14860
- **Authors / organisation:** Joshua Engels, Eric J. Michaud, Isaac Liao, Wes Gurnee, Max Tegmark — MIT for Engels, Liao and Gurnee; MIT & IAIFI for Michaud and Tegmark
- **Date:** 2024-05-23 (v1); last revised 2025-02-27 (v3)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14860
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v3 HTML, including Appendices A–K)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The linear representation hypothesis (LRH) had been used in two senses — that concepts live on one-dimensional lines, and that hidden states are sparse sums of such lines. The authors keep the second sense and attack the first: some concepts may be irreducibly spread across a low-dimensional subspace, and a dictionary of one-dimensional directions then cannot describe them without splitting them. The larger goal is the field's stated end point — recovering the "variables" a model actually computes over so that circuits can eventually be written as verifiable programs — and the paper's claim is that this fails at the first step if the variables themselves are the wrong shape. They therefore want a definition of "genuinely multi-dimensional", an unsupervised way to find such objects in a real model, and causal evidence that a model uses one.

## What they studied
GPT-2-small (SAE for every layer, trained elsewhere, roughly 25k dictionary elements at layer 7) and Mistral 7B (their own SAEs at layers 8, 16 and 24 of 32, 16× expansion, 65,536 dictionary elements each), with Llama 3 8B used only for the causal experiments. The unit of analysis moves between levels: first clusters of SAE dictionary elements (i.e. subspaces), then two- and three-dimensional point clouds reconstructed from a cluster, then a hand-built circular probe on a PCA subspace, then the residual stream at specific token positions across named tasks.

## How they did it
Two stages. **Stage one — definition and search.** A feature is a function from inputs to a low-dimensional vector; it is *reducible* if some rotation and translation splits it into parts whose joint distribution is either separable (independent) or a mixture (one part zero whenever the other is active); otherwise it is irreducible. Because those conditions are never exactly met, they are relaxed into two measurable scores — a separability index (the smallest mutual information achievable over rotations, estimated on a 40×40 histogram over a 1000-angle grid) and an ε-mixture index (the largest fraction of the point cloud that some linear band can hold within ε = 0.1, found by gradient descent with a soft-thresholded objective and annealed temperature). Search itself is cheap: build a cosine-similarity graph over dictionary elements, cluster it (spectral clustering with 1,000 clusters for GPT-2; k-nearest-neighbour graph with k = 2, then edge pruning at τ = 0.5, connected components for Mistral, giving roughly 2,700 clusters), then for each cluster re-run the SAE on a large corpus, keep only points that fire some element of the cluster, reconstruct with all other latents zeroed, and score the PCA planes of that reconstruction. They first check the pipeline on synthetic data: activations drawn from one or two orthogonal circular planes in 10 dimensions, where the clustering and reconstruction recover the planted circles. **Stage two — causal tests.** Two tasks: "day of the week arithmetic" (7 starts × 7 durations = 49 prompts) and "month arithmetic" (12 × 12 = 144), posed as natural sentences rather than modular notation. They fit a linear map from the top 5 PCA components of the α-token hidden state to a point on a unit circle at the token's angular position, then replace the model's projection onto that subspace with the circle point of a different α while mean-ablating the rest of the activation. Baselines are the same replacement over the top 5 PCA components taken from a clean run, replacement of the whole layer from a clean run, and replacement of the whole layer with the task average; the metric is the average logit difference between the original correct answer and the counterfactual one, over 49×6 and 144×11 prompt pairs. They also sweep a grid of (radius, angle) positions inside the circle to see whether the angle alone drives the answer, re-run the probe fitted in the plane found by SAE clustering (the "SAE probe") and test its layer-to-layer robustness, and probe continuity with prompt templates that place a phrase at an intermediate position between two weekdays. A regression-based residual technique (subtract what one-hot functions of α and β can linearly explain, then look at the PCA of what remains) is used to find the representation of the answer token.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **geometric account**: a stated hypothesis about the shape of the representation (a one- or two-dimensional manifold, usually a circle) plus a subspace that carries it, plus a definition that says when a shape counts as primitive rather than composite. The irreducibility scores make the claim statistical and gradable; the intervention makes it causal, but only in the sufficiency direction — patching the circle to another label and getting the other label's answer, with the whole layer as ceiling and top-5-PCA patching as floor. It is deliberately not a circuit: the paper uses patching and the regression residuals to sketch where the answer is computed (MLPs on the two operand tokens, a copy to the position before the answer, more MLPs there), but the account predicts nothing beyond the subspace it names, and the authors say outright that they do not know why they did not find more interpretable multi-dimensional features.

## How they judged success
Three instruments, no held-out benchmark. (1) Irreducibility scores of real clusters against synthetic reducible and irreducible clouds, plus each cluster's rank among the 1,000 (GPT-2) clusters by the product of separability and one-minus-mixture index — the day, month and year clusters rank 9, 28 and 15 under that product but 8, 105 and 12 under an alternative rank-by-both-lists scheme. (2) Intervention effect as average logit difference, with 96% error bars from standard errors across prompts, compared against clean-run subspace patching, whole-layer patching and whole-layer mean ablation, on all layers of both models. (3) Task accuracy by highest valid-token logit: Llama 3 8B scores 29/49 on weekdays and 143/144 on months, Mistral 7B 31/49 and 125/144, GPT-2 8/49 and 10/144 — the last being the paper's own negative control, since GPT-2 has the circles but not the behaviour. Cluster robustness is reported as a Jaccard overlap of the found cluster as the graph parameters move.

## What they found
Circles exist and are used. GPT-2 layer 7 and Mistral 7B layers 8, 16 and 24 contain clusters whose PCA planes arrange the weekday, month and twentieth-century-year tokens around a circle in calendar order; the useful plane is usually components 2 and 3 because component 1 is an intensity/radius direction, so the object is arguably a cone rather than a ring. Mistral's picture is richer than GPT-2's: an extra "weekend" region sits between Saturday and Sunday, and season tokens like "winter" fall between the December and January positions. On the tasks, the circle is causal. Early-layer subspace patching on the circular subspace comes close to whole-layer patching and beats clean-run top-5-PCA patching, with effects decaying later once the α value has been copied forward (around layers 15-17); sweeping radius and angle shows the answer token is indexed by angle, with radius acting as an intensity knob; the SAE-plane probe costs little on its own layer (average logit difference −2.01 against −2.58 for the PCA-fitted probe) and travels across layers far better than the PCA-fitted probe trained on the same layer (−2.32 at layer 6 against 0.029). Representations are continuous in a weak sense: "very early Monday" and "very late Monday" land between Monday and the neighbouring weekdays. After regressing out one-hot α and β, a clear circle appears for the answer token γ in late layers, suggesting the model lands the answer by iterated rotation rather than by lookup. On plain notation ("5 + 3 mod 7"), both large models are near chance — the circle is bound to the natural-language framing, not to the arithmetic.

## Limits and open problems they name
The definitions are purely statistical, not intervention-based, and had to be relaxed into subjective degrees; the alternative causal definition (a subspace is reducible if separate computations on its halves compose to the same answer) is proposed in an appendix but not used, because it needs a task set. Only circles of period 7 and 12 were found despite thousands of clusters inspected — the authors cannot say whether that is a limit of the clustering, of two-dimensionality as a hypothesis, of the SAEs, or of the models. The search is human-in-the-loop: 500 GPT-2 and about 2,000 Mistral reconstruction plots were inspected by eye, and the paper notes that some of the highest-scoring clusters remain unexplained. The Mistral clustering is a hand-rolled graph method whose quality is admitted to be improvable, and its behaviour changes qualitatively with the prune threshold (everything merges when τ is small, everything fragments when it is large). Causality is shown for sufficiency on two small, templated task families, with the whole-layer patch as the only ceiling; there is no test of whether removing the circle while leaving everything else loses the behaviour — the mean-ablation of the complement is a substitute for that, not the same test. One model's circular representation does not guarantee the behaviour (GPT-2), and the largest models tested are 8B with no scaling study.

## What we take from it
- **Framing.** "Feature" as a low-dimensional object, not a direction, is the right vocabulary for a white-box account, and the reducible/irreducible distinction gives us a principled reason to refuse a decomposition that merely reshuffles a subspace. For us the operational version is: when our account of a layer splits a phenomenon into several units, test whether the split is *independent* or *non-co-occurring* — those are the two ways a decomposition is fake — and report the score rather than the verdict.
- **Method (reusable, cheap).** Cluster the dictionary by cosine similarity, reconstruct each cluster alone with everything else zeroed, and score the PCA planes. This costs a clustering plus one forward pass per cluster and requires no new training, so it transfers directly to any account we build; the "SAE plane beats a per-layer probe under layer shift" result is the strongest argument that the unsupervised route is finding something more invariant than a fitted probe.
- **Measure.** Two scalars are enough to make a shape claim auditable: a separability index and an ε-mixture index. Both are model-free distribution statistics on a point cloud, so we can apply them to any subspace we claim is primitive.
- **Warning, two-sided.** (i) The paper's own negative control: representation present, behaviour absent (GPT-2). Finding the geometry is not evidence of use. (ii) Its own admission: definitions that are statistical rather than interventional produce a *degree*, and the authors had to relax a crisp property into a score — exactly the failure mode to watch for when we turn a crisp claim ("this is the variable") into a metric.
- **Prediction under intervention.** The radius/angle sweep is the pattern worth copying: rather than patching in a known-correct state, sample the entire interior of the claimed variable and check that the model's read-out is a monotone function of the coordinate the account says matters. That is a genuine functional test of the account, and it is what makes "the angle encodes the day" stronger than "patching the day changes the answer".
- **Cheap trick worth stealing.** Explanation-via-regression: regress the activations on one-hot encodings of the inputs you already understand, then look at what is left. The answer circle only became visible after the operand representations were linearly removed. For us this is a completeness probe at zero training cost and it pairs naturally with the residual-predictability instrument in `arxiv-2410.14670`.
