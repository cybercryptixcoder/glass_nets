# CircuitTree: Composing Circuits into A Syllable Transcriber

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.12520
- **Authors / organisation:** Jiankun Wei, Ewan Dunbar, Gerald Penn — University of Toronto (from the printed address line, which reads "123 Univerity of Toronto"; single affiliation for all three authors, no departmental or postal detail printed).
- **Date:** 2026-10-07
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12520
- **Kind:** paper (method + empirical composition study)
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Circuit discovery has been sold on a modularity promise: decompose a monolithic network into small pieces, then recombine the pieces like building blocks so that a new task does not need a new search from scratch. The authors think that promise rests on thin evidence — earlier composition work stopped at a hand-verified pair, or at a handful of circuits from a toy Tracr model, and judged success only by whether the composed module still did the tasks its parts were pruned for. They ask two sharper questions: does composition survive past a couple of circuits, and can a composed set compute something **none** of its constituents was individually pruned for? The setting is speech rather than text, with phoneme identity as the elementary capability and syllable transcription as the capability that is supposed to emerge.

## What they studied
HuBERT-base, frozen, with a frozen mean-pooled linear head; the probes are 39 binary phoneme circuits (15 vowels, 24 consonants) pruned with **DiscoGP**, a method that prunes both weights and edges. The unit of analysis is therefore a **circuit as a set of binary masks**, in two flavours: weight circuits over 144 logit tensors (12 layers × {Q, K, V, O, MLP-in, MLP-out} × {weight, bias}) and edge circuits over 49 logit tensors, one per downstream node holding a logit per eligible upstream head or MLP. Data is the Articulatory Index corpus: 10,329 consonant-vowel and vowel-consonant monosyllables from 20 English speakers, with per-phoneme train/validation/test splits. A separately pruned DiscoGP circuit serves as the syllable-type gate at the top of the pipeline.

## How they did it
Pair first, then generalise. For two phonemes they turn two binary classifiers into a three-way one (phoneme i, phoneme j, other) and compare three strategies: running the two circuits independently, merging them by element-wise mask union, and their own merge. Their diagnosis of why naive union fails names two mechanisms. **Density domination**: the constituent circuits differ wildly in density (2–3 per cent of entries for some phonemes against 15–20 per cent for others), so the union behaves like the dense circuit and swallows the sparse one; their fix is to rescale every circuit's mask at every key by a factor equal to the geometric mean of the N densities at that key divided by that circuit's own density, clipped into a fixed range. **Interference**: a weight or edge may be essential to one phoneme and harmful to another, and because the objects are binary masks the weight-averaging tricks from model merging do not transfer. Their answer is a greedy acceptance walk: sort the keys topologically, and for each key in turn take the union of both masks only if every member's binary F1 measured on the merged model stays within a small absolute tolerance of its unmerged score; otherwise leave that key private. For many circuits they add a merge **schedule**. The obvious scheme adds one phoneme at a time into an ever-growing cluster, which re-validates every existing member on every key at every step and costs quadratic work in the number of circuits; the balanced scheme merges singletons into pairs, pairs into fours, and so on, so every round costs the same number of forward passes and the total is N log N. A third schedule merges the two most similar remaining clusters first (Kruskal-style, using mask overlap as distance) and serves as an upper bound. The schedule choice is stored in a **CircuitTree**: a binary tree whose leaves are the original per-phoneme circuits and whose internal nodes record only which keys were successfully shared among their children — a member's effective mask for a key is resolved by walking down from the root and taking the first level where that key was shared, falling back to its own leaf otherwise, with scale factors accumulated along the same walk. Finally the vowel and consonant trees are joined into a two-stage pipeline with the order-detecting gate in front, and the whole thing is scored on consonant-vowel and vowel-consonant syllable transcription, a task nothing was pruned or optimised for.

## What counts as an explanation here
The account is a **decomposition plus a composition operator**: circuits are the parts, and the merge procedure — density scaling, greedy tolerance-gated sharing, a merge order, and the tree that records which sharing was validated where — is the theory of how parts assemble. The claim is deliberately partial and operational rather than semantic: nothing is said about what any mask *means*, only that a structurally specified union of masks preserves each member's behaviour within a tolerance and, in aggregate, produces a capability none of the parts had. It generates predictions of a specific kind — about accuracy loss under composition, about merge order, and about how much circuitry ends up shared — and those predictions are what get measured.

## How they judged success
Binary F1 per phoneme before and after merging, against an absolute tolerance (0.02 F1) as the accept criterion inside the greedy walk; macro-F1 heatmaps over all 15 vowel pairs for the three pairwise strategies; N-way vowel accuracy for each merge schedule with post-merge circuit density reported beside it; the full frozen HuBERT model as a reference point at full density; and, for the central claim, syllable transcription accuracy against a ceiling defined by running the unmerged circuits independently, with merge ratio — the fraction of keys shared — reported both partially and across all phonemes. Accuracy and sharing are printed as a pair, so a merge that buys sharing by degrading accuracy is visible.

## What they found
Naive union loses substantially to independently run classifiers across all vowel pairs, and the authors attribute the loss to the two mechanisms they named; their merged pair recovers performance comparable to the unmerged case while sharing weight and edge masks. On the 15-way vowel merge, the unbalanced linear schedule degrades accuracy to 77.9 per cent with post-merge density 1.11 per cent, the balanced schedule reaches 81.8 per cent at density 1.10 per cent, Kruskal merging reaches 82.2 per cent at 1.10 per cent, and the unmerged reference is 82.3 per cent at 1.13 per cent; the frozen full HuBERT head sits far above all of these at 86.4 per cent and 100 per cent density, so merging is being judged against the unmerged circuits, not against the whole model. The balanced schedule therefore matches a similarity-aware upper bound at N log N cost rather than quadratic. The headline result is the pipeline: 64.8 per cent syllable transcription against an unmerged ceiling of 66.0 per cent — a 1.2-point gap — while 99.5 per cent of circuit keys are partially shared and 30.3 per cent are shared across all phonemes. So the transcribing capability is assembled out of circuitry that is overwhelmingly common property rather than one private copy per phoneme, and the composition generalises to a task no constituent was pruned for.

## Limits and open problems they name
The paper is short and its limits are largely structural rather than confessed. The demonstration is one model family and one modality: HuBERT-base, a frozen linear head, one English syllable corpus, and a single capability (phoneme identity) as the primitive. The tolerance is an absolute F1 slack applied to each member at each key independently and, as far as the text shows, in one pass in topological order, so the accepted sharing is whatever survives that single sweep rather than a global optimum, and no search over merge orders beyond the three schedules is attempted. The "new capability" is close to an assembly of existing ones — the gate is itself a circuit pruned for the order judgement and the phonemes are slotted into a template — so composition here means re-arrangement plus sharing, not the emergence of computation whose parts are absent. Accuracy is also never compared against the full dense model, only against the unmerged circuit ensemble, so how much is lost relative to the trained network stays unmeasured.

## What we take from it
(a) **A composition operator has to be defined against the failure mode of composition, not against union.** Density domination is a structural property of the parts that makes the naive operator behave like the densest part, and the remedy is a per-key normalisation with a stated target; any composition rule we adopt needs the same named failure modes and a stated rule for the regime where the parts differ in "how much they touch". (b) **The honest composition score is a pair — behaviour preserved per part, plus the share of the model that ended up common.** They print 64.8 against 66.0 beside 30.3 and 99.5, and reading the accuracy alone would tell a completely different story about how modular the model is. This is the composite check run 564 demanded, in its cheapest available form. (c) **Validate sharing once and remember where it was validated.** The rejection of the memoryless merge — a later failure discarding sharing that two members had already earned — is the crispest lesson in the paper, and it generalises directly: a white-box account that re-derives its own shared structure from scratch at each revision forgets which agreements were tested and which were merely asserted. Any account of ours that carries shared structure should carry the provenance of each sharing decision, keyed by the scope in which it was validated. (d) **Order matters more than theorem.** The linear schedule is asymptotically defensible and measurably worse than the balanced one at equal density, and the balanced one matches the similarity-aware bound without ever looking at similarity — so a merge discipline fixed in advance can beat a greedy one that uses the data, which is a useful counterweight to the folder's frequent preference for information-driven selection. (e) **A warning about what "emerges" means**: the pipeline's new capability is a template-filling arrangement of existing circuits behind a gate, so a composition result of this shape must not be cited as evidence that composed parts compute something none of them computes. (f) **A warning about the reference point**: every accuracy number here is relative to the unmerged circuit ensemble, and the frozen dense model is nine points higher on the vowel task — a composition that looks lossless can still be far from the model it came from, and we must always print all three numbers.
