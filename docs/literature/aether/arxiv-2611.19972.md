# The structure of variational autoencoder latent spaces and its consequences for representation and optimization

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.19972
- **Authors / organisation:** Sergei V. Kalinin (single author), Department of Materials Science and Engineering, University of Tennessee, Knoxville
- **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19972
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (PDF text layer, 60 pages including Supplementary Information; figures not inspected)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Practitioners who build variational autoencoders for scientific imaging keep seeing the same thing in the latent map: separate families of data sitting in separate regions, narrow transition zones between them, small islands for uncommon states, and a representation that loses all its distinctions when the regularization is turned up. Reporting that the decoder is continuous and the map looks smooth does not explain any of it. The author wants a derivation, not a catalogue: given the objective, the class frequencies, the similarity between classes, the data's symmetries and the number of latent coordinates, what layout *must* the representation adopt, and what does that predict about interpolation, rare-state coverage, generation, and optimization in the latent space. It is a step toward knowing which features of a latent geometry come from the data and which are imposed by the choice of representation, so that scientific uses of such coordinates can stop treating them as physical variables.

## What they studied
The unit of analysis is the latent geometry itself — class centroids, posterior widths, the transition boundaries between families, the prior volume each family receives, and the topology of continuous factors — studied in an analytically tractable model (one Gaussian prototype per class, squared-error reconstruction, an optimal decoder, standard-Gaussian prior). The model is then tested against trained two-dimensional VAEs: scikit-learn digits 0 and 1 (8 × 8 images, 128-unit tanh hidden layers, Bernoulli reconstruction, three seeds at four regularization strengths); ten-class MNIST maps; randomly rotated digits for orientation structure; and a synthetic card set with rotation and shear as two independent factors of variation. A nonparametric encoder (one free Gaussian posterior per data point) supplies the low-loss layouts, and a comparison arm swaps the objective for deep kernel learning (DKL) and hybrid VAE–DKL.

## How they did it
The objective is reduced by three steps. First, with a fixed encoder, the loss-minimizing decoder at a latent point is the responsibility-weighted blend of prototypes, so wherever two posteriors overlap the decoder traces a finite transition between them. Second, an affine reparameterization of the latent leaves reconstruction untouched and changes only the KL, so any stationary optimum of the stated family has zero mean and identity second moment — the model is therefore packing class posteriors into a fixed overall volume while paying for overlap. Third, splitting the class-averaged KL into an aggregate-density term and a mutual-information term turns the objective into a local mixing functional whose interaction coefficients are the reconstruction couplings, which is the form of the Flory–Huggins regular-solution free energy with responsibilities playing the role of local composition. From there the two-class case is solved exactly (a `tanh` decoder profile, a wall width set by posterior overlap, a separation that grows only logarithmically in the coupling), the collapse threshold comes from the second variation of the mixing functional, and the rare-class problem is split into two questions — whether a separate domain lowers the loss (a binodal) and whether training can reach it from a mixed state (a spinodal) — so a metastable window is predicted. Continuous factors are handled by quotienting the symmetry (the orbit of a rotation group by the subgroup that leaves the object fixed), dimensionality by the standard count of independent factor directions plus composition directions, and the oversubscribed case by a folding calculation balancing residual variance against the log cost of the number of strands. Trained VAEs and the nonparametric encoder are then used as checks in both directions: does a trained map show the predicted feature, and does an optimized map actually reach the predicted optimum.

## What counts as an explanation here
A reduced statistical-mechanical model of the representation: a free-energy functional over class responsibilities, plus derived laws (wall profile and width, binodal and spinodal, logarithmic contact distances, a trap-plus-repulsion picture of centroid placement), with an explicit dictionary mapping each term to the VAE quantity it stands for. It is a partial and idealised explanation, and the paper is unusually careful to say so: the identity is exact as a rewriting of the objective but is *not* a variational equivalence, because responsibilities cannot be chosen independently at each latent point, the aggregate density is itself penalised, and no separate gradient term exists. It predicts the *shape* of the latent geometry and the *thresholds* at which qualitatively different layouts become favourable; it does not predict training time, and it explicitly refuses to fit its asymptotics to trained heterogeneous networks.

## How they judged success
No benchmark and no external leaderboard. The evidence is (a) exactness of the derived correspondence for the stated model, (b) direct numerical optimisation of the model's objective under several posterior families — isotropic, diagonal per class, and full covariance via a Cholesky factor — with multistart L-BFGS-B and structured starts, compared against the analytic predictions (fitted exponents 0.504 for the order parameter, 2.007 and prefactor 1.00 for the condensation gain, 0.997 for the Widom combination at a coupling one-thousandth above threshold), (c) pair-model versus full-objective agreement for three to ten equal classes (layouts within 4 × 10⁻⁴ of the full optimum, with the pair sum exceeding the full loss by 0.001–0.007, the junction correction), (d) checks that the analytic laws break where they should (the square-gradient expansion fails as soon as walls exist and misses the wall cost by about a factor of three), and (e) trained-network observations reported as illustrations with the diagnostic limits stated. Estimators are named and kept apart throughout: encoded-mean density versus full aggregate posterior, fitted-responsibility threshold versus classifier confidence, encoded-mean moment diagnostic versus the variance-inclusive control.

## What they found
Two equally frequent classes separate continuously at a reconstruction coupling of one half, with square-root onset and inverse-square-root wall broadening near the threshold, and far from it a wall width that shrinks like the inverse of twice the log coupling — so lowering the regularization sharpens the wall but never removes it at finite coupling; the decoder across a wall is a `tanh` with the transition width set by posterior overlap. The KL term acts as a harmonic trap on class centroids whose stiffness is the class frequency, so frequency behaves as mass, and separation between neighbouring classes grows only logarithmically with how dissimilar their prototypes are — latent distance is therefore not a calibrated measure of image difference. A rare class is compressed, pushed toward the prior tail, and receives far less prior mass than its data frequency (a class with 5% of the data receiving 18% of a proportional share at one coupling and 4% at a much sharper one); it can also dissolve into a common class, survive as a tail island, be adsorbed *inside* the wall between two common classes without ever owning a domain, or carve out its own layer — and which of these happens depends on the frequencies and on the reconstruction metric itself, since smoothing the prototypes moves one rare shape from "island, layer" to "adsorbed" across the same frequency grid. A third structural fact: changing the pixel metric moved a rare state between regimes as decisively as changing its frequency did.

For dimensionality, an explicit circular angle latent removes the core entirely, whereas a real-valued angle head must have a cut, and a periodic-symmetry quotient requires the pose variable to live on the orbit quotient. The number of latent coordinates needed for a locally disentangled representation of m continuous factors meeting s classes is m + s − 1; where that is unavailable, the objective prefers folding one factor into a coarse staircase along an occupied axis, with a preferred strand count growing like the square root of the excess factor's strength — but in trained two-dimensional networks on three independent synthetic factors the third factor was usually suppressed or entangled rather than folded, which the paper reports as a negative control on *optimisation accessibility* rather than on the existence of the folded optimum. Comparing objectives, a predictive (DKL) representation organises classes by target value rather than by structural difference and merges classes whose targets differ by less than a noise-derived threshold, bounding the number of structural classes it can keep apart by the number of resolvable target levels; the hybrid objective can often satisfy both at once by re-optimising its kernel length scale, and only starts merging target-indistinguishable classes above a weight whose expression is given. Separately, any continuous generator from a connected Gaussian latent must pass through intermediate outputs between separated data families, and a Lipschitz bound makes "few intermediate outputs" cost a correspondingly sharp generator.

## Limits and open problems they name
The physical vocabulary is explicitly a dictionary, not a claim: there is no material transport, no independent interfacial energy, no Cahn–Hilliard gradient penalty, and the square-gradient analogy holds only near collapse and even there misses the wall cost by about a factor of three. Wall width, binodal and folding laws are exhibited for a one-dimensional or shared-isotropic prototype model; whether trained image networks realise them is stated as open, and the trained two-class measurement is likened by the author to a qualitative illustration rather than a quantitative confirmation of the wall-width law (because the encoded-mean proxy omits encoder variance, which matters most near collapse). The trained digit-0/1 experiment is a proxy with fitted Gaussian domains; the rotation-winding diagnostic reuses training-image identities, so it is a representation diagnostic rather than a test of generalisation; higher-dimensional winding cases were never tested with trained networks. Optimisation dynamics are analysed in a reduced gradient flow whose rates are explicitly not wall-clock training rates, and one trained trajectory showed non-monotonic wall-width evolution. Finally, the paper states that equilibrium folding being an available low-loss solution does not mean gradient training finds it.

## What we take from it
Three things, all transferable to how we read a network's internal geometry, plus one measure.

**A stationarity identity we can test cheaply.** The paper's second derivation is that an affine reparameterization of the latent leaves reconstruction unchanged and only moves the KL, so any optimum of the stated family satisfies zero aggregate mean and identity second moment. Two restrictions come with it and both matter: the full matrix identity needs an encoder family that can actually make that transform, and a diagonal posterior family only guarantees the identity coordinatewise, while a decoder with a fixed absolute resolution breaks the stationarity outright (the paper shows the second moment then exceeds one). The transfer is a diagnostic we do not currently run: if our read-out of an internal distribution does not satisfy the scale identity that its own objective implies — because of a fixed smoothing scale, a diagonal family, or a probe fitted at a finite checkpoint — then the read-out is describing a training checkpoint, not the geometry, and the honest sentence names which one.

**Among several, one.** Splitting the class-averaged KL shows that the objective's own minima are constrained: only the arrangements realisable by the posterior family are available, which is why the *same* isotropic, diagonal and full-covariance families select different layouts for identical prototypes (compact shells versus axis-locked stacks), and why a full-covariance stage followed by a diagonal restriction lands in a different local minimum. For us the analogue is that a probe's hypothesis class is part of the result, not a nuisance: two probes over the same activations that differ in family can report different "structures" with neither being wrong.

**Similarity is a metric choice, not a measurement.** Moving from pixelwise to 4-pixel-smoothed comparison, at identical frequencies and couplings, moved one rare prototype from island-and-layer regimes to the adsorbed regime across the whole grid, and moved another from tail island everywhere to dissolved at the lowest frequency. That is the denominator lesson in geometric form: our probes' notion of "these two internal states are similar" is a choice of metric, and a different metric is a different read-out rather than a better estimate of the same one — so the metric has to be stated beside the claim.

**The measure worth copying: two thresholds, not one.** The paper separates the point at which a distinct rare domain becomes the lower-loss representation (binodal) from the point at which the mixed state loses local stability (spinodal). The gap between them is exactly the region where initialisation and training path decide the outcome — the paper uses it to explain why initialisation can change whether a rare state gets resolved. Our instruments have an obvious counterpart: a feature that is *decodable* and a feature that is *used* have separate thresholds, and the width of the interval between them is the quantity that says how much of "the model represents X" is being decided by optimisation luck.
