## What they wanted, and why
Topic models summarise a document collection as a small set of themes, and the theme is normally *a list of words*: LDA counts co-occurrences, and neural variants such as ETM and BERTopic replace the counts or the embeddings but still hand back words. The authors argue this representation caps what a topic can say — "style, tone, discourse patterns" and abstract themes do not survive as a bag of words, and short documents give the word-count view almost nothing to work with. Their move is to put the *unit of the topic* at the level interpretability research has spent five years making legible: the SAE feature direction, which comes with a learned direction in activation space **and** an auto-generated natural-language description. The larger goal is less about topic modelling than about a claim: that interpretability artefacts (features and their directions) can be the substrate of a downstream *application*, not just of an audit — and this paper's conclusion states that intention explicitly, against recent negative results on SAEs in downstream tasks.

## What they studied
Eight text corpora of roughly 2.4k–33k documents each: 20 Newsgroups, US congressional bill summaries (the 110th–114th congresses), "good" Wikipedia articles, GoEmotions Reddit comments, PoemSum poems, AGNews, Yelp Polarity reviews and WritingPrompts stories. The last three are labelled *hard* by the authors because the documents are short (GoEmotions) or the themes abstract (poems, fiction). The unit of analysis is the **SAE feature direction and its count within a document**: GemmaScope SAEs on Gemma 2-9B activations, the 16k-width SAE at layer 10 throughout, with feature descriptions pulled from Neuronpedia. Six baselines run on the same corpora: LDA, ETM, BERTopic, Dirichlet VAE and FASTopic, each in both a *Top Features* representation (words, or feature descriptions) and a *Summarization* representation (one LLM-written sentence over the top items), so that format is a crossed factor rather than a confound between the word models and theirs.

## How they did it
Three stages. **(1) Featurisation.** For each token position in a document they read the layer-10 hidden state, run it through the SAE encoder, and *count* a feature once if its activation on that token exceeds that feature's own 80th-percentile nonzero activation on the SAE's training distribution; the document becomes a sparse vector of feature counts rather than word counts. Two filters then remove features they consider topic-irrelevant: any feature whose description concerns programming, mathematics, grammar, formatting or stop words (judged by GPT-4o mini), any feature active on more than 1 per cent of the SAE training data, and any feature present in more than 90 per cent of corpus documents. They say the filter is essential — without it, benchmarks degrade. **(2) Topic learning**, three variants of standard algorithms on those counts: mechanistic LDA treats features as words and uses a multinomial likelihood over feature counts with Gibbs sampling or variational inference; mechanistic ETM learns each topic as a vector *in Gemma's activation space*, passes it through the frozen SAE encoder to get topic-feature probabilities, and uses a binomial likelihood because a feature can fire at most once per token; mechanistic BERTopic builds each document's embedding as the count-weighted mean of the SAE decoder directions, then UMAP plus HDBSCAN, then class-based TF-IDF over features. Hyperparameters are chosen per model-dataset by 25 iterations of Bayesian optimisation on NPMI coherence times topic diversity — the same objective for every arm. **(3) Use.** A topic is rendered either as its top-n feature descriptions concatenated, or as one LLM summary of those descriptions; and because every topic is a weighted sum of decoder directions, the topic itself becomes a **steering vector**: they normalise the weighted sum, then at every layer and token position replace the activation's component parallel to that direction with a scaled copy of it, so existing topic signal is removed before the chosen strength is added. Optional post-training refinement asks an LLM to strike up to two features from each topic before the top-n are kept — applied identically to all arms, word lists included, expressly so the refinement cannot inflate the mechanistic scores.

## What counts as an explanation here
A topic model: a fixed number of topics (50 or 100), a weight of every SAE feature in each topic, and a per-document mixture over topics. The description is the top-weighted features' own natural-language labels, optionally summarised into one sentence. Two things make it more than a description: the weight vector writes straight back into activation space as a steering direction, and the document-topic matrix is a behavioural statistic that can be compared across models by correlation. The claim is partial by construction — a topic is a cartesian summary of a corpus, not a mechanism and not a prediction about a single forward pass.

## How they judged success
Four instruments. **topic judge**: for each pair of models and each of 100 sampled documents, take the document's top topics under each model (top-2, or all topics with cumulative mass below 0.75, whichever is fewer), render them in the matched representation, and ask GPT-4.1 at temperature 0 which set better captures the document's meaning including affect and style; fit a Bradley-Terry model to all 2,800 comparisons per dataset and report Elo normalised to mean 1,500, with bootstrap intervals and a significance marking. The judge is validated against **68 paid participants** ranking five randomly sampled models per document over ten documents, decomposed into pairwise comparisons and refit the same way. **Coherence** is GPT-4.1's 1–3 relatedness rating over each topic's top ten items and a six-option intrusion test (25 trials per topic). **Topic diversity** is the share of unique features among the top-25 items across all topics. **Novelty** is measured by greedy-aligned cross-correlation of two models' document-topic matrices — a topic pair correlating above 0.5 is taken to be the same theme. **Steering** is judged three ways: a topic-relevance win rate where a judge picks the most on-topic of L and L samples, a relative log-likelihood difference between on-topic and off-topic held-out documents as steering strength rises, and the perplexity of steered generations under the unsteered model. Stability is reported in the appendix: five seeds, rank-biased overlap of the top 500 weights, Hungarian matching.

## What they found
An MTM tops the topic-judge table in 14 of 16 dataset-by-representation rows, and MTMs beat their own word-based counterparts in 42 of 48 pairwise comparisons; 71 per cent of the 48 gaps are at least 50 Elo (about 57 per cent win probability), half exceed 100, a fifth exceed 200. The advantage is concentrated where the authors predicted: on GoEmotions, PoemSum and WritingPrompts an MTM wins by 195 Elo on average, whereas on Bills with the description representation *all three* word models outscore their mechanistic counterparts — the judge prefers the bill's precise keywords to the features' generic descriptions, and the summarisation format recovers two of the three. Human Elo agrees with the judge at Spearman 0.76–0.95 across the four Wiki and PoemSum conditions, and humans put an MTM first in three of four. On the standard metrics the picture is flat, as the authors say: coherence ratings 2.5–2.9 on the benchmarks, diversity comparable for the LDA and ETM variants but poor for the clustering variant (0.38 and 0.36 diversity on PoemSum and WritingPrompts, with a 2.28 coherence on PoemSum). On novelty, more than 70 per cent of mechanistic-LDA topics correlate at least 0.5 with an LDA topic on Bills and Wiki, while on GoEmotions and PoemSum nearly all fall below 0.5 and many below 0.2 — the same split as the judge result, which the authors read as abstract and short corpora yielding genuinely new themes rather than redescription. Steering works: the relevance win rate is above 84 per cent on every corpus (98.9 per cent on Bills), wins cluster at the higher strengths, and perplexity rises gently at first (about 6.3 against 6.2 unsteered at the lowest strength) and steeply at the top, so topic-following and fluency trade off along one dial. Ablating the direction at zero strength *lowers* the likelihood of on-topic documents, and using all of a topic's features steers better than the top one, five or ten — the direction, not the label, carries the effect. Stability is at least as high as the word baselines under the authors' metric.

## Limits and open problems they name
Two constraints, both stated in a Limitations section: dependence on a pre-trained SAE ecosystem that currently covers few models, with quality sensitive to the chosen layer, the SAE's own training corpus and the accuracy of the auto-generated descriptions (they suggest training SAEs on the target corpus as the remedy); and a heavier pipeline — an LLM forward pass plus SAE plus a filtering LLM, requiring GPU memory and adding runtime they tabulate per dataset. Their own figure set is candid about a failure mode they do not repair: one mechanistic topic whose features are individually apt but collectively "too broad to be useful", with spurious features surviving refinement. Also noted without resolution: the clustering variant's steering does not reliably raise on-topic likelihood, which they attribute to its class-based TF-IDF weighting but leave untested; and coherence metrics are declared not comparable across word and feature vocabularies, so NPMI — the very quantity their own hyperparameter search maximises — is excluded from the cross-vocabulary comparison.

## What we take from it
Three things, one of them a warning. **The measure:** a *behavioural* cross-model comparison that works across two incomparable vocabularies — have a judge compare two summaries against the same item, then fit a Bradley-Terry model to the pairwise outcomes — validated against humans at 0.76–0.95 rank correlation, with per-dataset significance marking rather than a single headline number. This is the cheapest defensible way to compare an account built out of features with one built out of tokens, which is exactly the comparison our own account will face. **The construction:** the topic weight vector normalised and written back into activation space, with the ablation-then-add intervention on the parallel component, gives a *composition* of feature directions whose effect is measured both as likelihood on held-out items and as a judged win rate — the pattern for turning a set of directions into one intervention whose magnitude has a dial and whose fluency cost is printed. **The warning:** their decisive finding is that the direction steers while the label misleads — the summary loses to plain keywords on legislative text, and one of their own topics is well-described and still too broad to be useful; and the pre-filtering, which they call crucial, is an LLM deciding which features have topic-irrelevant descriptions, that is, **a description-based filter imposed before any measurement**, with the honest admission that hyperparameter search kept choosing the filtered formulation. For us: any pipeline that removes features, topics or directions because their *labels* look irrelevant is pruning the object under study by its own commentary, so the filter's contribution has to be reported as its own ablation; and a claim that a direction "captures" a concept must be supported by the intervention, never by the description that names it.
