## What they wanted, and why
Much mechanistic interpretability rests on the hope that a network's computation decomposes into a list of unit "features" that is simultaneously unique (no arbitrary choices of basis), complete (nothing missing) and atomic (nothing made of something more basic) — the authors call a list with all three properties *canonical*, and note that individual neurons and attention heads were the first candidates before polysemanticity killed them. Sparse autoencoders are the current candidate, and a working assumption in the field is that with a large enough dictionary an SAE converges on that true set. This paper sets out to test whether any such list is recovered, and finds two separate failures: dictionaries of different sizes do not contain the same information, and within one dictionary the units are frequently compounds rather than atoms. The larger aim is to stop the field treating a dictionary as ground truth and to make dictionary size an explicit, task-driven choice.

## What they studied
Units are SAE latents: the encoder/decoder pairs in a sparse dictionary learned on a model's residual stream. Dictionaries studied: eight SAEs trained here on the residual stream of **layer 8 of GPT-2 small** with sizes 768, 1536, 3072, 6144, 12288, 24576, 49152 and 98304, plus two released **Gemma Scope** SAEs on **Gemma 2 2B** layer 12 at 16,384 and 32,768. Units of analysis also include the *decoder directions* of an SAE (treated as data in their own right for the second method) and the meta-latents of a second-level dictionary trained on them.

## How they did it
Two instruments, both cheap to describe and both built from decoder vectors rather than activations alone.
*Stitching.* An SAE's reconstruction is a sum of per-latent decoder contributions plus a bias, so latents from one dictionary can be poured into another's sum directly, with the two biases mixed in proportion to how many latents come from each side — unlike model stitching, no learned transformation is needed. For a latent in the larger SAE they compute its maximum cosine similarity to any decoder direction in the smaller SAE; below a threshold it is called *novel* and is added on its own, above it is called a *reconstruction* latent and is swapped in as part of a connected component of a bipartite similarity graph, after removing the smaller SAE's near-duplicates. They sweep the threshold (0.7 for the GPT-2 pairs, 0.4 for the Gemma pair, both chosen from exploratory analysis) and watch reconstruction error move at each step, so the interpolation from one dictionary size to the next proceeds in two phases per step — add novel latents (raising L0), then swap reconstruction families (lowering it).
*Meta-SAE.* They train a BatchTopK SAE with 2,304 latents on the 49,152 decoder directions of the largest GPT-2 SAE, using a batch of 4,096 for 2,000 epochs at learning rate 1e-4, and ask whether each original latent can be written as a sparse combination (about four on average) of the meta-latents. Explanations are generated with GPT-4o-mini and scored by a five-way multiple-choice test against four randomly drawn latents.

## What counts as an explanation here
A dictionary of directions with an accompanying claim about granularity: the understanding has the form "this many units, at this grain, with these compositional relations", not a circuit and not a causal abstraction. The paper's product is closer to a diagnosis than a model — it shows the form people want (a canonical list) is not what the method gives, and offers two probes (stitching, meta-decomposition) that make the non-uniqueness visible. Neither probe predicts behaviour; the closest thing to prediction is that maximum cosine similarity predicts the *sign* of a single latent's effect on reconstruction error.

## How they judged success
Reconstruction MSE and average L0 (how many latents fire per token), reported across the size sweep; the sign and size of MSE change from adding or swapping latent families, with an ROC curve over thresholds for the novel-versus-reconstruction classification; the fraction of variance of the decoder matrix explained by the meta-SAE; a five-option multiple-choice accuracy for the meta-latent explanations over a random sample of 1,000 latents; and two downstream probes — sparse probing (a one-latent and a top-50-latent logistic probe on eight benchmark datasets, after Gao et al.) and targeted probe perturbation (ablating a concept's top latents and checking that only that concept's probe degrades). Controls of note: within-size comparisons between two runs of the same width, a retrain of the meta-SAE encoder after substituting the most similar ordinary SAE directions, and the low-cosine latents tested on inputs where they are and are not active.

## What they found
Reconstruction and cross-entropy recovery improve monotonically with dictionary size while average L0 first rises and then flattens: L0 35.2 at 768 widening to 42.4-43.9 through the middle of the sweep, MSE falling from 2.72 to 1.14, and cross-entropy recovered against a mean ablation rising from 0.876 to 0.970 — so the useful gains keep arriving without any natural stopping point. Small dictionaries are incomplete: adding every latent from the 1,536 SAE with maximum cosine below 0.7 into the 768 SAE lowers its MSE by nearly ten percent, and a named example is a "make sure" latent in 1,536 with nothing closer than about 0.3 in 768, where the small dictionary's error is markedly worse on the subset of inputs the latent fires on (0.589 against the large SAE's 0.293 rise). Within a size, the two runs agree: 94% of latents in one 3,072 SAE are reconstruction latents of the other at the 0.7 threshold, so the non-uniqueness is a function of *width*, not of seed. Larger dictionaries are also not atomic: the meta-SAE explains 55.47% of the variance of the 49,152 decoder directions, GPT-4o-mini picks the right latent from its meta-latent explanations 73% of the time against a 20% chance rate, the meta-latents resemble latents of ordinary SAEs of a comparable width, and substituting the nearest ordinary direction into the meta-SAE costs only a small amount of reconstruction — the whole apparatus pointing at a picture in which a big dictionary buys sparser compounds of the smaller dictionary's vocabulary. The paper's own example decomposition is a person-named latent into scientist, country and prominence meta-latents. Sparse probing accuracy and the targeted-perturbation score move non-monotonically with width, which the authors read as one more sign that width should be picked per task.

## Limits and open problems they name
Only two language models, both small; the largest current-scale SAEs were not included. The threshold that separates novel from reconstruction latents is set by hand per model pair (0.7 versus 0.4 in Gemma Scope) from exploratory inspection. The narration is unchecked: the evidence that stitching improves reconstruction is quantitative, while the claim that the meta-latents *mean* scientist or country rests on generated explanations scored in a five-way choice. The methods identify neither canonical units nor the dictionary size appropriate to a given task, and the authors steer future work toward either a different route to units or pragmatic per-task width selection.

## What we take from it
A **measure**, first: report the *grain* of any unit we name, and prove the unit is not a compound by finding a dictionary in which its information is carried by several smaller pieces — that is the same demand as the folder's separability controls, made concrete and cheap. Second, a **baseline family that costs almost nothing**: two runs of the same width from different seeds agree at 94% of latents, so a claim that a structure is a property of the *model* rather than of a particular training run can be made by comparing across widths instead of across seeds, and a claim of uniqueness has to survive the width sweep. Third, a **warning**: monotone improvement in reconstruction and cross-entropy recovery across a size sweep is exactly what we would see if the smaller object were merely incomplete, so an intervention gain measured against one dictionary size has an unrun arm — the same size, a different seed is not enough. Fourth, a reusable **construction**: treating a set of directions as data and fitting a sparse dictionary to them is the natural white-box analogue of probing a set of vectors, and it lets a compound direction be resolved into a combination of things we can name, which is the shape our accounts will need when the unit we started from turns out not to be atomic.
