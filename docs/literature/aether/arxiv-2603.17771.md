# Attention Sinks Induce Gradient Sinks

- **Key:** arxiv-2603.17771
- **Authors / organisation:** Yihong Chen, Quanming Yao; Department of Electronic Engineering, Tsinghua University
- **Date:** 2026-03-18
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17771
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v1: abstract, §1-§6 including limitations; Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 statements and the V-scale Jacobian eigenvalues; all five figure captions and the prose describing them; figures not inspected as images; reference list read only far enough to resolve the works cited in §§1-5)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Attention sinks (a few tokens carrying most attention mass) and massive activations (a few tokens whose activation norm explodes) are known to co-occur and to sit almost always on the first token of pre-norm transformers, and the field has explained each of them separately from the forward pass. The authors ask the question the forward stories cannot answer: in a pre-norm block every sublayer reads a normalised input, so the forward computation depends on the direction of a token more than on its magnitude, which means the optimiser has no obvious reason to pay for a huge norm. Their proposal is that the reason is on the backward side. If at most one token accumulates attention mass, it should also accumulate gradient, because every later token's gradient arrives weighted by the attention it pays to that token. They name this a gradient sink and argue that a massively large activation is the network's way of buying normalisation-induced gradient attenuation for it. The larger goal is causal rather than correlational: to say which quantity the optimiser was actually pushing on when the two observable phenomena were learned together.

## What they studied
Llama-style decoder-only transformers trained from scratch on C4 with AdamW at roughly 0.1B and 0.3B parameters, with pre-norm residual blocks, RMSNorm, RoPE and SwiGLU MLPs, plus the same configurations with their proposed value-path modification. The unit of analysis spans three levels at once: token positions (the sink token versus the other early tokens), per-pathway token-wise gradient norms (query, key and value separately, aggregated over a full batch exactly as one optimiser step would see them, then l2-normed per token), and per-layer scalar ratios relating gradient size before and after each normalisation. Activation magnitude is defined token-wise at explicitly named computational sites (layer input, layer output, post-attention hidden state, MLP output, value state) rather than as a single large coordinate.

## How they did it
They train baselines, save a checkpoint every thousand steps, and at each checkpoint run one forward-backward pass to collect the statistics. Forward: a thresholded sink rate (fraction of heads whose average attention column on the candidate sink exceeds 0.3, averaged over the first 64 queries after it), plus the mean and second moment of the attention column routed through the sink by all causally permitted queries, and the token-wise activation norms at each site. Backward: the same column statistics are shown to be exactly the multipliers in the value-gradients, since the gradient on a token's value state is the attention-weighted sum of upstream output gradients; they bound this in expectation under a mean-plus-noise model of stochastic gradients. Then they define three ratios per block - bloat (gradient norm on the normed input over gradient norm on the branch residual), compress (the extra gradient arriving through the branch over that on the normed input), and change (gradient on the block input over gradient on the block output) - and scatter each against the block's input activation norm, coloured by token group. The intervention: a radial squash of each value vector, multiplying it by r/(r+C) where r is its squared norm, with C parameterised per head from the standard init scale and either fixed or learned as one scalar per head. This is near-identity for large-norm values and strongly contracting for small-norm ones, so it selectively damps the backward path of the sink token, whose values are already known to be small. They give the map's Jacobian eigenvalues on the orthogonal and radial subspaces and then train matched models with it.

## What counts as an explanation here
A training-time causal chain with a stated mediator: attention structure creates gradient pressure, the normalisation converts that pressure into a penalty on activation size, so growing the activation relieves it, and the observable phenomena are the equilibrium of that trade. Understanding takes the form of a mechanism, partial by construction - it explains why an already-formed sink would be accompanied by a big activation, not how either is discovered over the course of optimisation. It has a predictive part: if the chain is right, supplying an alternative valve on the dominant gradient path should leave the sink intact and shrink the activation. That prediction is what the intervention tests.

## How they judged success
Empirical separation of two co-occurring phenomena rather than a benchmark score. The forward metrics are the thresholded sink rate and the activation norms at the named sites for token 0 versus the mean of tokens 1-15; the backward metrics are the per-token gradient norms per pathway and the three bloat/compress/change ratios as functions of input norm and layer. The test of the hypothesis is whether a model trained with the value-path valve retains sink behaviour while showing lower residual-stream and MLP output norms on token 0, across two model sizes, at matched data, optimiser and configuration. Validation loss is reported only to establish that the modified models still train (slightly lower than baseline). The theoretical support is two bounds: an expectation bound tying the squared value-gradient norm to the sink column's mass and second moment, and an operator-norm bound on the RMSNorm Jacobian showing attenuation grows inverse to the root-mean-square of the input.

## What they found
The empirical asymmetry is the paper's core observation: the value-path token-wise gradient spike at the first token is large and reproducible across checkpoints and both model sizes, the key-path spike is present but smaller, and the query path is flat because under the causal mask the first token's query gradient is exactly zero. The mechanism for the asymmetry is in the algebra: value and key gradients sum column-wise over all later queries, whereas query gradients are row-local. The reshaping analysis shows the sink token's large activation sits in the high-bloat regime (strong amplification inside the attention branch) but is compensated by an almost linear inverse relationship between compression and activation scale, so the net block-level change stays near unity except for a few first-layer points - the local pressure is real but is converted into a stable residual-scale signal. The RMSNorm bound explains why an activation that is large on purpose is exactly the kind that gets this compensation. In trained valve models, sink rate is preserved and even slightly strengthened in some layers, while the residual-stream and MLP output norms of token 0 clearly fall, with the largest reduction in MLP outputs; direct attention-output contraction is modest by comparison, which the authors read as the MLP having stopped supplying the large activation it no longer needed. The ablation of only the value path cannot remove the sink gradient entirely, since the key path still aggregates column-wise; it is enough to change what the optimiser is willing to pay for.

## Limits and open problems they name
Dense Llama-like language models only; mixture-of-experts and multimodal transformers are untested. The account is of why an existing gradient sink makes massive activations useful, not of how either is learned through the whole of optimisation. The experiments are built for mechanistic validation, not for architecture or capability comparisons: no scaling sweep, no search over alternative interventions, no downstream task evaluation. Two further limits are in the construction rather than the text: the valve is a fixed radial form chosen because sink values are already small, so it presumes the structural prior it exploits, and the value path is only the dominant, not the only, channel by which the sink token receives gradient.

## What we take from it
Two reusable things. First, a *measure* with an exact justification: token-wise gradient norms per pathway, computed on a batch aggregated as one optimiser step, are the right observables for any claim about what training is doing to a particular position, and the gradient-on-a-token's-value-state identity (attention column times upstream gradient) means the forward attention column is a directly readable control parameter for a backward quantity - a forward-pass number that bounds a training signal. Second, a *framing* for prediction under intervention that is stronger than correlation: the paper's causal claim does not rest on the co-occurrence it starts from but on inventing a third object (the valve) that changes one link of the chain and leaves the others alone, and then checking that the downstream effect appears while the upstream cause persists. Concretely for us, when we claim a weight- or activation-level object is what the optimiser reacted to, we should name the intermediate quantity that transmits the reaction and intervene on that quantity, not on the observable. The warning is sharp too: a normalisation that attenuates in proportion to magnitude makes magnitude and direction inseparable by construction, so any ablation that changes one will be reported as changing the other unless the two are separated with ratios like theirs (bloat versus compress versus change) rather than with a single end-to-end norm. Cross-reference run 706 (the same sink traced down to named weight objects on the forward side, with an explicit variance-not-magnitude control), run 705 (the sink as a spectral spike, i.e. a third causal story for the same object) and run 704 (the sink given a path-product bound, a forward-computable surrogate for a sensitivity claim - the same move as their bloat ratio, one level up).
