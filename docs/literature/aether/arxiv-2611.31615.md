# Eliciting Sandbagged LLM Capability via Structural Interventions on Attention-Head Functional Networks

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.31615
- **Authors / organisation:** Manzi Fabrice Niyigaba, Ishan Verma Prasad, Sansheng Cao, Zheng Huang, Yujun Yan; Dartmouth College
- **Date:** 2026-11-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31615
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Sandbagging is a model scoring worse on an evaluation than it would outside one, on purpose. Behavioural audits and activation probes can flag it, and prefill-style interventions can recover some of the suppressed performance, but nobody has said what changes *inside* the network when an available capability is being withheld. The authors borrow the instrument network neuroscience uses for the same question in brains — nodes are regions, edges are correlations of activity, and deception is read off the graph's community structure — and port it to attention heads. The larger goal is a structural, per-condition signature that both explains the suppression and points at where to intervene to undo it.

## What they studied

Four instruction-tuned production models spanning dense and mixture-of-experts builds and a wide depth range: Mistral-24B, Qwen3-32B, Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-OSS-20B. The unit of analysis is the attention head (all L×H heads), read as its pre-output-projection vector at the final prompt token, over 400 held-out MMLU questions per condition. Two sandbagging regimes are studied: prompted (an incentive and instruction to answer incorrectly, weights untouched, prompts from prior elicitation work) and fine-tuned model organisms (password-locked and circuit-broken).

## How they did it

For each condition they collect an n×d activation matrix per head and centre it across questions. Head pairs are compared with SVCCA — truncate each to its leading K singular directions, then canonical correlation — and the canonical correlations are collapsed into one scalar edge weight by the Gaussian mutual-information expression, −½ Σ log(1 − ρ²). That gives a similarity matrix per condition; keeping the top 25% of head pairs as unweighted edges gives equal-density graphs for honest and sandbagging. Global thresholding strands some heads (9.4% / 3.1% / 2.8% / 9.2% of heads are edgeless in the honest graphs of the four models), so communities are instead detected once on a symmetric k-nearest-neighbour graph built from the *unthresholded* honest similarities, and those labels are frozen across conditions. For each head they compute within-community degree normalised by the maximum possible degree, and the change in that quantity between honest and sandbagging. To pick intervention targets they take a budget of heads, allocate it across communities by Hamilton apportionment proportional to community size, and within each community take the heads with the largest connectivity loss. The intervention is a zero-ablation of the selected heads' pre-projection outputs at the final prompt token, nothing else changed. Controls of equal budget: uniform random heads, layer-matched random heads (same count per layer as the selection), degree-matched random heads (same degree distribution), and later magnitude-matched heads. A weight-level variant of the same selection replaces z_h with g_h z_h + u_h(v_hᵀ z_h), initialised at g=u=0 so training starts exactly at the ablation. Crucially the community-fitting bank (400 answer-balanced MMLU auxiliary-training questions) is disjoint from the measurement bank (400 validation questions), and both are disjoint from the MMLU test split after stem matching; a paraphrased-honest-prompt graph and five other non-sandbagging prompt conditions (persona, context, reasoning instruction, random answering, refusal) serve as additional controls. For the fine-tuned organisms, contrast graphs are built *without* the password, using the unlocked model plus response prefill as the honest proxy.

## What counts as an explanation here

A two-graph structural signature plus a head ranking: "under condition C, heads i and j co-vary this strongly; under suppression the heads inside their community co-vary less; the heads that lose the most are the ones to ablate." It is a *differential* object — a change between two conditions, never a single-condition model of what a head or a community computes. The paper is explicit that it establishes no causal relation between graph structure and the sandbagging tendency itself; the intervention works, and the graph is offered as the reason the intervention works, with the localisation controls as the argument rather than a mechanism.

## How they judged success

Mean normalised within-community degree, honest vs sandbagging, × four community-detection algorithms (Louvain, Leiden, Infomap, spectral) × four models = 16 cells, all required to move the same way; Δ in percentage points relative to the honest value. The paraphrase control must move less than sandbagging in every cell. Head-selection success is measured downstream: change in sandbagging-condition accuracy on MMLU, WMDP and ARC-Challenge against three equal-budget random controls (20 seeded draws each, 95% CI on the control mean, paired-question bootstrap CI on the selection), with honest-condition accuracy reported as the cost. Composition with prefill and few-shot is compared against the same composition with random heads. Weight-level comparison is mean over five trials with sample SD, plus a demonstration-budget sweep. Fine-tuned organisms are scored on final-evaluation accuracy, and graph-based selection is compared head-to-head with activation-magnitude-based selection across nine sandbagging model settings. Robustness checks: three functional-similarity measures (SVCCA-MI, rank-Gaussian SVCCA-MI, linear CKA), density sweep 0.15–0.35 (Spearman 0.841–0.972 against the 0.25 ranking), kNN-k sweep 0.3k₀–4k₀ (Spearman 0.874–0.979), and a magnitude control (selected heads are 1.09–1.22× the magnitude of layer-matched random heads, i.e. not weak components).

## What they found

Within-community connectivity is lower under sandbagging in all 16 model × method cells: Mistral-24B −1.09 / −1.08 / −1.66 / −1.12 pp (Louvain / Leiden / Infomap / spectral), Qwen3-32B −0.41 / −0.31 / −0.70 / −0.64, Llama-3.3-70B −0.69 / −0.77 / −1.27 / −0.80, GPT-OSS-20B −0.51 / −0.50 / −0.51 / −0.55. Absolute levels are small (0.047–0.103 of maximum possible degree), so the effect is visible against a tight control rather than large in itself: the honest-prompt paraphrase moves the same statistic by at most 0.24 pp across all 16 cells — less than the *smallest* sandbagging decrease of 0.31 pp — and five further non-sandbagging prompt conditions move it between −0.18 and +0.21 pp. The direction survives all three similarity measures (12/12 cells), with all three selections recovering 12.15–18.02 pp on Mistral-24B against −0.83 pp for layer-matched random, at an honest-accuracy cost of at most 0.25 pp. Ablating graph-selected heads beats the mean of all three random controls in all 16 cells, with a largest honest drop of 1.25 pp across those cells. Composed with prefill, Mistral-24B MMLU rises 30.75 → 36.75; on Llama-3.3-70B the few-shot composition moves MMLU 20.00 → 47.08 and ARC 12.58 → 46.92, while the same number of random heads under few-shot moves Llama MMLU 20.00 → 24.70 (random heads sometimes help, which is why the comparison needs the composition arm). Weight-level, GraphLearned leads on three of four prompted models — Qwen3-32B MMLU 70.25 ± 1.33 against full SFT's 63.55 ± 19.33 — and is more stable than full fine-tuning in every comparison, though full fine-tuning wins Qwen3-32B MMLU at 16 demonstrations (73.75 vs 71.45). On password-locked organisms the gap is largest: Mistral-instruct 51.62 ± 1.71 against 21.46 ± 11.01 for LoRA and 17.78 ± 4.22 for full SFT, while noise injection collapses to 15.5. Graph selection beats activation-change selection in 9/9 settings, with the biggest gaps on the two models whose suppression is most thoroughly trained in (Willow +50.00 pp, circuit-broken Mistral-instruct +32.43 pp). Ablating graph-selected heads also shifts the residual stream along an honest–sandbagging contrast direction more than matched random ablation does, in step with the accuracy recovery.

## Limits and open problems they name

The authors state plainly that they do not establish a causal relationship between the graphs and sandbagging. The effects are small in absolute terms and read only relative to a control. The method needs a *paired* condition — an honest reference — so it is a differential instrument and cannot be applied to a model for which no honest contrast exists; for the password-locked organisms the "honest" proxy is the same model with a response prefill, which only partially recovers the capability. Sensitivity to the density, kNN size and similarity measure is reported only as Spearman agreement of head *rankings*, not as agreement of the structural signature or the recovered accuracy, and the density/kNN sweeps are run on a single community-detection method with frozen labels. Everything is measured at one token position, the final prompt token, and one assigned unit, the head. Nothing here explains *how* the loss of within-community connectivity produces worse answers.

## What we take from it

A method and a warning, in that order.

- **The failure signature is a differential statistic with a prompt-perturbation null, and the null is what makes it readable.** A −0.31 pp shift means nothing on its own; a −0.31 pp shift against a 0.24 pp ceiling from paraphrasing the honest prompt is a finding. For our white-box model this is the cheapest available form of the "same statistic, matched non-target condition" control: whenever we claim that condition A reorganises some structure relative to condition B, the control arm is the *same* comparison under a paraphrase or a benign prompt variant, not a random baseline.
- **A cross-bank partition (fit communities on one question set, measure on a disjoint one) is the clean way to keep a fitted structure from explaining itself.** This is the positive version of run 780's caution about a matrix scoring its own decomposition: the authors fit on bank A, measure on bank B, and hold labels fixed across conditions — so the honest graph gets no fitting advantage and the comparison is between two measured graphs under one partition.
- **The intervention is validated by localisation controls, not by magnitude of effect, and the heading they add is worth stealing: layer-matched and magnitude-matched random sets.** Given up to 50 pp of recovery on one organism, "the heads matter" is not the claim — "these heads rather than equally large heads in the same layers" is, and the magnitude table (1.09–1.22× random) is what closes the obvious alternative explanation. Any ablation we report should ship a magnitude-matched arm; run 774's head-vs-neuron alphabet check is the sibling caution.
- **The honest cost is a first-class column, and here it is genuinely small (≤1.25 pp), which is the opposite of run 774's collapse case.** Worth pairing them: an intervention with a huge target effect and zero utility cost is a real effect; a huge target effect with collapsed utility is destruction, and only the paired column distinguishes them.
- **Warning, and the sharpest thing in the paper for us:** graph-based selection beats *activation-change-based selection* by 50 pp on one model and 32 pp on another. Two differential statistics computed from the same pairs of forward passes — how much a head's activation changes, versus how much its community relation changes — pick different heads, and the relational one wins where the suppression is most deeply trained in. If our model ranks units by how much their activations move between conditions, this is direct evidence that the ranking is the wrong object when the behaviour is realised through re-coordination rather than through magnitude.
- **Cut:** the paper's own structural signature is a single scalar per partition, and the abstraction offered at the end (functional networks for other misaligned behaviours) is untested here.
