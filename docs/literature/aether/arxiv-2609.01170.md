# Pre-carved Niches: The Formation Dynamics of Modular Task Partitions in Early LLM Training

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.01170
- **Authors / organisation:** Guangqi Li, Yongxin Li; Zaozhuang University
- **Date:** 2026-09-01
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01170
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Han et al. (2026) showed that large instruction-tuned models divide their most-attributed neurons into domain-selective modules whose overlap mirrors human cortical networks, but left the causal question open: where does the partition come from? Two competing stories exist. One says modularity emerges because separate task populations must avoid interfering with each other while learning; the other says the final partition is a path-dependent accident of early training noise. Both stories had never been tested directly because everyone had measured finished networks, never the process. This paper asks what a task partition looks like at the very first steps of training and how it gets there, on the theory that a partition which exists before any learning is not produced by learning pressure.

## What they studied

A Pythia-410M trained from scratch (24 layers, 4,096 MLP neurons) on a pure Pile stream, in two trajectories: one bf16 and one fp32 at seed 1, both with the same architecture and data order. Fourteen minimal-pair tasks in four domains — eight language tasks (including three determiner–noun agreement variants: regular, irregular, and with an adjective), two theory-of-mind tasks, four physical-reasoning tasks — all borrowed from the Han et al. battery. The unit of analysis is the per-task *roster*: the top-983 MLP neurons (1% of the 98,304) by signed attribution, i.e. the same attribution-patching instrument Han et al. used, applied unchanged at every single training step rather than once to a finished model. Pairwise structure is measured as a single-sided overlap fraction |A∩B|/983, deliberately *not* Jaccard and deliberately not the earlier paper's roster size.

## How they did it

Attribution patching is run at every step on the same minimal pairs: a neuron's score is the interestingness of its activation difference between the original and alternative prompt, multiplied by the gradient of the logit-difference between them, aggregated over examples at the final prompt position; a task's roster is its 983 neurons with positive signed score. Alongside it the authors record five per-neuron arrays (gradient norm, effective update norm, weight norm, activation frequency, second moment), task-conditioned gradients for all fourteen tasks, and a first-order loss decomposition per neuron per task, with each probe assigned in the paper's own table to one confound it is there to rule out. Overlap between two tasks is reported as a *deviation* — raw overlap divided by an "attribution substrate" measured as 0.0422, the mean step-1 overlap of two non-dominant sibling pairs — rather than as a multiple of the random expectation of 1%. Event timing is obtained from first differences of the mean dominant-pair overlap series, with every event size given in sigma units under two calibrations at once (a robust MAD estimate over the quiet pre-event plateau, sigma = 0.00754, and the same estimator over the whole run, sigma = 0.0249). Rosters are adjudicated for turnover and migration on a fixed grid of 117 per-step cases against frozen thresholds. Two attribution "calibers" (signed top-k and absolute-value top-k) exist in the pipeline; the paper freezes one as primary because their rosters overlap at Jaccard 0.28 and their scores correlate at Spearman 0.30, and never mixes them.

## What counts as an explanation here

A developmental, feature-level account of a modular partition, explicitly not a mechanistic one. The object is a time series of overlap between task rosters, decomposed into three levels which are kept separate on purpose: what shapes the *map* (a construction-level structural separation, output word-form, and an architecture-level shallow-layer concentration band), what shapes the *dynamics* (two sharp lock-in jumps with a stable neuron skeleton inside a churning periphery, plus gradient-supply asymmetry that never reaches weights), and what the authors say they cannot explain at all (why structural separation reshapes attribution alignment in a *random* network, where the 40% layer-0 band and the substrate floor come from). The paper pre-registers a scale threshold and a 22-task orthogonal family rather than claiming generality. Eleven falsification cards are treated as first-class results, each with an explicit verdict, and a public anomaly registry lists the observations that remain unresolved rather than dropping them.

## How they judged success

Event significance is reported under two sigma calibrations simultaneously, since the same jump is 20.4 sigma quiet-window and 6.2 sigma global — the paper treats the disagreement as the reason to publish both. Gradient supply to the loser population is scored against random same-size rosters, with the measured ratio sitting 9.5–11.5 sigma below the control mean at every step. Overlap effect sizes are scored against the substrate, not against chance (the paper notes 15.26x chance equals about 3.6x substrate and treats conflating the two as an inflation). Reproducibility gates: step-1 dominant overlap is reproduced across two trajectories (0.1404 vs 0.142) and across initialisation seeds (coefficient of variation 0.036); the layer-0 band is checked across six corpus variants. Roster stability is adjudicated with frozen retention < 50% (turnover) and centroid shift >= 2 layers (migration) thresholds on 117 cases. Every false hypothesis gets a card with its own quantitative bar (a hyperbolic fit R^2 <= 0.21, a supply/norm correlation of -0.27, a retention floor of 72.7%).

## What they found

The map is there before training: at step 0, with random weights and no update applied, attribution patching already recovers the partition — the dominant pair (regular–irregular) shares 0.153 raw overlap, about 3.6x the substrate, while the adjective-sibling pairs sit at 0.028–0.029, *below* the substrate. Step 1 reproduces it (0.140, about 3.3x). The winner tasks place 39–45% of their rosters in layer 0, and that band is invariant across six corpus variants before and after training; the adjective variant's layer-0 attribution is exactly zero across all 98,304 neurons, and a mismatch corpus restores it to 0.405, so the zero is a corpus property and not a pipeline boundary. The partition locks in through two sharp transitions rather than growing: a first jump at step 18→19 (+0.067, 8.9 sigma quiet-window) and a second at 79→80 (+0.154, 20.4 sigma quiet-window / 6.2 sigma global, the largest event of the run), carrying overlap to 0.53–0.61. The second jump does not track the learning rate (which grew 3.33x while jump amplitude grew 2.36x; LR-normalised jump size declines; event-time correlation +0.021), and no LR ablation was run, so the trigger is left as an open candidate set. The locked state is high-churn rather than a frozen set: old-member retention inside the first lock window is only 60% (run2) / 44% (run1), and at neuron resolution run2's shared set is a 165-neuron skeleton that constitutes >= 90% of the set from step 24 onward, surrounded by 1,124 neurons that enter and leave at least three times, 240 transient, 353 late recruits and 421 unclassified, out of 2,303 distinct neurons that ever entered. Anchoring — final winners that already carried positive attribution at step 1 — is 79% in run1 and 53–64% in run2. Winners receive 2.25x the loser's gradient supply rising to 2.73x (task-conditioned caliber 4.37 → 4.19), yet the Adam-normalised effective-update ratio stays near 1 and weight-norm ratio near 1 with only 0.40–0.82% drift, so the deprivation is confined to the gradient layer. The language domain's loss decomposition drifts from -0.29 to -1.77 while physics and theory-of-mind stay flat within ±0.05, the adjective task runs at about a tenth of the winners' magnitude with weak learning after step 92, and physics within/cross-domain overlap sits on the substrate (ratio median 1.0199, 81 of 85 steps inside [0.8, 1.25]) with only a two-task burst at steps 19–23 that decays. Eight quantitative falsifications are reported (no turnover or migration, 0/117 cases; no absolute gradient cutoff, loser supply never below 0.59 of the control baseline; no one-shot irreversibility, overlap retracing to 0.19 and recovering to 0.30; a weight-equilibrium relation rejected at r = -0.27; three mechanistic factor hypotheses rejected), plus three from the extended window. The authors conclude that interference avoidance cannot be the origin and that contingency survives only in a weak form, since topology is reproducible while roster identity and event timing vary.

## Limits and open problems they name

The feature-locking result holds inside a single construction family with three template variants; generality is untested and the 22-task orthogonal family that would test it is design-frozen and hash-bound but not run. Dynamics rest on two trajectories of one 410M model, and the two differ in precision (bf16 vs fp32) with seed, LR schedule and batch identity *unconfirmed*, so cross-trajectory differences are not cleanly attributable — the conflict-and-cleaning cycle appears in run1 only and is reported as a property of that run. Relative deprivation is correlational with no supply-equalisation intervention. Physics is not learned at 410M, so the scale threshold is a pre-registration, not a measurement; the whole of the 2.8B third act has frozen criteria on one side and zero measurements on the other. Two localisation calibers are non-interchangeable and which one defines module identity is an explicit open question (a loser module whose attribution roster is fluid at 4.8% anchoring while its loss-decomposition working set is stable at Jaccard 0.652). The step-0 separation experiment produced a direct contradiction the authors register instead of resolving: a random word inserted between determiner and noun does not collapse layer 0 while an adjective interruption does.

## What we take from it

**A framing, and the one our model most needs.** This is a white-box reading taken at every step of a training trajectory rather than at a checkpoint, and it shows that a partition can be *present before there is anything to explain* — at step 0 the network has no circuits, only statistical alignment between input structure, random features and the output head, and the task-overlap map already exists at 3.6x its own floor. For a model that claims to predict what a network will do, this is the sharpest possible warning: recovering structure from units is not the same as recovering a computation, and a readout run on an untrained network is a readout of the *task and the architecture*, not of the learning. Concretely, any per-unit attribution we produce needs a step-0 (or random-init) arm as a routine control, and a structural reading that survives at initialisation must be reported as substrate.

**A measurement discipline worth adopting wholesale.** Three practices transfer directly: (1) score overlap as a deviation from an *empirically measured substrate* (mean overlap of a non-dominant but structurally similar pair) rather than from the random expectation, because the random expectation is not the relevant zero; (2) publish every event size under two calibrations when the calibration is a choice, since 20.4 sigma and 6.2 sigma are the same event and only the pair is honest; (3) freeze a caliber when two defensible ranking rules disagree (roster Jaccard 0.28 between signed and absolute top-k) and label every conclusion with the caliber it came from, rather than mixing — this is the same failure mode as run 766's composite score without its two factors.

**A warning about aggregate stability, with a mechanism.** The stable-looking overlap curve is carried by a 165-neuron skeleton over a periphery where 1,124 neurons are in and out at least three times. Any claim of the form "this module persisted" is a claim about a core that must be separated from a fluid boundary layer, and the separation is cheap: track per-unit membership over time and report fate classes. This is run 763's "a description that is not more similar to itself than to its neighbours is a single-sample description" arriving as a *time* split instead of a data split.

**A useful negative about causality.** Gradient supply to the loser is 2.25–2.73x below the winner at 9.5–11.5 sigma from a matched random control, and it produces no asymmetry in effective updates or weight norms. Deprivation at one layer of the pipeline is not deprivation at the next, and the paper declines to call it a mechanism. When we report a per-unit score that we hope is causal, we owe the same propagation check downstream.
