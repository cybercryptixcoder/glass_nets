## What they wanted, and why
Training dynamics are normally read off the loss surface — curvature, sharpness, gradient noise — which describes where optimisation sits right now, not where it has been. The author starts from the observation that in a high-dimensional model these two readings can diverge sharply: step-level gradients may be large and erratic while their sum over ten thousand steps concentrates into one coherent direction. The goal is to characterise that cumulative geometry directly, and then to establish *who* makes it: the objective, or the optimiser. The larger ambition is to move the vocabulary of implicit bias from instantaneous gradient properties to accumulated update dynamics, as a measurable property of the training process that a small model can exhibit and a reader can re-run.

## What they studied
A single 51M-parameter decoder-only GPT-2-family transformer: 8 layers, width 512, 16 heads, feed-forward width 2048, trained on TinyStories. The load-bearing complication is a second objective: with probability 0.10 each sequence is replaced by a synthetic codeword–value probe, and the loss is next-token cross-entropy plus twice (later four times) the probe cross-entropy. The unit of analysis is the **cumulative parameter displacement since initialisation**, vectorised over the "trunk" — the attention and MLP weight matrices only, excluding embeddings, masks, positional encodings and layer norms, roughly 25M numbers per checkpoint and 3.1M per block. Dynamics are sampled at 200-step intervals for 51 checkpoints over 10,000 steps, two seeds (42, 271).

## How they did it
1. **Trajectory PCA without centring.** Stack the displacements from the first checkpoint as rows of a 51-row matrix and take its SVD; report the fraction of squared drift carried by the first singular vector. Centring is deliberately skipped, on the argument that subtracting the mean displacement would strip out the monotone drift the analysis is trying to isolate.
2. **Rolling windows.** Recompute the leading direction inside windows of 10 consecutive checkpoints (about 2,000 steps) and measure adjacent-window alignment, plus alignment of each window to an "early" direction computed over steps 1–4k and a "late" direction over 4k–10k. This separates local tangent stability from global reorientation.
3. **Decomposition.** Split each checkpoint's displacement into a signed coordinate along the global direction and the orthogonal residual, then fit piecewise power laws in four time windows.
4. **Alignment of updates versus gradients.** Compute the cosine between the global direction and (a) per-batch gradients and (b) the 200-step cumulative parameter change. The comparison is the paper's core evidence that the direction is an integration artefact.
5. **Optimiser controls.** Same model, data, seed, schedule and clipping under AdamW, plain SGD, SGD with momentum, and SGD with Nesterov momentum and decoupled weight decay; plus a sweep of AdamW's second-moment coefficient β₂ over 0.99, 0.95, 0.90, 0.80 and 0.0.
6. **Curvature.** A Rayleigh quotient along the dominant direction, computed as the mean squared projection of per-sample gradients (one matrix–vector product against the Fisher diagonal approximation), divided by the mean quotient over ten random orthogonal directions — an anisotropy ratio.
7. **Reheating.** From the final checkpoint, restart with fresh optimiser state and a doubled probe weight, and watch whether probe accuracy can be driven back up and whether it stays.

## What counts as an explanation here
A geometric decomposition of the trajectory into one slow direction plus a transverse residual, given an operational mechanism — momentum integrates a weak persistent sign bias, second-moment normalisation down-weights high-variance coordinates and so keeps the coherent ones — and then anchored to an existing theory (the author's own intra-signal gap framework) by showing that the slow direction is the single mode above the largest consecutive singular-value ratio in the rolling-window Gram spectrum. It is predictive in a weak, ordinal sense: it says what should happen under an optimiser swap or a β₂ change, and the monotonic degradations it reports are consistent with that. It does not predict magnitudes, and it explicitly declines to claim a formal dynamical-systems separation, offering the slow–fast picture as an observation about two regimes of update geometry rather than a theorem.

## How they judged success
Rarely against a task benchmark. The claim "the trajectory is dominated by one direction" is judged by variance explained (PC1 fraction, per block and globally), with the reference value being what an isotropic random walk would give — essentially zero concentration. The claim "it is an optimiser artefact, not gradient structure" is judged by a 20–30x ratio between accumulated-update alignment and per-batch-gradient alignment against an isotropic noise floor, plus the optimiser and β₂ ablations held at matched model and data. Two matched-loss comparisons are attempted but, as below, neither is a proper match. Probe out-of-distribution accuracy is carried along as the behavioural label and used in correlations, and its oscillation is reported as phenomenology without being explained. Stability across two seeds is quoted as agreement within 1–2%.

## What they found
- **One direction dominates.** The leading component explains 78–81% of squared drift in every one of the eight blocks, in both seeds; over the full trunk, 71.4% and 71.7% of row-normalised variance (79.1% / 79.3% un-normalised), with the second component at about 12%.
- **Locally smooth, globally curved.** Adjacent rolling windows align at a mean cosine of 0.80 (0.79 in the other seed), with a minimum of 0.75 / 0.70 near step 5000. Early-versus-late phase directions agree at only 0.32 — a rotation of about 71° — and the late phase is *more* rank-1 concentrated (81% versus 60%) while pointing elsewhere.
- **There is a dead zone.** Around step 3500 the rolling direction aligns 0.02 with the early direction and 0.16 with the late one: the trajectory passes through a region belonging to neither, just before the point where the probe weight doubles. The author shows a handoff table (0.83/0.16 → 0.61/0.28 → 0.02/0.16 → 0.21/0.59 → 0.20/0.69 → 0.18/0.29 → 0.18/0.02) and reads it as continuous bending of a slow manifold across a roughly 2,000-step zone.
- **Three phases by exponent.** Backbone growth exponent goes +2.07 (0–2k), +0.67 (2–4k), +0.15 (4–6k), −0.19 (6–10k) for seed 42 (seed 271 within 0.02–0.05); residual exponent goes +1.24, −0.39, −1.33, +0.36. Regime-averaged, the backbone exponent drops from +1.74 to −0.08 across the reweighting. Fits are reported at R² above 0.85, most above 0.97.
- **The correlation with task performance flips sign.** Residual norm and probe accuracy correlate at +0.85 / +0.76 in the first 4k steps and at −0.73 / −0.79 in the last 4k. Early transverse motion is productive exploration, late transverse motion is drift away from the probe solution. The interpretation is the author's reading of a changing correlation, not a measured causal effect.
- **Optimiser integration, not gradients.** Per-batch gradients align with the direction at about 0.008–0.012 (0.0004 in the figure caption), while 200-step updates reach 0.15–0.32 in early training — 20–30x above the isotropic floor — and flip sign from negative to positive at about step 5100, coinciding with the rotation.
- **SGD-family controls.** Plain SGD never trains (validation loss stays above 8, probe accuracy at chance). Both momentum variants train slowly to validation loss near 5.1 with probe accuracy at or below 0.015. Geometrically the contrast is stark: over the analysis window AdamW's leading component explains 61.5% with 9 components needed for 95% of drift, while the SGD runs are essentially collinear — 100% and a single component. The paper notes the difference is already present before the first probe oscillation.
- **β₂ is a graded dial.** As β₂ falls from 0.99 to 0.80 to 0, concentration falls 68.8% → 63.8% → 52.5%, early update alignment falls 0.37 → 0.33 → 0.27, drift magnitude rises 120 → 141 → about 160,000 (!), probe accuracy falls 0.951 → 0.682 → 0.005, and the early residual–performance correlation inverts from +0.86 to −0.57.
- **Reheating works transiently.** Restarting at the best learning rate (6×10⁻⁴) reaches probe accuracy 0.782 within 1,000 steps, slightly above the training-time peak of 0.777, then decays to 0.279 by step 2,000. Seed 271 shows the pattern at a lower peak (0.36–0.42). The accumulated drift is barely moved, which the paper reads as the probe basin surviving as a transient saddle inside a stiffened slow manifold.
- **Stiffening.** Curvature along the direction rises about 3,000x from step 200 to 9,600, with the anisotropy ratio spiking to 12.4 at step 4800 and relaxing to about 4.8 late.
- **Switching is transverse.** The direction between a probe-accuracy peak and the adjacent trough aligns only 0.20–0.31 with the backbone, so 80–90% of its variance is orthogonal; different switching events are near-orthogonal to each other (pairwise cosine below 0.08) and span at least ten dimensions. How much of that transverse direction the leading residual components capture differs by seed: 15–22% in one, 60–67% in the other.

## Limits and open problems they name
One 51M model, one synthetic dual objective, two seeds. Curvature is a mini-batch (32 batches) diagonal approximation, so the reported anisotropy is admitted to underestimate the true structure. The window width of 10 checkpoints sets the temporal resolution and is said to change absolute alignment values without changing the qualitative picture. Scaling to larger models, natural multi-objective settings and broader seed variation are deferred.

Two things the paper does not flag that a reader should. First, the **matched-loss comparison is not matched**: the AdamW windows used for comparison sit at validation loss below 3.4 while the momentum-SGD plateau sits at 5.1–5.2, and AdamW's pass through that loss range happens inside the first 200 steps where no checkpoints exist — so the claim "even at matched loss AdamW is non-collinear" rests on windows that are not at the same loss. Second, the **β₂ drift-magnitude multiplier is reported twice with different values** for the same quantity (about 2,000x in the mechanism section and the discussion, about 1,300x in the ablation section, against the same 120 → 1.6×10⁵ endpoints, which is arithmetically about 1,300x), and the SGD control's "PC1 ≈ 81%" is quoted against a Table 4 whose AdamW row reads 61.5% — the two numbers come from different windows, but the prose does not say so where the comparison is made.

## What we take from it
- **A measure.** Uncentred trajectory PCA with a rolling window, reported as a variance fraction plus a window-to-window alignment curve, is a cheap and direct read on how much of a model's motion is committed versus wandering — and it is only cheap because the residual is computed relative to the starting checkpoint rather than to the sample mean. That choice should be stated whenever we use it, because centring changes the answer.
- **A framing that corrects our own read-outs.** The paper's cleanest methodological product is the pair of numbers for the *same* direction: how well instantaneous gradients align with it, and how well accumulated updates do. A 25x gap between them means any conclusion we draw about a direction from a gradient-based attribution is measuring a different object from the one the optimiser is actually moving along.
- **A worked warning about hyperparameter-shaped mechanism tests.** The optimiser swap is the strongest causal element in the paper and is still confounded by loss: SGD variants fail to train, so "no backbone under SGD" may be "no training under SGD". The author is honest that momentum alone does not produce the structure and names adaptive scaling as the ingredient, but no run isolates adaptive scaling at matched loss. This is the same structure run 719 flagged — a treatment variable that is a hyperparameter, with no direction-level intervention — and it should keep us from treating this as the decisive answer to "is the edge the optimiser or the loss surface".
- **A specific instrument we can reuse.** The Rayleigh quotient computed as a mean squared gradient projection against ten random orthogonal directions needs one matrix–vector product per sample and no Hessian; if we ever want "how stiff is this direction", this is the cheapest honest version and it reports its own control.
- **A caution for probes.** The correlation between residual geometry and probe accuracy reverses sign across training. A feature that tracks a capability in one window and anti-tracks it in another is exactly the failure mode a probe fitted on a single checkpoint will inherit, and the paper demonstrates it in the simplest possible setting.
