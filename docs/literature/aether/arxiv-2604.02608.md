# Steerable but Not Decodable: Function Vectors Operate Beyond the Logit Lens

- **Key:** arxiv-2604.02608
- **Authors / organisation:** Mohammed Suhail B Nadaf (sole author; independent)
- **Date:** 2026-04-03 (v1), v2 read
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02608
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (43 pages, 14 figures, 34 tables; main body plus Appendices K, L, M, N, O, R, S read in full, A–J and P skimmed)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Activation steering presupposes the linear representation hypothesis, and a natural corollary of the hypothesis is that a task direction should be both *writable* (adding it steers behaviour) and *readable* (projecting it through the unembedding surfaces the answer). Function vectors extracted from in-context demonstrations are the canonical test object, and the standing prediction is that the two succeed or fail together. The author's practical motivation is safety: if only behaviour is checked, an injected direction that no monitoring tool can see is an unmonitored edit. The paper asks four questions — which task types survive mean-difference extraction, whether cosine alignment predicts transfer across prompt templates, whether the model's own logit lens can decode what a working function vector steers, and how this differs by family — and the third one returns the surprise.

## What they studied

Six models, size-matched at 7–9B and taken in base and instruction-tuned form: Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Mistral-7B-v0.3. Twelve tasks in five computational categories (lexical retrieval, factual retrieval, morphological transform, character/surface, compositional), each with eight task-specific templates across four surface styles (natural, symbolic, question, formal), with no template string shared between tasks. The unit of analysis is a per-task, per-template, per-layer mean-difference direction plus two parameter-free read-outs of the zero-shot residual stream. The matrix of interest is 72 task-by-model cells and, for transfer, 4,032 directed template pairs.

## How they did it

Function vectors are the difference of mean final-token residual-stream activations between 15 correct in-context demonstrations and 15 zero-shot prompts, computed separately at each template and layer, then added back at inference with a scalar gain swept over eight values with adaptive refinement, reporting best accuracy across gain and layer. In-distribution evaluation reuses the extraction template; out-of-distribution evaluation crosses all 56 ordered template pairs per task. A single threshold on mean in-distribution accuracy admits a task into the causal analyses. Decodability is measured three ways with increasing capacity: the raw logit lens (intermediate activation through the model's own final LayerNorm and unembedding, top-1/5/10 accuracy, no learned parameters); a diagonal tuned lens per layer that corrects scale and offset toward the final-layer activation; and a two-layer MLP probe over every other layer — 1,272 probes in all — each paired with an identical Hewitt–Liang control trained on a shuffled input-to-label map, with decodability gated on the *selectivity* between real and control. A separate vocabulary projection asks which tokens a vector pushes toward. Cross-template transfer is then attributed causally by patching the residual stream between a wrong-template and a right-template run on up to 60 stratified cases per model. Cosine alignment versus transfer is tested both pooled and with a hierarchical regression that first absorbs task identity.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a circuit and not a dictionary: the explanation is a **behavioural/read-out dissociation with a cost-theoretic reading attached** — the vector is argued to encode an instruction (a computation to run) rather than an answer (a token to emit), and the evidence is that the instruction survives editing while the answer never becomes readable through the output head. It is explicitly partial: the paper says it quantifies *how far apart* steerability and decodability are and gives three decoder tiers to bound why, without claiming to know the pathway. What it does predict is testable and was pre-registered: which tasks extract well, and which cells of the 2×2 matrix should fill.

## How they judged success

Every claim is a cell count or a correlation on the same 72/4,032 grids. The 2×2 matrix {logit lens decodes, fails} × {steering succeeds, fails} at a 0.10 threshold is the headline instrument; its robustness is checked by the tuned lens (how many dissociations *close* under dialect correction) and the MLP probe (how many close under a bounded nonlinear decoder, and how many survive), with the Hewitt–Liang selectivity gate as the veto on probe shortcutting. Transfer is scored by the in-distribution/out-of-distribution gap and by Pearson correlation between cosine alignment and transfer, pooled and within task, with an incremental-R² hierarchical regression. Causality is scored by residual-stream patching recovery, seeking perfect (≥0.99) and zero (<0.01) endpoints per task. A pre-registered prediction grid for per-task in-distribution accuracy (with explicit ranges and a status column per task) is printed and scored, including its failures. Permutation tests (1,000 shuffles per task, Bonferroni-corrected) test whether the rate of high-alignment-but-failing-transfer pairs exceeds chance.

## What they found

The predicted cell is nearly empty and the opposite cell is populated: decodable-without-steerable has 3 of 72 instances, all on Mistral, while steerable-without-decodable has 10 of 72 spanning Llama, Gemma and Mistral, and both-succeed has 58. The gap is negative everywhere — steering accuracy meets or beats best-layer logit-lens top-10 on every task on every model — with extremes of −0.91 (first_letter on Llama Instruct: 0.960 steered against 0.047 readable) and −0.82 (country_capital on Llama Base: 0.880 steered at layer 4 against 0.056 readability at layer 32, the model's last). Correcting for representational dialect barely helps: of 14 steerable-not-decodable pairs, tuned-lens translators close 1 (93% persist), with a mean change of −0.005 (p = 0.113), and the correction magnitude *anti*-correlates with readability improvement across layers (r = −0.478, p < 10⁻⁶) because the reconstruction objective favours the dominant syntactic/positional directions. A two-layer MLP probe closes 5 of the 10 remaining cells by finding nonlinearly encoded answers (up to 0.912 top-10 from a 0.081 logit-lens floor, selectivity +0.57) and leaves 5 invisible to every decoder tried — and the Hewitt–Liang control is what stops the probe from falsely declaring object_color readable on all six models, where real top-10 of 0.82–0.88 is matched or beaten by a control that never sees the input. Steered vectors project to incoherent token distributions even at above 0.90 accuracy (correct-output fraction in the top fifty of 0.003–0.035); the sole partial exception is a character task whose vector projects onto single-character tokens without naming the right letter. Layer-wise, steering is best very early (mean 11.0, 6.5, 5.2 on three cases) while readability appears only late; the median gap between the best injection layer and the first decodable layer is 12 layers, maximum 26 — the paper's most portable new statistic. Patching gives the difficulty hierarchy causal teeth: past_tense, plural, first_letter and antonym reach perfect recovery on four or five of six models while reverse_word and sentiment_flip recover nothing on five of six at any layer, and recovery peaks land in the same layer bands as optimal injection. Families then diverge: Mistral vectors rewrite intermediate representations (a post-steering readability delta up to +0.906), whereas Llama and Gemma vectors leave near-zero traces yet steer just as well, so the author posits representational versus modulatory mechanisms and corroborates it with patching recovery and vector-norm correlations. Finally, the earlier pooled negative cosine–transfer correlation dissolves at task scale (pooled r between −0.199 and +0.126; cosine adds at most ΔR² = 0.011 over task identity, whose R² alone is 0.402–0.585) — within-task geometry is near-zero and easy/hard task mix alone manufactures the pooled sign. Instruction tuning raises steering accuracy (+0.21 Llama, +0.09 Gemma, +0.07 Mistral) without changing the dissociation. Pre-registered difficulty predictions were badly beaten in both directions: four tasks greatly exceeded their range and two of three character-task "negative controls" were falsified (capitalize 0.473 against a predicted ceiling of 0.05), which the author reports rather than re-tunes, using one frozen threshold instead.

## Limits and open problems they name

All tasks have single-token or short answers; only 7–9B models, so larger ones may become more unembedding-aligned and shrink the gap. Only mean-difference extraction is tested. The logit lens may fail at early layers for reasons no translator fixes; the MLP probe is one architecture and the paper concedes a larger or attention-based decoder might recover more of the 5 surviving cells, though the selectivity gate bounds what any decoder could claim. Eight templates do not cover adversarial phrasings. The dual-mechanism claim is argued from three correlational signals and not tested at head level — the paper names attention-head patching as the next step. Patching is run only on in-distribution-gated tasks, so the hardest tasks are excluded from the causal read-out by construction.

## What we take from it

- **The warning that matters for our own harness:** a direction that reliably moves behaviour is not evidence that the network's output head can see it. Every handle our white-box model publishes should carry two separate scores and, crucially, the *joint* verdict, because our folder has repeatedly reported one and inferred the other.
- **A three-tier decoder ladder with a selectivity gate.** The specific design to copy is the Hewitt–Liang control trained on a shuffled label map with decodability *gated* on real-minus-control, not on raw probe accuracy. It caught six false positives in one low-entropy task whose label marginals alone reach 0.88 top-10. Any probe we report on a task with a small answer alphabet needs this control or it is measuring the alphabet.
- **A temporal-gap statistic.** Best injection layer versus first decodable layer, reported as a number, distinguishes "the model holds the answer and is about to say it" from "the model was handed an instruction it must execute". Our entries have repeatedly conflated the two; the median gap of 12 layers is the calibration that makes them separable.
- **A framing that our predictive goal needs:** the vector as *instruction* rather than *answer* — which is precisely why additive steering predicts behaviour but not the token, and why our model should predict the trajectory (which computation gets selected) rather than the output distribution.
- **A pre-registration that failed and was printed anyway**, plus an IID gate frozen before the causal analyses rather than re-tuned after the misses — the antidote to the threshold-after-the-saw-it problem that run 840 showed can flip a headline's sign.
- **A caution about pooled correlations:** task identity absorbed 40–58% of transfer variance and cosine added 1.1% at most on top; the pooled sign was a composition artifact. Same structure as the marginal-subtraction lesson from run 839, with the extra step of an incremental-R² test.
