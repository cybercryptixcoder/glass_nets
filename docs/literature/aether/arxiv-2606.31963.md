# Signed-Permutation Coordinate Transport for RMSNorm Transformers

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.31963
- **Authors / organisation:** John Sweeney; Sideplane AI
- **Date:** 2026-06-30 (v1), revised 2026-11-07 (v2)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31963
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v2 HTML)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Almost every workflow that treats a model's internal units as named objects — a steering vector, an SAE dictionary, a top-k neuron list, an attribution ranking, a LoRA adapter, an optimizer state, a merged checkpoint — has to be moved from one checkpoint to another. The authors argue that "which neuron is this?" is not a well-posed question until you say which coordinate system (which *gauge*) you are speaking in, because a network can be relabelled internally without changing its function at all. Their claim is that the relabelling group is architecture-dependent, so the alignment tooling the field inherits from LayerNorm-era work is wrong for the RMSNorm models that are now standard. The larger goal is to make coordinate-indexed interpretability claims reproducible and auditable across checkpoints.

## What they studied
The unit is the residual-stream coordinate index (dimension *d*, e.g. 896 in Qwen2-0.5B, 2,048 in TinyLlama, 4,096 in a 7B). Models: Qwen2.5-1.5B/0.5B/7B, Qwen2-0.5B/1.5B, Llama-3.2-1B and Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-2-7B and Llama-2-7B-chat, TinyLlama-1.1B (including its intermediate 3T and v1.1 checkpoints and its Math/Code and Chinese variants), SmolLM2-360M, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, plus small-layer transformers, BERT-base, T5-small and ViT-B/16 as cross-architecture checks. Three settings are separated throughout: an *exact reparameterization* applied and recorded by the experimenter, a *natural* pair of checkpoints related by training, and a *trajectory* of saved checkpoints along one fine-tuning run.

## How they did it
First they characterise, algebraically, which residual-coordinate changes leave the native parameterization intact: they write down READ-side and WRITE-side weight transforms for every module and ask which orthogonal maps survive a normalization-covariance constraint. Then they derive transport rules for each artifact class (vectors, SAE encoder/decoder, LoRA factors, AdamW moments, RMSNorm gains) under the resulting group, and verify them numerically by applying a sampled group element in float32 and checking behaviour is preserved. The recovery problem — given paired activations, find the relabelling — is reduced to a linear assignment by marginalizing the sign out of the cost, i.e. matching on the absolute correlation and then reading the sign off the matched entry, solved with SciPy's Hungarian routine. They test recovery at three levels: recorded gauges (exact ground truth), natural same-lineage checkpoints (which mostly keep their inherited slots), and cross-generation checkpoints (where they keep only sparse, multi-prompt-stable subsets and then run single-coordinate activation interventions at 2, 4 and 8 standard deviations to check whether the fitted negative signs actually predict the target's logit shift). Trajectory transport composes step-to-step maps through saved intermediate checkpoints. A separate audit protocol applies an exact relabelling to a model and asks whether published coordinate-indexed artifacts (knowledge neurons, a refusal direction, a sycophancy direction, gradient-times-activation rankings) survive as names when moved back.

## What counts as an explanation here
A group-theoretic statement plus exact transport laws: the residual-stream gauge is B_d (permutations composed with independent per-coordinate sign flips) for RMSNorm and S_d up to a global sign flip for LayerNorm; each artifact type has one correct transformation; and "which neuron" is only meaningful relative to that group. It is meant to be complete within its stated scope — the native explicit-gain parameterization — and explicitly *not* a claim about unconstrained input-output identifiability, nor a recipe for aligning independently pretrained models, which they show cannot be canonicalised from function-level information alone.

## How they judged success
For the symmetry claims: held-out logit preservation under an applied relabelling in float32, with off-group controls expected to fail by orders of magnitude; an exact census of RMSNorm gain tensors over five checkpoints to check whether the joint per-coordinate gain tuples ever collide (which would enlarge the group). For recovery: permutation accuracy, sign accuracy and combined recovery against known gauges, and against the fitted-map's held-out R². For artifact transport: downstream accuracy of a transferred LoRA adapter, log-probability margin shift for transferred steering vectors, SAE reconstruction NMSE, and interpolation loss barriers measured as peak excess NLL above the endpoint chord. For the cross-generation claim: held-out causal correlation between source and target logit shifts under single-coordinate perturbation, against a sign-removed arm, a separately fitted unsigned matcher, a random-coordinate arm and a dense orthogonal map — with a prompt-block bootstrap interval, plus a pairing-destroyed control that permutes token rows to measure the null negative-sign rate.

## What they found
The LayerNorm group is S_d times a global sign flip; the RMSNorm group is the hyperoctahedral B_d of order 2^d·d!, and it is maximal whenever the gain tuples distinguish coordinates. Their census of stored gains finds layers with floating-point ties (up to 345 coordinates in one layer) but zero coordinate pairs tied across all 2L+1 residual normalizations in any checkpoint, so the joint stabilizer really is B_d. Applied relabellings preserve held-out logits to at most 1.28e-4 across seven architectures, while off-group controls (independent signs on LayerNorm GPT-2, untransformed WRITE biases) fail at 105.8 and 10.1 — six orders out. Signed-cost matching on the same activations recovers only 49.4-49.9% of the permutation, right at the predicted ceiling, whereas sign-marginalized matching recovers 100.0%; on a recorded-gauge TinyLlama this holds from 22 probe tokens upward. Transporting a Qwen2.5-1.5B SST-2 LoRA adapter keeps 0.944 accuracy (99.7% of the source adapter's gain) under signed transport, against 0.523 — the unadapted base — under permutation-only, and 0.622 for untransported raw copy; TinyLlama shows 100% vs 26.2% and 14.3%. Multi-behaviour steering on TinyLlama preserves effects to under 3e-5 mean absolute error while raw top-10 coordinate overlap falls to 0.00-0.05 out of 10. On natural checkpoints the answer splits by lineage: same-base descendants keep their inherited slots (96.2% on Qwen2.5-1.5B Base→Math, 100% on Base→Instruct and TinyLlama continued training, and same-index matching identifies the true parent of DeepSeek-R1-Distill at 1.000 self-match vs 0.915 for its nearest sibling and 0.002 across generations), so identity is the strong default; loosely fitted maps do *worse* than identity there — SAE NMSE 0.673 for identity against 0.817 for fitted S_d and 0.942 for fitted B_d on Base→Math. Across distinct pretraining runs no global map survives (held-out R² is negative for identity, S_d and B_d alike: -0.971, -0.742, -0.722), but a three-prompt-bank stability filter keeps 28 of 896 Qwen2/Qwen2.5-0.5B pairs, 10 of them negative-signed, and on those the fitted negative sign is causally decisive: +0.185 vs -0.010 at 8 sigma (sign removed), and 10/10 pairs win at every dose, replicating on TinyLlama 3/3 and Qwen1.5B 15/15 overall. Trajectory composition recovers 91.1% of cross-run Qwen2.5-1.5B coordinates at 1,500 steps against 60.3% for direct endpoint matching, with 6,728 of 6,750 single steps exact; but on Llama-3.2-1B the ordering reverses at 1,500 steps (66.9% vs 70.3%), traced to two bad local transitions six steps apart in one chain that displace ~2,000 of 2,048 coordinates, and a retained known step-0 map is 100% throughout. Merge barriers: under a planted relabelling, permutation-only alignment is *worse than no alignment* (Llama-2-7B barrier 7.69 vs 6.14 unaligned) while signed alignment drives it to 0.00; on independently seeded 10M models fitted B_d beats fitted S_d (0.42 vs 0.66, ahead of 1.68 unaligned). The audit table is the practical punchline: four published coordinate-indexed pipelines preserve behaviour under exact relabelling to ~1e-5, but raw coordinate names drop to 0/2 or 0/10 overlap until the map is applied, at which point they return to full overlap.

## Limits and open problems they name
Natural checkpoints that were not created by a relabelling compute different functions, so nothing here guarantees a global discrete correspondence between them; the sparse cross-generation subsets are exactly that, small and prompt-selected. They state the optimizer-state result as an algebraic characterisation without a resumed-training experiment. FFN gauge tracking is less stable than residual-stream tracking over long horizons, and some probe budgets are large (36K-99K tokens for same-base natural descendants; 3-4 prompt banks for cross-generation subsets) — the small-model ~500-token budget does not transfer. The sparse subsets require held-out causal validation that only worked on ~0.5-3.1% of coordinates. The independence of the initial-map 100% from any actual recovery method is acknowledged: keeping a known step-0 map is not a transport result. And dense rotations do slightly better on raw intervention correlation because they move a single coordinate into all d others, so the B_d preference is a preference for sparsity of support, not for score.

## What we take from it
This is the precondition paper for any per-unit white-box model that spans more than one checkpoint: it tells us the exact symmetry group to quotient out, the exact transport rule per artifact type, and — most usefully — that the sign half of the group is the half that tooling drops and the half that silently destroys artifacts. Concrete takes: (1) a method — sign-marginalized Hungarian matching, which is one line of change and lifts recovery from ~50% to 100%; (2) a measure — "raw coordinate overlap vs overlap after mapping" as an audit that separates a real artifact from a name; (3) a warning — the fitted negative-sign rate near 50% is the pairing-destroyed null (0.497 ± 0.008), so a diag-heavy or sign-heavy fit is not evidence of correspondence; (4) a scaffold for our own records — every unit id we publish should carry its gauge group, the mapping it was read under, and the artifact class it belongs to, since vectors, LoRA factors and first moments transform with signs and second moments do not; (5) a size/lineage prior — identity is the correct default across a fine-tuning step and a fitted map can be *worse* than identity there, so the burden of proof is on the fit, not on the identity.
