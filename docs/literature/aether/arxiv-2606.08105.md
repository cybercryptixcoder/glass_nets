## What they wanted, and why
Every account of attention sinks in this folder has treated one visual signature — a vertical stripe in the attention map — as evidence of one thing, and then argued about which thing the mechanism is: a null destination, a spectral spike, a variance artefact, a brake on over-mixing. Fesser and colleagues ask the prior question: given the same stripe, what algorithm is the head actually running? They answer with two canonical mechanisms and then turn that answer into architecture, because the practical motive is that sink mitigation has been done as if the sink were one disease. The larger goal is the one this survey exists for — a model of what the network computes that can predict what an intervention will do to it — and their specific contribution is to show that two interventions which both remove the stripe do very different things to the computation that produced it.

## What they studied
Vision transformers, both trained from scratch and pretrained. Pretrained: DINOv2-L and DINOv2-G, OpenCLIP-L-16, EVA-G, CLIP OpenAI-L, and the register-equipped DINOv2-G. From scratch: two-layer and single-layer transformers on two synthetic tasks with isotropic Gaussian tokens, and LeJEPA ViT-L trained for 50 epochs on ImageNet-1K in four variants. The unit of analysis is a *(layer, head)* pair and the sink position inside it, scored at the level of the attention row, the value carried at the sink, and the rank of the resulting attention update.

## How they did it
They first define a sink only as a position that attracts disproportionate attention, keeping the definition deliberately mechanism-blind, with an ε-strict version and a continuous strength score. Then they build the two mechanisms into synthetic tasks so the ground truth is known. The NOP task trains one head to reconstruct the input for most tokens but output a scaled-down or zero copy for tokens whose activation along a fixed gate vector is positive — so the head must suppress conditionally, and a permanently dead head cannot pass. The broadcast task trains a two-layer model to add a rotated copy of one designated source token to every position, which requires identifying the source and sharing its payload. With those solutions in hand they derive the diagnostic axes — sink value norm relative to the head's ordinary value norms, and the stable rank of the attention update — and project every detected sink in DINOv2-G onto them. Causal validation then comes in two flavours kept strictly apart: interventions on the *payload* (zero the sink value, replace it with another image's sink value, replace it with an ordinary non-sink value, zero an ordinary value as a control) which preserve the learned attention matrix, and an intervention on the *routing* (delete the sink column and renormalise the rest), which they explicitly label a necessity test rather than selective removal of a payload. For the broadcast task they add cross-example patching — swap the selected value with a donor sequence's — and score whether the induced output change aligns with the desired donor-minus-recipient payload and whether the error against a donor-patched target falls. Finally they train the four ViT-L variants: baseline, Qwen-style elementwise attention gate, the penalised global pathway, and both, counting sinks per image on 1,984 ADE20K validation images with a relaxed rule requiring the concentration on 90% of queries.

## What counts as an explanation here
Two named algorithms plus a pair of measured signatures that distinguish them in a checkpoint, and a small zoo of constructive propositions that say what each algorithm forces about the weights. It is deliberately partial and says so: real models may spread broadcast across layers or use higher-rank shared signals. It does predict: a head that is doing NOP must have a near-zero sink value (Lemma 1), a head that broadcasts must produce an approximately rank-one update with a content-scale sink value (Lemma 2), a shared additive update must not contract centred variance (Lemma 4), and conditional routing to a null can be achieved by a spectral spike in the query-key interaction *or* by a massive sink activation, interchangeably (Lemma 3). The architecture section adds a weaker kind of prediction — a statement about where the computation will go when you give it somewhere else to sit.

## How they judged success
Observational separation of sink clusters on the two diagnostic axes, then causal agreement with that separation: for NOP-like sinks, near-zero effect of zeroing or donor-replacing the sink value against a large effect of ordinary-value insertion or rerouting; for broadcast-like sinks, the reverse, plus payload-alignment cosine and donor-target error reduction under cross-example patching. Controlled-task task MSE with per-query-type RMS decomposition and a shared-component energy fraction. For the ViT-L interventions, sink counts per image under a fixed counting rule (with Appendix E.2 reporting the same models under a stricter definition), ImageNet top-1 from the trainer's online linear probe, and frozen-backbone ADE20K and Pascal VOC 2012 linear segmentation mIoU. The honest part of the evaluation design is the register and gating sweep in Appendix C.1 — three seeds per variant — which the authors explicitly say is a *different* set of backbones from the four single-seed models behind Table 1 and is not comparable with it.

## What they found
Both mechanisms are real in pretrained ViTs and coexist. Sinks hand off from [CLS] in early layers to patch tokens in deeper layers in all three of DINOv2-L, DINOv2-G and OpenCLIP-L-16, and sink behaviour is concentrated in a sparse set of heads — some acting as sinks for nearly 80% of inputs while their neighbours never do. Projected onto the diagnostic axes, DINOv2-G sinks split into two modes: an early-to-middle cluster with value norms below 0.2× the baseline norm (NOP-like) and a deeper cluster with standard norms and approximately rank-one updates (broadcast-like). Registers relocate rather than eliminate this: register tokens absorb most of the sink mass and then divide into the same two phenotypes. The causal tests confirm the split — for NOP-like sinks, zeroing the sink value moves task representations barely at all while putting an *ordinary* value at the same high-attention destination is strongly disruptive; for broadcast-like sinks zeroing is disruptive, but replacing with the head's mean or a donor's sink value changes much less than inserting an ordinary value, which the authors read as broadcast content that is largely image-invariant in these heads, qualifying their own mechanism. In the controlled broadcast model, patching the second layer's sink with a donor's moves 99.99% of the output-change energy into the component shared across positions, aligns with the desired payload at cosine 0.944, and drops donor-target MSE from 1.914 to 0.230; the same patch at layer one aligns at only 0.162, so a layer that is visibly a sink and causally necessary need not be the layer carrying the transferable payload. On architecture: gating removes detected NOP sinks (0.25 to 0.00 per image) and *creates* broadcast sinks (0.09 to 3.80); the penalised global pathway shrinks broadcast sinks (0.09 to 0.04); the two combined give 0.53 broadcast and 0.03 NOP. On dense probes the combination is best (ADE20K 0.217, VOC12 0.610 against baseline 0.212 and 0.601) while the most effective sink suppressor alone is worst (0.181 and 0.527), so sink prevalence is not a proxy for representation quality in either direction.

## Limits and open problems they name
Real models may distribute broadcast computation across layers or use higher-rank shared signals, which would need a richer global pathway, and the right pathway design may vary with scale and objective. Their framework is not exhaustive — it separates two canonical regimes, not all possible ones. The ViT-L results behind Table 1 are single-seed per variant, evaluated at an epoch-50 checkpoint before the learning-rate schedule has annealed, and the authors state plainly that these numbers come from a different backbone set and probe protocol than the three-seed register and gating sweep. Broadcast content in the pretrained heads looks largely image-invariant, which they present as a qualification rather than a confirmation. Their penalty needed the energy ratio to carry gradients through both terms — with a stop-gradient denominator the model collapses the attention branch instead of relocating the computation, and they report both failed attempts.

## What we take from it
A **framing** and two **measures**. The framing: an attention read-out is not a mechanism until it is paired with what the attended position *writes* and in what rank, because a stripe is compatible with a null destination and with a shared broadcast and the two have opposite predictions about what happens when you touch the value. The measures are the pair worth stealing literally — sink value norm relative to the head's own mean value norm, and stable rank of the attention update — since both are one forward pass and neither requires knowing the algorithm in advance. The **method** worth stealing is the intervention taxonomy: payload interventions that hold the attention matrix fixed, separated from routing interventions that destroy it and are labelled necessity tests, plus cross-example patching for "does *this* content travel". The **warning** is the strongest thing in the paper for us: gating deleted one sink type and manufactured forty times as many of the other, so any intervention we evaluate on a component must be scored on the whole zoo of behaviours that component was borrowing from, not on the one statistic we designed it to move. Mechanical rule for us: **whenever we claim a component implements a computation, we report the read-out that identifies it together with (i) the norm of what the attended destination writes, (ii) the rank of the resulting update, and (iii) at least one intervention that removes the destination so the model can re-route — because an intervention that makes a signature disappear has only been shown to move the computation, and the place it moves to is where the next failure will be.**
