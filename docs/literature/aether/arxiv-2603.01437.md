# Post-Hoc Reasoning in Chain of Thought: Decoding and Steering Pre-Committed Answers

- **Key:** arxiv-2603.01437
- **Authors / organisation:** Kyle Cox; Darius Kianersi; Adrià Garriga-Alonso — independent; Cambridge Boston Alignment Initiative (CBAI)
- **Date:** 2026-07-23 (v2; v1 2026-03-02)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01437
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v2 body §1–§6, references, Appendices A–H; Figures 1–10 are images and were not inspected, so every number here is a table cell or a prose sentence)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Chain-of-thought is often proposed as a free interpretability channel: read the rationale, learn why the model answered. That only works if the rationale reflects the process that produced the answer. The authors want to know whether, in instruction-tuned models on simple binary tasks, the answer is already fixed in the residual stream before the first reasoning token is emitted, and whether that fixing is casual or causal — a decodable state versus a state the model actually reads out. Their larger goal is to turn "the CoT is unfaithful" from a prompt-level observation into a mechanistic one, and to say what unfaithfulness looks like from the inside when the pre-formed answer is wrong.

## What they studied
Five instruction-tuned models across two families — Gemma 2 (2B-it, 9B-it) and Qwen 2.5 (1.5B-, 3B-, 7B-Instruct) — on four binary BIG-Bench-style tasks (Anachronisms, Logical Deduction, Social Chemistry, Sports Understanding), four few-shot CoT demonstrations per prompt, 500 train / 500 test examples per dataset, A/B contents randomised to kill position bias. The unit of analysis is a **direction**: a difference-of-means probe on the residual stream at the single last pre-CoT token (the colon of the "Let's think step by step:" scaffold), one per layer, plus its unembedded token read-out. A sixth model, GPT-OSS 20B, is carried along as a reasoning-model contrast in an appendix.

## How they did it
Four stages, each conditional on the last. (1) *Premise:* accuracy with and without CoT, plus two CoT interventions on 50 correct test generations per model–dataset pair — replace the rationale with ellipses (can the answer survive the removal of its rationale?) and replace it with an incorrect rationale that entails the opposite answer, generated per-model so it resembles the model's own reasoning. (2) *Decode:* build the difference-of-means direction from training activations at the last pre-CoT token, score held-out examples by cosine with it, and report AUC per layer. (3) *Steer:* at the best-AUC layer, add α·w at **every decoding position after the prompt** (α swept in steps of 2 up to ±20), and compare answer-flip rates against a per-example random direction that is orthogonal to w and norm-matched (⟨r,w⟩ = 0, ‖r‖ = ‖w‖), 50 test examples, 95% Wilson intervals, settings with fewer than 20 parsed generations dropped. (4) *Classify the traces:* GPT-5-mini labels each steered-and-wrong trace on two booleans (any false premise? does the conclusion follow from the stated premises?) crossed into a 2×2 of sound / non-entailment / confabulation / hallucination; 200 traces re-judged for consistency. A logit-lens table applies the unembedding to ±w for every pair.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **direction plus an equivalence claim**: the probe direction is meant to be a causal representation of the pre-committed answer, and the evidence standard for "representation" is explicitly two necessary conditions — it predicts the answer, and intervening on it moves the answer more than an equally large arbitrary perturbation. The authors then argue against two rival readings (generic reasoning collapse; a CoT-mediated upstream feature) using the *typology of induced reasoning failures* rather than a circuit. So the explanation is a probe, partial by construction, with a stated-and-narrowed interpretation: not a dedicated "my answer is A" feature, but task concepts (implausibility, invalidity, temporal inconsistency) whose activation in context suffices to fix the answer. It is predictive in the concrete sense that the direction should forecast the answer before any reasoning exists to read.

## How they judged success
Held-out AUC of the layerwise probe; accuracy with versus without CoT, and answer-change rates under the two CoT interventions (the premise test); steering flip rates over a coefficient sweep with 95% Wilson CIs, against a norm-matched orthogonal-direction baseline, plus a parse-failure ledger at large |α|; a four-way reasoning typology from an LLM judge with a repeat-judging consistency table; a logit-lens token table as an independent (if weak) semantic check on the direction. There is no held-out-by-model or held-out-by-dataset split, no seed variation, and no test of the direction's *specificity* against other probes of unmatched semantics (e.g. a second task's direction at the same layer).

## What they found
The premise holds on most pairs: with CoT removed, flip rates sit at or below 20% in 18 of 20 model–dataset pairs (exceptions both on Sports, Gemma 2 9B at 52% and Qwen 2.5 1.5B at 32%), while substituting an incorrect rationale flips more often and task-dependently — above 50% on Anachronisms for every model (52–78%). Pre-CoT probes are strong where CoT buys little: AUC 0.988–1.000 on Social Chemistry across all five models, 0.924–0.961 on Sports, and 0.688–0.878 on Logical Deduction — the one task where no-CoT accuracy collapses and CoT helps most, so probe strength anticorrelates with CoT usefulness. Steering along the probe direction flips answers more than the orthogonal baseline on every model–dataset pair; the margin is largest on the bigger models, and the authors attribute that to larger models resisting arbitrary directions rather than to better probes. The baseline is not negligible by design — a binary task under general reasoning degradation converges on 50% flips, and the orthogonal curves saturate near that — which is the honest reason the comparison is against saturation, not against zero. Failure typology: increasing |α| shifts steered traces toward hallucination, Logical Deduction has the highest hallucination rates throughout, and confabulation cases are coherent and conclusion-aligned (a false premise planted early, supports selected to fit it) — the argument against "this is just collapse". Logit lens on ±w returns task-appropriate concepts for the larger models (impossible, wrong, inconsistent, unacceptable, vs positive/plausible), and near-uninterpretable multilingual or code fragments for Gemma 2 2B and Qwen 2.5 7B. Judge consistency over 200 re-judged traces is 81.5% on the four-way label, 92.0% on "false premise" and 89.5% on "conclusion follows". On GPT-OSS 20B the probe is much weaker on three of four tasks (only Anachronisms above 0.9) and steering barely beats the orthogonal baseline — the authors read this as the answer being computed inside the reasoning trace for reasoning-trained models.

## Limits and open problems they name
Two are load-bearing and stated. First, the steering is applied at every position after the prompt **including the tokens that emit the final answer**, so part of the effect could be direct biasing of answer-token selection rather than propagation from a pre-CoT belief; restricting the intervention to the CoT region is left to future work. Second, the direction is recovered from a difference of means, so superposition is unaddressed, and whether a "pre-committed answer" exists as a discrete feature at all is questioned by the authors themselves; their logit-lens evidence points to general concepts rather than answer labels, and they settle for "concept whose activation suffices for a human to infer the answer". I add three limits of my own: the CoT-removal premise is a *context* intervention (the demonstrations still contain rationales) and not a clean ablation of the computation; all four tasks are binary with a 4-shot template, so the 0.99 AUCs are on a two-class separation whose denominator is one bit; and the negative evidence (Logical Deduction probes ~0.69, GPT-OSS mostly flat) is read as "the answer is computed in the CoT" without a positive control showing the probe would find such an answer if it existed.

## What we take from it
- **Measure (import):** the two-condition test for calling a direction a representation — held-out AUC *and* a norm-matched orthogonal steering baseline with a stated saturation point. The orthogonal arm is the control this folder has repeatedly found missing: it holds perturbation magnitude fixed and varies only semantic content, so it separates "the direction matters" from "moving the state matters".
- **Warning (import):** the predictive/anticipatory read-out is not a licensed explanation of the computation. A probe with 0.99 AUC on a two-class task has shown that the answer is linearly present, not that the model uses that direction; the causal arm is what upgraded this paper above the folder's other probe entries, and it still could not rule out a direct answer-token bias because the intervention plane covered the answer tokens.
- **Method (reusable diagnostic):** classify what a steered model *says* into entailment × premise-truth, because the two failure modes discriminate rival explanations — coherent confabulation argues against generic collapse, and non-entailment argues against a CoT-mediated pathway. A cheap LLM-judge rubric with a published re-judge consistency table is the form to copy.
- **Framing:** "anticorrelation between read-out strength and the usefulness of the written reasoning" is a usable routing rule for us — on tasks where the answer is computed in text, an activation-side predictor is not the right instrument, and the probe's own weakness is the signal that says so.
- **Warning (my own):** the paper's own open question — is the effect on the belief or on the answer token — is exactly the kind of "where does the intervention act" question (write site versus direction) that appears in this folder repeatedly; any predictor of ours that scores an intervention must state its intervention plane.
