# Contrastive Covariance Subspaces for Activation Steering

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.14025
- **Authors / organisation:** Alexandros Xenos, Manos Plitsis, Niki Foteinopoulou, Yannis Panagakis, Ioannis Patras — Tavus.io (1), University of Athens (2), Queen Mary University of London (3); Xenos and Plitsis marked equal contribution, with a joint correspondence address at Tavus.io

## What they wanted, and why
A steered model is only useful if the injected direction carries the target concept and nothing else; whatever else rides along is written into the residual stream and shows up in the text. The authors' complaint is that the standard way of building that direction — average activations for the concept minus average activations for a reference set — cancels only the confounds that were present identically in both halves of the contrast. A topic, a style of writing, a response length, a formatting habit that happens to correlate with the concept class in the demonstration data survives the subtraction and gets injected as if it were part of the concept. They want to remove that nuisance component with no training and no auxiliary model, and to keep the result a single additive vector at one layer. The larger goal is the everyday control setting: a direction that raises the intended behaviour without the fluency collapse that strong steering usually causes.

## What they studied
Three instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-2-9B-Instruct), one fixed injection layer per model (15 for Llama, 21 for Qwen and Gemma), and concepts drawn from four domains — AxBench open-ended concepts, the Big Five personality traits, Ekman's basic emotions, and TruthfulQA true/false statements. The unit of analysis is the steering vector itself, treated as a displacement of the residual stream's mean for one class of prompts relative to the mean over many classes, and the object they build is a low-dimensional subspace plus a projection of that displacement into it.

## How they did it
Mean-pool a prompt's residual-stream activations at the chosen layer, giving one vector per prompt. Rather than centring on a single negative reference class, centre every activation on the grand mean of the class means, so the target's displacement is measured from the middle of the concept set instead of from one arbitrary other class. Then, for each class, form its scatter matrix about that grand mean — which contains both the class's internal spread and its displacement from the origin — and solve a contrastive trace-maximisation: find the k-dimensional orthonormal frame in which the target class's activations keep their norm while the average non-target class's activations have near-zero norm. Because the objective is a difference of averaged scatter matrices, it is a single symmetric matrix and the solution is its k leading eigenvectors — one eigendecomposition, no gradients, no learned encoder. Project the target's grand-mean displacement onto that frame and use the projected displacement as the steering vector. At generation time the vector is unit-normalised and added at every token scaled by a fraction of that token's own activation norm, so the strength knob means the same thing across layers and models and transfers between the three architectures. Nuisance classes come from a separate pool of other concepts; the contrastive weight r sets how hard shared variation is suppressed relative to preserving the target's.

Their diagnostic is a planted-confounding experiment on ten AxBench concepts: cluster the instruction activations into five topic groups, then keep approximately ninety per cent of examples from a single cluster so the target class and one topic are entangled in the demonstration data. Against that they compare plain difference of means, grand-mean centring alone, their projected version, and an oracle that deletes the planted topic direction using ground-truth topic labels.

Evaluation is mostly model-judged: a prompted Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct judge rates concept presence, instruction relevance and fluency separately, one judge call per axis; the AxBench score is the harmonic mean of the three, and the BIG5 and Emotion scores are the peak effectiveness among steering strengths at which both quality ratings clear a fixed bar. TruthfulQA uses the benchmark's own fine-tuned truth and informativeness judges, reported as their product. Seven baselines are on the same grid, each tuned per concept by three-fold cross-validation over prompts with a majority vote on the chosen parameters. A blinded human study of 296 generations — ten held-out concepts, five methods, twenty-three raters, byte-identical text to what the judge scored, gold items used to exclude raters who fail them — checks the judge's ordering. A twenty-turn conversation experiment tests whether the direction keeps working when the same vector is applied turn after turn against a persistent cache.

## What counts as an explanation here
A steering vector, plus a subspace that is offered as the account of why that vector is the right one: the claim is that the directions along which the target concept varies and the other classes do not are the concept's discriminative signal, and everything the projection discards is nuisance. It is meant to be operational rather than a full description of the model — the direction is justified by its behaviour and by its geometry relative to other concepts, not by any claim about what the network computes. As a partial account it is unusually explicit about its own mechanism: the authors check the projection's effect by measuring the cosine between the resulting vector and the planted topic direction, which turns "this vector is clean" into a number.

## How they judged success
Four benchmark scores on three models (twelve model–domain cells, all won), LLM-judge ratings decomposed per axis with Pareto fronts of effectiveness against the minimum of fluency and adherence over a range of quality gates, an ungated variant for AxBench, a blinded human rating study with exact-agreement and chance-corrected agreement statistics plus per-axis win/tie/loss rates against four other methods, a planted-nuisance diagnostic with an oracle arm, component ablations (centring alone, projection alone, both) on all three models, a sweep over the number of nuisance classes, and a twenty-turn multi-turn retention experiment. Hyperparameters are tuned per concept by cross-validation; the strength is then chosen afterwards by taking the maximum score, which is the selection rule they use for every method.

## What they found
The fully tuned method leads every activation-steering baseline in all twelve model–domain cells. On AxBench the gain over the strongest baseline is 0.28 to 0.32 points on a two-point scale — roughly half again to seventy per cent — and over plain difference of means it is 0.36 to 0.47 points (seventy-five to one hundred and fifty per cent). Scores on the main table for Llama, Qwen and Gemma respectively are 80.3/76.8/92.2 on TruthfulQA's truth-times-informativeness, 0.78/0.86/0.84 on AxBench, 83.1/86.3/82.4 on BIG5 and 77.9/80.1/89.4 on Emotion, against 72.8/63.7/84.8, 0.31/0.41/0.48, 75.2/77.2/77.2 and 58.6/61.9/78.9 for difference of means. A fixed subspace rank of 32 with no per-concept tuning still beats every non-their baseline in eleven of the twelve cells, and fixing both rank and contrastive weight at 32 and 1 does so in ten; concept-specific tuning pays off mainly on AxBench, adding 0.14 to 0.19 points. Prompting (naming the concept in an instruction) remains the strongest single method on AxBench but is beaten on TruthfulQA, BIG5 and Emotion on all three models.

The planted-nuisance result is the cleanest mechanism evidence. Under topic skew the centred-but-unprojected direction's AxBench score falls 46 per cent (0.565 to 0.306) while the projected one reaches 0.543 — retaining 88 per cent of its balanced score with no topic labels — and the oracle that deletes the planted direction with ground-truth labels gets 0.583. The geometry agrees: the unprojected vector's cosine with the planted topic direction rises to 0.849 under skew, while the projected vector sits near orthogonal at 0.071.

Both components are doing separate work. Adding the projection on top raises the plain contrast by 0.28–0.36 points and the centred displacement by 0.17–0.19 across the three models. On the nuisance-pool sweep a handful of other concepts is enough — five to ten recovers most of the benefit over the zero-nuisance baseline, performance peaks at twenty (0.814 against 0.811 at ten) and declines by fifty.

Human raters reproduce the judge's ranking of all five methods; the composite is 1.01 (human) and 1.01 (judge) for this method against 0.50/0.66 for the next best, and it wins a majority of shared instructions against every baseline (58 to 67 per cent). Agreement statistics are 0.780 and 0.796 on fluency and adherence, 0.508 on concept presence; the authors attribute the low fluency rank correlation (0.194) to a ceiling — 77 per cent of items are at the maximum human score while the judge places 175 of 296 at one. In multi-turn use the joint on-concept-and-fluent fraction goes 93 to 80 per cent across twenty turns while on-concept alone rises from 95 to 98; difference of means collapses from 36 to 10 per cent as repetition sets in, the transported-activation baseline retains 52 to 37, and system prompting drifts from 91 to 71.

## Limits and open problems they name
The intervention is static and single-layer; they suggest extending the projection across layers or attention heads. Scatter matrices need enough per-concept samples and can be ill-conditioned in low-data regimes, which is a real constraint for rare concepts. Long horizons beyond twenty turns may need a decay schedule rather than a fixed vector. They note the method is dual-use — the same cleanliness helps elicit sycophancy or deception as much as it helps suppress them — and that a subspace built from second-order statistics is only as good as the concept set used to build the nuisance pool, a reliance we would add: their own sweep shows a large pool hurts, so the pool is a hyperparameter that encodes an assumption about what counts as a nuisance.

## What we take from it
A method and a warning, both usable. The method is that a concept direction can be cleaned without any labels for the confound, by keeping only the part of the contrast that lives in a subspace where the target varies and other classes do not — a cheap, closed-form construction we can run on our own residual streams whenever we claim a direction is "the concept". The warning is sharper and is the part that changes how we write: their own numbers show the un-projected vector's cosine with the planted confound rising to 0.849 while steering performance silently halves, so a direction can be a good probe of a concept and still be a mixture. Two further things we should copy: the oracle arm — deleting a confound using ground-truth labels to establish the ceiling any unsupervised filtering could reach — and the gate sweep, which re-runs the entire selection at six quality thresholds and reports where coverage shrinks, because their own table shows a comparison that looks like an improvement purely because a harder gate removed the targets the weaker methods handled badly.
