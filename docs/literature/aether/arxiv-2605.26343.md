# MechRL: Reinforcement Learning Agents perform Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.26343
- **Authors / organisation:** Barsat Khadka; School of Computing Sciences and Engineering, The University of Southern Mississippi
- **Date:** 2026-06-28
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26343
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Every automated circuit-discovery method re-does its search from nothing for each behaviour: ACDC prunes greedily, attribution patching ranks edges, edge-pruning learns a mask — but the effort spent finding one circuit buys nothing for the next. The author's framing is that circuit discovery has been automated but not amortised. The question is whether the discovery *procedure itself* is learnable — whether one policy, trained across many behaviours, can acquire a general pruning skill that transfers to behaviours it never saw. The larger goal is a cheap, reusable instrument for producing faithful subgraphs, which is the step a predictive white-box model would sit on top of: if discovery is a learned procedure rather than a per-task search, then the predictor and the discoverer can in principle be the same object.

## What they studied
GPT-2 small only (144 attention heads, 12 MLPs), as an edge-level computation graph of 158 nodes and roughly 32,000 edges, with attention query/key/value counted as separate inputs. Twelve training behaviours in three families of four surface variants each — IOI, greater-than, docstring completion — and seven held-out behaviours for transfer: gendered pronoun, subject-verb agreement, acronyms, simple syllogism, opposite syllogism, country-capital, multiple choice. Twenty clean/corrupted pairs per behaviour. The unit of analysis is the **edge** of the graph; the object learned is a pruning policy over edges.

## How they did it
First a prefilter: rank all edges by EAP-IG attribution and keep the top K = 3000 as the candidate set (K chosen as the smallest set whose faithfulness ceiling clears 0.9 for every behaviour, including the hardest). Then the MDP. State = fixed 16-dim edge features (signed attribution, importance rank, parent/child layer, layer distance, parent/child type one-hot, Q/K/V channel one-hot), fixed 7-dim node features, a binary pruning mask, and six globals (step fraction, current faithfulness, fraction of edges retained, last-step faithfulness change, candidate-set ceiling, headroom to target). Actions: stop, batch-cut of N ∈ {1, 3, 10, 30} edges, or a node cut that removes every remaining out-edge of one component; cuts are irreversible. The transition runs one forward pass with activation patching and measures the new faithfulness, so a batch cut of N is priced at one model call rather than N — the structural reason the method can compete on cost at all. Reward is potential-based shaping on Φ(f, k) = w(1 − k/K) − λ max(0, τ − f): sparsity reward minus a hinge penalty for falling below the faithfulness target, with λ set large so the target behaves as a near-hard constraint and the reward being the *increment* of Φ rather than Φ itself, which leaves the optimum unchanged. The target is per behaviour, τ_i = c_i − δ, because a single shared target is unattainable for low-ceiling behaviours and trivial for high-ceiling ones, making the objective incomparable across them. Faithfulness is f = 1 − KL/KL_cut, where KL_cut is the divergence with every edge cut, so f = 0 for the empty circuit and f = 1 for an exact match. The policy network scores each edge and each component independently with shared weights, so its parameter count does not depend on candidate-set size and one network covers every behaviour; a context vector pools mean and max of the edge and node embeddings plus the globals, and four heads (type, edge, node, value) produce the action distribution and the value estimate. Batch cuts are sampled one edge at a time, re-scoring the remaining edges after each pick so the agent does not remove two edges that only matter together. Training is PPO with GAE over mixed-behaviour rollouts, 1200 iterations, H = 128, 150 actions per episode, with per-behaviour gradients de-conflicted by PCGrad.

## What counts as an explanation here
A sparse faithful subgraph — the same output form ACDC and EAP-IG produce, measured on the same faithfulness scale, with interpretation of what the components compute explicitly left out of scope. What is new is not the form of the explanation but its *provenance*: the explainer is a policy trained once, and the claim is that it is a reusable procedure rather than an artefact of one search. Predictiveness is claimed in a narrow, checkable sense — the frozen policy predicts, for a behaviour it has never seen, which edges survive pruning — and no claim is made that the circuit predicts the model's outputs beyond the patching abstraction it is scored under.

## How they judged success
Three quantities per circuit: faithfulness f, minimality |C| in retained edges, and cost in forward passes. Because faithfulness and minimality trade off, the primary comparison is at **matched faithfulness f ≥ 0.9**. A validity battery guards against reward hacking, since a high-f score could in principle be an artefact of the reward: ground-truth component recovery (fraction of the heads named in prior human work that the circuit contains), necessity (faithfulness after ablating the circuit from the full model, near zero desired), and specificity (faithfulness of a random same-size subgraph, near zero desired). Transfer is zero-shot on seven held-out behaviours under frozen weights with best-of-sixteen rollouts and no tuning; the warm-start condition resumes training on one held-out behaviour for eighty iterations. The key control compares warm-start against a policy trained from random initialisation on the same behaviour with reward, candidate set, target and hyperparameters held fixed and more than twice the iterations (200 vs 80), so initialisation is the only difference. Baselines are ACDC (smallest circuit over a τ sweep of {0.001, 0.003, 0.01}) and EAP-IG greedy (selection under logit-difference and under KL attribution), both re-run per behaviour on the same graph and the same f.

## What they found
Zero-shot transfer works on all seven held-out behaviours: f runs 0.826 (simple syllogism) to 0.997 (gendered pronoun), circuits run 480–1175 edges, and named components are recovered at 7/7, 11/12, 7/8, 5/5, 9/9 and 3/3 where prior work names them (country-capital has no published sparse circuit and is reported as the harder cross-family test). Necessity and specificity both sit at or just below zero (−0.05 to 0.04), so the circuits are load-bearing and their identity matters rather than their size. The warm-start improves faithfulness everywhere it was not already saturated and shrinks circuits, dramatically on simple syllogism (1175 → 290 edges at f 0.826 → 0.920) and modestly elsewhere. Against training from scratch at matched faithfulness, the warm-started policy returns circuits 1.3–3.3× smaller (median 1.65) on six of seven behaviours and is no smaller but less faithful on the seventh — the author's most direct evidence that what transfers is a pruning procedure and not a per-behaviour solution, since faithfulness itself cannot separate the two (the prefilter makes the target easy to clear) and minimality is where they split. Against the prefilter's own ranking, the policy's circuit is at least as faithful as the top-|C| edges by attribution on nine of ten behaviours, with the largest gains where attribution is most diffuse and none where the score-ranked circuit is already near the ceiling; it keeps as *discordant* 30–50% of its edges from below the size-matched cutoff (against 63–85% for a random subset), and deleting those rescued edges lowers faithfulness on nine of ten behaviours. Against per-behaviour search the honest result is that the policy **loses on both axes of the trade-off plane**: ACDC returns smaller circuits on nine of ten behaviours (e.g. 84 vs 464 edges on greater-than, 67 vs 390 on gendered pronoun, 108 vs 290 on simple syllogism) at 3.4k–19.9k forward passes, and EAP-IG greedy is effectively free at selection and finds ~100-edge circuits on sharply localised behaviours; the policy sits between them, costing about 2.5k passes per deployment plus ~40k for a warm-start plus ~6×10^5 for training, so no total-compute saving is claimed. ACDC also fails outright on country-capital — no τ in the sweep reaches f = 0.9, its best being 0.72 — and EAP-IG greedy never reaches 0.9 within K = 3000 on the syllogisms and docstring under one attribution target, with the failures moving rather than disappearing when the target switches from logit-difference to KL.

## Limits and open problems they name
The prefilter is the main one: it already does part of the discovery, and it caps what the agent can reach, so a behaviour whose faithful circuit is diffuse may lie outside the candidate set entirely. Induction is the named casualty — its faithful circuit in GPT-2 small needed a candidate set of order 16,000 edges before it was reachable, far beyond a tractable action space, so it was excluded. The circuits are larger than ACDC's at matched faithfulness by design, since the policy optimises for amortised transfer rather than per-task minimality. Validation is on GPT-2 small alone, the only model with component-level ground truth, and scaling is described as a separate challenge. The stated future direction is precisely the thing our project is: learn a model of the environment — a predictor of how an edit changes faithfulness — so the agent can plan over the full 32,000-edge graph without a prefilter.

## What we take from it
A framing and a warning, both useful. The framing: if discovery is a *learned* procedure, it is a model of the network's edit-response surface, not a lookup — and the author says outright that the prefilter's replacement is a learned predictor of faithfulness under intervention. That is a clean statement of the target our own white-box model needs, and it also names the right evaluation for it: prediction quality on held-out behaviours, at matched faithfulness, against a per-behaviour search. The warning, and the sharpest methodological lesson in the entry: **when the prefilter already clears the target, the success metric stops discriminating** — faithfulness saturates within tens of iterations for both warm-started and from-scratch policies, so the only axis that still separates them is minimality. This is the folder's recurring "metric that cannot fail" defect in RL costume, and the fix here is worth copying: hold faithfulness fixed, compare size, and state that the choice of axis is what makes the comparison informative. Two smaller transfers: per-behaviour target calibration against the achievable ceiling (τ_i = c_i − δ) is a general recipe for comparing results across tasks of unequal headroom, and the discordant-edge accounting — what fraction of the answer came from below the ranking, and does deleting it hurt — is a cheap test of whether a method is doing work beyond the proxy it started from.
