# Contrastive Projection: Reading Transformer Internals by Differencing Logit Lenses

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.09902
- **Authors / organisation:** Olli Tuomi; Evident Solutions Oy (single author; LLMs used as coding/drafting assistants and disclosed)
- **Date:** 2026-09-09
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09902
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML v1: abstract, §1-§8, Tables 1-34, limitations; references skimmed)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
A logit lens on one hidden state is cheap but, at intermediate layers, mostly reads high-frequency function words that the model would say for almost any input, so it tells you little about what distinguishes two inputs. The author asks what happens if you subtract two closely matched prompts' hidden states before projecting through the unembedding, i.e. read the *difference* rather than the state. The goal is a training-free tracer that says where, in which sub-layer and which head, a particular meaning distinction is written and how it reaches the prediction site, and then to establish how much of such a reading is a fact about the network rather than about the token basis it happened to learn. The framing is a mechanistic version of contrastive explanation: you explain one case relative to a chosen foil, and the foil fixes what the explanation can be.

## What they studied
Phi-2 (2.7B) is the main subject; the compound-noun circuit is re-run on Pythia-1.4B (dense attention, GELU, parallel residual) and Qwen2.5-1.5B (grouped-query attention, SwiGLU, sequential residual). A seed control uses five Pythia-410M models that share architecture, tokenizer and data and differ only in initialization (base plus seeds 1, 3, 6, 7). The unit of analysis is a *direction in the residual stream at one position and layer* (plus its separation into per-MLP and per-head writes), and the read-out unit is the top-K tokens it aligns with after projection.

## How they did it
For a matched pair (c, k): run both, take hidden states at every layer at a chosen read position, compute the difference, project through the unembedding matrix (bypassing the final LayerNorm, since the learned shift largely cancels for a matched difference), and read the most positive tokens as belonging to one pole and the most negative to the other. No parameters are fitted; the free choices are the pair, the read position and K. The same operation is applied per position (to see where a distinction first appears and how it flows), per sub-layer via forward hooks (attention write vs MLP write, split by subtracting the layer outputs, which the author argues is valid for parallel and sequential residual streams alike), and per head for the routing head. Three rules govern contrast design: put the contrasted change several tokens into a shared preamble (the first token carries an activation roughly twenty times the norm of the rest, and any first-token difference is amplified and illegible), match the token at the read position, and match the predicted next token when you want to see content stored for downstream use rather than the position's own prediction. Two optional refinements: subtract the mean residual of same-length prose snippets when no second prompt exists, and multi-contrast triangulation — average the difference over several baselines that share the intended axis and differ on incidental ones, so the incidental axes cancel (the author gives a visibility threshold for a token-aligned component surfacing above an isotropic bath, and notes averaging lowers that bar; averaging removes unaligned remainder rather than sharpening signal). Reads are then tested: candidate concept axes are built as noun-set-mean differences, the target difference projected onto them, and each axis injected alone to see whether it moves the prediction; causality for the extractions is checked by activation patching at several positions and layers.

## What counts as an explanation here
A token-space reading of a direction: ranked vocabulary (frequently sub-word fragments) at a named position, layer and component, plus a decomposition into which axes a raw difference carries and a qualitative causal trace of where those axes are written and routed. It is deliberately partial and honest about it — the projection only sees what aligns with the unembedding, a component that aligns with no token direction is invisible, and absence from the readout is explicitly not evidence of absence in the state. It predicts where a distinction becomes legible, not what the model will output. The author labels the whole thing exploratory, with causality claimed only where patching or injection was actually run.

## How they judged success
No benchmark and no aggregate metric: the evidence is legible-versus-soup reads on hand-built minimal pairs, cross-contrast cosine alignment of the recovered direction, per-axis injection with a food-fraction readout (probability mass on food over food plus animal at the next token), leave-one-out injection of a routing direction built from three pairs into a held-out fourth, a cross-domain injection test that discriminates a source-specific mapping from a generic figurativity axis, greedy continuations as behavioural confirmation, a token-length-matched control for the real-versus-fictional entity contrasts, an energy-fraction threshold applied by hand to one example to separate a high-ranked-but-weak token from a genuinely aligned one, a probe as a sensitivity check where the token read is illegible, and the five-seed control with a concept-anchor margin (does the direction boost the concept anchors over their opposites). The author also names the failure modes they do not rule out (a subspace can look causal without being the mechanism; self-repair can mask a single-layer patch) and reports patching as a qualitative trace rather than a measurement.

## What they found
The opening contrast works as advertised: the raw lens on either "hot dog" or "cold dog" reads function words at every layer, while the difference reads food vocabulary from layer 8 and the reverse difference reads animal vocabulary, with the two poles naming what each side carries that the other does not. The compound-noun chain in Phi-2 is a two-hop structure: attention at layer 0 copies "hot" onto "dog", the MLP at layer 4 writes the compound (its contrastive norm far exceeds attention's at that layer, and "fried" appears in the MLP write but not the attention write), and one attention head at layer 5 routes it to the prediction position. Patching confirms the shape — patching "dog" early destroys the food reading and does nothing by layer 20, patching "was" is the mirror image, and patching "hot" never matters. The MLP write is distributed: the ten strongest neurons carry only about a tenth of the direction. Decomposing the contrast into edibility, petness and temperature shows all three axes are present but only two are causal, and they act as a plane: neither edibility nor petness alone carries the cold-dog state across the food/animal boundary, both together recover almost all of the effect, and temperature is inert. Across three architectures the prediction-site read is robust (food reaches it in all three) while the noun-internal read is not — legible in Phi-2, recovered only by triangulation in Pythia, and still partial in Qwen — yet a linear probe trained on food versus animal nouns classifies all three models' "dog" correctly with near-certainty, so the distinction is computed everywhere and only its token face varies. Metaphor reads as domain routing, not a figurativity flag: each adjective's direction maps onto its own target domain, is stable across sentences, survives leave-one-out injection into a held-out pair, and, decisively, imposes its own target domain when injected into another word's context. Fictional entities read as name fragments only while matched real entities read factual associations, and entropy cannot separate them — a fictional mountain and a real one predict confident heights at nearly the same entropy. The cross-seed control is the boundary the author draws: across five networks differing only in seed, the top-ranked tokens barely overlap (mean pairwise top-10 overlap around 0.08, with a wider per-contrast range reported in the table), yet the direction boosts the concept anchors in all twenty seed-by-contrast cells with positive margins. So what a computation looks like in token space is network-specific; the distinction it draws is not.

## Limits and open problems they name
Hand-constructed minimal pairs and hand-selected triangulation baselines, on models no larger than 2.7B, with mechanistic depth concentrated on Phi-2; raw contrastive norms are not comparable across layers because the residual norm grows with depth; readability of the difference through the unembedding is not guaranteed and coherence is judged by eye; triangulation was exercised on six cases with no rule for how many baselines suffice; computed-but-unemitted distinctions are invisible to any such readout and detecting them without it is called open; the causal status of the difference vector is inherited from the steering literature rather than re-established (the author ran steering tests, found nothing new, and omitted them); and the metaphor hypothesis — a metaphorical use as the literal representation plus a linear source-to-target offset — is offered as a hypothesis with a compositional test (build a target-domain direction from unrelated cues and inject it into new source words) left undone.

## What we take from it
Three things. (1) **A measurement object**: a direction's *readable token face* and the *distinction it draws* are separately measurable, and the seed control is the cheap way to see the difference — a white-box model of ours that reports "unit/feature X encodes concept C" should be scored on an anchor margin or an injection on held-out material, not on which tokens happen to top the ranked list, because here the token identity is a property of one trained network while the distinction holds in all five. (2) **A control we can copy verbatim**: the cross-domain injection test is a one-experiment discriminator between "this direction carries the domain pair" and "this direction carries a generic property of the contrast", and it disambiguated a plausible single-axis story without any additional training. (3) **A framing**: the contrastive-explanation point — what the readout surfaces is chosen by the foil, not discovered — is a warning for every probe and steering result in this folder that reports one contrast as if it read a concept; the same state difference read against a different baseline names a different axis. Also useful: the diagnostic that a high-ranked token can be below the alignment threshold, so rank is not alignment, and the honest practice of reporting patching as a pointer rather than as a causal measurement because of subspace illusions and self-repair.
