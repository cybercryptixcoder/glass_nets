# Geometry of Confabulation and Refusal in Small Language Models

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

A small model asked a factual question it cannot answer has two options: refuse, or answer anyway in fluent confident prose that happens to be wrong. The paper asks whether that choice is readable from the hidden state before the first generated token, and whether it can be pushed one way or the other. The motivating worry is methodological rather than empirical: correctness probes in this literature routinely report 70–90% accuracy against a majority baseline, and the author's argument is that most such numbers may be reading the *question* rather than the model, since a classifier that sees "which film did X direct in 2025" can predict trouble almost perfectly without any access to internals. A second confound follows from benchmark design — mixing easy pre-cutoff questions with post-cutoff ones makes "correct" and "pre-cutoff" nearly the same label, so a correctness probe degenerates into a date-mention detector. It is a step toward the same end this survey is after: deciding what a linear read of late activations is actually evidence of, and separating "the state encodes a decision the model makes" from "the state encodes a property of the world the model does not represent".

## What they studied

Four instruction-tuned models in the 1–4B range: Qwen3-1.7B as the subject (28 blocks, hidden 2048, tied embeddings), Gemma 2 2B and Llama 3.2 3B as cross-architecture replicates, and Qwen3-4B as a within-family parameter-count control. Data is a new 784-item benchmark, ConfabQA (4 domains × 3 categories), plus 800-item samples of PopQA and TriviaQA scored end-to-end through the same generation pipeline. The unit of analysis is the last-prompt-position residual at every layer — the prefill state immediately before the first generated token, chosen so the probe sees the model's commitment point rather than a state contaminated by tokens it has already emitted. The object being explained is the refusal-versus-confabulation decision, and secondarily correctness; the instruments are linear probes, a TopK sparse autoencoder, and directly optimised intervention directions.

## How they did it

Each question gets one prefill pass with hidden states cached at every layer; nothing is probed from generated tokens. Answers are graded three-way — correct, refusal, wrong — by the subject model acting as its own judge under a structured prompt, with two external annotators (a web-search-enabled Gemini deep-research pass over all 784 items, and a Claude sample) for calibration. At every layer and for every target the pipeline is standardise → PCA(16) → logistic regression under stratified 5-fold cross-validation, with scaler and PCA fit on the training fold only. The central control is a four-rung prompt-feature ladder fit on the same folds with no access to the hidden state: bag-of-tokens TF-IDF, engineered question-text numerics (length, has-year, capitalisation, digits), plus a domain one-hot, plus a human-assigned category dummy. Everything is reported as Δ, the probe's peak accuracy minus the strongest of those baselines, so the hidden state is credited only with what survives. The confound work is done by construction: the benchmark's obscure pre-cutoff category holds facts likely present in training data that the model still gets wrong, which fills the cell a pre/post-cutoff design leaves empty, and the disconfounded tests fit the correctness probe inside pre-cutoff items alone and inside obscure items alone. From the probe they move to tokens by lifting the weight vector through RMSNorm and the tied LM head into vocabulary space; to causation by a forward hook adding a unit-normalised direction to the post-block residual at the last prompt position, one shot, swept in doses expressed as fractions of the state's own norm; to composition by decomposing that direction against Qwen-Scope SAE features at its declared hook, then causally testing the one feature that survives a three-view selection; and to objective-recovery by optimising a single vector to raise the opener log-probability under the final norm and head. Random norm-matched directions and a shuffled-label probe direction are the intervention controls, and a prefix-forcing arm generates the same items with the literal opener forced as the first token with no hidden-state edit at all.

## What counts as an explanation here

A direction plus a decomposition, taken to be partial and to be about a *readout site* rather than a circuit. The claim is explicitly that the late-layer state is where the model writes its commitment to a refusal opening — one linear map from the output head — not where the upstream computation that reaches that decision lives. The SAE decomposition is offered as the composition of the direction into register features, but only one member of the ensemble (the opener feature) is causally tested, and the rest are declared correlational. The paper's most important explanatory move is a negative one: no concentrated dictionary feature or token signature for confabulation is found, so the asymmetry is explained as one pathway being marked (abstention) and the other being the default attempt, rather than as two opposing directions.

## How they judged success

Three criteria, kept separate throughout. For reading: probe accuracy against the strongest prompt-feature baseline, Δ in percentage points, with per-fold spread and a bootstrap over class-balanced 50/50 subsamples (ranges reported, explicitly not confidence intervals), plus a label-permutation null that measures the layer-selection bias — the probe maximises over all layers and the baseline over four, so the null is slightly positive and margins of a couple of points are inside it. For intervening: two outcome measures on the same items, a first-token refusal-opener rate and the strict three-way judge label, because an opener is a propensity and not a commitment; controls are three norm-matched random directions and the shuffled-label probe. For localisation: logit-lens vocabulary of the direction, and the dose at which each candidate direction first flips half and then all held-out wrong items (dose-for-dose comparison in units of the added vector norm). Class-imbalance-aware metrics (balanced accuracy, ROC AUC, confusion matrix) are reported at the refusal-probe peak.

## What they found

The disconfounding works and then declines to behave. Of 784 items, 235 (30.0%) are judged correct, 147 (18.8%) refusal, 402 (51.3%) wrong; accuracy is 62.2% on well-known pre-cutoff, 33.3% on obscure pre-cutoff and 19.5% on post-cutoff, so the obscure cell holds a population the model fails on despite likely exposure. All 147 refusals fall in the post-cutoff cell, which is what makes the refusal probe separable from a cutoff detector and also the paper's most awkward fact — on Qwen3, abstention is triggered by the knowledge-cutoff cue rather than by intrinsic uncertainty.

Against the prompt-feature ladder, correctness gains almost nothing (Table 7: probe 0.824 against the +category baseline 0.800 at the peak, and the author states the disconfounded within-stratum margins are within per-fold noise and consistent with zero under the bootstrap), while refusal-versus-wrong clears it — 82.0% TF-IDF at the full subset against a probe margin of +7.4 pp on Qwen, and the cross-model table shows the same comparison at 67.6% → +16.3 pp on Gemma and 93.6% → +2.2 pp on Llama, the last being a near-ceiling baseline rather than a weak probe since Llama refuses 97.5% of its post-cutoff failures. The paper's reading is that refusing is a decision the model itself makes, so an encoding must exist, whereas correctness is a relation between output and world that the model need not represent.

The refusal direction reproduces the known safety result in an epistemic setting and then shows how little that settles. The lens of the layer-28 probe direction puts "as" at the top of the vocabulary, with the Chinese "as" openers at ranks 2–3 and "there" at rank 11 — the literal opening vocabulary of the model's refusals. Pushing along it flips wrong items into refusal openers almost without exception from a sub-norm dose, but only about one third of those generations survive the strict judge as refusals, the rest reverting into content; the reverse push turns refusing items into confabulations while leaving originally correct answers intact. Forcing the literal opener "As" as the first token with no hidden-state push yields the same roughly one-in-three judged-refusal rate — which is the entry's sharpest single number, because it says the hidden state adds nothing the first token does not already determine.

Several distinct vectors, all working, is the technical core. The three candidates are pairwise 50–81° apart; the probe direction and the class-mean difference are one family (data-side) and the SAE opener feature with the directly optimised vector are another (output-side), weakly coupled at chance level in 2048 dimensions. Causal potency tracks output-family membership rather than discriminative quality: the optimised direction is the most dose-efficient, the SAE feature saturates at roughly half the probe's dose, and a probe refit natively on pre-norm residuals splits the classes equally well yet carries about half the causal effect of the same probe in normed space. The Pythagorean accounting is given — the probe direction owns only part of its norm on the opener feature's channel, and the rest of its norm still pushes neighbouring opener variants, so the naive projection argument overstates the gap. And the apology feature, nudged at doses that saturate the opener, produces zero sorry/oops openings, confirming that the register ensemble is descriptive and only one member writes tokens.

The SAE search has a clean negative. The refusal direction resolves into a register ensemble (moment hedge, deferential officialese, polite modality, the opener itself, first-person, apology), with the opener feature the only one in the top eight of all three ranking views; the same search on the wrong side finds topic, phrasing and attempt-formatting cues, and no confabulation marker. The paper sets this against reports on Gemma 2 and Claude 3.5 Haiku where abstention is the default and recognised-entity features suppress it, and offers the interpretation that which pathway is default is a post-training property — Llama 3.2 3B, which refuses 97.5% of its post-cutoff failures and gets 0.2% of them right, sits in the other regime.

The cross-model comparison is the part that overturns the paper's own single-model read. Llama carries a correctness signal its prompt baseline does not: on balanced PopQA the probe reaches 82.37% against a 61.61% strongest baseline, on TriviaQA 74.23% against 56.52%, and the bootstrap ranges exclude zero on both; dropping refusals leaves most of it (the refusal channel is credited with roughly a sixth), and a direct refusal probe independently recovers 84.47% and 90.23% against 66.03% and 64.80%. Qwen3-4B lands at essentially Qwen3-1.7B's level, so the gap is not parameter count. Transfer without refit separates the two properties cleanly: Llama keeps most of its within-dataset margin under cross-dataset transfer, Gemma has the largest within-dataset margin and collapses on PopQA as target, and Qwen's retention is high only because an almost flat probe stays almost flat. The author's framing — that within-dataset peak and transfer portability are different orderings — is the correct one and is why a single-architecture claim here would have been unsafe in either direction.

## Limits and open problems they name

The benchmark's gold answers were not systematically checked before the experiments, and the author's own audit found wrong golds, false premises and near-duplicate templated prompts; a fifth domain (sports) produced zero correct answers in any category and was excluded because the within-pre-cutoff probe would have had no positive examples. Cross-validation folds are stratified by label but not grouped by source file. The bootstrap resamples balanced subsets rather than items, so the reported interval describes subsample spread rather than population uncertainty; an item-level bootstrap is run as a check and it withdraws both of Llama's within-stratum positives. The prompt-feature baseline is hand-crafted and admitted to be a lower bound on what a generic text classifier could extract. All probes read the pre-generation state, so the Qwen correctness null is a statement about that state and not about every state the model produces. The cross-model contrast confounds family with post-training recipe, a recipe swap holding family fixed is named as the missing experiment, and the judge for Llama outputs is still Qwen — de-risked only by the intervention runs reproducing under an external judge.

## What we take from it

A **measure**, first: score every probe as Δ against the strongest cheap classifier on the inputs alone, and print the label-permutation null beside it. That single column is what turns "the probe reads the model" into a testable claim, and this paper is the clearest demonstration in the folder that the same probe pipeline gives opposite answers on three architectures — which means any single-model claim of this form should be treated as a hypothesis about that model.

A **warning** with teeth, second: a causal test of a direction is not a test that the direction is the vehicle. The prefix-forcing control here produces the same downstream refusal rate as the intervention, so the hidden-state push and the token-forcing are observationally equivalent at the judged level; a steering result therefore needs an arm that forces the *output* the direction is credited with causing, or the claim shrinks to "this direction raises the logit of the output we then measure". Any steering battery we build should carry that arm by default, since it costs nothing but a prompt prefix.

A **framing**, third: distinguish behaviours that are marked states from behaviours that are default pathways, because only the former should be expected to have a concentrated feature. Finding a refusal register and finding no confabulation marker is a positive result about the model's organisation, not a search that failed — and the honest scope condition is printed in the paper (one dictionary, one hook, per-item explained variance, so absence of a feature is not absence of signal).

A **shortcut** worth keeping: when several recoveries of "the same" direction disagree wildly in angle but agree in effect, compare them by dose-for-dose potency on held-out items and sort them by how directly their recovery was shaped by the output objective. That sort reproduced the causal ordering here, and it predicts where the effect will be found in any case where the readout is close to the output head.
