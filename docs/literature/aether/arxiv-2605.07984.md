# Where's the Plan? Locating Latent Planning in Language Models with Lightweight Mechanistic Interventions

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.07984
- **Authors / organisation:** Nicole Ma, Nick Rui; Stanford University
- **Date:** 2026-05 (v2 revised 2026-06-11)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07984
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (v2 HTML; body, appendices A-D, tables and all figure captions read; figures not inspected as images)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Autoregressive models emit one token at a time yet sometimes produce text whose late tokens are bound by something stated much earlier. The authors ask whether a model builds an internal representation of a not-yet-emitted token, and — the sharper half — whether that representation is *used* at the point where it is found. They separate two notions deliberately: a *planning-compatible representation* is one from which the future token decodes better than at other positions, and a *causally active planning site* is one whose replacement during generation redirects the output. The framing exists because prior work established that such information is present in some models but never localised it position by position or asked whether it moves during the forward pass. The larger goal is an accounting of capacity that behavioural evaluation cannot see, which they motivate as both a scientific and a safety question.

## What they studied
Three open-weight families at more than ten scales: Qwen3 (0.6B to 32B), Gemma-3 (1B to 27B) and Llama-3 / Llama-3.1 (1B to 70B). The unit of analysis is the pair (layer, token position), with position defined relative to the newline that ends the first line of a couplet — negative for tokens before it, positive for tokens generated after. The task is rhyming couplet completion: given a first line ending in a word, produce a second line ending in a rhyme. Two units of intervention are used at different stages: the residual stream at a position, and later individual attention heads writing into that position.

## How they did it
Evaluation material is 1,200 couplets generated by a frontier model, prompted for topic and rhyme-scheme diversity, split 1,000 train / 200 validation; the second line is truncated and the model's own greedy completion supplies the target token, so the label is what the model would actually have said. **Probing:** a single linear map plus softmax from a hidden state to the vocabulary, trained with cross-entropy, used to predict (a) the token k steps ahead on general text and (b) the second line's rhyme word from a given layer and position; accuracy is reported as raw top-5 and as rhyme accuracy under a pronouncing dictionary, with Wilson intervals. **Negative control on the probe side:** the same k-step-ahead probe is run on Pile sequences and compared to a unigram model fitted to the same completions. **Patching:** a clean prompt whose first line leads to one rhyme family and a corrupt prompt differing in that word are run; the corrupt hidden state at (layer, position) is substituted into the clean forward pass and the fraction of completions that land in the corrupt rhyme family is measured. Every layer is swept at two positions, and controls replace the patched state with a zero vector or with a hidden state taken from an unrelated sentence.

**Localisation stage.** Single-head replacement at the newline produced no measurable effect for any head, so heads are first *ranked* by attention weight from the newline to the last word and then patched in top-k sets. Two reference points make the size of those effects readable: the fraction of completions redirected, and a *full-residual reference* obtained by overwriting the entire residual at that position at the best single layer. A stricter two-stage path patch then isolates the route last-word → head → newline → output: stage one runs the clean prompt with only the last-word residual corrupted and caches each candidate head's output at the newline; stage two forwards the unmodified clean prompt and injects those cached outputs. A head ranking by attention is therefore never credited on its own — the route has to carry the effect end to end. Equality checks: the same question is re-asked with a mean-difference steering vector at every (layer, position), which requires roughly an order of magnitude more data, and the head sets are compared against random sets and a set built from a near-adjacent punctuation token.

## What counts as an explanation here
A located, causal site: a set of (layer, position) coordinates — and at the second stage a set of five named attention heads and one route between two token positions — at which an information state provably matters, plus a claim about how the site behaves across layers ("the driver migrates from the last word to the newline around layer 30"). It is partial by construction: nothing here is a substitution model, nothing is a circuit over features, and the paper is explicit that it does not show the newline representation is read in normal generation rather than only when inserted artificially. The prediction it buys is narrow and checkable: patch at this coordinate and the output should move toward the donor, patch elsewhere and it should not.

## How they judged success
Decoding side: top-5 and rhyme accuracy per (layer, position), Wilson 95% intervals, with the Pile k-step-ahead curve and a unigram model as the null and an explicit "the k=8 band overlaps the unigram baseline" criterion. Causal side: corrupt rhyme rate, with 95% cluster bootstrap intervals over prompt pairs (prompt pair is the unit of independence since the same word pairs are reused across layers; comparisons between two conditions use a joint bootstrap with shared pair indices). Controls: zero-vector and donor-sentence replacements, random head sets, a comma-position head set, and the MLP analogue of the head patch. Efficiency is also a stated criterion: no transcoder training, and steering is treated as a cross-check rather than the primary method because it costs one to two orders of magnitude more data for the same conclusion.

## What they found
Probing finds rhyme information decodable at the line boundary in all three families, strengthening with scale: the largest gap between the newline probe and the first generated position grows monotonically in Gemma-3 from about 0.11 at 1B to about 0.38 at 27B, while in Qwen3 and Llama-3 only the largest model in each family has an interval clear of zero. On general text the k-step-ahead probe falls to the unigram baseline by eight tokens ahead, and the newline probe beats that baseline even though the target sits about eight tokens past it.

Patching divides the families sharply. Only Gemma-3-27B has a causally live site at the newline: last-word patching is strong in early layers, falls off around layer 30, and newline patching rises to a peak corrupt rhyme rate of 0.63 at layer 33 (interval 0.48–0.78), with per-pair rates spanning 0.40–0.90. In every other model the newline effect stays near zero across all scales — the all-layer patching table gives 67% for Gemma-3-27B at the newline against 0–7% for every other cell, with intervals overlapping zero — while last-word patching is effective from roughly 3B upward in all families. The same split reappears under steering (newline steering 0.85–0.95 across layers 30–40 in Gemma-3-27B, noise elsewhere).

Localisation: attention from the newline to the last word is concentrated in three heads (layer 30 head 4 near 0.99, layer 28 heads 14 and 15 near 0.97 and 0.95). Patching top-k sets gives near-zero at k=1–3, a jump to 46% at k=5 (73% of the full-residual reference), and a plateau to k=10; the two-stage path patch peaks at k=5 with 57%, which the authors report as about 90% of that reference, holding in a 44–57% band through k=9 and declining at k=10 and k=15. Random and comma-control head sets stay at zero, and the MLP patch at the same position yields zero at every k.

## Limits and open problems they name
One task family (couplets) and one structured constraint (phonology), so generalisation to prose, code or multi-step reasoning is untested. The recovery fraction's interval extends past 1.0 because only five prompt pairs underlie it, and whether the same head set serves every rhyme family is unknown. Why Gemma-3-27B hands off while Llama-3.1-70B, which is far larger, does not is unexplained — the paper offers no architectural or training account. Most consequentially, the authors say a causal-scrubbing or steering test on the planning heads is needed to tell whether the newline representation is genuinely read during generation or only exerts influence when inserted. They also list the two confounds their probing cannot exclude: strong attention from newline to rhyme word would elevate probe accuracy with no downstream reader, and rhyme accuracy under a pronouncing dictionary under-covers valid rhymes unevenly across families.

## What we take from it
Method: a reusable three-tier ladder for any "is this representation used?" question — decode at the coordinate, patch at the coordinate against a *strongest-available* sub-component reference rather than zero, then path-patch to attribute the effect to named components — with the reference number (fraction of the full-residual effect recovered) as the headline rather than the raw rate. Measure: the corrupt-output rate with cluster intervals over the natural repeating unit, plus a near-adjacent-token control that shows the effect is specific to the structural position rather than to "patching near a boundary". Framing: encoding and use are *dissociable*, and the overwhelming majority of models here are in the encode-but-do-not-use cell, which is a warning against reading our own probe accuracy as mechanism. Warning that lands hardest for us: attention-weight ranking plus top-k simultaneous patching can produce a sparse-looking five-head story whose recovery interval is so wide it is compatible with far more than five heads doing the work — the k-sweep declining past k=5 is doing the localisation argument, and it is five pairs deep. And one shortcut worth stealing: substituting a whole-residual patch at the same coordinate as a ceiling turns "46%" into "73% of what is available", which is the number a reader can act on.
