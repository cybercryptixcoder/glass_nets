## What they wanted, and why
Transformer variants are routinely justified by a three-step move: measure a statistic on internal activations, find it large, remove that component from the architecture, report a training improvement. The authors ask what has to be true for that move to mean anything, and split it into three questions that they turn into reusable checks. Is the behavioural effect being correlated with the statistic even measurable, or is it buried in noise? Does the statistic exceed a null built from the model's own background geometry? And does the training gain depend on the *specific* direction removed, or would any rank-one edit of the same form do? They then run the whole programme on one concrete case: exclusive self attention (XSA), an architecture that deletes each attention head's output component aligned with that head's own value vector. The larger goal is methodological - a recipe for deciding when a representation-level statistic licenses an intervention at all.

## What they studied
Twelve pretrained decoder models for geometry measurements: nine multi-head models (GPT-2 family and Pythia suite, 124M to 6.9B parameters, 5,408 heads in total) plus three grouped-query models (Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, TinyLlama-1.1B). Intervention effects and reliability were measured on GPT-2, Pythia-160M and Pythia-410M, later extended to Pythia-1.4B and Pythia-2.8B. The training experiment used a from-scratch decoder with eight layers, eight heads per layer and hidden width 512 (about 50.9M parameters). Unit of analysis: a **direction inside a single head** - the unit-normalised own value vector at the same position — not the whole head, which is what the pruning literature studies.

## How they did it
The intervention (Eq. 1) subtracts from head output y_i a gated multiple of its projection onto the own-value direction v̂_i, i.e. y_i − tanh(α)·⟨y_i, v̂_i⟩·v̂_i, applied before the output projection. A learned scalar gate α per layer and head enables or reverses the edit; in frozen measurements the multiplier is fixed at one and heads are edited one at a time with the first token excluded. **Check 0 (resolvability):** split the evaluation corpus in half, measure each head's loss effect Δ and the structural statistic on both halves, take Spearman correlations across heads to get split-half reliabilities r_Δ and r_stat, and label the effect reliable at r_Δ ≥ 0.6, attenuated at 0.3–0.6, unresolvable below 0.3; a disattenuated correlation ρ_disatt = ρ_raw / √(r_Δ·r_stat) is reported as a diagnostic. **Check 1 (anisotropy null):** for each head and position compare cos(y_i, v_i) with cos(y_i, v_j) where j is drawn uniformly from earlier positions, and report the mean paired difference as "excess"; separate diagnostics test attention-sink mass (first token versus the next three) and massive activations (mean maximum absolute hidden coordinate against √(2 log d)). **Check 2 (matched intervention):** a pre-registered three-arm by eight-seed paired factorial - baseline, XSA, and a fixed arbitrary rank-one direction sampled as a normalised isotropic Gaussian per layer and head and held constant across positions, training steps and seeds, with identical gate parameterisation - 399,900,672 tokens per run (FineWeb-Edu sample-10BT, disjoint validation holdout), two-sided paired t-tests, percentile bootstrap intervals, Holm correction on secondary endpoints only, and a realised minimum-detectable-effect calculation. A **reconstruction gate** rebuilds each head output from the captured attention matrix and values and rejects runs whose relative Frobenius error exceeds 1e-2, so a silent head-layout bug cannot pass as a result.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanism. The understanding offered is a set of **admissibility conditions on a representation-motivated argument**: a claimed link from an internal statistic to an intervention effect is only interpretable if the effect is resolvable, the statistic beats a within-model geometric null, and the gain survives a direction-matched control. The explanation of XSA itself is partial and one-directional - the value vector direction is treated as potentially redundant, and "large alignment implies redundancy" is explicitly refused as an inference.

## How they judged success
Split-half Spearman reliability of the head-level loss effect (r_Δ) and of the statistic (r_stat), with fixed thresholds; excess cosine over an earlier-position null; raw and disattenuated rank correlations between statistic and effect; a paired eight-seed factorial with pre-registered primary endpoint (random versus baseline), Holm-corrected secondary (XSA versus baseline) and a direct XSA-versus-random paired test; realised MDE against observed paired standard deviation; Cohen's d_z; a tensor-reconstruction gate; and a repository-level checker that recomputes all 48 table cells from archived result files plus a manifest that recomputes 33 numerical claims.

## What they found
**The geometric control takes most of the headline statistic away.** Across nine multi-head models (5,408 heads), the earlier-position null accounts for 48.1% to 63.1% of the raw self-value cosine, with no monotone trend in scale; at Pythia-6.9B the raw cosine is 0.340 and 0.198 of it is attributable to the null. What survives as excess ranges 0.14 to 0.21 and does not track model size, while the raw cosine falls from 0.483 at GPT-2 124M to 0.340 at 6.9B. Under grouped-query attention the alignment is specific to the head's own key-value group: for Qwen2.5-0.5B the own-group excess is +0.2415 and the neighbouring-group excess is −0.1876, with the same signs in Qwen2.5-1.5B and TinyLlama-1.1B. The two extra GPT-2 diagnostics do survive their nulls (attention mass on the first token 0.403 versus 0.003 on matched early positions; massive-activation statistic 12.87 against a Gaussian reference of 3.65).
**The trained benefit is real and direction-specific at small scale.** In the eight-seed factorial the arbitrary-direction arm is indistinguishable from baseline (+0.001056 nats, 95% CI [−0.000298, +0.002390], p = 0.197), while learned self-directed removal lowers validation loss (−0.002924 nats, CI [−0.003967, −0.001709], t = −4.67, Holm p = 0.0023, d_z = −1.649) and beats the random arm directly (−0.003980 nats, t = −4.86, p = 0.0018). XSA's layer-mean final gates are positive everywhere (0.14 to 0.47, i.e. partial removal, not full), and the random arm's later-layer means go negative, i.e. it learns to *add* along its direction — the two arms therefore did not match projection magnitudes.
**The statistic does not predict the effect.** Raw correlations between self-cosine and head loss effect are +0.462 (GPT-2), +0.149 (Pythia-160M) and −0.025 (Pythia-410M); disattenuated, +0.519, +0.231, −0.034. The null-corrected excess does better on two models (+0.313, +0.292, +0.342 adjusted) but inverts at the largest: Pythia-1.4B gives excess 0.307 against self-cosine 0.232, and Pythia-2.8B gives raw self-cosine +0.003 and excess −0.158 despite a reliable effect (r_Δ = 0.632) and near-perfect statistic reliability (0.967). Reliability itself is model-dependent: r_Δ is 0.795 for GPT-2 but 0.419 and 0.531 for the two small Pythias, and 0.666 and 0.632 at 1.4B and 2.8B under half the document budget — the authors decline to call that a scale trend because the budgets differ and the sequence is not monotone.

## Limits and open problems they name
The training result covers one 50.9M model and says nothing about signs or magnitudes at 0.7B–2.7B; the frozen and trained settings differ in gate constraints and first-token handling, so they are related but not identical interventions. Both structural statistics are cosine-based, so other statistic families (attention probabilities, projection norms) are untested. Seven of the twelve ladder models have no intervention measurement. Disattenuation applies half-sample reliabilities to pooled correlations and is a diagnostic, not a corrected ground truth. The control is rank-one like XSA but differs in projected norm and converged gate strength, and it is **fixed across positions** while XSA removes an input-dependent direction at every token — so direction specificity is confounded with per-token versus static projection, and the authors name a per-token control (projecting out an earlier token's value, as the Check 1 null does) as the missing arm. Departures from the pre-registered plan are disclosed: 8 seeds instead of 12, the larger-scale training check not run, the meanval and diagmask arms dropped. A reference-triple mismatch for GPT-2 geometry is chased down by a length sweep: the null moves 0.096 across 64–1024 tokens (nine times the stated tolerance), and the self-specific fraction for the same model is 0.235 at 64 tokens versus 0.412 at 1024, so such fractions are not comparable across studies without a stated length.

## What we take from it
**Method — Check 0 as a gate before any statistic-to-effect correlation.** Split-half reliability of both sides, with explicit thresholds, is the cheapest thing in the folder that prevents "weak correlation means no association" from being read off a noisy effect, and its two example rows are exactly our failure mode (structural statistic near-perfect at 0.99, behavioural effect attenuated at 0.42–0.53, so the denominator is pure noise on the effect side). Rule for us: no rank correlation between a computed statistic and a downstream effect enters a report without both split-half reliabilities and the unresolvable/attenuated/reliable verdict printed beside it. **Method — the direction-matched training control.** Training the same edit with a fixed arbitrary direction at the same gate parameterisation, and comparing arms *directly* rather than through two separate baseline tests, is a stronger design than a post-hoc random band on a frozen model; the companion lesson is their own disclosed confound — matching parametric form while leaving projection magnitude and per-token structure unmatched does not isolate direction. **Measure — within-model null, stated length.** Computing the null inside the same run, at one stated sequence length, on the same documents, and reporting the length everywhere, is the pattern we adopt for any anisotropy or "background geometry" claim; a self-specific fraction quoted without a length is not comparable (0.235 versus 0.412 for the same model). **Warning — a large internal alignment is not redundancy.** Raw cosine 0.483 for GPT-2 is mostly background (excess 0.184), and at 6.9B 58% of the alignment is the null; a head whose output resembles a vector already present in the model is not therefore a head whose removal is free. **Infrastructure — the reconstruction gate and the cell checker.** Rebuilding y = A·expand_kv(V) and rejecting on Frobenius error (their own observed class of bugs: 1.39–1.50 errors from a reversed head axis, a rolled axis, or a missing transpose) is a directly reusable guard for our head-level patching code, and a checker that recomputes every table cell from archived results and flags rows it cannot verify is the reporting discipline our own results tables should carry.
