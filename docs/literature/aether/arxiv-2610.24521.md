# Comparing Sparsified MLPs and Transcoders: Many Transcoder Features Are Neurons in Disguise

- **Key:** arxiv-2610.24521
- **Authors / organisation:** Stepan Shabalin, Mateusz Piotrowski; Anthropic Fellows, Georgia Institute of Technology, Anthropic
- **Date:** 2026-10-17
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24521
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML v1: abstract, §§1–6, Appendices A–R, tables read as cells; figures via captions only)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
MLP neurons are assumed polysemantic, and that assumption is the main justification for transcoders — sparse learned replacements for an MLP layer that are supposed to give cleaner features and better circuit graphs. Recent work showing circuits can be recovered directly from neurons puts the necessity of transcoders in doubt. The authors ask a sharper question than "which is better": if a transcoder is only a re-parameterisation of the layer it replaces, how much of what it learns is actually the layer's own neurons, and how much is new structure that the original model does not use? Their answer is meant to decide whether dictionary learning can resolve same-layer superposition at all, and what the non-neuron remainder might be.

## What they studied
Thirteen open-source per-layer transcoders over Gemma 2/3 (1B–27B), Qwen 3 (1.7B–14B) and Llama 3.1 8B / 3.2 1B, plus their own TopK and JumpReLU transcoders trained on Gemma 3 4B L22 at widths from 32k to 1M, two cross-layer transcoders (Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.2 1B), MoE transcoders on Qwen 3.5 35B-A3B with attribution graphs on Gemma 4 26B-A4B, and MLP-output SAEs. Unit of analysis: the sign-split gated-MLP neuron (two features per neuron, one per sign of the activation), compared against a transcoder feature on weights, activation behaviour, Jacobian contribution, circuit role and seed stability.

## How they did it
Gated MLPs are approximately bilinear at high activation magnitude, so each neuron is given an effective encoder — the up row plus or minus the gate row, depending on the sign of the pre-activation — and the down row as its decoder. Activations are then top-k thresholded per layer (k near 50–64) to put the pruned MLP on the same sparse footing as a transcoder, with an optional learned linear map on the pruned output. A transcoder feature is called a **neuron feature** when both its encoder and decoder exceed a cosine threshold (0.6–0.7, up to 0.9 for some dictionaries) to the same neuron's effective encoder and decoder; the paper shows the per-feature joint similarity distribution is bimodal, which is what licenses the cut. They then compare the two bases on five axes: KL and FVU of the replacement; sparse probing on 113 binary tasks plus 1,500 FineWeb bigrams; sort evals of monosemanticity at top-k versus quantile sampling; per-feature rank-1 Jacobian alignment against the true MLP Jacobian (Frobenius cosine); and cross-seed recurrence of features between two runs differing only in seed. Two training interventions test causality — initialising encoder rows at neuron directions ("neuron initialization"), and k-means clustering of neuron activations.

## What counts as an explanation here
A replacement model with a chosen basis, plus a partition of that basis into the part that is the original network's own computation (neuron features) and a remainder that is a property of the training procedure. The claim is deliberately partial: the paper does not explain the non-neuron features, it argues they carry little of the model's behaviour. Prediction enters through KL under replacement and through Jacobian alignment, both of which are statements about what the surrogate will do at a point rather than descriptions of it.

## How they judged success
FVU (normalised L2 reconstruction error) and KL divergence of the replacement against the original layer; replacement score at matched L0 on 30 representative prompts from short Pile sequences; sparse-probing mean AUC at k=1 across 113 Kantamneni tasks plus a bigram benchmark with a top-1 max-activating-context check (does the chosen feature actually fire on the phrase); sort-eval accuracy on top-k versus quantile activation samples; Jacobian alignment (Frobenius cosine between per-feature rank-1 Jacobian contributions and the MLP's); cross-seed encoder and decoder cosine matching at 0.7 with the fraction reported both in dictionary and in activation mass; neuron-vs-feature firing-rate ratio and neuron rank within the top-K; causal ablations in the arithmetic case study.

## What they found
Neuron features are a small share of the dictionary but dominate use: in Gemma 3 the neuron-feature share at two-thirds depth rises 5.9% → 8.9% → 15.6% → 37.1% from 1B to 27B while their share of activations goes 57.9% → 82.8%, because they fire more than ten times as often as non-neuron features. They make up at least 40% of attribution-graph nodes in the late third of layers for every transcoder tested but one (Llama 3.2 1B mntss at about 38%), a share that barely moves from L0=10 to L0=100 (56–61% on Gemma 3 4B). Faithfulness concentrates there: summed Jacobian alignment of the transcoder with the MLP is about 0.3, the neuron-aligned subset alone reproduces it, and the non-aligned subset reaches only about 0.05. Cross-seed, most features that recur between two seeds are neuron features, and widening from 32k to 1M features does not buy many new stable ones — the 1M dictionary found 22 thousand, comparable to the model's own 20,480 sign-split neurons, with neuron coverage (|cos| ≥ 0.7) rising 78% → 93% from 32k to 256k and then plateauing. Trained encoders of neuron features rotate away from their random initialisation (median cosine near zero) while TopK non-neuron features stay near it (median +0.48). With a linear correction on the pruned output, neurons beat transcoders on KL while remaining worse on FVU; neurons match or exceed the transcoder on sparse probing for every model (AUC 0.834/0.851/0.847 against 0.792/0.780/0.802) but lose badly on bigrams, where the feature matches the target phrase in its top-1 example 75.6% of the time on Gemma 3 4B against 0.0% for neurons. Sort evals say neurons are comparable at the very top of their activation distribution and degrade under quantile sampling. Case studies route through the same finding: line-break/character-count features track single neurons, the 28 layer-18 arithmetic neurons of Llama 3.1 8B appear as neuron features whose ablation drops addition accuracy from 94.3% to 30.6%, and neuron-initialised training lifts encoder alignment from 11% to 92% (95% of activation mass against 65%).

## Limits and open problems they name
Base models, mostly English, 1B–27B dense, per-layer transcoders only; MoE and cross-layer results are preliminary. Only sparse high-magnitude single-token activations are characterised — low activations and multi-token mechanisms are out of scope. The neuron-feature fraction depends on dictionary architecture and on the threshold chosen (0.6/0.7/0.9). Weight-based classification is not fully robust because non-neuron features partly retain their initialisation. No systematic human evaluation; all interpretability metrics are automated, so a feature that wins sparse probing may be correlational rather than the concept. Faithfulness is first-order Jacobian only, not faithfulness under intervention. They do not explain where non-neuron decoders come from, nor characterise the diffuse many-neuron combinations. They state plainly that the findings cast doubt on resolving same-layer MLP superposition with dictionary learning, and are only cautiously optimistic about cross-layer supernode graphs.

## What we take from it
Three things transfer directly. (1) **A basis-alignment audit before trusting any dictionary**: compute per-feature encoder and decoder cosine to the nearest neuron, plot the joint distribution, and report what fraction of *activation mass*, not of dictionary entries, is aligned — the difference between 37% of features and 83% of activations is the whole argument, and our own read-outs should quote activation-weighted shares. (2) **Reconstruction and replacement are different criteria and can point in opposite directions**: adding terms improved FVU without improving KL, which is the cleanest statement in the folder that a better-reconstructing surrogate need not be the better model of behaviour; our evaluation order should be causal first, reconstruction second. (3) **A seed-recurrence and initialisation-drift test is a cheap reality check on any learned component**: features that stay near their random init and do not recur across seeds are candidates for training artefacts, and a top-1 max-activation check on the target concept is what turned a 0.988 probing AUC into 0.0% actual detection. Warning to carry: high probe accuracy is not evidence the feature represents the concept.
