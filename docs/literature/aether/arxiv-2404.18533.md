# Evaluating Readability and Faithfulness of Concept-based Explanations

- **Key:** arxiv-2404.18533
- **Authors / organisation:** Meng Li, Haoran Jin, Ruixuan Huang, Zhihao Xu, Defu Lian, Zijia Lin, Di Zhang, Xiting Wang; Renmin University of China, University of Science and Technology of China, Kuaishou Technology (Li and Jin listed as equal contributors, Wang corresponding)

- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18533
- **Kind:** paper

- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why
Concept-based explanations describe a model's high-level regularities rather than single inputs, but the field had no shared yardstick for the two things a concept is usually credited with: reflecting the mechanism (faithfulness) and being understandable (readability). The authors attribute the gap to three specific difficulties — concept methods come in incompatible shapes (a linear classifier, an overcomplete basis, a hand-built module), concepts are non-local so a human cannot judge them across many samples, and existing measures were never themselves tested for reliability or validity. They want one formalisation that covers supervised and unsupervised, post-hoc and interpretable-by-design methods, plus a discipline for choosing among measures. The larger goal is methodological: stop each paper from inventing its own concept score and give the field a way to say which score has earned trust.

## What they studied
Black-box language models: Pythia-70M (6 layers, 512-dim) as the main backbone, GPT-2 small (12 layers, 768-dim) as a cross-backbone check, over text from the Pile. Concepts are drawn from three methods — an OpenAI-style neuron explainer (MLP-output dimensions), a sparse autoencoder in the Cunningham et al. style (dictionary width eight times the hidden size, L1 coefficient 0.5 at the chosen layer), and TCAV with a linear classifier trained on harmful-QA versus random-text hidden states — with a hundred randomly sampled concepts per unsupervised method. The unit of analysis is a *virtual neuron*: a scalar activation function over the hidden state, with an accompanying semantic expression written by a human or an LLM. An image-domain sketch is included in an appendix but not run.

## How they did it
The paper first unifies concept methods as activation functions $a(h) = v^\top h + b$ in various forms, tabulating TCAV, CBM, ProtoPNet, NetDissect, Neuron and SAE against that template. Faithfulness is then defined as the mean output change when the hidden state is perturbed so the concept changes, with two canonical perturbations expressed as optimisation problems: an epsilon-addition that raises concept activation by the smallest possible displacement, and an ablation that kills the concept while moving the hidden state as little as possible. For linear activations both have closed forms, which become the paper's gradient step and its projection-removal step; a derivation for ReLU-style activations is in an appendix. Three output-difference functions are crossed with two perturbation strategies to give the measure family (loss difference, KL divergence between output distributions, and logit difference for the predicted or the true class), and each combination is named by a prefix/suffix pair such as gradient-versus-predicted-class. Readability is automated by mimicking OpenAI's human protocol: gather tokens that most activate the concept, keep their top contexts, and score how coherent the resulting token set is using topic-coherence statistics (UCI, UMass over corpus co-occurrence) or embedding similarity (Euclidean distance, cosine) on the backbone's own embedding matrix, computed separately on the input side and on the output side (top-likelihood tokens when the hidden state is pushed along the concept direction). They then meta-evaluate every measure: test–retest correlation on a repeated measurement, Cronbach's alpha over ten data batches as subset consistency, and Kendall's tau between three paid labelers on 200 concepts as inter-rater reliability. Validity is assessed as concurrent (correlation with the human scores), convergent (correlation among measures of the same construct) and divergent (correlation between readability and faithfulness measures), assembled into a multitrait-multimethod table whose diagonal is the reliability figure.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanism and not a circuit: the artefact is a *concept* — a scalar projection with a name — plus a measured pair of numbers attached to it (how much the model's output moves when the concept is removed or added, and how coherent the text that drives it is). The paper's real product is meta-explanatory: a formal definition and a four-part certificate (test–retest, subset consistency, inter-rater, validity) that any concept score can be run through. It predicts nothing about held-out behaviour on its own; it is a procedure for deciding which of several candidate scores to believe.

## How they judged success
Reliability: two-run Pearson correlation with 0.9 taken as the conventional floor; Cronbach's alpha with the same 0.9 floor; Kendall's tau across labelers. Validity: Kendall, Pearson and Spearman correlations against the human readability scores, a multitrait-multimethod matrix for convergent and divergent structure, and a sensitivity sweep over layers and the second backbone. Comparisons are between measures (which to keep), not between methods, though a final section plots the three concept extractors against representative measures. The human study is blinded to the source method and scored on a five-point scale, one score per side, with a five-minute window per concept.

## What they found
Four measures fail the reliability floor and are struck out before any validity claim: the LLM-based explanation score (non-deterministic), gradient-versus-loss faithfulness (explained as coupling between gradient and loss), and both co-occurrence readability measures on the input side (UCI and UMass), which are additionally shown in a case study to rank a clearly coherent token set near the bottom simply because its words co-occur rarely. Among the survivors, embedding cosine is the best automatic readability proxy on both sides — input side Kendall 0.56, Pearson 0.68, Spearman 0.70, and output side 0.67 / 0.75 / 0.80 — beating the GPT-4-based score, which reaches only 0.54 input-side Kendall and collapses to 0.09 output-side. Embedding distance is much weaker (0.19 and 0.15–0.17 Kendall). Input and output readability behave as different constructs: their mutual correlation stays under 0.15, and readability and faithfulness measures correlate only between 0.0 and 0.3, which the authors read as correct divergent structure. Faithfulness measures correlate around 0.5 with each other, with same-perturbation and same-difference pairs agreeing more. Human labelers agree well (Kendall 0.73–0.81, averaging about 0.77 input-side and 0.75 output-side) but their scores are compressed into 2–4, which is offered as a reason the automated/human gaps shrink when methods are compared rather than measures. On the method comparison, the SAE beats the neuron baseline on all evaluated measures but does not beat the supervised TCAV concept, which the authors read as evidence that an unsupervised dictionary's average concept is not as good as a purpose-built supervised one. Layer and backbone sweeps (layers 1, 3, 5 of Pythia-70M; layer 6 of GPT-2 small) keep the ordering, with subset consistency slightly better in deeper layers.

## Limits and open problems they name
The formalisation does not cover the whole landscape of concept methods, and robustness and stability are explicitly named as future axes they did not measure. Coherence is called an approximation, not a definition, of readability; aspects such as meaningfulness are left out. Compute is the binding constraint — small backbones, a single main layer, and an LLM-score comparison restricted to 200 concepts because the simulator cost roughly a dollar per concept. Also unaddressed and worth naming separately: the "adequate perturbation" optimisation is only closed-form for linear and ReLU-linear activations, so for other concept shapes the faithfulness number is only as good as a numerical solve; the human criterion itself is a compressed three-point band, which caps the correlation any automatic measure can reach; and the paper evaluates *scores for concepts*, never *concepts for prediction*, so nothing here is tested against downstream behaviour.

## What we take from it
Three things. (1) A reusable instrument: the reliability/validity certificate is the cheapest defensible way to argue that one of our measurements is a measurement and not a convention — repeated measurement, split-half consistency, agreement with a blinded human, and a divergent check that the quantity is not secretly the same quantity as its neighbour. We should run every metric in our white-box account through that pattern before reporting a ranking built on it. (2) A construct-separation result to hold onto: input-side coherence and output-side coherence are nearly independent (under 0.15), so a single "readability" number for a feature description is measuring one side and silently borrowing credibility from the other — our entries should print both sides or say which side. (3) The perturbation formulation itself is the useful bridge to prediction: "remove the concept while moving the hidden state as little as possible, then measure the output change" is an intervention with a defined minimality criterion, which is exactly the shape of experiment our predictive claims need; the paper stops at measuring the effect and never checks whether the effect was *predicted*, and that gap is where our model has to earn its keep.
