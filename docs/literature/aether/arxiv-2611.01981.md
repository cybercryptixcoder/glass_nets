## What they wanted, and why
Learned auctions — networks that set allocation and payment rules for ad markets from reported valuations and public context — are deployed as black boxes even though the rules they encode decide who wins and what they pay. The authors want a way to inspect a *trained* mechanism after the fact, without retraining it and without imposing architectural constraints or formal verification, which they say cost either expressiveness or scale. Their contribution is framed as a post-hoc white-box audit: decompose the hidden state, attach each learned unit to a definite economic quantity, then check both that the unit's description predicts when it fires and that changing the unit changes the quantity. The larger goal is trust in automated mechanism design, and the audit is meant to produce economic knowledge as a by-product — whether a learned auction internally represents anything like Myerson's reserve.

## What they studied
CITransNet, an open-source transformer for contextual auctions, held fixed throughout. The primary testbed is a two-bidder, five-item mechanism with continuous public context (the paper's c setting); two auxiliary mechanisms are audited independently — single-item continuous (c) and five-item with discrete ten-bidder/ten-item type distributions drawn from truncated normals (d). The audited site is the first fully connected layer, after contextual self-attention and before the output heads, and the unit is then the **per-bidder–item-pair hidden representation**, not the whole auction state. Seven economic targets are defined per auction: seller revenue, social welfare, focal bidder utility, focal payment share (payment divided by allocated value), allocation entropy, winner margin (gap between the two highest allocation probabilities), and incentive score — the last being a counterfactual, not a forward-pass quantity.

## How they did it
An SAE is fitted to the standardized per-pair representations: TopK with a 512-feature dictionary, 32 active latents per pair, and the standard auxiliary loss that revives up to 128 dead features during training only. Fidelity is established on two levels before anything is interpreted: reconstruction error on the representation, and downstream behaviour after the reconstructions are patched back in place of the real hidden states, measured as mean allocation and payment differences. Each feature's *economic association* is then characterized as a Spearman correlation with each of the seven targets, computed on a generation partition of auctions; an LLM explainer (gpt-5.6-luna, in a separate context) sees the top-activating and random inactive examples plus the full auction profile and percentile ranks of all targets, writes a description under 35 words, and picks one target and a direction from the sign of the generation-partition correlation. A blinded predictor then gets description, target, auction profile and the focal pair's activation percentile and must say active or inactive on a disjoint prediction partition with balanced base rates. Causally, the feature's activation is multiplied by a dose across all pairs (dose zero is an ablation, dose one is the SAE baseline) and the target is measured at four doses on held-out auctions; a one-sided t-test on the directional hypothesis, Benjamini–Hochberg corrected over the dictionary and extended with a sign test, defines a "strong-response" feature set. Explanations, targets and directions are frozen before any of this. Three downstream audits follow: re-optimising each bidder's misreport under every dose to get a regret slope; comparing against the Myerson oracle in the c setting by one-versus-rest AUC for the three theoretical regions and by MAE change when features are ablated or doubled; and case studies of two extreme auctions via the five most active features and their spatial pattern over the bidder–item grid.

## What counts as an explanation here
A dictionary of 512 sparse features, each carrying a frozen natural-language description, one designated economic target, and one designated direction of effect. The claim is deliberately partial and operational: an explanation counts as validated only if it passes two separate tests, a predictive one (it lets a blinded reader anticipate activation on unseen auctions better than shuffled, frequency-matched, target-only and guessing controls) and an interventional one (scaling that feature moves its designated target monotonically in the designated direction). What remains after both filters — 165 of 512 features in the primary setting — is what the paper calls verified. Prediction here is of an activation from a description, not of an outcome from internal state.

## How they judged success
Representation fidelity: standardized reconstruction MSE, realized mean sparsity, and fraction of dictionary features that never fire. Behavioural fidelity: mean allocation and mean payment difference after patching reconstructions into the frozen network, plus mean signed shifts of the auction-level targets. Explanation fidelity: pooled precision and recall on the balanced prediction benchmark across all 512 features, with feature-level bootstrap confidence intervals, and ablations of the prompt's components plus shuffled and frequency-matched semantic controls. Causal strength: directional one-sided t-tests at BH-corrected thresholds, dose-response OLS slopes (standardized by the target's standard deviation at the baseline), and near-linearity of the fitted curve. Robustness: an extended six-dose grid for retention. Downstream: AUC for Myerson regions on a discovery/evaluation split of 1,000/1,000 auctions, paired MAE changes with auction-bootstrap intervals, and BH-corrected regret slopes with multi-start misreport search.

## What they found
Reconstruction is essentially lossless in behaviour: standardized MSE around 1.2×10⁻², realized sparsity 31.998 of the budget of 32, only 0.977% of dictionary features never active, mean allocation difference 0.003013 and mean payment difference 0.000534. Feature–outcome associations are stable across disjoint partitions (correlations between per-partition Spearman vectors 0.872–0.999). Blinded prediction over the whole unfiltered dictionary reaches pooled precision 0.630 and recall 0.672, with lower bounds of the intervals (precision [0.619, 0.640], recall [0.655, 0.688]) above the upper bounds of every informative control; explaining text adds 0.149 precision and 0.206 recall over target-only, and adding the target reference to the text adds 0.038 and 0.086. One featured pair shows the discrimination the controls are for: F33 (revenue, correlation 0.55) finds 15 of 25 active and rejects all 25 inactive auctions for precision 1.00 and recall 0.60, while F1's overbroad low-utility description produces 17 false positives and 16 false negatives. Of 512 features, 165 meet the strong-response criteria — 32.2% of the dictionary — and 158 of those survive an extreme dose of the same test (95.8% retention) under 162 BH discoveries out of 492 finite hypotheses. 163 of the 165 have near-linear dose curves (R² ≥ 0.95), and features aimed at the same target can act in opposite directions (F173 lowers welfare, F189 raises it). Regret is bidirectional and concentrated in pricing and surplus representations: 88 features raise the regret slope and 77 lower it, with 26 regret-increasing and 17 regret-reducing after correction; the steepest increases are payment-share features (17/11) and bidder-utility features (3/0), and the leading surplus-enhancing representatives F335 and F364 add 0.0331 and 0.0296 to mean empirical regret at high dose, while the extreme slope for payment share is two to four orders of magnitude larger than those for revenue, welfare, margin and entropy. In the single-item setting, the mechanism's unsupervised features recover Myerson's structure without oracle supervision: F62 separates the no-sale region with AUC 0.9636, and bidder-level readouts of F73 and F421 identify the two winning regions at 0.9129 and 0.9277, while taking the auction-level maximum across bidders collapses the two winning regions to 0.6789 and 0.7161. Ablating F62 raises allocation and payment MAE and doubling lowers both. The cross-setting audit finds 165, 106 and 142 strong features (32.2%, 20.7%, 27.7% of each dictionary) with regret splits 26/17, 37/43 and 43/10. The two case studies read as coherent mechanisms: a high-revenue auction couples surplus-increasing features on the winners with a decisive-margin feature on the unallocated side, while a near-no-trade auction is dominated by a no-trade revenue feature active across all ten positions plus payment-share features that nearly consume the tiny allocation — the authors diagnose failure to clear trade as the bottleneck.

## Limits and open problems they name
The paper's own framing is that this is diagnosis, not refinement: near-linear, bidirectionally signed feature effects are offered as a *basis* for constrained steering that balances revenue against incentive compatibility, and the Myerson result is offered as a way to generate hypotheses in analytically intractable multi-item settings rather than to certify them. No causal claim is made for features outside the monotonic-response definition, and seven features drop out at extreme dose from saturation or reversal. Structural guarantees are explicitly out of scope — the audit complements architectural constraints and formal certification rather than replacing them. What is not discussed: there is no second SAE width or depth sweep despite the reconstruction being near-perfect at the audited site, no study of features that fire but have no target, and the per-setting comparison is a count comparison rather than a test that the same economic concept is recovered in different settings.

## What we take from it
Three things, all methodological. **A measure** — the two-axis filter is the reusable object: a feature counts as located only when a description written from its own examples predicts its activation on held-out inputs *and* a dose sweep moves its designated output monotonically, and the paper prints the control ladder (shuffled, frequency-matched, target-only, explanation-only, random, always-active) that shows the first test is doing work. For our own dictionaries this is a cheap standing protocol, and the frequency-matched control is the one worth adding first, since it prices "the description names something correlated" against "the description names the feature's condition". **A warning** — anchoring to a target before looking is what makes the second test falsifiable, but it also means features outside the seven targets are invisible: the audit's 165 verified features are 32.2% of the dictionary and the paper never asks what the other 67.8% represent, so "verified set" and "the dictionary" must be kept distinct in any use of this design. **A framing** — readout level is a choice with consequences inside the model, not only in the probe: at bidder level two features separate the two winning regions at 0.91–0.93 AUC, at auction level (max over bidders) the same task degrades to 0.68–0.72 because max-pooling destroys positional identity, which is the same position-preserving lesson our own pooling decisions keep meeting. The domain is also a useful reminder that this pipeline needs no interpretable input features at all — the activations here are dense numeric auction profiles, and the anchors come from the outputs.
