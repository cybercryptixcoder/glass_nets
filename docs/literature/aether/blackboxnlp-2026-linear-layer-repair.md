# Linear Layer Repair: Residual-Controlled Layer Profiling for Fault-Tolerant Transformer Inference

- **Key:** blackboxnlp-2026-linear-layer-repair
- **Authors / organisation:** Jiya Sheetal Rathi (Illinois Institute of Technology); André Bauer (University of Chicago, Booth School of Business)
- **Date:** 2026-10-29 (BlackboxNLP 2026, pages 514–522)
- **Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2026.blackboxnlp-1.31.pdf
- **Kind:** paper (workshop)
- **Read from:** full text (PDF, 9 pages / 35,362 chars, all sections incl. §3–§7, References, Table 1 and Table 2; the three figures — the Δ-R² depth profiles, the per-layer perplexity sweep and the k-layer sweeps — were **not** inspected as images, so every number below is a prose sentence or a table cell)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The practical motive is served-model reliability: when a model is split across devices and one node dies, a run of layers vanishes mid-forward-pass and needs an instant drop-in substitute rather than a retrain. Answering whether a layer can be substituted requires a number that says how much of a layer's computation is linear — and the paper's real contribution is that the number everyone uses for this is broken. It is a small workshop paper with a large methodological point for anyone who wants to read a transformer's internals as a linear system, which is exactly the premise our predictive models lean on.

## What they studied
Six frozen pretrained models across three architecture families: Qwen2-1.5B and LLaMA-3.2-1B (decoder-only), BERT-base and RoBERTa-base (encoder-only), BART-base and T5-base (encoder–decoder, both stacks). Unit of analysis is a whole layer seen as a map between consecutive residual-stream states — not a neuron, feature or head. Data: 6,000 OpenWebText documents of 512 tokens in each model's own tokeniser for fitting, about 3M tokens accumulated in one pass, plus a held-out validation split of roughly 30k tokens for choosing ridge strength; evaluation on WikiText-103 perplexity and four zero-shot benchmarks.

## How they did it
For every pair of consecutive hidden states they fit a ridge regression of the next state on the current one, recovering the exact solution for all candidate regularisation strengths from accumulated sufficient statistics so that one forward pass supports the whole analysis. They then compute three quantities per layer: the conventional full-state R² (predicting the output state from the input state), an identity baseline (the fit of output ≈ input, i.e. how much of the output is just the carried-forward residual), and a Δ-R² that predicts the *update* — the difference the layer writes — rather than the state. Two controls carry the argument: the identity baseline as a floor for the conventional metric, and randomly initialised models put through the identical fitting pipeline. They next turn the profile into a decision policy with three branches (omit the layer, substitute a fitted affine map, or declare it irreplaceable), then test the policy by actually deleting layers from the running model and measuring perplexity and accuracy. Finally they probe the envelope: reduced-rank maps, a budget-matched nonlinear MLP substitute, reuse of a neighbouring layer's map, a small mixture of maps routed by k-means over the layer's input space, and transfer of maps to other corpora and to code.

## What counts as an explanation here
A per-layer profile plus an affine surrogate for the layer, and a policy that maps the profile to an action. The explanation is quantitative and mechanical — "this layer's update is 72% linearly predictable and its output is 4% residual" — not linguistic. It is explicitly partial: the surrogate is meant to reproduce *function* at a cost, and the authors are careful (in limits) that token-wise linear maps structurally cannot represent a layer's cross-token attention computation. Prediction is real and tested: the profile is claimed to predict, ahead of the experiment, whether substituting a layer will be cheap, and the paper checks that claim by measuring the damage.

## How they judged success
Held-out R² for the fits, with refit stability checked by refitting each map on three disjoint thirds of the data (per-layer standard deviation never above 0.002, stated as the reason error bars are omitted). Functional validation is WikiText-103 perplexity under skip versus linear patch for single layers and for runs of one to four adjacent layers, plus zero-shot accuracy on ARC-Easy, HellaSwag, LAMBADA and PIQA through the LM Evaluation Harness at 500 examples per task (binomial standard error about 2.2 points, so they treat differences under 4–5 points as noise). Encoders are validated with masked-token accuracy. The policy's profile is correlated against the realised repair gain with Spearman rank correlation, and every baseline (skip, neighbour-map reuse, reduced-rank map, mixture, MLP) is run on the same held-out set.

## What they found
- **The conventional metric cannot distinguish a trained model from an untrained one.** Full-state R² averaged over non-boundary layers is 0.977 for Qwen2 and 0.984 for LLaMA (0.84–0.96 for encoders and encoder–decoders), but the identity baseline alone reaches 0.955–0.962 in the decoders, and randomly initialised models still climb to 0.95–0.99 with depth. Since the residual is passed through untouched, a map close to the identity scores well regardless of what the block computed.
- **Controlling for the residual leaves a real but much smaller linear component, with a U-shaped profile.** Mid-stack Δ-R² is 0.35–0.53 for the decoders, about 0.36 for the encoders and 0.40–0.46 in the encoder–decoder stacks; at the boundaries it jumps (Qwen2 first and last 0.72 and 0.86; LLaMA 0.81 and 0.82; BERT 0.45 and 0.81; RoBERTa 0.55 and 0.93). LLaMA's layer 1 is 99.6% linearly predictable while the identity baseline captures almost none of its output — read as large, near-linear basis changes at the stack boundaries, small nonlinear refinements in the interior.
- **The learned signature is the shape, not the level.** Untrained decoder Δ-R² is flat and low (about 0.17), untrained encoder Δ-R² is flat and high (about 0.93), and update magnitudes relative to state norms are comparable between trained and untrained networks, so the level is architecture-dependent but the structureless-versus-structured contrast survives. Trained models with very different architectural priors converge on the same mid-stack band.
- **Skipping is cheap mid-stack and catastrophic at the boundaries; the linear patch rescues the boundaries.** Qwen2 mid-stack stays at 15.6–17.5 perplexity against an intact 15.2, LLaMA at 20.4–27.3 against 19.0. Skipping Qwen2's layer 0 gives 1.2×10⁵ and the patch brings it to 85.7; skipping LLaMA's layer 1 gives 1584 and the patch 26.0. The map beats skipping at 26 of 28 Qwen2 layers and all 16 LLaMA layers.
- **Multi-layer runs behave the same way with the gap widening.** Four consecutive mid-stack layers cost Qwen2 17.7 perplexity (+2.5) against 19.3 for skipping; LLaMA reaches 33.2 against 95.3. The worst-placed window goes from 2.5×10⁴ to 298 under patching.
- **The mixture is where the piecewise structure shows.** On Qwen2 at layer 1 — the one layer a single map cannot rescue — four ridge maps routed by k-means reach 84.8 against 126.7 for skipping, despite fitting on a quarter of the data; on LLaMA, whose layer 1 is already 99.6% linear, the mixture merely ties. Reusing a neighbouring layer's map fails at both models' boundaries (10⁴–10⁶ perplexity), so the maps are genuinely layer-specific.
- **Perplexity does not track task behaviour, and the one task that does is honest about it.** ARC-Easy, HellaSwag and PIQA stay within noise under every condition. LAMBADA discriminates: substituting a mid-stack pair in LLaMA recovers 0.132 (skip) to 0.398 (linear) against 0.626 intact, and the best single layer 0.388 → 0.568. One negative case is reported as predicted: at Qwen2's layer 25, the late-stack layer with the lowest Δ-R², the patch scores *below* skipping on LAMBADA (0.534 vs 0.590).
- **Encoders confirm the policy's branches rather than only its map.** Where the profile says patch, patching wins decisively (RoBERTa layer 0: skipping collapses masked-token accuracy to 0.101, the patch restores 0.571; windows containing it go 0.025 → 0.387 at k=2 and 0.006 → 0.196 at k=3). Where the profile says skip, forcing a patch hurts: over BERT's mid-stack skip-branch windows the composed patch underperforms skipping (0.286 vs 0.386 for four layers from layer 5, in seven of nine four-layer windows). Two encoders whose layer 0 plays different roles are handled without special-casing, because the policy reads each layer's measured profile.
- **The envelope.** The maps are effectively full-rank (truncating to rank 512, a third to a quarter of width, already gives 35–269 perplexity; rank 128 drops Δ-R² below 0.4 with perplexity above 10³), a full map is only 4.7 MB (Qwen2) or 8.4 MB (LLaMA) in fp16, and a modestly trained two-layer GELU MLP does not exceed the closed-form ridge solution at any probed depth (0.895 vs 0.940 on Qwen2 layer 21; 0.912 vs 0.966 on LLaMA layer 12). Maps fitted on OpenWebText transfer to WikiText-103 and to doubled sequence length with no qualitative change; on code the mid-stack profile survives but early-layer behaviour is unstable and model-dependent.
- **The profile predicts the outcome it was built to predict**: Δ-R² correlates with log-perplexity recovery of patch over skip at Spearman ρ = 0.65 (p < 10⁻⁵) across all 44 decoder layers, and ρ = 0.59 (p = 0.045) restricted to the twelve low-identity layers where patching is consequential.

## Limits and open problems they name
Functional experiments cover two decoder models at 1–1.5B, and policy validation on encoders uses masked-token accuracy only; encoder–decoder models contribute to the representational analysis but are excluded from replacement because T5 threads relative position bias between blocks and a drop-in patch would break it. Multi-layer maps are fitted on intact-model activations, so multi-layer results are lower bounds relative to sequential refitting. The MLP control is budget-limited (about 10⁵ tokens for five epochs against an exact solution over about 3M) and so does not bound nonlinear substitutes in general. Random-init controls cover four of six models. The branch thresholds, while cleanly separated in their models, are descriptive rather than calibrated, and a systematic sensitivity analysis across architectures and training stages is left to future work. Task evaluations use 500 examples per task. Their last limitation is the one that matters most for us: the maps are token-wise and therefore cannot capture attention's cross-token computation, which they suggest is part of the mid-stack nonlinear residue, and disentangling the two is future work.

## What we take from it
- **Measure and warning (the main one):** a linear-fit R² between consecutive residual-stream states is not evidence of learned linear structure, because an untrained model reproduces it and the identity term dominates. Any "the network is approximately linear" or "this direction survives a linear read-out" claim of ours must be accompanied by the identity baseline, the update-target version of the statistic, and a random-init control, or it says nothing. This is the concrete, cheap form of the general "check the statistic can move before scoring it" habit, and it supplies the exact three controls to report.
- **Framing:** separate what a component *passes through* from what it *writes*. The residual share and the update's linear predictability are two nearly complementary axes, and the pair is more informative than either — a boundary layer can be almost purely linear in its update while passing almost none of its input, and a mid-stack layer can be nearly all pass-through. For our own per-component profile, the pair (identity share, update linearity) is a better description than a single "linearity" number.
- **Method:** turning a measurement into a three-way *decision rule* and then validating it by intervening in the running model is the structure our evaluation should copy — the prediction is stated per component before the experiment ("this one is substitutable, that one is not"), and the experiment tests both halves, including the cases where the predicted-cheap action is made worse by forcing the expensive one.
- **Shortcut:** sufficient statistics give exact ridge solutions for every regularisation strength and both targets from a single forward pass over a few million tokens, which makes a full per-layer linear profile of a small model nearly free — worth doing as a baseline object before building anything more elaborate.
- **Caution:** a layer-level affine surrogate is a description of *this* model's token-wise computation only; the authors concede the cross-token part is out of reach by construction, so a good R² on a curated text distribution is not a licence to predict behaviour under intervention on attention.
