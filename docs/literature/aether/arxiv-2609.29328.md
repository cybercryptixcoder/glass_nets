# Grammatical "grandmother neurons" are rare in LLMs

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.29328
- **Authors / organisation:** Linyang He, Nima Mesgarani (Zuckerman Mind Brain Behavior Institute, Columbia University)
- **Date:** 2026-09-24 (v1)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29328
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (abstract, SS1-SS5, limitations, references, Appendices A-J)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The question is a granularity question: when a model behaves grammatically, is any of that competence carried by single units that fire in a language-specific way - the "grandmother neuron" idea transplanted from neuroscience - or is it spread thinly across many units that each contribute a little? The authors argue that the existing toolkit cannot answer this. Minimal-pair benchmarks treat the model as a black box; linear probes read a whole activation vector and contribute capacity of their own, so a probe's success says nothing about individual units; and mechanistic case studies reach the unit level but each is built around one or two phenomena, so nobody knows whether strong single-unit selectivity is the norm or the exception across a grammar. They want a measurement that is defined on one unit, comparable across dozens of linguistic contrasts, and needs no auxiliary training. It is a step toward deciding which unit of analysis a white-box account should use at all.

## What they studied

One main model, Qwen3-0.6B (28 layers x 1024 hidden, last-token hidden state), plus six replication checkpoints covering four families: Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Pythia-410M, TinyLlama-1.1B, Llama-3.1-8B. Data: BLiMP (67 paradigms) plus one pooled COMPS conceptual paradigm, giving a three-level hierarchy of 4 domains, 13 phenomena and 68 paradigms, and 116,300 templated English minimal pairs in total (human agreement 96.4% for BLiMP and 93.1% for COMPS). The unit of analysis is the single residual-stream coordinate at a layer - one scalar dimension of the hidden state - indexed as (layer, neuron).

## How they did it

For each paradigm, each grammatical/ungrammatical pair is run through the model and the last-token hidden state is recorded. For one neuron, the activations across all pairs are collected into two vectors, one for the grammatical members and one for the ungrammatical members, and the raw separability score is defined from the Pearson correlation between those two vectors: one minus the correlation, halved. Responsiveness is thus an item-wise profile question - whether the neuron's relative pattern across matched items is reorganised by the grammatical status - and the score is blind to uniform offsets and pure gain changes, both of which score the minimum. Because a raw score can be inflated by lexical overlap or sampling noise, they build a null by swapping the good/bad labels within exactly half the pairs (keeping each pair's two sentences whole, so lexical content is preserved and only the label alignment breaks) and repeating 500 times; the Neuron Separability Index is the observed raw score's distance from the permutation mean in units of the permutation standard deviation, floored at zero. Two operational thresholds are used: positive NSI means sensitive, NSI above 2 means strongly sensitive. Tuning breadth counts how many paradigms (or phenomena, or domains, via a boolean-OR over constituent paradigms) a neuron is responsive to, and a dendrogram over a phenomenon-by-neuron matrix checks whether broadly responsive neurons have a dominant preference. Three follow-ups: whole-vector logistic-regression probing at layer 14 with five-fold cross-validation, model behaviour scored by whether the grammatical member gets the higher total log-probability, and a frozen-candidate ablation in which the three units above threshold, plus their highest-scoring same-layer neighbours, are zeroed at k in {5, 10, 20} dimensions and compared with 100 same-size random same-layer groups on the change in the log-probability margin. Controls include random good-good and bad-bad pairings, deranged cross-item pairings, deleting the annotated critical token versus a matched non-critical token, Spearman and cosine variants of the score, and a threshold sweep from 0 to 3 with Benjamini-Hochberg calibration.

## What counts as an explanation here

A selectivity number per neuron per contrast, normalised against a label-permutation null, plus a breadth count over the paradigm hierarchy - a probe-free localisation statistic, deliberately not a circuit and deliberately not a causal claim. The authors are explicit that the observational half measures separability and that the ablation half tests only whether the top-scoring coordinates are a privileged set; they decline to treat a positive NSI as evidence of behavioural reliance. It is partial understanding by construction: a map of where a contrast is discriminable at unit granularity, with the causal question left open.

## How they judged success

Fraction of units with positive NSI per paradigm and per domain; the maximum NSI per paradigm and the count of paradigms containing any NSI above 2; mean NSI computed over sensitive units only; tuning breadth distributions with 95th percentile and the share of units responsive to 10 or more paradigms; reproduction of the same sparsity statistics across all seven checkpoints; a threshold sweep and empirical-null diagnostics (skewness, excess kurtosis, attainable minimum p-value) to justify treating 2 as descriptive rather than as a Gaussian cutoff; Pearson and Spearman correlations across the 67 BLiMP paradigms between behaviour, layer-14 probe accuracy, mean NSI and maximum NSI; and a targeted-ablation comparison of top, bottom and 100 random groups with paired-bootstrap confidence intervals and an empirical p-value over the random draws.

## What they found

Grammar-sensitive units are sparse everywhere. On Qwen3-0.6B the mean share of sensitive units (NSI above 0) is 8.27% for Concept, 3.31% for the Syntax-Semantics Interface, 3.88% for Morphology and 2.50% for Syntax, highest in early-to-middle layers and falling with depth. Even within that sensitive subset the average NSI sits around 0.5, i.e. weak. Strong selectivity is close to absent: for 65 of the 68 paradigms not a single neuron reaches NSI above 2, and for each of the remaining three exactly one unit does, out of 28x1024 coordinates (determiner-noun agreement 1 at layer 20 neuron 389, NSI 10.073; determiner-noun agreement with adjective 1 at layer 20 neuron 389, NSI 6.037; left-branch island echo question at layer 4 neuron 646, NSI 8.927). Breadth is narrow: an average paradigm recruits 2.52-3.94% of units across the seven checkpoints, the mean neuron responds to only 1.72-2.68 of 68 paradigms, the 95th percentile is 5-7, and fewer than 1% reach 10 paradigms in every model; poly-selective neurons, once clustered, still show a dominant within-domain preference rather than being general grammar detectors. The three measures dissociate: mean layer-14 probe accuracy is 0.917 against mean behavioural accuracy of 0.758, and the two are uncorrelated across paradigms (r = -0.032), with sentential_subject_island at probe 1.000 and behaviour 0.238. Mean NSI is also uncorrelated with behaviour (r = -0.021) and is negatively correlated with the probe (r = -0.557). Ablating the three above-threshold units and their neighbours is no more damaging than 100 random same-size groups: all nine group comparisons have empirical p >= 0.109 and accuracy changes at or below 0.009, and the one sizeable margin drop (left-branch island, k = 20, margin change -0.543, CI -0.630 to -0.455) is matched by its own bottom-20 control at -0.439. Controls behave as intended: random same-label pairs have raw scores near 0.50 but produce no units above 2, deranged cross-item pairs produce sporadic high-scoring artefacts, and deleting the critical token in the clean one-prefix subset drops mean raw separability from 0.0753 to 0.0011 while deleting a matched non-critical token leaves it at 0.0746. No unit survives Benjamini-Hochberg at q of 0.05 or 0.01, which the authors report as a conservative sanity check limited by a minimum attainable p-value of about 0.005 with 200 permutations. The raw-score layer ordering runs Concept (90% saturation layer 19.0) later than Syntax-Semantics Interface (10.3), Syntax (9.5) and Morphology (8.4), and corresponds only weakly with probing saturation layers (Spearman rho 0.23).

## Limits and open problems they name

Seven decoder-only English checkpoints on two templated benchmarks, so the result is scoped to those models and resources and does not speak to natural discourse, gradient acceptability or other languages. Separability is not causality, and the intervention covers three paradigms in one checkpoint with the selection and evaluation pairs shared - a within-benchmark test, not a replication - so the null is compatible with redundancy, superposition, or the coarseness of zeroing residual coordinates. Reading only the last token and treating each scalar as a neuron can miss information carried earlier or in attention and MLP signals, and neuron definitions vary by architecture. The permutation null does not model residual lexical, tokenisation or template dependence, and which similarity metric is used changes what the statistic measures. Next steps they name: patching and mediation to turn neuron clusters into circuits, and multilingual minimal pairs.

## What we take from it

Four things. (1) **A null that preserves the data and breaks only the label is the right cheapest substitute for a trained probe** - swapping good/bad within half the pairs keeps lexical content fixed and destroys only the contrast, so the null distribution is paradigm-matched rather than drawn from unrelated data, and it costs no parameters. This is the probe-free panel our white-box model should carry beside every probe we report. (2) **The dissociation is the strongest argument in the entry and it is quantitative**: probe 0.917 against behaviour 0.758 with r = -0.032 across 67 paradigms, so decodability, unit-level selectivity and behavioural reliance are three separate columns, and their own probe-versus-mean-NSI correlation is *negative* (r = -0.557), which says the three columns are not even monotonically ordered. (3) **A frozen-candidate ablation with 100 random same-size same-layer groups is the standard our causal claims should meet**, and the specific failure mode worth copying is that they report the bottom-set control alongside the top set - a top-20 drop of -0.543 that is not the right comparison when the bottom-20 also drops -0.439, and a requirement stated as "no more damaging than same-size random at p < 0.05" rather than "there is an effect". (4) **The threshold-and-null diagnostics are published as a reason to distrust the threshold**: the threshold sweep is monotone-flat from 1.645 to 3, kurtosis is 4.45, and the attainable p-value is too coarse for 28,672 tests, so they demote "NSI above 2" to an operational label. Warnings, all three: thinning the unit set can mimic localisation when the statistic is uncalibrated; a null built by breaking item pairing rather than labels manufactures outliers (50 of 68 paradigms above threshold in their cross-item control); and the paper's own raw-score ordering across linguistic domains should not be read as a depth finding, since the saturation layers agree only weakly with the probe (rho 0.23).
