# Explainable Federated Learning via Global–Local Attribution Alignment

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.20445
- **Authors / organisation:** Dawood Wasif, Terrence J. Moore, Chang-Tien Lu, Jin-Hee Cho — Virginia Tech (Dept. of Computer Science, Falls Church VA) and DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory, Adelphi MD
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20445
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML; abstract, §§1–6, appendices A–J, all tables; figures read only through the sentences and captions that state their numbers)
- **Relevance to us:** 2 (useful)

## What they wanted, and why

Federated learning keeps data on clients, so a model can be trained without centralising anything — but that same fragmentation means the *explanations* of the model are fragmented too. The paper names the resulting failure **explanation drift**: different clients legitimately arrive at the same prediction through different features, so client-local attributions disagree with each other, and a system-wide summary assembled from them is either incoherent (purely local) or homogenised in a way that erases minority behaviour (naive server-side averaging). Their target is a federated explainer that is simultaneously locally faithful, cross-client consistent, privacy-preserving and cheap in bandwidth — three requirements they treat as first-class rather than as afterthoughts. The larger goal is trustworthy deployment of federated models in regulated settings, where an auditor needs a single explanation artefact that holds across silos.

## What they studied

Six standard benchmarks spanning three modalities — MNIST and CIFAR-10 (small CNN), AG News and IMDb (TextCNN), UCI Adult Income and German Credit (shallow MLP) — plus two further testbeds in an appendix: an MRI Alzheimer's-versus-control slice classifier and a credit-card-fraud detector. Each dataset is split over eight clients, either IID (near-uniform sharding emulated with a very high-concentration Dirichlet draw) or non-IID (label-skewed Dirichlet with a small concentration). The unit of analysis is a **group-level attribution distribution over the simplex**: pixels for images, tokens for text, feature groups for tabular data — deliberately low-dimensional and modality-agnostic rather than a dense per-input saliency map. Comparisons are against plain FedAvg without explanations, client-local Integrated Gradients, a server-aggregated attribution template, and an interpretable-by-design federated model whose weights are the explanation.

## How they did it

The design separates two spaces and lets them co-evolve only through a bounded channel. Each client (a) trains the ordinary task model by FedAvg, unchanged; (b) fits a small on-device surrogate by temperature distillation of the task model's class distribution, regularised for sparsity in the group space; (c) explains the *surrogate* rather than the network, with a path-integrated gradient in group space from a zero baseline, normalised to a probability distribution over groups and averaged per class; (d) compresses that per-class distribution to its top-k entries as index–magnitude pairs, clips their L2 norm, quantises the magnitudes, and adds Gaussian noise; (e) uploads only that artefact, under secure aggregation. The server aggregates the artefacts per class with a coordinatewise median (or trimmed mean) and renormalises to a simplex, producing a Global Explanation Prior, which it broadcasts. On the next round each client adds a Jensen–Shannon divergence between its own top-k explanation distribution and the prior, as a penalty on **surrogate** parameters only, with a linear warm-up over the first six of fifteen rounds to the target weight. The task model never sees the prior, so — the authors' central design claim — accuracy dynamics are exactly FedAvg's while explanation semantics are steered. Appendices give dispersion-contraction, surrogate-fidelity, per-round DP-sensitivity and standard convergence statements, each resting on explicit assumptions (smoothness of the surrogate map, nonexpansiveness of the median aggregator, Lipschitz dependence of client summaries on the prior).

## What counts as an explanation here

A class-conditional probability distribution over groups, on the simplex, exchanged as a sparse top-k vector — a *feature-group attribution dictionary* that is shared across clients and updated every round. It is partial by design: only k groups per class survive compression, so the artefact is a summary of where explanation mass concentrates, not a complete account of any single prediction. It is also explicitly a surrogate's explanation of a surrogate's imitation: the attributions are computed on a distilled student, and the paper's own fidelity lemmas are about the student tracking the teacher, not about the student's attributions being the teacher's mechanism. Nothing in the artefact is causal, and the impact statement says so — alignment improves consistency and does not guarantee fairness, calibration or causal faithfulness.

## How they judged success

Four measured quantities, each with a matched reference. **Task accuracy** (top-1, five seeds, fresh client partitions per seed). **Explanation Drift Index (EDI)**: the mean Jensen–Shannon divergence between each client's per-class explanation distribution and a *method-specific* global reference — the prior for their method, the aggregated template for FedAttr-Agg, the unweighted client mean for Local-XAI, the interpretable model's class-weight vector for Fed-XAI — so the comparison is client-to-global within each method and every method gets its natural system-level object. **Deletion AUC and insertion AUC** over the top-ranked groups, read as perturbation fidelity. **Privacy**: membership-inference accuracy and advantage from a shadow-model logistic attack on confidence/loss/entropy statistics. **Robustness**: attribution poisoning by a fraction of clients that inflates a target group set, scored by the change in the top-k support index and the change in EDI. Ablations are one-factor-at-a-time over alignment weight, noise scale, sparsity k and surrogate epochs.

## What they found

On MNIST (IID / non-IID) their method holds FedAvg accuracy at 0.986 / 0.930 while EDI falls to 0.000 / 0.076, against 0.110 for client-local explanations and 0.159 for aggregated ones under non-IID; the fairness of running each method against its own reference is what makes those numbers comparable, and the interpretable-by-design model reaches EDI 0.300 at FedAvg-level accuracy only by collapsing to 0.449 accuracy under label skew. CIFAR-10 non-IID: 0.460 accuracy at EDI 0.027; IMDb non-IID is the largest accuracy gap in their favour (0.702 against 0.550 FedAvg); UCI Credit leads on both accuracy and explanation quality. The ablation isolates the alignment term as the driver of consistency: EDI 0.006 at alignment weight 0.0 down to 0.0006 at 0.4, with deletion and insertion AUC essentially unmoved, which is a genuinely clean separation of the consistency axis from the fidelity axis. Two results cut against the paper's framing and it reports them anyway: **adding light DP noise lowers drift** (EDI 0.0091 at zero noise to roughly 0.0007–0.0012 once noise is on), because noise before the coordinatewise median smooths client-specific idiosyncrasy; and **more surrogate refinement makes drift worse** (0.0007 at one epoch to 0.0012 at four). Privacy effects are small and noisy — membership-inference advantage differs by hundredths, several baselines tied to the third decimal, so the "reduces leakage" claim is directionally supported and not strongly measured. Poisoning is the strongest robustness result: at a 0.2 poisoned fraction the change in top-k support is 0.0008 against 0.0041–0.0043 for every other method, and the advantage holds to 0.3 before all methods converge at 0.4. Scalability to a much larger client population is reported as a nearly constant EDI with accuracy tracking FedAvg, but the supporting cells in Table 8 do not survive the build. Overhead: roughly 1,600 KB per round against 1,589 KB for FedAvg, a ten-thousand-parameter surrogate that never leaves the device, and training time up about 22% on GPU.

## Limits and open problems they name

The framework presupposes a meaningful and reasonably stable group space; when groups are ambiguous or their semantics drift, the prior would need adaptive grouping or drift-aware updates, and nothing in the paper tests that regime. Extensions to regression, to federated language models, and to adaptive adversaries are listed as future work, as is strengthening the coupled task–explanation convergence and privacy guarantees beyond the honest-but-curious, secure-aggregation, Gaussian-mechanism assumptions. Byzantine adversaries beyond the breakdown point of a median aggregator are explicitly out of scope. An expert-in-the-loop utility study is deferred, so no human ever rates whether the aligned explanations are better. The ablation's most interesting effects — noise helping, refinement hurting — are given a mechanism story (implicit regularisation, overfitting to local idiosyncrasy) but no test that could refute it.

## What we take from it

Three transferable instruments and one warning. **Instrument:** a drift metric needs a *method-specific* reference, not a single shared ground truth — scoring each explainer against its own natural global object is the fair version of a consistency comparison, and we should copy that whenever we compare two classes of read-out that produce different kinds of artefact. **Instrument:** the poisoning metric (change in the top-k support set) and the drift metric (change in cross-client divergence) are two different kinds of instability and the paper reports both — the same magnitude-versus-ranking split this folder keeps meeting, here applied to explanation *support* rather than attribution size. **Instrument:** the ablation's zero-alignment row is a built-in control that isolates the coordinating term from the surrogate pipeline, which is the cheap way to show a consistency win is not a fidelity win in disguise. **Warning:** an explanation may be coordinated to near-zero drift at almost no cost to the model, because the alignment penalty is absorbed entirely by a disposable student that is scored only on how well it imitates. Here that is the selling point; for a white-box model of a black box it is precisely the surrogate-of-a-surrogate hazard — a prior that every client's explanation agrees with is evidence about the aggregation channel, and the paper's own two inverted ablations show how easily such agreement can be manufactured by regularisation rather than by the mechanism.
