# Signatures of Steerability in Activation Space of Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.14151
- **Authors / organisation:** Prajjwal Bhattarai, Tuka Alhanai; New York University Abu Dhabi (and NYU Tandon)
- **Date:** 2026-09-12
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14151
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Steering with a contrastive direction works for some concepts and barely at all for others, and the field's usual explanation is that the *dataset* decides - a vector works when the concept is cleanly expressed in the activations it was averaged from. The authors want that claim turned into something usable before an intervention is attempted: a quantity computable from the training activations alone that says whether the resulting vector will have a usable dose-response at all. Their framing is deliberately modest - the aim is a diagnostic, not a method - but the ambition is real, because a cheap pre-hoc score would let an experimenter choose which concepts, layers and datasets are worth steering rather than discovering the answer after a sweep. The paper also asks *why* such a score would work, and answers with a synthetic superposition experiment in which the true feature direction is known by construction, so a predictor can be attributed to one structural property rather than another. The larger goal is to move steering reliability from anecdote to a measurable property of the activation distribution.

## What they studied
Six instruction-tuned models at two scales - Qwen3 14B and 4B, Gemma3 12B and 4B, Llama3.1 8B and Llama3.2 3B - over 38 binary-choice datasets: 26 persona sets from the Anthropic model-written evaluations, 5 truthfulness sets from a "truthfulness spectrum" suite (FLEED: definitional, empirical, logical, fictional, ethical), and 7 SafetyBench categories, English only. Each item is a prompt with a desired and an undesired completion, so a steering vector can be built as a contrastive mean and success can be read as a logit difference. The unit of analysis is the *dataset-layer pair*: for every model, every dataset and every candidate residual layer, one vector, one set of separation statistics, and one behavioural steerability number. The controlled counterpart is synthetic: vectors in ambient dimensions 512 to 5,120 built from a dictionary of near-orthogonal features plus one designated target direction, with dictionary-to-dimension ratio, maximum coherence, number of active noise features and class mean gap all swept.

## How they did it
A 50-50 split per dataset fixes the discipline: vectors and every separation statistic are computed on the train half only, and steerability is measured on the test half. The vector is the standard mean difference of last-token residual activations between the desired and undesired completion, at each layer in turn. Steerability is taken from prior work as the *slope* of a linear fit between the steering multiplier and the mean propensity (the desired-minus-undesired logit difference) over a range of multipliers - so the target quantity is how reliably more strength buys more behaviour, not whether one chosen strength moves the metric. Four separation statistics are then correlated against that slope across the dataset-layer grid: a signal-to-noise ratio of the squared mean-difference norm over the summed traces of the two class covariances; the mean cosine between each individual contrastive pair difference and the aggregate vector; a manifold-capacity measure derived from the number of random projections needed for the two activation clouds to become linearly separable; and the intrinsic dimension (Two-NN estimator) of the cloud of pair-difference vectors. Confounds are handled by partial correlation: both the statistic and the steerability are residualised by ordinary least squares against dataset indicator variables, or against layer indicators, and the correlation is recomputed on the residuals, with Spearman versions obtained by rank-transforming first. Bootstrapped confidence intervals over 5,000 resamples are reported for the point estimates, an ablation correlates the SNR numerator and denominator separately, and a layer map compares the layer of peak average steerability with the layer where each statistic correlates best. The synthetic arm runs 300 structural configurations crossed with five mean gaps and four seeds - 1,500 configuration-gap cells per seed, 6,000 runs - drawing 400 contrastive pairs each, with noise coefficients drawn from the same location and scale as the target coefficient so the target is not recoverable from marginal statistics; the recovered direction's cosine with the true direction, the number of active noise features and the maximum dictionary coherence are the three candidate explanations, and within-configuration correlations are reported beside the pooled ones to check that no association is an artefact of pooling.

## What counts as an explanation here
A statistic, not a mechanism: a scalar computable from the same activations that build the vector, reported as a rank-order predictor of how well the vector will behave. The authors are explicit that this is diagnostic rather than causal - separation is claimed to *track* steerability, and the synthetic arm is there to supply the only mechanistic story they are willing to tell, namely that the statistic tracks how close the empirical mean difference sits to the true behavioural direction. Completeness is not claimed at any point; there is no feature dictionary, no circuit, no surrogate. It is a screening instrument for an experiment that has not been run yet, and its explanatory content is the claim that a steering vector's reliability is a property of how well a handful of pairs pin down one direction.

## How they judged success
Pearson and Spearman correlation between each separation statistic and the test-set steerability slope, reported per model across the whole dataset-layer grid, then again after residualising against dataset indicators and against layer indicators separately; all cells are claimed significant at the 0.05 level except the cells explicitly marked non-significant. Robustness comes from bootstrapped 95% intervals, per-family breakdowns (persona, truthfulness, safety reported as three separate tables), and the layer-alignment table. The synthetic arm is judged by Spearman correlation between each statistic and each of the three structural quantities, with a within-configuration re-run (median and interquartile range over configurations, twenty observations each) so that pooled and stratified estimates can be compared. Controls against triviality are the numerator/denominator split of the SNR and the fact that the best-predicting statistics are also the ones that behave identically inside a fixed configuration.

## What they found
Separation predicts steerability, and one statistic does it best: the SNR, correlating between 0.69 and 0.82 (Pearson) across the six models, with the mean pair-cosine close behind (0.63 to 0.82) and manifold capacity well behind it (0.47 to 0.76). Intrinsic dimension is near-useless (0.16 to 0.39) and its correlation halves once layers are residualised, which the authors read as it tracking a depth property rather than anything dataset-general. The confound controls barely move the picture: residualising against datasets leaves the SNR at 0.69 to 0.78, and residualising against layers leaves it at 0.64 to 0.79, while manifold capacity loses a lot in the layer-controlled table. Splitting the SNR into its numerator and denominator is the paper's most interesting ablation: the numerator alone reaches 0.60 to 0.72 on five models but collapses to -0.03 and 0.12 on Llama3.1 8B and Llama3.2 3B, and the denominator alone is *negatively* correlated everywhere (-0.12 to -0.25 Pearson, -0.30 to -0.59 Spearman) - so the ratio is doing work that neither part does, and the part that helps is the one that subtracts class spread. Per family, everything is tighter on truthfulness (SNR 0.69 to 0.86) and safety (0.77 to 0.90) than on persona (0.58 to 0.81), and persona is the family where intrinsic dimension flips sign and goes negative. The synthetic arm gives the interpretation: SNR and the mean cosine correlate with the recovered direction's alignment to the true direction at 0.91 and 0.90, with the number of active features at -0.33, and with dictionary coherence at essentially zero (-0.01, not significant); manifold capacity tracks alignment at only 0.43; intrinsic dimension tracks alignment at -0.42 pooled but at +0.006 within a configuration, which the authors present as proof that it is a between-regime quantity. So the histogram of pairs is not measuring how crowded the overcomplete dictionary is, which is what the superposition literature would suggest it should measure. The layer map shows the statistic-peak layers landing within four layers of the most steerable layer for five of six models and within two for four of them, with the mean-cosine statistic matching most often.

## Limits and open problems they name
Everything is CAA - one construction, one position (last token), one linearity assumption - so nothing is known about optimised or non-linear interventions. The analysis is correlational, and the authors name the third-variable possibility outright: feature recovery and steerability could both be driven by something they never measured. Evaluation is multiple-choice propensity only, so a concept can look strongly steerable while fluency or unrelated-task accuracy degrades, which is not tested. Beyond what they name: the predictor is a rank order across dataset-layer pairs, not a decision, so no threshold is proposed and no calibration of "steerable enough" is offered; the truthfulness arm is five datasets and the safety arm seven categories from one benchmark, so the family-level reading rests on a small number of clusters; and the entire synthetic arm assumes the linear representation hypothesis it is meant to test.

## What we take from it
Three things, in order of use. First, a ready-made cheap screen: the ratio of mean-difference squared norm to summed class covariance trace is computable in the same pass that builds our vector, needs no labels beyond the contrastive pairs already in hand, and here out-predicts both the individual-pair cosine and the manifold geometry - so our own intervention reports can carry it as a pre-registered prediction, with the behavioural result reported beside it rather than substituted by it. Second, the numerator/denominator ablation is the pattern to copy for any composite statistic we invent: the ratio is only justified because neither part reproduces it, and the denominator's *negative* correlation is a genuine finding about what the spread of the contrastive classes does. Third, the within-configuration re-check is the cheapest available anti-pooling control and it earns its place here by destroying the intrinsic-dimension result outright - a statistic that correlates at -0.42 pooled and at 0.006 within a stratum was measuring which regime a point came from, not which point it is, and every pooled correlation in our own reports should be accompanied by the stratified version. The synthetic arm also gives us a template for attributing a predictor: hold the dictionary structure fixed, know the true direction by construction, and let the candidate mechanisms - recovery, sparsity, coherence - compete.
