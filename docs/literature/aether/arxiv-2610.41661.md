## What they wanted, and why
Activation steering is sold as a scalpel: add one contrastive direction to the residual stream and the model's behaviour moves. The authors' starting observation is that the collateral damage from that operation has only ever been measured as a *scalar quality drop* — perplexity, or a coherence score — which says the text got worse without saying in what respect. They want the damage itemised. If steering reliably makes a model less formal, less willing to cite evidence, more ambiguous, more contradictory, then "steered behaviour achieved" is an incomplete report of what was done to the model, and a safety practitioner reading that report would not know which capability they had quietly spent. The larger goal is a correction that is *orthogonal* in practice and not just in intent: restore the damaged property while leaving the intended behaviour where the steering put it. The setting is the standard contrastive binary-choice one, so the result is not tied to an exotic steering method.

## What they studied
Three instruction-tuned open-weight models of comparable scale from different families — Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B (thinking mode off) and Gemma-2-9B-IT — plus a fourth model used only as an annotator (DeepSeek-V4-Flash as the LLM judge). Data: eleven Model-Written Evaluation Datasets from the advanced-AI-risk and persona families plus a self-awareness set, 8,284 items total, spanning behaviours such as myopic reward, wealth seeking, corrigibility, willingness to use physical force and self-reported capability. The units of analysis are three different things at once: the **behaviour vector** (one contrastive mean-difference direction per dataset), the **discourse dimension** (a property of the generated text, scored 0/1/2 by the judge), and the **discourse vector** (a second contrastive direction extracted from generations labelled at the extremes of one dimension). Three models × eleven datasets × fifteen dimensions is the grid that makes the first result.

## How they did it
Steering is Contrastive Activation Addition: the behaviour vector is the mean residual-stream activation at the last prompt token for the positive option minus the same for the negative option, added at every generated token with a per-model multiplier α (four magnitudes each sign, ranging from ±0.5 to ±4 depending on model); layer and α range are chosen by maximising per-sample steerability, defined as the slope of the logit difference between the two options as α varies. For every question the pipeline generates one unsteered continuation and steered continuations at each α, then compares each steered text with its unsteered counterpart in four steps: an LLM judge is asked open-endedly what differs, over 288 sampled pairs, and produces 1,028 candidate dimensions; one human merges and prunes these to 26; the judge then scores both texts on all 26 on a 0/1/2 presence scale, three times at temperature 0.6 with majority vote; and the judge is validated against a human annotator on 120 pairs by *directional* agreement — whether the two raters agree on the sign of the shift — screened at a weighted κ of 0.41, which leaves fifteen dimensions. The restoration experiment extracts, for each dimension, a contrastive direction from unsteered generations labelled 0 versus 2 (averaged last-token activations, the 2-mean minus the 0-mean), deliberately preferring unsteered over steered generations because the latter are already displaced toward the behaviour vector. The correction is added beside the behaviour vector with strength tied to the steering strength (β = k·|α|, so no steering means no correction), in three variants: at the same layer, at a different layer, and with the component of the discourse direction lying along the behaviour direction projected out first. Only samples the steering pushed from one extreme to the other (0→2 or 2→0) are intervened on, capped at 400 per direction; success is the judge returning the text to the unsteered score, measured cumulatively as k rises and stopped for a sample once it succeeds.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **named dimension of the generated text plus a direction in activation space that moves it** — a two-layer account in which the description of the damage is a human-legible linguistic property and the mechanism behind the damage is a linear direction. It is predictive in a narrow, testable sense: if the direction is what carries the property, subtracting it should return the text to its unsteered score and leave the behaviour steering intact, and that is exactly what is measured. It is explicitly partial — the authors do not claim the dimension vector *is* the property, and they report the places where the account breaks (repetition, where the direction had to be extracted from steered text and performs worst) as evidence that the vector's provenance matters rather than as noise.

## How they judged success
For the identification half: a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the unsteered-minus-steered score difference for every (model, dataset, dimension) triplet, Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the tests within each model at α = 0.05, with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation as an effect size and Kendall's τ_b for inter-dimension correlations. Judge reliability is Cohen's weighted κ for directional agreement, with PABAK substituted on the three dimensions too sparse (<15% non-zero pairs) for κ, plus ICC, Krippendorff's α, Gwet's AC1 and absolute-score agreement reported in the appendix. For the restoration half: three outcome measures on the same regenerations — fraction restored to the unsteered score (judge-scored), per-sample steerability recomputed as a ratio to plain CAA, and behaviour retention defined as the logit difference keeping its original *sign*, which treats a preference reversal as the failure rather than a magnitude change. A human pass over 484 sampled successful interventions categorises defects.

## What they found
The damage is broad and mostly invisible to perplexity. Of 495 (model, dataset, dimension) tests, 424 (86%) are significant. Across models and datasets steering increases ambiguity (94% of tests), contradiction (91%) and metaphor (76%), while unsteered text scores higher on formality (46% of tests versus 30% in the opposite direction) and on claims supported by evidence (49% versus 33%). Repetition rises in 61% of tests, largely from Qwen and Gemma. Direction is model-dependent for reasoning depth and contextualisation — higher for steered text in Qwen, the reverse for Llama and Gemma — so a per-model sign is the honest unit and not a global claim. The perplexity defence fails quantitatively: the highest correlation between perplexity and any dimension score is 0.38 (contextualisation in Qwen), and the dimensions are largely distinct from each other, with the strongest inter-dimension Kendall τ_b at 0.52.

Restoration works at the level of the aggregate but not uniformly. Across dimensions and models the intervention returns 78% of samples to the unsteered score; removing a dimension steering introduced succeeds 83% of the time against 74% for adding one back. Qwen (85%) and Llama (84%) are comparable and Gemma (67%) the weakest, almost entirely on the add direction (62%). By dimension, evaluative language is corrected most reliably (93%), then terminology use (86%) and formality (83%); reasoning depth (80%) and contextualisation (79%) sit in the middle but are strongly asymmetric, with reasoning depth reaching 96% when subtracting and 63% when adding. Repetition is the outlier at 48%, and the authors attribute this to its vector being extracted from steered rather than unsteered generations, i.e. entangled with the behaviour direction. All three intervention variants perform comparably; steerability after correction sits at 0.86–0.89 of CAA, and the behaviour's logit sign survives in 92.8% of samples overall, with one clear exception — adding formality in Qwen keeps the sign only 61.0% of the time. Of 484 human-inspected successful interventions, 74.0% are clean, 16.3% repeat, 4.1% are nonsensical and 5.8% fall in other defect classes.

## Limits and open problems they name
The intervention is applied only where the steering moved a dimension from one extreme to the other, and those samples are not identifiable in advance, so a deployable system would need a detection step the paper does not build. The correction is itself steering, so it inherits steering's oversteering sensitivity. Dimensions are restored one at a time; correlated dimensions were checked and not consistently disturbed, but joint multi-dimension restoration is untested. Only contrastive activation addition is covered. The whole evaluation rests on an LLM judge whose scale use is expensive, validated on 120 pairs with mean κ 0.48 — moderate, not strong — and one dimension at a time is scored, so a dimension the judge cannot see is not in the list. Three of the fifteen dimensions were too sparse for κ and are carried on PABAK alone.

## What we take from it
(1) **Off-target effect is a measurable vector, not a scalar, and the scalar hides it.** This is the sharpest version of the folder's recurring point that one number per intervention is the wrong report: the same run that "succeeds" at steering produces a profile of fifteen discourse changes, and the profile is not recoverable from perplexity (r ≤ 0.38). The transferable measurement shape is a fixed, human-validated rubric of output properties scored before and after the intervention on paired generations, reported per property with the fraction of tests significant — which is cheap for us and directly usable for probe-driven edits and dictionary interventions, where "faithfulness unchanged" is currently our only side-effect check. (2) **The sign test on the intended effect is the right retention criterion when the aim is to show the intervention did not hijack the behaviour.** Requiring the logit difference to keep its sign is stricter than "steerability stayed high" and it catches the case a ratio hides: Qwen's formality adoption keeps a healthy steerability ratio but flips the preferred option in 39% of samples. For us the analogue is: after a corrective intervention, re-check the *decision*, not only the score. (3) **A direction extracted from displaced text is contaminated by the displacement, and the failure is visible in the aggregate.** The repetition vector had to come from steered generations and is the worst performer at 48%, entangled with the behaviour direction where the two vectors become more similar in later layers. That is a general rule for our own vector extraction: record which condition the contrastive set was drawn from, and expect a vector extracted inside the steered distribution to carry part of the steering. (4) **The per-model sign split on reasoning depth is a warning against pooling.** The same intervention raises a dimension in one family and lowers it in another, so an average over models would read as null — the same shape as the folder's aggregate-versus-stratum lesson.
