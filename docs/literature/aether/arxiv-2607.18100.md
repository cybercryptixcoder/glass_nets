## What they wanted, and why
Long reasoning traces are where frontier models spend most of their inference budget, yet almost all inference-time control acts on the prompt, on the surface tokens, or as one global direction applied uniformly for the whole response. The authors' observation is that a failing trace has a shape: it revisits the same kind of step without making progress. They therefore narrow the control question down to something a single experiment can answer — can an activation offset at the right moment push the model out of its *current* reasoning state on the very next step? The larger ambition is to make latent reasoning states a manipulable interface rather than a descriptive summary, with "exit the state you are stuck in" as the first control primitive. Everything else in the paper (the transition analysis, the vector bank, the online controller) is scaffolding around that one causal question.

## What they studied
Three steering targets: Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 (the main one), Qwen3-32B-Thinking, and Gemma-4-E2B (a different family). A separate non-chat model, Qwen3-4B-Base, serves as the embedding/classification model whose activations define the states — deliberately not fine-tuned on chat so the features depend on step content rather than on target behaviour. Data: four reasoning benchmarks (gsm8k, aqua, logiqa, math), roughly 254-500 traces each and 11k-25k segmented steps. Unit of analysis: a *step* (a span between sentence-initial discourse markers, mean-pooled into one vector) and its residual-stream activation at the token that opens the step, across a patched layer range. The intervention is indexed by state, not by token or by prompt.

## How they did it
Segmentation splits each trace at sentence-initial discourse markers rather than paragraph breaks, so a coherent thought is not cut in half. Each step is mean-pooled over the embedding model's last-layer hidden states, z-scored per dimension with statistics fitted once per dataset, and k-means with K=5 partitions all steps of a dataset into latent states; cluster ids are dataset-local and do not align across datasets (Hungarian alignment below 0.4). For each step, a binary label records whether the trajectory left the cluster on the next step (crosser) or stayed (stayer), and the authors cache the steering target's residual at the discourse-marker token of every step, one shot. Per (cluster, layer) the direction is simply the mean crosser residual minus the mean stayer residual; pools are not conditioned on trace correctness. At inference, the controller re-runs the embedding model over the just-completed step (reusing a KV cache), assigns the nearest centroid, and fires a gate only when two consecutive steps land in the same cluster — i.e. only after the model has already failed once to leave on its own. When the gate fires, the residual stream is patched at every token of the *next* step at every layer in the patched range, with the unit-normalised direction scaled by the ambient residual norm and a single strength alpha; the patch is removed when the step closes and is re-classified. K, seed, the layer-range scheme and the every-token patch mode are identical across datasets and models; only alpha is retuned per model (0.05 for both Qwen targets, 0.025 for Gemma, above which Gemma degenerates into token loops).

## What counts as an explanation here
A *state-indexed direction bank plus a discrete transition graph over states*. The explanation of a failure is not a text description and not a circuit: it is the claim that the trace re-entered a cluster, plus a vector that measurably raises the local probability of leaving that cluster. It is explicitly partial — source-conditioned, exit-only — and the formal section is candid that the vector is a correlational proxy for the causal gradient of the exit probability (a Fisher discriminant under a shared-covariance Gaussian assumption, exact only when the covariance is isotropic), never the gradient itself. The paper's own framing is that the empirical sign controls, not the theory, are what license calling the direction causal. It predicts a single-step event (does the next step fall in a different cluster) and nothing beyond that: no target state, no end-task accuracy, no token count.

## How they judged success
A deliberately local metric: the per-cluster *hit rate*, i.e. the fraction of held-out stayer prefixes (steps in incorrect traces where the previous step was also in the same cluster) whose replayed next step is re-classified into a different cluster. Four conditions per prefix: greedy decoding, norm-matched random Gaussian offset, the same vector with reversed sign, and the positive direction. Controls are the two ablations rather than any statistical test — the paper states plainly that per-cell sample sizes differ and that no confidence intervals or significance tests are reported. Baseline transition statistics (dwell, forward and backward mass, absorption) are computed from the empirical transition counts between clusters.

## What they found
Incorrect traces are longer than correct ones on all four datasets, and per-step transitions are dominated by staying: dwell mass is 0.937 (gsm8k), 0.953 (aqua), 0.955 (logiqa) and 0.948 (math), with backward mass zero and the last phase absorbing in the data (P(P5|P5) = 1.000). Clusters ordered by mean step position form a near-deterministic forward chain — reading, first computation, mid verification, late deliberation, rumination tail — a fact the authors flag as partly a consequence of ordering states by step position. On the main target, Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, the positive direction is best or tied-best in 15 of 16 applicable cells: gsm8k C3 goes from 25.0% to 100%, gsm8k C2 from 17.9% to 62.1%, aqua C0 from 0.0% to 51.7%, and logiqa C2 from 31.0% to 80.0%. On Qwen3-32B-Thinking and Gemma-4-E2B the picture is mixed: some cells gain heavily (32B aqua C0 53.3% to 92.9%; Gemma aqua C1 5.0% to 29.2%) but the positive direction is at or below greedy decoding in a non-trivial minority. Random steering at matched norm is usually indistinguishable from greedy decoding, and negative steering usually lowers the hit rate (Qwen3-4B aqua C2 27.6% to 0.0%), though a few cells improve under the negative sign too, which the authors attribute to clusters that are unstable equilibria. Gains vary sharply between clusters of the same dataset, which they offer as suggestive evidence of state-specificity while conceding it is not proof.

## Limits and open problems they name
The metric certifies that the model left a looped state and nothing else — no accuracy, no token-efficiency, no coherence claim is made, and the authors call end-to-end measurement the most important next step. Control is undirected: the vector is built to raise exit probability, not to route to a chosen successor, and target-addressable control is explicitly not claimed. The state-specificity argument rests on cluster-to-cluster spread with no comparison against a single pooled global exit vector. No confidence intervals, and few-point differences are inside binomial noise; transfer to the 32B and Gemma targets is partial. The self-loop failure mode is evidenced indirectly, since the aggregate transition statistics are not split by trace correctness. The pipeline depends on a second model as an online classifier, on per-dataset k-means, and on a per-model alpha; the gate can fire repeatedly and push a trace into the absorbing rumination phase.

## What we take from it
Three things. **(1) The design pattern we wanted**: this is a per-step re-selection loop — classify the just-completed step, re-decide whether to intervene, patch the next step, then re-classify and remove the patch — and it is the cleanest in-folder demonstration that a selection made per prompt cannot follow a generation. It also names the failure mode of that design honestly: the selector is a *second model*, so the re-selection is only as faithful as the embedding model's cluster assignment, and the vector bank was built in *its* coordinate system rather than in the target's. For our predictive white-box model the rule is: when the model's own state estimate drives the intervention, the state estimate must come from the same network that is edited, and any external classifier used to choose the edit is reported as an ablation, since a cross-model classifier's error is invisible in the edit's hit rate. **(2) The gate as a reusable object**: "intervene only after the model has failed once on its own" is a two-line control with no learned parameters, and it is the cheapest thing in the paper that could be lifted whole; but the paper also shows its limit — the same gate cannot tell a productive third pass from a destructive one, which is why a trace can be pushed into the absorbing tail. **(3) A warning about measuring what was asked**: every headline number in this paper is about the intervention's own target event, and that is also true of much of what we surveyed; the hit rate rises while nothing is claimed about whether the trace got better. For us, a loop-exit rate is a legitimate *predictive* claim (the model said the next step would leave this state, and it did) but a bad *control* claim, and our model should report both the transition the intervention targeted and the downstream quantity it did not.
