## What they wanted, and why

Steering work inherits two assumptions the authors want to drop at once: that a concept is a fixed direction in activation space, and that one intervention vector per concept is enough. They argue that a fixed representation becomes an information bottleneck once many behaviours must be distinguished, since one vector has to encode every difference the task cares about. Their alternative keeps a single set of learned parameters but lets the induced activation change vary with whatever the model is currently reading. If that works, one training run on pooled preference data buys a reusable steering device rather than one bespoke vector per concept. The larger goal is the standard steerability-versus-utility trade-off, approached by buying context-dependence from architecture instead of from per-input fitting at inference.

## What they studied

Six instruction-tuned backbones in three families at two scales each: Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3.2-3B and Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B. The unit of the intervention is a set of low-rank updates (rank 16) applied to all four attention projection matrices — query, key, value and output — at every transformer layer, so 4L adapted matrices per model. Training data is a pooled preference corpus built from AxBench's Concept16K and Concept16K-v2 generation sets: tuples of instruction, steering-concept description, a positive response and a concept-free negative response, spanning roughly 21,000 concepts for the v1 pool and 15,700 for the v2 pool after filtering. Evaluation is over held-out concepts on three text-generation benchmarks plus three interactive/agentic settings.

## How they did it

They first restate the DPO-based steering baseline: the log-probability gap between a preferred and a dispreferred completion is exactly an inner product of the final hidden state with the difference of the two output embeddings, so under a local linearisation the DPO update looks like adding a fixed vector. They then replace the added vector with LoRA factors on the four attention projections, and derive what that does to attention. Expanding the adapted query-key dot product leaves the original logit plus three cross terms, which collapse exactly into a single bilinear form in the query token's and key token's hidden states mediated by one matrix built from the frozen weights and the trained factors; the derived gradients with respect to either token are generically non-zero. The authors extend the derivation to multi-head and grouped-query attention under RoPE, where the matrix becomes an explicit function of the relative offset between positions and one shared key-side adapter perturbs every query head in its group. A second nonlinearity follows because the softmax weights and the output projection depend on the current representations, so the induced update's second derivative in the input is not identically zero. Training is standard DPO with the base model frozen as reference: only the adapter factors are learned, intensity scalars are fixed a priori, and hyperparameters are shared across all six backbones with no benchmark-specific tuning. Splits are concept-disjoint with instructions deliberately shared, and only the final checkpoint exists, so checkpoint selection on any benchmark is precluded. Evaluation feeds the instruction and the steering-concept description through one fixed prompt template, and at inference prepends a generic helpful-assistant system prompt.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not an explanation of the model — a controllable intervention plus a partial geometric characterisation of what it does. The formal object is a low-rank parameter delta whose attention-level effect is shown analytically to be a rank-two-ish bilinear interaction between token pairs, i.e. a structured, input-dependent perturbation rather than a fixed offset. The geometric analysis adds two diagnostics: an alignment score, the cosine between the mean steering displacement induced by the method and a contrastive-activation-addition direction for the same concept, reported per concept across six models; and a trajectory-similarity analysis in which sentence-level hidden states are pooled into segments and compared on position, velocity, acceleration and Menger curvature under instruction, concept and genre groupings against a shuffled-order control. The authors are explicit that the alignment cosine is not a rank measure and does not estimate the intrinsic dimensionality of the trajectory.

## How they judged success

Three text-generation benchmarks: AxBench (concept score, instruction score, fluency score and their harmonic mean, with the strongest reported method — prompt steering — as the reference row and a direct head-to-head against the closest learned-operator comparator), CLaS-Bench (language forcing, relevance, harmonic mean; epsilon-baseline-I as reference), and PersonalityBench (personality and fluency scores, arithmetic mean; P2 as reference). All methods were re-implemented and scored inside each benchmark's own framework so the rows are comparable. Agentic transfer used the Dictator and Ultimatum Games steered toward five emotions, scored as a Simulation Score over 15 emotion-role conditions against the human-reported direction of effect (two points for agreement, one for ambiguous, zero for contradiction, maximum 30, 200 runs per condition at temperature 0.7), and SocialEval with three social traits, scored by macro- and micro-averaged goal-achievement against the SocialEval-En configuration. Capability retention used MMLU and TruthfulQA MC1/MC2 at 0, 2 and 4 shots under multiple-choice log-likelihood scoring, comparing base against adapted. Judge reliability was assessed with three independent LLM judges (Krippendorff's alpha, ICC variants, weighted Cohen's kappa, Spearman rank agreement) plus a 50-item human cross-check, and separately across six model-based raters on the concept-level evaluation.

## What they found

On text generation the method's aggregate scores exceed the best reported task-specific reference on AxBench in all six models and on CLaS-Bench in all six; the two components are not uniformly better, with instruction score often below the reference while concept score rises (for example AxBench concept 1.65 against 0.63 on Llama-3.2-3B, but instruction 1.75 against 1.78). PersonalityBench is essentially a tie, within a few hundredths of the reference on every model, and in two models the method is fractionally below. The head-to-head against the closest learned-operator comparator on one backbone is a 2% utility gain and 0.6 points of steering effectiveness. Agentic transfer is the more mixed result: the Simulation Score beats the reference in four of six models (15 against 11 and 20 against 14 on the two Llama models, 22 against 16 on Gemma-2-2B, 17 against 16 on Qwen3-4B) and loses on two (17 against 16 reversed on Gemma-2-9B, 16 against 18 on Qwen3-1.7B), and SocialEval macro and micro goal-achievement improve in five of six settings with Qwen3-1.7B the exception. Capability retention is genuinely close to null on the six main backbones: MMLU moves by at most about 0.3 points and TruthfulQA by at most 0.37 points on the main models, with Gemma-2-2B gaining roughly 2.5-2.9 points on both TruthfulQA metrics rather than losing; the largest TruthfulQA drop anywhere is on a smaller sibling model not in the main comparison. The geometric results are the odd ones: position similarity is high everywhere and barely moves under shuffling, so it weakly discriminates ordered structure, while velocity, acceleration and curvature similarities rise steeply from near zero under shuffling to roughly 0.27-0.39 under instruction grouping — evidence that the intervention changes where responses sit while partially preserving the local dynamics of the trajectory. The per-concept alignment score is systematically high for broad global concepts (language switching, output format) and systematically low for concepts requiring layered local-plus-global change (discourse development, argument framing), which the authors read as the linear baseline capturing the first class and undercapturing the second. Judge reliability was strong in aggregate (alpha about 0.81 pooled for three judges) but collapsed by method: DPO outputs scored near 0.33, which the authors attribute to the concept being expressed more subtly and hence harder to binary-score. Inter-rater agreement across six model raters was moderate for a single rater (about 0.40) and rose to about 0.80 when averaged — meaning any single-model reading of the concept-level results is close to noise. A safety side-check found that transferring a harmful intent into the concept slot weakens refusal in some contexts.

## Limits and open problems they name

The alignment diagnostic is not a dimensionality measure and cannot rule out some other fixed linear direction, so "the effect is nonlinear" is supported by the construction and the trajectory statistics rather than by an identifiability result. Code and checkpoints are promised for a future version, not released. Models from the same family are not independent samples and the authors say so. The shuffled control destroys response coherence, making it a negative rather than a realistic condition. Menger curvature is scalar and correlation-based, hence touchier on short trajectories than the vector cosines. The training data has an admitted asymmetry: for every instruction the concept-free negative response is drawn once and reused across all concepts sharing that instruction, so the contrastive signal is effectively carried by the positive alone, and the internal held-out split shares instructions between train and test — only the external benchmarks test unseen instructions. The safety finding is framed as a caution rather than a measured risk profile.

## What we take from it

**Method, and the one thing we should copy immediately — the derived interaction form.** Because the perturbation to an attention logit is a bilinear form in two token states through a matrix that depends only on fixed weights and frozen adapters, this folder finally has a case where a learned intervention's *analytic* form is known: its effect on the attention pattern is a context-conditioned kernel, not a vector. For our white-box model that is a template — when we predict what an intervention will do downstream, an attention-projection edit is one of the few objects whose first-order effect we can write down in closed form without simulating, which makes it the natural test bed for a predictive model. **Measure — paired nulls at the same operating point.** Capability retention is measured as base versus adapted on the same harness at three few-shot settings, and the honesty of it is that two settings move *up*. The lesson for us is the comparison, not the number: a retention claim is only interpretable against an identically-scored unsteered row, and a near-zero delta with the sign varying by model is the strongest negative result available. **Warning — single-rater reliability.** The single-rater/inter-rater split (about 0.40 alone, about 0.80 averaged over six) is the sharpest caution in the paper for us: a concept-level geometric score produced by one model, or read by one judge, carries agreement so low that the ranking is barely above chance, and this folder has several entries whose per-concept conclusions rest on exactly one evaluation channel. **Warning — the split protocol.** Concept-disjoint but instruction-shared splits measure transfer to unseen concepts on *seen* instructions; the released statistics make that explicit, and any of our own probes trained with a shared instruction pool inherit the same ambiguity. **Framing — steering as trajectory displacement, with position and dynamics scored separately.** The position-versus-velocity/acceleration/curvature decomposition with a shuffled-order negative control is a cheap, reusable instrument: it distinguishes "the intervention moved the response somewhere else" from "the intervention changed how the response was built", and in this paper the two come apart in the direction that matters (position weakly diagnostic, higher-order structure strongly diagnostic).
