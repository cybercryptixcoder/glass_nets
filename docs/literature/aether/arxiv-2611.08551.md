## What they wanted, and why

Training a sparse autoencoder requires storing a large cache of model activations, and a single token's residual vector costs kilobytes against the token's two bytes of text, so the storage bill is what actually bounds how many sites, layers and seeds a research group can afford. The obvious economy is to store the cache in a compressed format — half precision, 8 or 4 bits — but nobody had measured what compression does to the *features* an SAE learns from that cache, as opposed to what it does to reconstruction error. The paper's sharper motive is methodological: while trying to answer that question the author finds that the natural metric (how many features a retrained SAE recovers) is wildly non-monotone in the size of the perturbation, and therefore that a codec's cost cannot be read as a level at all unless it is placed against a control that says how much turnover is unavoidable. The larger goal is a usable calibration protocol for any treatment of an SAE's training data, not just compression.

## What they studied

Two models at one residual-stream read-off per site: GPT-2 small at layer 6 (20M OpenWebText tokens, TopK SAEs, largest of 32 latents firing per token), and Pythia-1.4B at layers 8 and 16 (10M Pile tokens, computed in a bf16 forward, same TopK, so its 33M token-visit budget corresponds to 24.6M visits). Sixteen codecs of increasing oddity: fp16 and bf16 storage, per-token symmetric int8/int6/int4 with one absolute-max scale per token, versions that keep the largest channels in fp16, versions that standardise each channel before quantising, versions that also apply a fixed random rotation, plus two dimension-reducing baselines (projection onto 256 principal components, and a 256-dimensional random projection). One non-codec treatment is included as a contrast: computing the cache with a bf16 forward pass and storing it in fp32. The unit of analysis is the **feature**, compared across two independently trained SAEs by best-match Pearson correlation over held-out tokens on which the feature is alive. A JumpReLU panel and a sweep over sparsity, threshold, stability definition and training budget check which conclusions travel.

## How they did it

Step one is to make the comparison clean of everything but the treatment. One fp32 master cache is built per site from a single forward pass; every codec store is a shard-by-shard transcode of that master, and the loader's shuffle is derived from the seed alone, so two runs at the same seed see identical tokens in identical order and differ only in bytes. The **ceiling** is a retrain on the original bytes at the same seed, which the stack reproduces bit for bit; the **floor** is a retrain at a different seed, which changes no bytes at all yet costs most of the features, so it is the noise that a codec's damage has to beat. Every codec is then reported as *survived* — the share of the reference SAE's alive features whose best match in the retrained SAE clears a correlation threshold — and as a **ratio** to its own site's floor. Three perturbations that are not codecs are run to map the response surface: isotropic Gaussian noise at exact relative magnitudes spanning five decades, dense noise at fp16's own distortion level, and changes to a fixed small number of scalars (a millionth of the cache's 15 billion values, down to a single scalar) by one unit in the last place, all stored in fp32 so nothing is quantised. The retraining test is then checked by three other instruments: the **frozen-SAE test** (the original fp32 SAE applied to the compressed cache, correlated against its own fp32 activations), plain distances between caches that need no SAE at all, and downstream uses of the same cache — linear probes on SST-2 and AG News with direction cosines, and a difference-in-means steering vector injected into the residual stream and scored against the variation between two disjoint halves of the same data. Two appendices test whether matched features do the same thing (removing a feature's own contribution from the residual stream on its strongest rows and comparing the log-probability changes of matched pairs) and predict which features survive from fp32 statistics alone. Experimental outcomes, thresholds and reading rules were written down in advance and released with the artifacts; the appendix tables are generated programmatically from the run artifacts and a lint script checks main-text numbers against them.

## What counts as an explanation here

Not a mechanism and not a description of a network — this is a **measurement-validity paper**, and what it produces as understanding is a *calibrated instrument*: a count, reported as a ratio, whose numerator (features retained under a treatment) is read against a floor (features lost to a seed change) and a ceiling (features retained on identical bytes) defined in the same experiment, on the same data, with the same pipeline. The secondary product is a *null model* for the frozen-SAE reading: because a TopK SAE is piecewise affine, a feature's frozen activation disagrees with its clean one either by shifting inside its linear region or by jumping when a token crosses into another region, and the paper builds a Gaussian prediction of that disagreement from the codec's error second moment plus the feature's margin over the runner-up preactivation. That model is claimed as an explanation of what the cheap test measures, and it fails exactly where the cheap test is uninformative (codecs that remove signal rather than add noise). The explanation is explicitly partial: the paper states that no count of retraining agreement, its own included, can settle whether sparse dictionary learning finds the features the model itself uses.

## How they judged success

The primary metric is **best-match correlation at threshold** over alive features (alive = fires on more than the paper's firing-rate cut, which keeps roughly 15% of the dictionary at each site) — the share of the reference SAE's features with a partner above the correlation cut in the retrained SAE. Success is not a high score but a **calibrated verdict**: a codec is viable if its ratio to its own site's reseeding floor exceeds one, with single-seed differences under a 3.5–4 pp tie band treated as seed noise. Baselines: the identical-bytes ceiling, the reseed floor, the empty-set and misalignment controls (a cache against itself must return exactly 1.000, and the same cache misaligned by one token must collapse), and a shuffle control on best-match luck. Secondary instruments with their own floors: held-out FVU, the frozen-SAE continuous and thresholded readings, rank agreement (Spearman) between cheap readings and retrained agreement across conditions, Hungarian weight-space matching on encoder/decoder cosine as an independent definition of stability, linear-probe accuracy change against a seven-split resample floor, causal-steering effect against a half-versus-half floor, and a per-feature survival predictor scored by cross-validated AUC. Every headline claim is checked across three seeds, across budget tiers, across sites, and against its own pre-registered pass marks.

## What they found

**The retraining test is not a level, it is a clock.** On the GPT-2 layer-6 site the floor is 23.0% survived; fp16 keeps 60.6%; but one ulp on a millionth of the values — 1.59e-12 relative L2, eight orders of magnitude below fp16's distortion of 2.03e-04 — keeps only 70.5%, and one ulp on a single scalar anywhere in the corpus keeps 68.6%. Trimming the exact count barely moves the number (153 scalars → 69.3%, 77 → 69.9%), while a single scalar moves it to 99.98%, so the gap between 70.5% and 68.6% cannot be attributed to count alone: replaying the one-scalar experiment step for step shows the loss most likely comes from the change's timing (its altered value re-enters training at a later step), not its size. Across four noise levels at or below fp16's distortion, agreement stays inside a band of 60.6–66.0%, and only the nine orders above (1e-2 relative) take it to 51.2%; the frozen SAE reads at least 0.9994 throughout all of it. The paper's own framing of the consequence: the count responds to almost any change in the bytes and, up to fp16's distortion, only weakly to their size.

**The floor is what makes any of it readable, and it beats every naive alternative.** Naive per-token int4 is below its own site's floor everywhere: 15.6% survived against a 23.0% floor on GPT-2 (ratio 0.69), 2.8% against 15.7% at Pythia layer 8 (0.18), 0.8% against 18.4% at layer 16 (0.05). Standardising each channel before quantising repairs it at identical raw size (388 B/token): 38.0% survived, ratio 1.65 on GPT-2 and roughly 1.6 at both Pythia sites, while the rotation on top adds nothing detectable (rot-int4 37.7% against std-int4 38.0%, a tie). PCA to 256 dimensions keeps 7.8% (0.34 of floor) and a random projection 2.8% (0.12) — the outlier-channel problem at 4 bits is the whole story, and per-channel scaling is the fix.

**Reconstruction error cannot certify a cache, and neither can the frozen SAE as a level.** FVU sits flat at 0.1355 for fp16 and 0.1361 for int4-o16 while agreement falls from 60.6% to 37.6% across the same span; naive int4 alone moves FVU (0.1968). The frozen-SAE test ranks the codecs correctly within a site (Spearman 0.98 on GPT-2 over 15 conditions, 1.000 at both Pythia sites) but the same frozen reading of 0.9992 corresponds to 53.8% retained on GPT-2, 43.0% at Pythia layer 8 and 38.4% at layer 16; and the cheap orderings are matched by distances that need no SAE at all (relative L2 0.982, mean cosine 0.982). The one place the frozen SAE wins is across codec families, where the continuous reading orders PCA-256 above naive int4 correctly while the thresholded one inverts it.

**A compressed cache is a storage win, not a speed win.** At B/token the ladder is fp32 3072, fp16 1536, rot-int8 772, rot-int6 580, rot-int4 388 zstd-3 to 303.8 for std-int4 against fp16's 1419 — 4.7 times the text in fp16's bytes — and at the 98.3M-visit budget fp16 on 20M tokens and std-int4 on 79.0M tokens reach the same cross-seed agreement (32.6%, 0.974 of the fresh-text reference, a 0.02 pp difference with std-int4's margin entering on the third seed), so the extra text buys nothing measurable at that budget. Training throughput on an A100 is 1.09 times fp32 from local disk and 1.04 from a network volume against a pre-registered 1.15 threshold for a material gain; the loader matters more than the format (std-int4 on the CPU decoder is 0.79 times fp32's fastest path, and moving decode to the GPU puts every format between 119 and 129 thousand tokens/s).

**Three smaller results.** Budget scaling rises the floor rather than the codecs: from 33M to 328M token-visits the floor climbs from 23.0% to 59.4%, fp16 stays flat at 95.7% on a fixed stable set, the rotated codecs improve, and naive int4's agreement stays flat so its ratio falls from 0.69 to 0.10 — more training does not recover what the codec lost. Seed stability is predictable from one fp32 SAE (cross-validated AUC 0.868 on all eight measures, 0.846 from the three activation statistics alone), and the model fitted to seed stability also predicts which features survive each codec at AUC 0.808–0.902; neither dictionary-learning predictor held (co-firing with the best partner and decoder coherence both run slightly the wrong way), while the strongest single predictor is the feature's TopK margin over the runner-up. And the cost of a perturbation is set by *when* it reaches training: an identical change applied at step 0 costs 64.7% agreement and at step 4,000 costs 99.9%, with the step-0 arm ending a relative decoder distance of 0.6561 from the reference and the late arm near zero.

## Limits and open problems they name

Two models, residual-stream sites only, unnormalised activations, research-scale budgets (33M token-visits, fresh text tested at one budget only); graded claims are TopK-only, since on JumpReLU the viable codecs land within a few points of the floor and are too close together for a verdict (that run also ended with a third of latents dead and a truncated mean latent count, and is excluded); Pythia's own reference is a bf16 forward and so is already perturbed; the panel omits fp8 and block-scaled formats; and the timing experiment is one seed, with the step range between the tested points untested. The paper is explicit that a viable codec causes less of what a reseed causes, not that the surviving features are the model's own. It also declines to attribute the gap between its floor and the published seed-instability numbers, since width, matching criterion and training length all differ and its own sweeps move sharing in opposite directions.

## What we take from it

A **measurement discipline**, and the sharpest statement of it in the folder: any number of the form "X% of the components survived" needs a floor and a ceiling measured in the same pipeline, because this one is nearly flat in perturbation size across six orders of magnitude and 60.6% versus 70.5% can be the difference between trusting a cache and distrusting it. The reusable artifact is the floor itself — a seed change, or any resampling of a nuisance the claim says is irrelevant, costs a measurable amount and becomes the unit every effect is quoted in — which is the same move as the folder's equal-mass random removal and its label-shuffle null, arriving on the *data* side rather than the intervention side. Second, a **positive test that a metric is uninformative before using it**: reconstruction error, which is the SAE field's habitual reconstruction-plus-sparsity scorecard, is flat across codecs whose feature agreement spans 60.6% to 37.6%, so any claim that a dictionary is "as good" because FVU is unchanged is unsupported by exactly this much. Third, a warning for our own battery: if a downstream instrument (probe, steering vector) is used to certify a representation, it needs its own floor, and here no single instrument is uniformly the strictest — PCA-256 passes the causal steering test on average while failing the retraining test badly, and int4-o16 fails one probe floor that the retraining test passes, so agents of comparison disagree and the disagreement is the information. Fourth, a design habit that transfers directly to our probe work: the paper's a-priori pass marks and its programmatically generated tables are how a measurement study survives contact with its own results, and its pre-registration is visibly amended mid-stream when a planned arm turned out to be unreachable (the one-ulp-on-first-batch copy stayed bit-identical) — recorded as an amendment rather than quietly dropped.
