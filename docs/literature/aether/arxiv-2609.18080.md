# Decodability is Not Causality: Dissociating Probe Readouts from Behavioral Drivers via SAE Decomposition

- **Key:** arxiv-2609.18080
- **Authors / organisation:** Devesh Tiwari, Camille Davis, Shivank Sinha, Talia Weaver, Aditya Shah (Google), Maheep Chaudhary (independent, senior author); five of six list high-school affiliations
- **Date:** 2026-09-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18080
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Linear probes decode concepts from activations, and probe accuracy is routinely read as evidence that the concept is *used* by the model. The authors take that inference apart. Their claim is narrower than the usual "correlation is not causation" warning: even the *geometry* of a probe — which weight directions it points along — is not enough to say which features carry that probe's behavioural effect, so the only way to answer the question is to intervene. They also want to know whether a sparse-autoencoder decomposition can *localise* a probe direction's causal effect into a small explainable subset of features, which turns an SAE from an illustration into a locating instrument. The larger goal is a testable criterion for when a probe readout is a diagnostic window and when it is a behavioural driver.

## What they studied
Gemma-2-9B-Instruct in bfloat16, residual stream at layer 20 (chosen because a matching GemmaScope SAE with 16,384 features exists there; the probe's accuracy peaks at layer 21). Probe: the Truth-Truthful-Polarity-Direction of Bürger et al., replicated from released code — a hyperparameter-free direction obtained by regressing centred activations on truth label and affirmative/negated polarity. Task: the instructed-deception factual-verification setting of Long et al., twelve statement sets (six base categories and their negations), 1,600 statements, deceptive instruction condition, behavioural signal = the logit margin between True and False at the final prompt token. Unit of analysis: individual SAE features, and sets of them.

## How they did it
Five stages. (1) Replicate the probe and check it against reported behaviour. (2) Rank all SAE features by *probe alignment*, the inner product of the probe's weight vector with each SAE decoder column — a purely activation-free, geometric score. (3) Independently rank the same features by *gradient sensitivity*, the dataset-averaged magnitude of the derivative of the True/False margin with respect to each feature's activation. (4) Ablate each chosen feature set by subtracting its reconstructed contribution from the residual stream and letting the forward pass continue, then measure three things at once: behaviour flip rate (sign of the margin changes), probe-readout shift (relative change in the probe margin), and coherence (fraction of outputs still a valid True/False token). The sets are: shared (top-k in both rankings), probe-only (highest probe-ranked minus shared, size-matched), random (size-matched draw), gradient-only, a *learned sparse mask* trained to reconstruct the probe margin from alignment-times-activation with an L1 penalty, and the full probe direction projected out as a ceiling. (5) Decompose and compare, including a held-out split where rankings come from one half of the data and ablation is measured on the other, and five seeds/resamples.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **causal attribution to a set of SAE features**, plus a decomposition of a probe's readout into a behaviourally load-bearing part and a merely diagnostic part. It is explicitly partial: the authors say their ablations establish which features move this True/False decision in this setting, not that those features implement deception as a mechanism. The interesting object is the *dissociation* — the same feature set scores differently on behaviour and on the probe — and the explanation takes the form "the probe reads N features, of which only a minority are what the model uses". Nothing here predicts an intervention it was not run on; no surrogate model, no circuit.

## How they judged success
Flip rate on the True/False output under ablation, against size-matched random controls and against the full-direction ceiling; probe-readout shift as a second, dissociating outcome; set overlap and Spearman rank correlation between the two rankings against an analytic chance-overlap baseline; stability over five seeds and a half-data held-out split; and four coherence gates — valid-token rate, SAE reconstruction fidelity at the layer, the fraction of residual-stream norm removed (ablations were small, roughly three percent for the shared set), and mean KL between baseline and post-ablation next-token distributions (reported as small for the shared set and near zero for random controls). Probes are chosen to be hyperparameter-free specifically so that "the features the probe relies on" is well defined; an included logistic-regression probe was excluded from the main analysis because its top features shift drastically with the regularisation strength.

## What they found
The two rankings barely overlap: the top 16, 50, 100 and 200 probe-aligned features share 2, 7, 12 and 22 features with the gradient ranking — one to two orders of magnitude above chance (chance 0.02, 0.15, 0.61, 2.44) but only about 11-14 percent at every scale, with Spearman ρ around 0.10. Ablation separates them in the opposite direction from what the reader expects: shared features flip behaviour on roughly 13 to 27 percent of statements (0.130 at 16 features, peaking 0.269 at 54), probe-only features on about 6 percent, random on about 1 percent, with coherence at 1.00 throughout. Probe-only ablation instead moves the probe's own readout most (relative shift 0.24 against 0.05 for shared) — the cleanest statement of the dissociation. Ablating the full probe direction flips about 39 percent; the 16-feature shared set recovers roughly two-thirds of that, the geometric top-16 about a third, random near zero. An activation-aware learned mask overlaps the geometric top-16 in only 3 of 16 features yet flips 17.6 percent of statements, close to three times the geometric top-16's 6.1 percent. Gradient-only features are weak (around 6 percent), which the authors attribute to gradients being a local first-order signal that can pick features with the wrong sign, that rarely fire, or that lie off the visited manifold. Directionally, flips are overwhelmingly True→False (86 against 4 at the matched 16-feature size), so ablation removes the probe's truth-direction evidence rather than restoring factual accuracy — baseline accuracy under the deceptive prompt does not rise.

## Limits and open problems they name
One model, one layer, one SAE, one behavioural setting; generality across families, layers, SAE architectures and domains is untested. Ablations identify features that move this decision, not a general mechanism for deception. They also report a *boundary case* rather than a positive result in their auxiliary diagnostic: on a Qwen3-8B reward-hacking code probe, only about 26 percent of the raw probe margin survived the SAE reconstruction and 74 percent lived in the reconstruction error, so SAE-feature ablations changed predictions in reconstruction space while leaving raw-activation predictions nearly untouched — hence their precondition that the probe signal must be verified as represented in the SAE basis before any SAE intervention is read causally. Unstated but visible: the headline comparison is between 16-feature sets that were chosen by different procedures, so "alignment alone is insufficient" is demonstrated against a specific activation-free baseline rather than against all activation-free baselines.

## What we take from it
- **Method, and the one we should copy first:** the *dual readout* — every intervention is scored on the behaviour **and** on the measurement's own instrument (here, the probe's margin). A feature set that moves the instrument and not the behaviour is a readout artefact, and this single pairing makes that visible in one table. For our white-box model, any probe or statistic we propose as a predictor gets ablated and scored on both the model output and the statistic itself.
- **Gating discipline:** four cheap preconditions before any causal claim (valid output, reconstruction fidelity, intervention size as a fraction of activation norm, KL to the baseline distribution) — the norm-fraction and KL numbers are what let them say "small targeted perturbation, not gross corruption".
- **Control shape:** size-matched random sets, seed/resample stability, a held-out split separating ranking from measurement, and a ceiling intervention (the full direction) against which partial recovery is quoted as a fraction.
- **Warning:** gradient sensitivity alone — the cheapest saliency route to "which features matter" — did no better than the activation-free geometric route here (about 6 percent either way), and the intersection of the two rankings did far better than either. A predictor built on one attribution signal is not justified by this literature.
- **Reframing:** "the probe direction is causal" and "the features most aligned with it are the causal ones" are different claims; only the second needs an intervention, and this paper measures a wide gap between them.
