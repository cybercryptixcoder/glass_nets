# Mechanistic Interpretability as Statistical Estimation: A Variance Analysis

- **Key:** arxiv-2510.00845
- **Authors / organisation:** Maxime Méloux, François Portet, Maxime Peyrard; Université Grenoble Alpes, CNRS, Grenoble INP, LIG (Grenoble)
- **Date:** 2026-05-28
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00845
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v4; main body §1–§6, references, and the whole appendix — Additional plots, Tables 3–10, Figures 5–8)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Circuit discovery is normally reported as a single sparse subgraph plus a faithfulness score, and the subgraph is treated as a fact about the network. The authors argue that this is a category error: the pipeline estimates a population quantity from a finite sample through three lossy steps (per-input causal scores, aggregation over a dataset, a discrete selection heuristic), so the discovered circuit is a *statistical estimate* and ought to be reported with the uncertainty of one. The paper's target is the field's reporting habit rather than any single method — they claim that a circuit without a variance estimate cannot be distinguished from a fit to sampling noise, and that the usual proxy metrics (faithfulness, sufficiency, sparsity) measure internal validity while saying nothing about stability. The larger goal is to move mechanistic interpretability from point estimates to something closer to statistical science, where a finding is a distribution over plausible structures and the uncertainty is part of the result. This is directly our question in reverse: a white-box model that predicts what the network will do has to know how much of its own reading is estimator noise.

## What they studied

Three models: gpt2-small, Llama-3.2-1B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (the last to test whether fine-tuning destabilises mechanisms). Three standard tasks with clean/corrupted pair constructions: indirect object identification, subject-verb agreement, and greater-than. The unit of analysis is the **edge** in the computational graph, and the composite object is the circuit — an edge set produced by an estimator (exact causal mediation, first-order attribution patching, integrated-gradients variants, and a two-point clean/corrupted average), an aggregation rule (mean or median), an intervention convention (corrupt-patching versus mean-patching, and Gaussian-noise corruption of varying amplitude), and a selection heuristic. The attribute under study is not the circuit but its variability.

## How they did it

They fix the causal-mediation formulation (the natural indirect effect: patch a mediator to its counterfactual source value and measure the change in the target output) and then treat circuit discovery as estimating its population mean over the joint distribution of clean input and counterfactual source. Because that mean is what every method implicitly targets, any fluctuation in how inputs or counterfactuals are sampled becomes estimator variance by construction. They measure it four ways. (i) **Bootstrap:** resample the dataset with replacement 100 times, re-run the entire discovery pipeline each time, and collect 100 circuits. (ii) **Distribution shift:** redraw from the same meta-distribution, or paraphrase the prompts, and re-run. (iii) **Intervention definition:** replace the fixed corruption with token-embedding Gaussian noise and sweep amplitude across nine values, so the counterfactual itself changes and the "question" being answered changes with it. (iv) **Methodological perturbation:** vary aggregation, patching convention and base estimator on fixed data, and separately vary all parameters at once (125 circuits for the headline heatmap, 330 for the selection-frequency analysis). To separate the intrinsic variability of the estimand from approximation error, they compute exact per-edge causal scores for gpt2-small on two tasks and compare their spread against the patched approximations on identical inputs. Stability is scored structurally as the mean and coefficient of variation of the pairwise Jaccard index over discovered edge sets, and functionally as the mean, variance and coefficient of variation of circuit error and KL divergence against the full model.

## What counts as an explanation here

No circuit is offered as an explanation — the paper is methodology, and its product is a **statement about what form an explanation can take**. The claim is that the right output of a discovery method is a distribution over graphs rather than one graph: a point estimate plus its sampling distribution, its sensitivity to the counterfactual definition, and its sensitivity to hyperparameters. That is explicitly weaker than a mechanism explanation and explicitly more honest: several distinct structures satisfying the same faithfulness and sparsity criteria are expected rather than pathological, and the authors are careful to distinguish non-identifiability (a theoretical impossibility, from their own earlier work) from estimator instability (the empirical symptom they measure), noting that instability is consistent with non-identifiability but does not prove it, since finite-sample and approximation noise might in principle be reduced.

## How they judged success

Structural stability is the mean pairwise Jaccard index across bootstrap circuits, and functional stability is circuit error — the fraction of held-out inputs on which the ablated circuit changes the model's prediction — with KL divergence as a companion (they report the two are highly correlated, so circuit error carries the main text and KL the appendix). Both are reported as mean, variance and coefficient of variation, because a mean over a heavy-tailed spread of circuits is misleading on its own. The paper's own proposed acceptance bar is a mean pairwise Jaccard above 0.8 under bootstrap with at least 100 resamples, offered explicitly as tentative pending community discussion. Two aggregate views are used for interpretation: the Jaccard matrix projected by MDS to show that no method clusters more tightly than the others, and edge-selection frequencies across hundreds of circuits to expose which edges are stable and which are noise.

## What they found

The instability is present at every stage and it is large. **At the base layer**, the exact causal score of an edge varies across inputs with a coefficient of variation around 0.5, so an edge's importance is a volatile random variable rather than a fixed property; the patched approximation not only shifts the distribution but raises the coefficient of variation above 1, i.e. the standard deviation exceeds the mean and the per-edge signal-to-noise ratio is below 1. **At the structural layer**, circuits found from bootstrap resamples overlap poorly: mean pairwise Jaccard 0.561 with coefficient of variation 0.335, and those least-overlapping circuits are also the least faithful (mean circuit error 0.440, the worst of the three resampling regimes). Shifting the input distribution is markedly more stable than resampling it — meta-dataset 0.790 and paraphrase 0.799 with errors 0.300 and 0.150 — which is the paper's most counterintuitive ordering: a semantically different prompt set produces a more reproducible circuit than a statistically resampled one drawn from the identical generator, because the mechanism is more robust to what the inputs *say* than to which inputs were drawn. **At the scale layer**, stability degrades from gpt2-small to the 1B Llama models, and instruction tuning changes little, so the ceiling is not an artefact of a toy model. **At the methodological layer**, the variants do not converge on a shared circuit but pick out different artefacts of the noisy score distribution: on Llama-Instruct greater-than, switching aggregation from sum to median and patching from mean to patching drops overlap with the median circuit to 0.086 — effectively a disjoint subgraph — and on indirect-object identification the integrated-gradients and clean/corrupted variants overlap at 0.071; on gpt2-small subject-verb agreement one variant returns a single edge while another returns 21 edges at the same circuit error of 0.06. **At the intervention layer**, the circuit is not invariant to perturbation strength: sweeping noise amplitude from 0.01 to 5 in gpt2-small peaks the coefficient of variation of overlap near an amplitude of about 0.2, so there is a band where the identified mechanism is at its least reproducible. **The most useful single number is in the appendix**: across 330 circuits spanning 32,491 possible gpt2-small edges, only 464 edges appear at least once, roughly half of those (231) appear in at least 10% of circuits, 94 in at least 30%, and just 7 in over 80% — a small stable core, a large fluctuating periphery.

## Limits and open problems they name

The analysis covers the attribution-patching family specifically; they argue the mechanism (per-input estimation, finite-sample aggregation, discrete selection) generalises to any sparse-circuit method, but only argue it, and name a method that bypasses gradient attribution as a possible exception whose noise profile is unmeasured. Exact-score variance was affordable only on gpt2-small for two tasks, so the intrinsic-variance layer rests on approximation evidence for the other models. All three tasks are comparatively discrete linguistic rules, and instability may look different on fuzzy or generative behaviour. Their stability metrics weight all edges equally, so a stable functional core under a fluctuating periphery would be invisible to them — a gap they name and do not fill, even though their own selection-frequency table is the evidence that such a core exists. The proposed 0.8 Jaccard threshold is a suggestion, not a calibrated bound.

## What we take from it

**A framing, a mandatory metric, and a warning that constrains our whole project.** (1) *Framing:* a circuit, a feature set, a probe, or any of our predictive read-outs is an estimator, and its report is incomplete without a distribution — this is the measurement-side counterpart of the causal-ladder argument in `arxiv-2602.16698` and of the equivalence-class argument in `arxiv-2602.06801`. (2) *Mandatory metric for our eval set:* resample the evaluation or calibration set with replacement, rerun the whole pipeline — not just the fit — and publish mean pairwise Jaccard of the discovered structure plus the coefficient of variation of the behavioural score. The two axes must be reported together because here they decouple: the paraphrase regime has the *best* faithfulness and the second-best stability, so a table showing only faithfulness would rank the regimes wrongly. (3) *The specific habit worth copying:* report the **edge-selection frequency distribution**, not just the top-ranked edges. It is the cheapest available answer to "which parts of this explanation are real", it needs no ground truth, and it splits a published circuit into a stable core and a noise periphery — the seven edges present in over 80% of circuits are the ones worth building a prediction on. (4) *The warning for a predictive white-box model:* the paper's central claim is that a single-input causal effect is a random variable, so a surrogate trained to predict effects from one input in one counterfactual setting is fitting the noise term as much as the signal. Any predictor of ours should be scored on its ability to predict the *aggregate* over held-out resamples, and the target it predicts should be stated with its own variance — a prediction whose error bar exceeds the effect it is predicting is not a prediction. (5) *And the ordering result is a design hint:* mechanisms are more robust to changes in what the inputs mean than to which inputs were sampled, which argues for evaluating our read-outs on distribution-shifted held-out sets first and on bootstrap resamples second, since the former is the easier test and the latter is the one that will actually fail.
