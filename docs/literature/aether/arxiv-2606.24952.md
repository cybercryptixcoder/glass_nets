# Perfect Detection, Failed Control: The Geometry of Knowing vs. Steering in Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2606.24952
- **Authors / organisation:** Cosimo Galeone, Anna Ettorre, Minsu Park, Giuseppe Ettorre, Daniele Ligorio; Alomana (Grottaglie, Italy)
- **Date:** 2026-06-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24952
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Almost every steering result in interpretability rests on an unstated premise: that the linear direction which *separates* a behaviour is the same direction that *causes* it. Arditi et al.'s refusal direction was read that way, and so were the honesty directions of Li et al. and the activation-addition paradigm of Turner et al. If that premise holds, a probe is not merely a description — it is a control knob, and knowing implies steering. The authors want to test the premise geometrically rather than assume it, by measuring the angle between the best detection direction and the best intervention direction for a behaviour. Their motivation is not to debunk steering but to find out when the collapse of detection and control actually happens and what the distance between them means structurally. The larger goal is a principled account of the detection–intervention gap: when a model knows something, what separates knowing from acting on it.

## What they studied

Gemma 2-2B-it as the main subject (26 layers, 2304-dimensional residual stream), with replication on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B-it, plus the Gemma-2-2B *base* model as an instruction-tuning control. Two behaviours in contrast: **output format** (whether a JSON answer is wrapped in markdown code fences) as the positive case, and **hallucination** (fabricating answers about entities that do not exist, versus refusing) as the case of interest. The unit of analysis is a **direction** in the residual stream, built two independent ways: data-driven difference-in-means over activations, or hand-picked as a contrast of `lm_head` token rows. All work is inference-time with forward hooks; no weights are updated.

## How they did it

They build a detection direction for the fake-versus-real entity distinction in two ways — a difference-in-means of last-prompt-token residual activations, and a hand-picked contrast between `lm_head` rows of uncertain openers ("I", "Unfortunately", "There", "It", "This") and confident or fabricating openers ("The", "In", "Paris", "Tokyo", "1"). The intervention direction is hand-picked from refusal openers ("No", "cannot", "doesn't", "I") against compliant ones ("The", "Yes", "is", "It"). They then compute the cosine between detection and intervention directions, which is the paper's central quantity, and cross it against chance in 2304 dimensions. Causal relevance is tested by adding alpha times the direction to the residual stream at target layers during generation and measuring the behavioural swing, with two control families: 100 random directions at matched norm and 10 semantically irrelevant token-pair directions. For the format case they use a logit-lens readout to find where the { vs ``` decision is made, then steer that same direction and run an activation-transplant test in which a clean-JSON forward pass's residual stream is injected into a fencing pass. For the hallucination case they first confirm detection six independent ways (supervised probes, one attention head, logit-lens top-5 entropy, one MLP neuron, one Gemma Scope SAE feature, and the entity token's input embedding norm), then ask why a perfect detector has no causal clout. They decompose the residual stream into attention and MLP contributions per layer by hooking pre-attention, mid-residual and post-feedforward positions, and they run franken-forward ablations zeroing attention output at specific layers. Finally they build a rotated direction by Gram-Schmidt — cosine of theta along detection plus sine of theta along the component of refusal orthogonal to detection — sweep theta, and validate the chosen angle on a held-out stress set split into obvious-fake, subtle-fake and three tiers of real entities. Cross-model replication rebuilds both directions from each model's own `lm_head`.

## What counts as an explanation here

A geometric relation between two directions, plus a mechanism for why the relation takes the value it does. The "explanation" of a failed intervention is not a circuit and not a feature dictionary; it is the statement that the detection subspace and the control subspace are nearly orthogonal, together with an account of what occupies the output mapping instead — here an entity-copy mechanism that outweighs the epistemic signal by an order of magnitude. It is explicitly partial and explicitly *not* predictive: the authors spend their whole discussion section arguing that the cosine is a signature of the dissociation and not an oracle for steerability, so the paper's explanatory form is a **characterisation** rather than a **predictor**. It also delivers a negative result about the obvious surrogate: a static angle does not tell you whether a behaviour will be steerable.

## How they judged success

Detection quality by AUC of a leave-one-out cross-validated logistic probe on residual activations per layer, plus accuracy for the single-neuron and embedding-norm readouts. Causal relevance by behavioural change under direction addition at matched alpha, required to significantly exceed both control families (100 random norm-matched directions, 10 irrelevant token-pair directions). The central geometric quantity is cosine to the intervention direction, benchmarked against the high-dimensional chance floor of about 1 over the square root of 2304. The geometric claim is corroborated by convergence across three tokenizers (SentencePiece, BPE, BPE) and by base-versus-instruct replication. The rotation result is scored on a pre-specified held-out stress set with per-tier refusal rates and a false-positive count over 55 real questions, at two alpha values. The steerability-oracle claim is a negative result scored on whether the cosine separates steerable from unsteerable behaviours when both directions are built the same independent way.

## What they found

For **format**, detection and control are literally one vector: the `lm_head` { minus ``` direction both classifies fencing and, added at layers 20–25 at alpha 3.5, eliminates fencing on 100% of queries with a sharp sigmoid (100% below alpha 2.5, 0% above alpha 3.5), 100% valid JSON and 100% correct answers, at an intervention magnitude of 0.6% of the activation norm, with 0/100 random and 0/10 irrelevant-token controls affecting it. The transplant test eliminates fencing 10/10 across Gemma 2-2B and Gemma 3-1B. For **hallucination**, all six detectors succeed — linear probe AUC 1.000 from layer 5, single head L9 H2 AUC 1.000, logit-lens entropy AUC 0.913 at layer 25, MLP neuron N578 at 88%, SAE feature F15356 at 88% fake and 0% real, embedding norm 83% — yet the hand-picked detection direction sits at cosine 0.12 (about 83 degrees) from the refusal direction, and the difference-in-means detection direction at −0.06, essentially the chance floor. The two detection directions are themselves nearly orthogonal (cosine 0.11), so detection is a class, not one direction. Interventions are correspondingly weak or wrong-signed: the difference-in-means direction *reduces* refusals and increases fabrication; the hand-picked detection direction pushed at alpha 15 on layers 20–25 drops fabrication from about 70% to about 40% with 0 false refusals on 50 real questions; SAE feature amplification up to 50× changes nothing; ablating the top 500 discriminative neurons at layer 15 changes nothing. The mechanism is stated numerically: at layer 25 the fake-versus-real gap along the detection direction is +49.8 (6.9× a random-direction mean) but the refusal gap along that same axis is −24.9, the wrong sign, while the residual component orthogonal to detection is a median about 12× larger — the entity-copy mechanism dominates, and it dominates *more* strongly on real entities, so it is a property of the output mapping rather than of the failure. Removing the detection projection leaves "Norlandia" still mapping to "Nor" at essentially unchanged probability. Signal autopsy: the MLP carries 5.66× more detection signal than attention (+42.3 versus +7.5), spread across layers 18–25 rather than concentrated; attention from layer 17 on is dispensable, and killing it raises fake-entity refusal from 60% to 65% with 100% of real answers intact, while a per-layer gate fails in all 9 tested conditions because the single-layer gap is about 0.01 — the cumulative ~50 exists only as a sum. A 15-degree rotation toward refusal recovers Type 2 (subtle) hallucinations from 13% to 60% refusal and Type 1 from 40% to 73% on the held-out stress set at 1.8% false positives (1/55). Across four models the cosine stays in [0.12, 0.20] — Llama 3.2-1B 0.20, Qwen 2.5-1.5B 0.16, Gemma 2-9B-it 0.13 — while the base-versus-instruct pair differs by 0.0003, and the ranking of the whole vocabulary along the detection direction returns the same semantic tokens across three unrelated tokenizers, including Chinese-script equivalents of the disfavoured city names in Qwen. The direction generalises but the alpha does not: Gemma needs 15, Llama needs |1|, Qwen needs 5, ratios spanning nearly 10×. The double dissociation is the paper's strongest structural claim: Llama refuses fake entities 100% by default with the *same* near-orthogonal geometry, so its correct behaviour cannot be travelling through the measured detection direction — detection without action on Gemma, and action not flowing from the measured detection on Llama.

## Limits and open problems they name

Sample sizes of 20–55 per condition, with the rotation sweep explicitly underpowered at N=20 and used only to pick an angle for validation. Three decoder-only families from 1B to 9B, so nothing above 10B, and Gemma 2-9B-it reproduces the geometry but resists steering, which is reported and left unexplained. One behaviour is studied in depth, with format as a positive control, and the authors say explicitly that whether the angle varies systematically across behaviours is open. The entity-copy dominance is documented but unexplained and untested outside entity-centric questions. Direction construction assumes approximate `lm_head` linearity, which holds at 1–3B but is untested at larger scale. The Type 1 / Type 2 difficulty tiers are boundary-soft despite being pre-specified structurally. Non-identifiability is conceded: five direction variants were tested and the contrast was invariant, but an untested direction could break the pattern. The single-layer gate failure (gap about 0.01) is reported as a root cause but no repair is offered. The relationship between this gap and the transport of representations under instruction tuning is proposed but not tested — testing it would need base-versus-instruct activation trajectories across the whole forward pass.

## What we take from it

A **measure** and a **warning**, and the warning is the more valuable half. The measure: the angle between the direction that best separates a behaviour and the direction that best causes it is a cheap, weight-computable statistic that comes out small-but-positive (0.12 to 0.20 against a 0.02 floor) and reproducible across tokenizers, which makes it a usable *signature* that a behaviour's read-out and its control do not share a mechanism. The warning: this number must not be promoted to a selection rule, because the paper's own section 8 kills that reading — measured consistently, the cosine sits near chance for steerable and unsteerable behaviours alike, format's apparent alignment comes only from using one vector in both roles, and what actually separates the steerable case is functional (does the control direction also work as a detector — AUC about 1 for format, about 0.7 for the refusal direction on hallucination). That is a direct constraint on us: a predictive white-box model may publish a geometric diagnostic, but the verdict on whether a component is a control handle has to be an intervention outcome, not an angle. Three further transferable pieces: the double dissociation is the cheapest template on disk for separating "knows" from "acts", and it is available for free whenever a second model in the family behaves correctly under identical geometry; the per-layer signal failure (each layer contributes about 0.01, only the sum reaches ~50) is a warning that a distributed signal can be real and still useless as a gate, which we should test before proposing any per-layer or per-feature trigger; and the order-of-magnitude dominance of an orthogonal component over the informative one, stable even on the correctly-answered cases, is a general caution that a large effect size along a probe direction does not imply the output mapping reads it.
